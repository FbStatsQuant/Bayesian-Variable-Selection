\section{The results in one page}\label{sec:results}

The data are $n$ observations of a response and $p$ predictors, with $p$ possibly much larger than $n$; the response
follows a sparse Gaussian linear model. The method is a sequential Monte Carlo sampler for the posterior over models
under a fixed prior, followed by a local search; nothing in it is tuned by the user. We prove five results. Table~\ref{tab:results}
lists them; Sections~\ref{sec:model-result}--\ref{sec:estimates} state them in the same format, with Result~5, a remark on
the rate of Result~1, in Section~\ref{sec:sharp}: what
the result says in words, why it matters, the statement, and its assumptions with the place in the assumptions section where each one is judged.

\paragraph{What ``asymptotic'' means.} The results are statements as $n\to\infty$, with $p$ allowed to grow polynomially
in $n$ and to exceed $n$. The explicit constants of the proofs are conservative; the
assumptions section judges the qualitative conditions, not the constants. One lower bound, Remark~\ref{rem:sharp}(a), holds at every $n\ge3$. At moderate $n$ and
large $p$ the posterior is not close to a point mass (Remark~\ref{rem:sharp}(c)); the theorems describe the limit.

\paragraph{What is not covered.} Parallel execution, and runs with more than the four independent sampler copies (the islands of
Section~\ref{sec:method}), are not covered by any theorem here; the
supplement (Section~\ref{sec:supp}) lists the further results we have, each with the reason it matters, and the open
questions.
