# Clarity read A4 (Opus): unified manuscript, §1–6

Reader: a statistician who knows Bayesian variable selection and Song & Liang (2015), new to this project. Source: `manuscript.pdf` (pdftotext -layout), draft of 17 Nov 2026, 22 pp. main text. Fidelity to sources was not checked. No item touches §5.1's first paragraph, a status box's existence, a theorem's mathematics, or a conditional comparison.

## 1. Ranked items

1. **Tables 2 and 3 are printed on pp. 42–43, after all appendices.** §4 opens with "Table 2 lists the five results", and §5.1 (fixed text) ends with "in the table". Both point to a page 20+ pages away. The tables are also numbered out of order (Tables 4 and 6 appear on pp. 29 and 31, before 2 and 3). This is the largest single cost to a first read.
2. **The order of Results in §4 does not match their numbers.** §4.3 is "Result 5", placed between Results 1 and 2, and its formal statement is "Remark 4.3", not a theorem. A reader looking for Result 2 finds Result 5 first, and then wonders whether a "Result" can be a remark.
3. **One run status has two names and two causes.** §3.4 Stage 1 says the run stops with status "resource limit" (in quotes) when its *pre-run estimate* exceeds the allocation. §3.5 says a run stopped by its *time guard* reports `resource_limit` (typewriter). It is not clear whether these are one status or two, or what "anything else it returns" refers to.
4. **§3 is not enough to read the calibration terms in §4.** "Tier 1", "diagnostic trigger", "fallback", and "restricted effective-sample-size fraction" all appear in §3.4 without definitions. Tier 0 is never named, and the particle count is called *N* in the text but *N_i* in Table 1. The event E_cal is introduced in §3.7 before the reader has been told that a run can still stop after its calibration has succeeded. That fact is only revealed in §4.6.2 ("…would produce if the run did not stop").
5. **The letter *b* is overloaded.** It is used for the block index in h_b and Σ_b (eq. 8, Δ), the inverse-gamma scale b₀, b_lo/b_hi in (FSβ), b̄ in 4.4(e), the leakage exponent n^b, and b_H. B is also the block-size cap. In §4.5 the text says "blocks … at most 16" and then "|Q| ≤ n^b" right next to it. Two smaller symbol issues: S̃₀ (start mode) sits beside S₀ (true model), and π is used both for the number and for the posterior, which the text has to explain twice in parentheses.
6. **The format promised at the start of §4 is not kept.** Results 2 and 5 have no Scope line. Result 4's Scope comes after its first subsection heading. The two Scope lines that do exist start in lower case ("Scope. every design…", "Scope. the independent…"). (FSβ) has no [§5.x] tag, although every other assumption has one.
7. **Section titles hide where the Song–Liang comparison is.** Both §1 and §5.1 send the reader to "Section 5.2 … theorem by theorem". But §5.2 is titled "Our designs, and what 'inside' means", and the comparison is its third paragraph. In the same way, §5.3.2 is titled "Assumptions of Results 1 and 2" but also judges (FSθ) and (GR), which Results 3 and 4 use.
8. **Result 4 says the same thing twice in a row.** "In words" and "Why it matters" (p. 15) are two long sentences with nearly the same content, and both carry the caveat "no claim … how likely". The caveat appears again in Remark 4.12(i) and §6.
9. **Seams between the three source documents show in duplicated text and internal vocabulary.** The C2+ sentence ("neither limit can be reached on any input R can represent") appears in both §3.7 and after Theorem 4.8. "Every completed run lies in E_cal" appears in both §3.7 and §4.6.2. Status boxes use house terms the reader cannot decode: "recorded master", "Theory department", "engineering experiments", "code of record", "frozen-run identities".
10. **There are two small defects and one unclear list.** p. 13 has "with the same constants, It also". In §1, "The designs are those of our simulation studies, a study of 2,400 fits, a study of strong common factors, and a planned set of runs…" can be read as four sets of designs or as one set followed by a list of three.

## 3. Notes

- The prose of §1 is clear. Its result list matches §4 except for the order of Result 5 (item 2).
- §3 is otherwise a good bridge into §4. Lemma 3.1, Example 3.2 and Figure 1 do real work.
- The repetition across §5.3.3 and §5.4 ("a numerical check of the conditions' population quantities, not yet complete, found…", stated twice, word for word) is a minor seam. It could be cut to a cross-reference in §5.4.

Overall: The argument is clear at the sentence level. The main costs are structural: tables printed 20 pages after the text that cites them, Results that appear out of their numbered order, and calibration and run-status terms that §3 uses before defining them. Fixing items 1–4 would make §3 → §4 → §5 a single read.
