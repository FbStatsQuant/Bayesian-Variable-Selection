The first design follows \citet[Sec.~4.1]{GriffinLatuszynskiSteel2021}, % EXACT: Biometrika 108(1) Sec 4.1 + Table 1; arXiv 1708.05678v3 pp.13--14
which is adapted from \citet[Sec.~3.4.1]{YangWainwrightJordan2016}: % SIMILAR: arXiv v1 p.12; beta, scaling, SNR, sigma^2 EXACT; their Sigma is e^{-|j-k|} (rho~0.37), not 0.6
autoregressive predictors with correlation $0.6^{\lvert j-k\rvert}$, ten nonzero coefficients scaled by
$\mathrm{SNR}\sqrt{\log p/n}$, and four signal strengths; \citet[Sec.~5.1]{LiangLivingstoneGriffin2022} use the same % EXACT: arXiv v3 Sec 5.1 p.15
design up to $p=50{,}000$.
The second design follows \citet[Sec.~3.1]{HastieTibshiraniTibshirani2020}: % EXACT: arXiv 1707.08692v2 Sec 3.1 pp.9--10 (Stat Sci page numbers unverified)
autoregressive predictors with $\rho\in\{0,0.35,0.7\}$, $(n,p,s)\in\{(50,1000,5),(100,1000,10)\}$, and a grid of ten
signal-to-noise ratios from $0.05$ to $6$.
The third design has equal pairwise correlation, $0.5$ as in \citet[Sec.~3.1]{ShinBhattacharyaJohnson2018} % EXACT: Stat Sinica 28(2) Sec 3.1; arXiv v4 pp.8--9
\citep[cf.][Ex.~4.2]{GeorgeMcCulloch1993} % SIMILAR: JASA p.886, "pairwise correlations of about .5"; never states Sigma
and the harder $0.8$ of \citet[Sec.~2.5]{DezeureBuhlmannMeierMeinshausen2015}; % EXACT: Stat Sci 30(4) p.543
its sample size and dimension, and the sparsity level $s_0=10$, are our own choice.
