## 3. The moderate-n figure (Remark 3.7; reviewer items 3.7 and top item 9)

**The reviewer's sum leaves out two factors. With them, the total is ≈ (s₀+1)√(log p/(πn)), which is of order one at
n = 200.**

This is a heuristic computed by the Theory helper. It has no registry row, so it must be worded as a heuristic.

Take the odds of S₀ ∪ {j} against S₀ for a null j. They are the product of three factors:

- **prior:** (s₀+1)/(2p−1−s₀) ≈ (s₀+1)/(2p), not 1/p (`results/sections/sec2_setting.tex:112-113`);
- **Occam factor:** ≈ n^{−1/2} (`sec2_setting.tex:134-135`);
- **fit gain:** (SS_{S₀∪j}/SS_{S₀})^{−ν} ≈ e^{Z_j²/2}, with Z_j² ≈ χ²₁.

The flaw is that **e^{Z²/2} has infinite mean**: φ(z)e^{z²/2} = 1/√(2π). So the average of the p factors is not O(1).
The largest Z_j² is about 2 log p. Truncating there gives E[e^{Z²/2}; Z² ≤ 2 log p] = **2√(log p/π)**, which is
≈ 3.4 at p = 10⁴ and ≈ 3.8 at p = 10⁵. The total is therefore

  Σ_{j∉S₀} π(S₀∪{j})/π(S₀) ≈ (p − s₀)·(s₀+1)/(2p)·n^{−1/2}·2√(log p/π) ≈ **(s₀+1)√(log p/(πn))**.

If p ≤ n^A, this is of order √(log n/n), the rate of Theorem 3.1. It is small only when n ≫ (s₀+1)² log p.

Comparison with Engineering's measurements (s₀ = 5, AR(0.6), strong signal, one draw each). The finite-n version
(Beta-distributed fit gain, ν = n/2 + a₀) changes the heuristic by less than 6%.

| (n, p) | heuristic | measured |
|---|---|---|
| (200, 10⁴) | 0.73 | 1.19 |
| (400, 10⁴) | 0.51 | 0.52 |
| (200, 10⁵) | 0.81 | 1.00 |
| (200, 10⁶) | 0.89 | 1.02 |

- The first three measured values are from `P1M:Docs/Experiments/P1M/reviews/PR12_review.md:111-113`. The (200, 10⁶)
  value is from `P1M:Docs/Experiments/P1M/FEASIBILITY_fable.md:151`.
- The measured best single addition is 0.9–4.2 nats below S₀; the heuristic's typical top term is about 3 nats below.
  Because the sum is heavy-tailed, one draw can exceed the heuristic.
- D-17 (`P1M:Docs/Experiments/P1M/DECISIONS.md:83`; "**CONFIRMED (HQ 11-15)**") records the range 0.5–1.2×.

## 4. The noiseless-gain / SS identity (reviewer top item 2; items 1.24, 2.31; Theorem 5.17)

**Answer:** γ(t|S) = ((n+1)/n)·(SS^μ_S − SS^μ_{S∪t}).

It is **not** SS_S − SS_{S∪t} at Xβ\*, for two reasons:

- **Factor:** there is a factor (n+1)/n.
- **Response:** the response is **μ = Xθ\*** with θ\* = Dβ\* (standardised X), not Xβ\*. μ is the mean of the centred
  raw response P⊥₁y^r (`sec2_setting.tex:73-78`), not of the method's y = P⊥₁y^r/s_y.

The ridge and g are the same: H_S = (n+1)G_S + I (g = n), λ₀ = 1/(n+1). b₀ and ν do not enter.

- Source: `Theory_work/Wave3/W3-SCR/STATEMENT.md:51-55`, which gives γ(t|S) := (x_tᵀR_Sμ)²/a(t|S) =
  ((n+1)/n)(Q^μ_S − Q^μ_{S∪t}), where Q^μ_S := μᵀμ − nμᵀX_SH_S^{−1}X_Sᵀμ, the draft's SS_S of (7) with y → μ.
- Status: W3-SCR:CC (claims.tsv:387, lines 20-72): "CHECKED".

**My algebraic check.** H_S = (n+1)(G_S + λ₀I), so n X_S H_S^{−1} X_Sᵀ = (n/(n+1))(I − R_S). Hence, for any vector v,

  SS_S(v) = vᵀv/(n+1) + (n/(n+1)) vᵀR_Sv.

By the block inverse of G_{S∪t} + λ₀I (Schur complement x_tᵀR_Sx_t + λ₀ = a(t|S)),

  R_S − R_{S∪t} = R_Sx_tx_tᵀR_S / a(t|S).

So SS_S(v) − SS_{S∪t}(v) = (n/(n+1))(x_tᵀR_Sv)²/a(t|S). Setting v = μ gives the identity. ✓

Numerical check (random X of size 30×6, S = {1,3}, t = 5): both sides are 6.092793336981 (they agree to 10⁻¹⁵).

The draft already prints this identity correctly (`results/sections/sec5_delivery.tex:336-343`). One phrase needs fixing:
"with $y$ replaced by its mean $\mu$" is inaccurate, because μ is not the mean of the standardised y.

**Proposed text for Writing** (`sec5_delivery.tex:341`): "where $\SSq^{\mu}_S$ is $\SSq_S$ of~\eqref{eq:marglik} with
$y$ replaced by the mean signal $\mu=X\tstar$ (the mean of the centred response $P^\perp_{\one}\yr$): up to the factor
$(n+1)/n$, the gain is the decrease of the method's own quadratic form when $t$ joins $S$ and the response is replaced by
