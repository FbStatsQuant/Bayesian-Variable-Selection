FIVE PRIORITY PAPERS FOR YOUR CURRENT SMC PROJECT

Scope of this selection

These are my five priority readings for developing, validating, and positioning
your existing collapsed subset-space SMC. They are selected for their direct
architectural fit and the questions raised by your experiments, not simply by
publication date or citation count. The numbering below is a suggested reading
order. Original bibliography numbers are retained in selection_manifest.json.

Your latest experiments show accurate evidence and especially accurate
Rao-Blackwellized PIPs for the control posterior at p=30. Strong correlation
still increases computational difficulty, while earlier experiments show that
accurately estimating a posterior need not imply exact recovery of the true
support. The five papers address complementary questions: what to benchmark
against, how to move through model space, how to estimate evidence, and how to
express discoveries and uncertainty from the joint posterior.

1. THE ESSENTIAL SMC REFERENCE AND BENCHMARK

Christian Schafer and Nicolas Chopin (2013).
Sequential Monte Carlo on large binary sampling spaces.
Statistics and Computing 23, 163-184.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-011-9299-z
Open version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.6037
File: 01_Schafer_Chopin_2013_Binary_SMC.pdf
Original bibliography number: 7

Why it is essential: This is the closest foundational computational reference
for your method: SMC on binary model indicators, with proposals that learn
dependence through conditional logistic models. It helps establish both a
credible benchmark and the distinction between established binary SMC and your
composite initialization and global correction approach.

Connection to your architecture: Its dependence-aware whole-model proposals
can inform rejuvenation using your collapsed scores. A Metropolis correction
must target the current annealed distribution. Learning proposals from the
population also requires appropriate treatment of adaptation.

Main question to take from it: Does your composite start improve efficiency
relative to a strong binary SMC baseline when both use the same posterior and
comparable computational budgets?

2. THE FIRST MUTATION-KERNEL UPGRADE TO INVESTIGATE

Xitong Liang, Samuel Livingstone, and Jim Griffin (2022).
Adaptive random neighbourhood informed Markov chain Monte Carlo for
high-dimensional Bayesian variable selection.
Statistics and Computing 32, Article 84.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-022-10137-8
Open version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11747
File: 02_Liang_et_al_2022_PARNI.pdf
Original bibliography number: 1

Why it is essential: PARNI combines adaptive random neighborhoods with locally
informed proposals. It is a strong candidate for improving movement among
models when coordinate-at-a-time updates become inefficient.

Connection to your architecture: It can use the current annealed subset score
and your score cache. It does not require restoring coefficient sampling or
changing the final statistical target. Retain a final coordinate Gibbs sweep
if preserving your present Rao-Blackwellized PIP construction.

Main question: Can it reduce PIP and evidence error per unit computation in
the difficult correlated designs? Improvement at your p=30 is a hypothesis to
test, not a consequence of its high-dimensional results.

3. THE MOST RELEVANT RECENT PAPER FOR ANNEALING AND EVIDENCE

Saifuddin Syed, Alexandre Bouchard-Cote, Kevin Chern, and Arnaud Doucet (2026).
Optimized annealed sequential Monte Carlo samplers.
Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B, advance article qkag082.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkag082
Open version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12057
File: 03_Syed_et_al_2026_Optimized_Annealed_SMC.pdf
Original bibliography number: 2

Why it is essential: Your project estimates the normalizing constant as well
as PIPs. This paper tunes annealing across rounds with a criterion related to
normalizing-constant variance. That addresses an important part of your method
which posterior-only selection algorithms do not address.

Connection to your architecture: Your normalized composite distribution is a
tractable reference. An independently learned schedule, frozen during a fresh
production run, is a useful comparison with your current online adaptation.

Main question: How should particles, temperatures, and mutation effort be
allocated for reliable evidence at a fixed budget? Its performance assumptions
do not automatically hold for your correlated binary target, and freezing the
schedule alone does not fix poor exploration.

4. THE FIRST PAPER FOR TURNING PARTICLES INTO CONTROLLED DISCOVERIES

Asher Spector and Lucas Janson (2025).
Controlled Discovery and Localization of Signals via Bayesian Linear Programming.
Journal of the American Statistical Association 120(549), 460-471.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2024.2347667
Open version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17208
File: 04_Spector_Janson_2025_BLIP.pdf
Original bibliography number: 5

Why it is essential: With correlated predictors, evidence can support a group
containing a signal while being insufficient to identify its exact member.
BLiP optimizes discoveries at different resolutions using posterior group
probabilities and Bayesian error-control constraints.

Connection to your architecture: Compute group events from your joint subset
particles. This is a postprocessing step and leaves the sampler and target
unchanged. Marginal PIPs alone do not determine these group probabilities.

Main question: Can your posterior produce more useful, appropriately qualified
discoveries than a fixed PIP threshold? A group containing at least one signal
does not establish that every active member has been identified. Bayesian error
control is not automatically uniform frequentist error control.

5. THE FIRST PAPER FOR EXPRESSING JOINT MODEL UNCERTAINTY

J. E. Griffin (2025).
Expressing and Visualizing Model Uncertainty in Bayesian Variable Selection
Using Cartesian Credible Sets.
Bayesian Analysis 20(4), 1371-1397.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/25-BA1568
Open version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12323
File: 05_Griffin_2025_Cartesian_Credible_Sets.pdf
Original bibliography number: 6

Why it is essential: Accurate individual PIPs can hide which variables enter
together or compete as alternatives. Cartesian credible sets organize model
configurations into interpretable collections. This makes the joint output of
your SMC useful beyond marginal inclusion summaries.

Connection to your architecture: Use the joint particles and evaluate proposed
sets against exhaustive posterior probabilities at p=30. Interpretation blocks
need not match the random blocks used to initialize the sampler.

Main question: Which competing configurations does the posterior actually
support? The general construction uses approximation and search, so verify
joint posterior coverage rather than assuming that separate block-level
coverage statements combine automatically.

Why this shortlist differs from a prior-selection shortlist

This set prioritizes the existing SMC architecture, its benchmark, its evidence
estimator, and the use of its joint posterior output. It is not a claim that a
sampler upgrade alone will increase exact support recovery under the same
posterior. If the sole objective is to change support-recovery behavior through
the prior, the powered-correlation paper remains a high-priority additional
reading; its PDF was unavailable in the downloaded collection. The mixtures of
g-priors and multiplicity-adjustment papers also remain in the main folder.
