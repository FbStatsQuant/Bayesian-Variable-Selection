**Within-dataset contrast** (the 3 datasets with one escalated and one non-escalated seed, which removes the dataset effect): AR r01 −0.01116, IND −0.01001, ARweak r01 −0.00217; mean −0.0078, paired t = −2.75 on 2 df, p = 0.11, **95 % CI −0.0199 … +0.0044**; all three contrasts negative.
Other checks: the 17 non-escalated alone have mean −0.0026 (95 % CI −0.0052 … −0.00005, p = 0.046), so V − T is below zero even without escalation. The AR-strong tier-0 fits (7) average −0.0042, and AR r04, which never escalated, has the largest per-seed gap of the rung (s2 −0.0171, larger in size than every escalated fit) and −0.0066 for s1. Exploratory: the gap is correlated with T cost (Spearman −0.47, p 0.036) and weakly with the tier-0 rebuild count (−0.34, p 0.15); the three escalated fits all hit the cap of 14 rebuilds and are the costliest, so escalation, a hard calibration and cost are the same fits and cannot be separated here.
**What this can show:** the three escalated T fits sit at the negative end (all negative, 1.3–2.4 island s.e., about four times the non-escalated mean); with fits treated as exchangeable this is unlikely under no difference (p ≈ 0.01–0.03).
**What it cannot show:** (i) **causation**: escalation is not assigned, it marks fits whose calibration was hardest (all three at the 14-rebuild cap), so "escalated" is confounded with dataset, seed and calibration difficulty; the within-dataset comparison, the only one that controls the dataset, has n = 3 and its interval includes 0. (ii) **Absence at tier 0**: the non-escalated mean is also negative and the largest single gap (AR r04 s2, −0.0171) is a tier-0 fit. (iii) **Independence**: the two seeds of a dataset share data, the tests treat 20 fits as exchangeable, no multiplicity adjustment, the grouping was chosen after seeing W0–W1 (by HQ's question, not pre-registered), so the p-values are descriptive. (iv) **Which arm is biased**: the gap is V − T; nothing here says whether V is low or T high (no truth for log Z; log Z_T lower bounds sit 0.09–0.22 below both). Back of the envelope, the log-of-mean (Jensen) bias of an island-mean estimator, ≈ σ_rel²/2 with σ_rel ≈ the island s.e. 0.003–0.01, is ≤ 5e-5, two orders below the gaps, so it is not the delta-method effect. A hypothesis for the precision study, not a result: finite-N bias of the SMC evidence estimate that shrinks with the particle count (V 16 × 2,500 per fit, T tier 0 4 × 10⁴, T tier 1 4 × 4 × 10⁴).
**Plainly:** the gap is larger where T escalated, and the data do not allow more than that. It does not alter E1 (pass), and no other criterion (HPM, PIP, F, predictive d_H ≤ 1.4 % of the limit) shows any difference between the arms. Any "tied to escalation" statement beyond the description above is a hypothesis for the precision study (MARGINAL's diagnosis, if the review lands there).

## 5. Tooling notes (not criteria)
**(a) The 1-ulp xeval self-check miss (HQ H-24 (2); one cheap check only).** W2 AR r01 T s2: xeval `h2` 0.007346601991972235 vs the engine's `predictive.h2` 0.007346601991972124, difference 1.1e-16 = 2⁻⁵³, deterministic in xeval at 1, 3 and 8 threads. **Over the rung 39 of 40 fits are bit-exact (W0 4/4, W1 12/12, W2 11/12, W3 12/12); no new miss in W3.** The check I could do without touching NOTS, from the two binaries' own `binary` blocks: the xeval build (Owls, `g++ 11.5.0 20240719 (Red Hat 11.5.0-17)`, glibc 2.34, `/lib64/libm.so.6` sha256 c8933ed5…) and the NOTS engine build (`g++ 14.3.0`, glibc 2.34, libm sha256 98677841…) have **identical flags** (`-O2 -fno-math-errno -ffp-contract=off -march=x86-64-v2`), the same glibc version string and the same libm self-test pass; they **differ in the compiler (11.5 vs 14.3) and in the libm file** (and the two hosts can pick different CPU-dispatched libm variants). So the cause is one of these, not isolated: **unconfirmed, not pursued** (no rebuild, no NOTS call). Effect: none on any criterion; the miss is a last bit of `1 − X` on one fit.
**(b) `bin_version.json` loader-error flaw (`collect_nots.sh`).** Line 18 runs `"$S/bin/smcbvs4" version > "$R/bin_version.json" 2>&1 || true` without sourcing the batch's `env.sh`, so every collected `_batch_*/bin_version.json` (all 10 batch units, W0–W3) holds `…libstdc++.so.6: version 'CXXABI_1.3.15' not found` instead of the version block. Binary provenance is not lost: every record's `binary` block (git_commit, sha256, ISA, libm self-test) and each batch's `scripts/bin/smcbvs4.sha256` carry it, and deploy/presubmit read `version` with `env.sh`. One-line fix: source `env.sh` in `collect_nots.sh` (not made here; ops tool).
**(c) xeval data-guard history.** `smcbvs4_xeval` (PR #85, 1b6ddd59) compared the whole-file `data.bin` hashes, which embed each arm's binary block, so it refused every T-vs-V pair ("laws built on different data.bin"). For W0 the two cells were computed with a tmp-only build with the guards bypassed (`XEVAL_SKIP_DATAHASH`, binary sha256 066b70f8…, nothing else changed; the `data.bin` X/y sections and `truth.json` X/y sha were equal). HQ H-21 (4) OK'd the fix; **PR #87** (6dbfe0fc, merged as b89cba4d) compares the X/y section hashes instead. W1–W3 (18 cells) used that build (afef0e46…) **with no bypass**, and the guard accepted every pair. **Re-check done here:** the two W0 pairs re-run with the PR #87 binary, guard on, give `h2` and `se` **bit-equal** to the bypassed values (`W3_readback/cross_w0_recheck/`), so all 20 cells now stand on the unpatched guard.
