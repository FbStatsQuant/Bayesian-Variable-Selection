### 0.3 Conventions used

- **Class** (exactly one per row): CONFIRMED-D/E/DE = a draft entry whose guarantee (in whole or in the part marked “partial”) is independently stated by Seat D and/or Advisor E; DRAFT-ONLY = draft entry with no fresh-eyes match; NEW-D/E/DE = in D and/or E, no draft entry (a “partial” match still counts as a match); OBLIGATION-ONLY = an N item matched by nothing else; KNOWN-FALSE / PARTLY-FALSE = restates, or relies for one part on, an index §4 known-false row.
- **“partial: …”** is written whenever an item matches only part of a draft entry or vice versa; the unmatched part is named.
- **One row per guarantee.** Multi-clause draft entries (S1, S2, A2, A4, R5, V1, V2, V3, D2) are split across rows; the draft entry then appears in several rows. Seat D's and E's items appear in every row whose guarantee they carry.
- **Status column.** Registry/index statuses are copied verbatim (author status, then the registry's SF/XF cells) with IDX and REG lines. Where the draft entry's own wording is the only source it is quoted with its LST line. “D/E side: proposal (author's PROOF SKETCH, unchecked)” is never upgraded, even when D/E restate a PROVED registry result: then the registry status shown is the *draft entry's*, and D/E *restate* it.
- **Known-false vs COUNTEREXAMPLE rows.** A COUNTEREXAMPLE row in the registry refutes some claim. An item that *asserts* the refuted claim, or relies on it, is a known-false hit (§3.1). An item that *restates the counterexample itself as a negative result* is consistent with the registry; it keeps its natural class, the row notes say “negative/limitation”, and §3.2 lists it so it is not mistaken for new. Rows with ⚑ in the index are registry CONFLICT rows.
- **Numbering of index conflicts.** “index §5.A Cn” = registry's own conflicts (IDX 1175–1200); “index §5.B Bn” = registrar-found conflicts (IDX 1208–1218); 26 + 11 = 37.
- **Fidelity flags** (v3 without LS0; ideal vs coded grid; equal-weight vs Ẑ-weighted pooling; H-ORTH only) are in the notes wherever the registry or source states them; “not stated” is not repeated.
