# Gaussian spike-and-slab BVS using PARNI-B-RM

The primary posterior is exactly the current SMC target. The sampler changes the computational method, not its prior or likelihood. There is no intercept, response centering, predictor rescaling or support-size cap. The supplied folds are recorded but unused.

For active indices S, G=X_S'X_S, b=X_S'y, Lambda=(G+I)/n and A=G+Lambda. The log unnormalized model posterior is

`0.5 log|Lambda| - 0.5 log|A| - (0.01+n/2) log(0.02+y'y-b'A^-1 b) + |S|log(0.2) + (p-|S|)log(0.8)`.

This integrates beta_S|sigma2,S ~ N(0,sigma2 Lambda^-1), sigma2 ~ IG(shape=.01,rate=.01), with independent Bernoulli(.2) indicators. Omitted constants do not depend on S. The conditional posterior beta mean is A^-1 b. Cholesky factorization avoids inverses in production, and log arithmetic avoids overflow in proposal weights. Models use 30-bit integer masks. The cache stops growing at 200,000 models; reaching this limit changes computation time, not the target or transition.

## Published transition and its implementation

Liang, Livingstone and Griffin (2022), *Adaptive random neighbourhood informed Markov chain Monte Carlo for high-dimensional Bayesian variable selection*, Statistics and Computing 32:84, DOI [10.1007/s11222-022-10137-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-022-10137-8), equations (4), (5), (28), (31), (33)--(35), Algorithm 3, is the primary source. Pages 10--13 define the ordered subproposals, reverse order and balancing-function acceptance simplification. Pages 4 and 15 provide PIP shrinkage/default adaptation and freezing after burn-in. The complete PDF and extracted text are retained in `Papers/Benchamrks/06_PARNI`.

The inspected [authors' repository](https://github.com/XitongLiang/The-PARNI-scheme) is pinned at commit `3b95d669e9bcf6df9e2ba46fb89fc536922e12e2` in the local paper collection with per-file SHA-256 records. Relevant functions are `R/sampler/PARNI.R`, `other supportive functions/updates.R` and `make_hyper_par.R`. Its default preprocessing centers y and its target supports different Gaussian prior families. Consequently this campaign does not execute that preprocessing or substitute its score. `engine/src/parni.cpp` implements the published kernel for our target and is independently checked against a dense Gaussian integral.

Each step includes coordinates independently with probabilities A_j=min(1,pi_tilde_j/(1-pi_tilde_j)) when absent and D_j=min(1,(1-pi_tilde_j)/pi_tilde_j) when present. Selected coordinates are uniformly randomly ordered. At a coordinate, the flip weight is `omega*min(1,r)`, where r includes BOTH the posterior ratio and the ratio of random-neighborhood inclusion probabilities. The stay weight is `1-omega`. The reverse proposal visits the same intermediate models in reverse coordinate order. The final Metropolis ratio includes the target, full neighborhood and forward/reverse ordered-path factors. The code also verifies its equality to the product of forward/reverse normalizers, independently at every step. Empty neighborhoods and self proposals are legal.

During warmup, each chain accumulates Rao--Blackwell conditional PIPs from the accepted current state, uses `pi_tilde=.001+.998*RB_average`, and adapts omega by the epsilon-logit Robbins--Monro update with target acceptance .65, step t^(-.7) and epsilon=.1/p. All adaptation freezes before retained samples. The starting omega is .5 and starting adaptive PIPs are .2. No parallel tempering or Kiefer--Wolfowitz adaptation is used.

## Frozen campaign choices and deviations

Four separately seeded chains run sequentially on one CPU. Their initial states are empty, full, Bernoulli(.2) and Bernoulli(.5). Chain c uses `(method_seed+104729*c) %% 2147483646 + 1`, with Mersenne-Twister/Inversion/Rejection RNG settings. Each chain adapts independently; this is four valid PARNI chains rather than the authors' shared-adaptation multiple-chain acceleration. Production has 5,000 warmup and **80,000 retained iterations per chain**, without thinning. The initial 20,000-draw pilot had a genuine S5 indicator Rhat failure (1.01155), in addition to the discrete-quantile diagnostic issue described below. A bounded 80,000-draw S5 rerun passed the corrected strict checks, motivating the uniform 80,000-draw budget for all scenarios. This is no assurance that all 500 datasets will pass; individual failures remain failures. Smoke mode uses 300/600 and is explicitly marked non-production. Any explicit budget override is likewise marked non-frozen validation.

The primary PIP estimator averages conditional inclusion probabilities across retained states and all four chains. Empirical indicator PIPs are saved separately. The primary selector uses strictly `PIP > .5`. The posterior-BFDR .1 sensitivity selects the largest descending-PIP prefix whose average 1-PIP is at most .1; it is not a claim of frequentist FDR control. The coefficient estimate averages conditional beta means over retained model draws.

## Diagnostics and retained evidence

The `posterior` package computes rank-normalized split Rhat, bulk ESS, mean ESS and mean MCSE. Required thresholds are Rhat <=1.01, bulk/mean ESS >=400 and RB-PIP MCSE <=.01. Model-size and log-target traces must satisfy their Rhat/ESS checks. Nonconstant indicator traces supply additional Rhat/ESS checks. Every failed or unassessable required diagnostic makes `converged=FALSE`; failures are not replaced with null-model results.

Standard `posterior::ess_tail` is retained verbatim, including NA. It is based on the 5% and 95% quantile indicators `x <= q`. In a discrete model space, the upper quantile can equal the maximum observed score, making that indicator identically one even when model scores mix well. The same issue occurs for binary inclusion indicators. Aki Vehtari explicitly explains why this quantile diagnostic is inapplicable for binary tails in the [Stan discussion](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/bernoulli-variables-in-generated-quantities-causing-tail-ess-warning/17740); see also the [official tail-ESS definition](https://mc-stan.org/posterior/reference/ess_tail.html).

For the required localized checks we therefore separately report **discrete tail-event diagnostics**: at each pooled 5% and 95% quantile, evaluate both `x <= q` and `x >= q`. Save every threshold, actual event probability, event ESS/Rhat and applicability status. The minimum bulk ESS across nonconstant event indicators must be >=400, and the largest event Rhat must be <=1.01. Globally constant events contain no information and remain explicitly unassessable; they are excluded from this event minimum. A nonconstant event with unassessable ESS/Rhat fails, and a trace with no estimable events fails except for the explicitly described boundary exemption. With no ties the opposing events are complements; with atoms they additionally assess mixing of the atom itself. These event diagnostics are not labeled standard quantile ESS and do not assert quantile MCSE. The original full-budget pilot exposed this diagnostic-applicability error before release; the correction does not waive genuine Rhat or precision failures.

Identical constant indicator traces have undefined ESS/Rhat, recorded as NA rather than invented numbers. A constant RB trace is exempt only when all chains have the same constant indicator and its conditional PIP is within 1e-8 of that matching boundary. Every exemption is listed, with an explicit warning that its mixing cannot be assessed. Constant nonboundary RB traces and constant model-size/log-target traces fail. These checks cannot rule out undiscovered modes, especially with correlated predictors.

Raw results preserve every retained model draw with no thinning, log-target/model-size traces, acceptance probabilities and indicators, Hamming jumps, neighborhood sizes, warmup model/log-target/omega histories and final frozen tuning values. Indicator matrices are represented exactly by the model's bits. RB conditional probabilities are stored once per unique retained model, at full double precision; they are deterministic functions of that model and the fixed data. `parni_expand_raw(fit$raw)` reconstructs the complete indicator and RB matrices, including their original ordering. Packing checks exact equality for every reconstructed RB and indicator entry before discarding duplicate rows; round-trip reconstruction is tested. All diagnostics are computed on full uncompressed chains before packing. This lossless representation reduces storage without thinning or changing any estimate. Acceptance rates include self proposals and are distinguished from mutation rates. Warmup is excluded from reported PIPs and coefficient means. Seeds, full controls, package versions, cache statistics, per-chain PIPs, diagnostic tables and warnings are saved.

## Build and tests

Dependencies are R >=4.0, Rcpp (currently installed NOTS version 1.1.2), posterior >=1.4.0 (the actual release must be locked by the shared environment) and the campaign engine bvsparni 0.1.0. There is no runtime download or compilation. In a NOTS bootstrap job, run `Rcpp::compileAttributes("PARNI/engine")`, then `R CMD INSTALL --library="$R_LIBS_USER" PARNI/engine`. Generated Rcpp wrappers must be copied into the local source snapshot before final release hashing. The installed engine must be rebuilt whenever its canonical C++ changes. The shared environment manifest records exact binaries and package versions.

`tests.R` defines `run_method_tests()` and runs no experiments on import. It checks the Gaussian integral using an independent n-by-n marginal covariance calculation; posterior coefficient means; nonzero response means; p=30 integer boundaries; invalid input failures; fixed-parameter detailed balance by exhaustive enumeration of every neighborhood, permutation and path in a three-variable target; explicit reverse-path probabilities; repeatability; finite-cache behavior; and MCMC PIPs/means against exact enumeration. These tests must run on NOTS compute nodes. Passing smoke plumbing is distinct from validating production MCMC precision across the five scenarios.
