Reproduced findings and concrete fixes:

1. **MAJOR - recycled PID can kill unrelated processes.** `ci/run_ci.py:284-302,344`: after `job.close()`, `kill_tree` snapshots descendants and repeatedly runs `taskkill /T /F /PID` using only numbers. A replacement process, or children whose recorded parent PID has been recycled, can be selected. Mocking the actual process table with unrelated replacement PID 4242 and child 4243 produced taskkill commands for **both**. A second probe with 4242 absent and only unrelated historical child `4243:4242` still requested killing 4243; its parent PID can refer to a generation predating this CI run. No real unrelated process was killed. Windows retained handles protect the original process identity while held, but do not establish the identity of historical descendants or retry victims; `/T` performs further unchecked traversal. POSIX also signals an unverified numeric process-group ID after waiting for its leader. **Fix:** on Windows use only the retained Job Object and process handles, including handle-based launch-failure cleanup; remove the PID fallback. On POSIX retain a supervised group identity/lifetime until cleanup, rather than signal a possibly reused group after reaping its leader. Test replacement identities and retry races.

2. **MAJOR - cleanup is not confined to owned disposable worktrees.** `run_ci.py:106-140,170`: `_inside(root, root)` is true. With a registered worktree equal to `SANDBOX_ROOT` and no valid owner, an intercepted actual `Sandbox.__enter__` requested worktree removal, `rmtree(root)`, and removal of **`root + ".owner"`, outside the root**. A non-CI worktree beneath the root with a missing/malformed owner is likewise treated as disposable. Additionally, unscoped `git worktree prune` can remove administrative files for missing unrelated worktrees outside this sandbox. `test_lifecycle.py:12-36,195-197` creates/deletes its copy outside that root, also prunes globally, and starts its `try/finally` only after copying/importing (setup failures leak it). **Fix:** require strict child containment, a tool-created authenticated manifest and valid ownership before stale removal; independently validate every deletion target, including owner paths; remove global prune. Put the test's temporary worktree beneath the same managed root and protect all setup with cleanup. A recycled live owner PID currently conservatively skips cleanup; it does not prove ownership.

No end-of-enumeration error is checked. Missing live owners can consequently be classified dead and their active sandboxes removed. Add initial-failure AND partial-failure scenarios to the suite.
