The definitions match 00_COMMON §1–2: G, z, H, D, Q, ν, a₀ = b₀ = 0.01, m(·) (the constant C_n > 0 cancels in every ratio), w, ϖ (with the indicator for |S| ≤ K), Ω, K, γ₀, d, γ_φ, q_φ, the round with m = p + B + 2 labels in uniform order, and X_{e⁻}. The explicit Gibbs kernel comes from T09(ii), which I have not seen (unverified). It does agree with 00_COMMON's "pre-draw inclusion probability" and with a_j.

I checked that both conclusions are true:
- **(i)** Only block b(j) changes, so a_j = [(k+1)/(2p−k−1)] · [BF(U+j)/BF(U)]^{1−φ} · [BF(S+j)/BF(S)]^φ, where U = S∩C_b.
  - For any S and j ∉ S, Q_{S+j} ≤ Q_S. This follows from X H⁻¹ Xᵀ = (n+1)⁻¹[I − ((n+1)XXᵀ + I)⁻¹], which is monotone in the Loewner order. Also Q_S ≥ yᵀy/(n+1) > 0.
  - The determinant lemma gives D_{S+j}/D_S = (1 + (n+1)u)/(1 + v), with u = x_jᵀM⁻¹x_j, M = (n+1)X_SX_Sᵀ + I, and v = x_jᵀ(X_SX_Sᵀ + I)⁻¹x_j. Since u ≤ v ≤ (n+1)u, this ratio lies in [1, n+1].
  - So every BF ratio is at least (n+1)^{−1/2}, and hence a ≥ r_k. Because x/(1+x) is increasing, o ≥ h_k. Setting φ = 0 gives the π₀ claim.
- **(ii)** The probability that the first a labels are J's Gibbs labels is exactly C(m,a)⁻¹. At step i the state has size k+i ≤ K−1, so that step adds its coordinate with probability at least h_{k+i}.

**Item 1 ((ii), last sentence; the a = 1 case and (ii-neg)): APPROVED**

The first half is the a = 1 case of (ii), since C(m,1) = m. Here "unconditional" means not conditioning on the order of the labels, as opposed to (iii). The theorem file identifies the v2 clause only by the quote. Its literal content is the universal claim P(visit S+j | S) ≥ h_k, and the reading is exactly its negation, over the same domain as (ii). This is not weaker than the original, which does not claim that the factor 1/m is sharp in order.

Using p = 2, where the exact branch applies, matches the original's own evidence. The J8 instance (p = 4, n = 200) is also on the exact branch, because Σ_{k≤4} C(4,k) = 16. So the original itself treats the round kernel separately from the branch.

The claim also looks true. Take p = 2 (so B = 1, singleton blocks and m = 5), with y = x_i, x_j orthogonal to 1 and to x_i, and S = ∅.
- o_j(∅) = h_0(1 + O(1/n)), because BF({j}) = n^{−1/2} and d({j}) = 0.
- The state leaves ∅ with probability ≈ 1 at whichever of Gibbs i or the independence step comes first. The probability that Gibbs j comes before both is 1/3.
- Every other route to {j} is exponentially small. There is no neighbour edge because r_ij = 0, and swaps or independence moves to {j} carry tiny ratios.
- So P(visit) ≈ h_0/3 < h_0.

This assumes the swap and independence steps are Metropolis–Hastings moves with π₀ proposals (unverified).

**Item 2 ((iii), filtration reading): APPROVED WITH CHANGE**

The reading is faithful. The expectation form is needed because, in the pilot, a random φ_e makes "P(X_{e⁻} = S) o_j(S, φ_e)" not a number. The label indicator is needed because without it the original display would also count steps that are not Gibbs-j. The original product form is recovered when φ_e is non-random. Conditioning on 𝓒 needs no CAL assumption, since 𝓒 is F_{e⁻}-measurable for final-run steps. Nothing is weakened.

Required changes:
- (a) Define F_{e⁻} as the σ-field generated by the data and every stream variate consumed before step e's own draw, including permutations, resampling and adaptive choices. "Everything realised before" could be read as including pre-generated stream values. That reading would make (iii-a) false, with conditional probabilities of 0 or 1.
- (b) Make φ_e ∈ [0,1] an explicit hypothesis or a sourced property (00_COMMON §2: "production φ ∈ [0,1]"), not a parenthetical remark. For φ > 1 the bound can fail. In that case a_j = prior × full × (full/block)^{φ−1}, where full = BF(S+j)/BF(S) and block = BF(U+j)/BF(U). The ratio full/block can be arbitrarily small, because the block ratio can be very large.

**Item 3 ((iv-a), visit versus terminal occupancy): APPROVED WITH CHANGE**

The original sentence mainly classifies the conclusions. Its minimal mathematical content is that the visit bound does not transfer to terminal occupancy: there is an instance with P(X_m = S+j) < h_k/m. That existence claim is fine.

The original's J8 evidence supports it. The reported endpoint occupancy is 6.9·10⁻⁷, against h_k/m = 0.0659/8 ≈ 0.0082 (m = 8, since B = 2). Even with k = 1 the bound is 0.0230/8 ≈ 0.0029, still far above.

The extra clause "moreover along a sequence of admissible data sets the ratio … tends to 0" is stronger than anything in the original. It appears true: in the p = 2 instance above, ending at {j} requires i to be absent at the end, which is exponentially small in n, while h_0/5 is of order n^{−1/2}. Even so, it is not the original's content.

Required change: delete the "moreover … tends to 0" clause, or move it to a remark labelled as supplementary and not part of the original, whose failure would not affect the verdict.
