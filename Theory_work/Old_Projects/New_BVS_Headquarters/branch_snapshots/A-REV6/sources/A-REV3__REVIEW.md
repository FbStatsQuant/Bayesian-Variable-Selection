## W3: step-by-step reasoning and required repairs

**Partition and eigenvalues.** The population covariance has a fixed positive spectral lower bound. A Gaussian sample-covariance tail plus a union bound over at most p squared entries gives the stated correlation error; normalization is harmless because sample variances concentrate uniformly away from zero. Multiplying the entrywise error by `O(log n)` gives `O(log^(3/2)(n)/sqrt(n))`, sufficient for locked SE. This is stronger than checking only the true support. W1-T03R's comparison unions are restrictions of that family: j0 is eventually below m_n and m-sharp is bounded by m_n. Its weak Gram condition follows from `(n-1) phi0^2 -> infinity`, not from a correlation bound exceeding every g0.

For P1, before any between-group merge, all remaining incomplete groups have an available merge at distance at most D_in. Every between-group merge has distance at least D_out. Once each group is a node, a node containing two groups has size at least 2g>B; the top-down cut cannot retain it. Next-fit cannot combine two retained groups. Ties within a group do not affect this argument. Eventually B=16, so 9<=g<=16 is necessary for this particular construction. Small-n groups of size five do not prove it.

**Lemma B.** The population residual `W = X_V - X_U Gamma` has Gaussian rows and is independent of `(X_U,y)` because its covariance with both X_U and the signal is zero. For a-group neutral columns, conditional independence through the hub survives further conditioning on other a-group neutral columns; independent components factorize even when A includes several components. Residualizing W on the random span of X_U leaves an isotropic Gaussian column space of dimension j in a space of dimension `n-1-|U|`. Its projection fraction onto a fixed residual response has exactly the displayed Beta law. The masked hubs and y are jointly Gaussian and mutually independent; conditional on y their normalized centered directions are independent sphere points with a distribution not depending on y. This yields the joint independent chi-square limit.

These claims are **not** statements that the same Beta law holds after conditioning on the event that the partition recovered the groups, or for arbitrary response-selected A. W3 does not need either statement: define group statistics everywhere first, bound their unconditional truncated expectations, and only then use equality with the actual-block quantities on the recovery event. This is the crucial random-partition quantifier check.

**Truncation and P3.** The Beta-density exponent after multiplying by `(1-X)^(-nu)` is exactly `-[(u+j+3)/2+.01]`. The Gamma-ratio bound works for integer and half-integer j/2. The chi-square ratio bound and union bound cover the `p 2^g` block subsets. Independence between different block statistics is unnecessary. On the good event, the score ratios telescope to the multi-column RSS ratio; Jensen is applied to `a_hj 1_good`, whose 1/j power is `a_hj^(1/j) 1_good`. Averaging the D_h bounds divides by p-s and yields the claimed order. A good event of probability tending to one is sufficient for O_P even without a polynomial failure rate. The finite family of reference-deletion gains has positive limiting partial covariances, so e is exponentially small. Locked T22(i) applies to any fixed feasible reference and any positive table partition. No use of locked T22(iii)'s truth-visibility assumptions is hidden here.

**P4 and P5.** For one column the exact determinant ratio is n, not n+1; H=n squared and the displayed kappa_n are correct. The size-prior increment is `(k+1)/(2p-1-k)`. Thus adding the c masked hubs gives the constant `sum log((s+i)/2)` and half a chi-square_c, with the reference log mass tending to zero. The asymptotic direct proof is valid for all p between the specified polynomial bounds. The locked T27 statement separately assumes p comparable to one fixed power of n; do not silently say that every literal premise of that statement holds for an oscillating sequence of exponents. The direct ratio law needs no such restriction. H2 is joint tightness, **not** a deterministic uniform leakage constant inferred from the notation `L=O_P(1)`. Reference mass above one half gives the unique mode immediately.

**P6: GAP and repairs.** The population comparisons are sound: the best missing hub removes G_max, a proxy removes at most eta_max G_max, and swaps deleting a reference truth incur a fixed positive residual loss. Different progress additions may tie; the sentence asserting a strict margin over “every other neighbour” must exclude other progress additions, as the subsequent bullet already does. Such ties do not obstruct lattice progress.

The uniform proof contains two concrete bookkeeping defects:

1. Lemma Lambda(a) calls Tr(c) over *all* sets of size at most s0+2. Their number is of order `p^(s0+2)`, not bounded by the `n^(A+1) 2^17` family in Tr(c). Moreover s0=2c+v is any fixed integer, so the projection dimension can exceed 16. Repair: state Tr for any fixed upper dimension J, take J=s0+2 here, and use a threshold `C log(n)/n` with C>4[A(s0+2)+2]. The same density/tail proof works with constants depending on J. Alternatively prove the projection loss using uniform covariance concentration and the Schur complement. This preserves the needed o(n) conclusion, but the cited lemma as written does not supply it.
2. Lemma U(U1) writes `nu*(1.02+1/(n phi0^2))/(c_* n) <= 1`. This arithmetic inequality is false: with sigma squared=1 and M=3, W1-EXP's c_*=1/40, and the left side tends to 20.4. Replace 1 by a sufficiently large fixed constant. U3 likewise must account for the RSS error by first shrinking the positive signal margin; its claimed displayed constant can be retained by using a sharper bound than the preliminary loose ratio, or simply weakened. Only positivity of a fixed linear-in-n margin is needed.

After these changes, the lattice route is short: each strictly preferred move adds a missing hub, the number of moves is at most c, and the final truth scan certifies once the local-maximum event holds. The truth additions are controlled by the conditional Gaussian maximum `max Z_l^2 <= 2 log p + 2 log log n`; this beats the size-prior plus determinant penalty. The same local-maximum event also follows from P8 and posterior mass above one half. Neither step proves that the original C++ chooses the LS0 center; the explicit LS0 tweak must remain.

## W4: verification and counterexamples

**D4.** Condition on the data and a successful frozen calibration throughout. Potentials, envelopes and eta_t must be fixed under this conditioning (or eta_t must have the stated predictable version). Ancestors are iid multinomial draws and each particle uses independent fresh round randomness. The convex chord bound on exp(-theta h) is in the correct direction. P_t is positive and known before the stage-t draws, including the H=0 convention. The map `P(1-exp(-theta/P))` increases in P. Killing the exponential process at the first envelope failure is legitimate because `{tau>t}` belongs to the preceding filtration. At theta_T=infinity the convention is the indicator of H_T=0; absence from W implies H_T=0 even for fractional potentials. The initial iid bound and both reciprocal-theta inequalities follow. The monotone-envelope constant is `g(1)=1/(e-1)`, not one half. No stopped-normalizer unbiasedness is presumed.

**D5.** Splitting weighted mass into h and 1-h gives the advertised bound even when h is fractional. H=0 gives r=1 by definition; all h=1 also gives r=1 and should be treated separately rather than multiplying an empty supremum by zero. The envelope `exp(-Gamma_t) product(1-eta_s)` is non-increasing. Its failure event is contained in confinement failure, so D4 applies with the exact exit product. This is a genuine replacement of SHARE by another stochastic control condition; it does not establish that condition for the BVS particle system.

**SH.** The one-stage exact binomial mixture, upper bound and Bernoulli lower bound all check, including cases where the lower bound is negative and hence vacuous. The informal transition assertion in (iii) needs a regime. Take `pi0(o)=1/N`, which satisfies its displayed lower bound. With probability `(1-1/N)^N -> exp(-1)` no outsider exists initially; then a cannot become extinct for any kappa. Even at `kappa=3 log N`, extinction tends to `1-exp(-1)`, not one. This does not contradict (ii), which explicitly assumes `pi0(a)^N -> 0`; that qualification must also accompany any zero-to-one “sharp threshold” reading of (iii). A clean repair assumes `N pi0(o)->infinity` and `N pi0(a)->infinity`; the initial count ratio concentrates and the transition parameter is `N pi0(a) exp(-kappa)/pi0(o)`. The author's exact formula remains the appropriate boundary-regime result.

**G.** `Gamma=sum Delta_t kappa_t <= max kappa_t` is just a convex combination, valid for every partition of [0,1]. For constant kappa the cumulative selection exponent is independent of refinement. Perfect restricted ESS supplies no missing-mass or realized-lineage control. As an elementary full-ESS example, take two states, d=0, identity moves and multinomial resampling: every interval has ESS ratio one, yet for fixed N repeated neutral resampling eventually loses either class. Increasing N and taking sufficiently many stages retains this obstruction. Thus an ESS condition alone is not a delivery theorem. This example does not obey the actual BVS move mechanism or necessarily the production T_e budget. A universal impossibility statement about every conceivable grid condition in that restricted setting needs a quantified theorem and a BVS counterexample; C1 does not supply that missing step. Keep that extrapolation at GAP.

**M(a).** With r the residual signal, Cauchy gives `||r||^2 <= sqrt(n-1)||theta_M||_1 max|delta_j|`. The Schur complement has minimum eigenvalue at least lambda, giving `||r||^2 >= (n-1)lambda||theta_M||_2^2`. Squaring yields the exact lambda-squared bound. The second inequality follows from `||theta_M||_1^2 <= |M| ||theta_M||_2^2`. Positive lambda already forces the relevant submatrices nonsingular. There is no missing factor |M|.

**M(b), ERROR in the expansion.** Let p=n-1, choose centered orthogonal standardized columns, one true coefficient theta=1, sigma squared=1, and T empty. G, R, SE and BM-infinity all hold. The population explained fraction tends to 1/2. The exact score gain (excluding the logarithmic determinant penalty) divided by n tends to `log(2)/2 = .34657359`. The asserted quadratic term divided by n tends to `1/4`. Thus its remainder is `.09657359 n + o_P(n)`, not O_P(1), o_P(log n), or the small remainder used by the sketch. The locked T27 statement explicitly keeps `-nu log(1-x)` and warns against assuming a quadratic-score identity. 00_COMMON's EXP is an open input with an upper-bound formulation, not the cited equality theorem. W1-EXP proves EXP-prime, which does not validate this Taylor replacement for fixed nonzero x.

Repair for the *lower bound*: use the exact RSS ratio expansion, `-log(1-x)>=x`, a uniform RSS upper bound, and `(delta+sigma Z)^2 >= delta^2/2 - sigma^2 Z^2`. For fixed d, the relevant family is polynomial and `max Z^2=O_P(log n)`. M(a) then gives `gain >= c M_n log n - C log n` under BM-infinity, with explicit constants depending on the fixed regime parameters. This avoids claiming an O(sqrt(log n)) uniform error: cross terms at large delta are larger and must be absorbed into a fraction of the signal. If d grows, the stated polynomial family count and fixed-d proof no longer apply.

**M(b), ERROR in the “T03 hypotheses” scope.** T03 permits BM-C, not just BM-infinity. In the preceding orthogonal design take `theta^2=C log n/n`, with a fixed C at least the locked BM-C constant (e.g. C=1024). At T empty, the score gain is `(C/2-3/2) log n + o_P(log n)` for p=n-1. The start-side lambda_n is `(3/2) log n+O(1)`. Therefore the displayed crossing-temperature ratio tends to `3/C>0`, rather than zero. More conservatively, any valid uniform lower gain is bounded by this particular gain and cannot make that ratio tend to zero. Repair: restrict this conclusion to BM-infinity; for BM-C give a nonvanishing threshold with the required constants.

**M(b), remaining GAP.** A path of favorable missing-truth additions from T=U union P ends at S0 union P. It does not delete extras. Existence of such a path also gives no bound on its traversal within L+4 actual rounds, nor on departures or competition along the path. Under 00_COMMON, GAIN_d explicitly includes an extra-deletion obligation; REPAIR and EXIT are separate inputs. Consequently “Proposition X cannot bite” and “only outsider competition plus exits remains” overstate what M(a) or the repaired addition bound proves. Retain those as unproved interpretations, with a specified region, feasible additions, fixed d and a separate uniform terminal repair theorem.

**Assembly exit-loss ERROR, with counterexample.** The last paragraph of W4 §7 replaces `-sum log(1-eta_t)` by `sum eta_t` with coefficient one. Consider an abstract two-state kernel with good state a and absorbing bad state o, h=1_a, d=0, all N starts at a, and survival probability 1/2 per round. TRACK is exact with eta=1/2; confinement is certain; r=1 and Gamma=0. Take `T=floor(1.6 log N)`. Then `sum eta=.8 log N`, so the stated final condition `sum eta <= log(N/T)-omega(1)` holds. But `E n_T(a)=N 2^(-T)->0`, hence delivery fails with probability tending to one by Markov. This example refutes the displayed simplification of the abstract D5 criterion, not the valid D5 theorem and not a claimed BVS design. Repair: retain the exact negative-log product; if eta<=1/2, `2 sum eta` is a valid sufficient replacement. W4 itself gives that factor two earlier in §2.
