## Q1. Exact selection and screening

**Verdict.** The deterministic odds margin Λ omits a stochastic loss of the same order at the boundary. Neither Λ→∞ nor the proposed 2+η condition has been justified by the R0 argument. A union bound that costs too much does not prove a counterexample to the unqualified conjecture. I recommend the following sufficient condition, which retains constant 2 when the proxy directions have small Gaussian width. The distinction between a bound for a rival and a bound for their *sum* matters.

**Assumptions and proof.** Work first under all of asm:dist at a fixed sufficiently large M, s=s₀ fixed and positive. Write T=S₀, S=(T∖D)∪N, d=|D|, m=|N|, U=T∪N. Use prop:dist **(i)** at that level to discard |S|>k*=s+C_s M at cost n^{-c_H M} on an event of probability tending to one. Choose integer cutoffs by rounding up. Check every m≤k*−s+d that occurs, or conservatively m≤k*. A cutoff only on total size does not imply m≤k*−s for underfits. For these bounded sizes the growing q_n covers U. Put L=log(n∨p), h=log(2p/s)+½log(n+1), ρ_S=R(S)/(nσ²). Let λ_S=‖(I−P_S)μ‖²/σ² in centered raw-response units, with standardized columns. On all the indicated sets, uniformly λ_S≥(1−o_P(1))nρ_S by Q4.

1. In raw centered units use b=b₀s_y² and ν=n/2+a₀. Let F_S=w(k)D_S^{-1/2}(2b+Q_S)^{-ν}, where D_S=∏[1+nλ_i(G_S)/(1+λ_i(G_S))], G_S=X_SᵀX_S and Q_S is the coded residual quadratic. With NEC transferred to the sample, λ_min(G_U)≥c>0. Hence c'^{|S|}(n+1)^{|S|}≤D_S≤(n+1)^{|S|}. The residual operator has eigenvalue (λ+1)/[(n+1)λ+1] on a fitted direction. Thus 0≤Q_S−RSS_S≤C‖y_c‖²/n=O_P(σ²), uniformly on the bounded models, by BS. Also b=O_P(σ²), and (2b+Q_U)/(nσ²)=1+O_P(n^{-1/2}+L/n) uniformly: RSS_U=‖ε_c‖²−‖P_U ε_c‖² and the maximum projection is O_P(σ²L). These bounds do not condition on the outcome-dependent standardization of y.
2. Conditional on X, write v_S=(I−P_S)μ/‖(I−P_S)μ‖, Z_S=⟨v_S,ε_c⟩/σ. Then
   RSS_S−RSS_U=‖(P_U−P_S)(μ+ε_c)‖² ≥ σ²(√λ_S+Z_S)_+².
   For each class (D,m) take a design-dependent upper quantile a_Dm of max_N(−Z_S), with failure probability o(1). Always valid is a_Dm=√{2[log binom(p−s,m)+t_n]}, t_n→∞, t_n=o(log n). Alternatively use a_Dm=E_X max_N(−Z_S)+√(2t_n) by Gaussian concentration (E_X here integrates noise conditional on X). This explicitly counts the residual directions, not just the number of models. It needs no independence across rivals. For m=0 use √(2t_n). Identical directions cost only this last term; an unverified count of nearby AR neighbors is not a width bound.
3. The total weight of the containing models at each bounded m satisfies
   Σ_N F_U/F_T=O_P((L/n)^{m/2}), including value 1 at m=0. Indeed H_N=‖(P_U−P_T)ε_c‖²/σ² has marginal χ²_m. Simultaneously H_N≤B_m=C(m+1)L with probability tending to one by χ² tails and a union bound. By step 1 the log fit gain is ≤(1+O_P(n^{-1/2}+L/n))H_N/2+O_P(1)=H_N/2+O_P(1) uniformly for polynomial p. For m>0,
   E[e^{H_N/2}1_{H_N≤B_m}]=B_m^{m/2}/[2^{m/2}Γ(1+m/2)].
   Markov on the *sum* gives O_P(binom(p−s,m)L^{m/2}); the BB weight ratio is O(p^{-m}) for fixed s,m and the determinant contributes O(n^{-m/2}). A maximum bound for the positive chi-square gain used on every summand loses this cancellation.
4. Define B_Dm=d h−(m/2)log(n/L)+γ log n, γ>0 fixed. A conservative explicit **population** assumption, for fixed δ>0, is
   √{(1−δ)nρ_S} ≥ a_Dm + √{2(1+δ)[B_Dm]_+}
   for all retained underfits, with probability 1−o(1) over X if using a design-dependent width. The combinatorial a_Dm gives an entirely deterministic population assumption. Step 2 yields Δ=(Q_S−Q_U)/σ²≥2(1+δ)[B_Dm]_+−O_P(1). Monotonicity of log and (2b+Q_U)/(nσ²)→1 give ν log(1+σ²Δ/(2b+Q_U))≥[B_Dm]_+−O_P(1), uniformly: substitute the lower bound of order L into the logarithm, even when the actual loss is order n. The prior ratio and determinant give log(F_S/F_U)≤d h−[B_Dm]_++O_P(1). Multiplying by step 3 and summing the finitely many D,m bounds the retained underfit mass by O_P(n^{-γ}). Therefore
   1−Π(T|y)=O_P(√(L/n)+n^{-γ}+n^{-c_H M}).
   This proves the summary conclusions for the exact posterior. The exponent γ is *chosen in the inequality*; no universal conversion from an empirical excess η is justified. δ may be arbitrarily small but fixed. A sufficient simpler version drops the negative m-term in B_Dm. A more conservative already checked alternative is thm:post-margin with its original c₁ and true-model floor after population-to-sample transfer.
5. Necessity: Π(T|y)→1 forces F_{T∖j}/F_T→0 for every true j. In the boundary regime λ_j=O(L) under the preceding assumptions, log(F_{T∖j}/F_T)=h_j−(√λ_j+Z_j)²/2+O_P(1), h_j=log[(2p−s)/s]+½log(n+1), Z_j marginally standard normal conditional on X. The bounded error follows from step 1 and L=O(log n); the nested increment has rank one. For deterministic design with h_j→∞ the necessary and sufficient condition for this single odds ratio to vanish is √λ_j−√(2h_j)→+∞. With random design require this in design probability (or transfer with error o_P(1) on the square-root scale). Equality of leading constants leaves a transition window of order √h_j in λ_j. A fixed 2+η works for that comparison, 2−η fails. The observed partial t² and population noncentrality are different quantities.

**Screening.** At the same M, require R(S)/(nσ²)≥C M log n/n for retained S omitting the designated j. For a candidate with mean error V including its intercept, E V²≥R(S)/n. Relative SUBG gives E V⁴≤C_L(E V²)² (the intercept changes the constant), hence P(V²≥E V²/2)≥1/(4C_L). For candidate SD τ in [σ/2,2σ], Gaussian affinity is √(2στ/(σ²+τ²)) E exp[−V²/(4(σ²+τ²))]. Thus 1−affinity≥c_L min(1,R(S)/(nσ²)); outside that interval the prefactor alone separates. This proves separation at the contraction radius for large enough C and the union of support sizes with T must lie in the SUBG window. Add the size tail, absorbing a factor two by reducing the tail exponent. The order version also suffices.

**Referee risk.** Low for deletion and screening; medium for the new sufficient comparison pending independent check; high for claiming universal sharpness for swaps. Precedent: SL Theorem 3 and supplement §1.2 for separation, Theorem S for nested noncentral comparisons; neither establishes our constant or a total posterior mass rate.

## Q2. Boundary sequences and reporting

**Verdict and assumption.** State two asymptotic regimes. A diverging residual-to-price ratio is a convenient corollary under fixed polynomial DIM. The fixed-slack inequality in Q1 is the boundary result. Use log p/log n→a∈(0,∞) and β_j/σ=c_j√(log p/n) on standardized coordinates, with a specified covariance sequence. If p is fixed, the same signal scaling gives O(1) deletion noncentrality against a diverging log n Occam price.

**Proof and calibration.** For independent standardized Gaussian rows R(S)/σ²=nΣ_{j∈D}(β_j/σ)². In the population orthogonal case contrasts for a fixed D coincide across N. On bounded sets sample correlations are O_P(√(L/n)), so their normalized sample residual directions differ from the common deletion direction by O_P(√(L/n)), uniformly (fixed d,m). The additional conditional expected Gaussian maximum is O_P(L/√n)=o_P(1) by the union bound. Thus Q1 covers fixed coefficients c_j with strict slack above the single-deletion boundary c_j² a>2a+1, for every j; sum over j∈D and take γ,δ small. This gives an actual S1-type boundary example. With correlated rows evaluate the residual and the width or conservative counting allowance. Merely having β of the stated order certifies neither success nor failure.

**Risk and precedent.** Low for distinguishing these regimes. Use P1's 9/11,466 below-threshold successes and the conditional success fractions 0.95/0.45 strictly as diagnostics of the measured deletion statistic and reported fits. Minima over a searched subset of rivals **upper** bound the exhaustive margin; calibration on visited models cannot certify sufficiency. A finite-n theorem requires its own bounds on all exceptional probabilities and constants. NH14 Condition 4.4 is a residual-identifiability precedent, not the BB threshold.

## Q3. Dimension by proof step

**Verdict.** Keep p≤n^A in the released statements for the current cap. There is no intrinsic 1/3 or 1/2 exponent common to all three targets. The following distinctions matter for a proposed DIM′.

* **Local design and signal.** With relative SUBG on unions of size k, whiten on the covariance range; a fixed-vector squared-norm Bernstein bound, a constant-radius net of size ≤9^k and a support union give failure ≤2 exp[C k log(ep/k)−c nδ²] for a relative δ<1 (with adjusted constants including centering). For constant k, log p=o(n) permits δ=o(1). At k≈M L, a fixed-slack transfer needs M L log(ep/k) sufficiently small compared with n; o(1) distortion follows if it is o(n). For fixed M, L=o(√n) suffices for this step. The IG scale b₀s_y² uses concentration of **one** standardized response with bounded signal-to-noise, no direct p bound.
* **Local underfits and variance integration.** At losses O(L) the RSS logarithm has relative error O_P(n^{-1/2}+L/n) around its leading term; no expansion at losses of order n is needed for a lower bound. The additive error O_P(L/√n+L²/n) is o(L) for L=o(n) but o(1) by this argument only for L=o(√n). Leading-constant boundaries and multiplicative rate bounds are different.
* **Local supersets.** There is an exact route avoiding the additive Taylor restriction. Conditional on X, let r=n−1−s. For an m-dimensional addition, B=(RSS_T−RSS_U)/RSS_T has Beta(m/2,(r−m)/2) law by independent orthogonal Gaussian projections. Uniformly on bounded m, Q_T≤RSS_T+O_P(σ²) and Q_U≥RSS_U, so fit odds are bounded by O_P(1)(1−B)^{-ν} if RSS_U≥c nσ². For log p=o(n), B≤b_m=C(m+1)L/n=o(1) uniformly with high probability by the two chi-square tails. The truncated expectation is bounded by
  C n^{m/2}∫₀^{b_m} x^{m/2−1}(1−x)^{(r−m)/2−1−ν} dx ≤ C_m L^{m/2};
  the exponent after tilting is fixed for fixed m,s. No independence across N is used. Thus the bounded-size superset sum can retain its √(L/n) rate throughout log p=o(n). This does **not** give the bounded posterior-size sieve under the coded K.
* **Global localization and density.** With t=M L, the true model alone costs roughly s log p+(s/2)log n in the denominator. The geometric prior tail against that cost gives a coarse cutoff k≈s L+t. The checked proof then uses a fractional exponent λ<1 and an upper bound per addition proportional to p^{1−λ} n^{-λ/2}(1−λ')^{-1/2}, λ'=λ(1+RSS-envelope error)<1. Fixed λ no longer works when log p/log n diverges. Even an n-dependent λ requires 1−λ=O(log n/L), adequate RSS control, and constants accounting for (1−λ')^{-1/2}. A uniform RSS envelope on k≈L sets costs O_P(√(t/n)+kL/n); multiplying the latter by L/log n explains a possible cubic restriction L³≪n log n *in that proof*. This alone neither establishes nor requires α<1/3 for the theorem. The checked intermediate-size comparison pays exp(O(sL)) to attach T; at Occam savings O(log n) per addition, its fine cutoff grows on the order of t/log n. Union tests on models of that size cost (t/log n)L, exceeding a testing budget O(t) when L≫log n. A direct substitution log n↦L misses both difficulties. Source: W3-DIST, lemmas/X.md, steps X3 and X4.
* **DIM′ proposal.** For selection with a **fixed cap** K≥s, log p=o(n), Q1's inequality and local NEC/SUBG on K+s suffice by the exact Beta argument and Q1, with an empty large-size tail (retain fixed k*=K). This is a different engineering regime. For unchanged K, log p=o(n) is a conditional extension if an independently proved tail bound for the required sieve is supplied. For T-DIST retain polynomial DIM with its published M-window; investigate q L=o(n) and a budget for entropy of the *posterior-effective* sieve before proposing the same √(M L/n) rate. A rate for a larger sieve of effective size k should budget k log(ep/k), possibly at the cost of a larger radius. For T-MIS inherit the corresponding DIM with p=p_C, s=s_C. The number of omitted coordinates need not obey that bound.

**Risk and precedent.** Low for local transfer and Beta calculation; high for an unconditional exponential-p extension at current K. SL Corollary 1 establishes exponential p with a different prior, hard cap and radius. NH14's diffusing slab and selection results likewise do not transfer by dimension alone.

## Q4. Eigenvalues and alternatives

**Verdict and assumption.** Drop the fixed positive population floor, including on T, in the Q1 route while retaining NEC on the existing DIST window and BS. With relative SUBG the Q3 covariance sandwich holds even for shrinking population eigenvalues. Taking the infimum of quadratic forms over coefficients yields (1−δ)R(S)≤‖(I−P_S)μ‖²≤(1+δ)R(S); including the intercept is exactly sample centering. Column scaling does not change projectors. Uniformly comparable sample and population column SDs give λ_min(G_U)≥c when λ_min(Corr_U)≥c_ψ/n. This is sufficient for the determinant and shrinkage bounds in Q1. A true-model floor does not follow from a margin at the vanishing detection scale.

**Weakest proof inputs.** Relative risk transfer, a small-model determinant bound D_T/D_U≤C^m n^{-m} (or a suitable average weighted by fit gains), a bounded shrinkage loss in the truth and containing models, and posterior-size localization suffice. For Q1's underfits D_U/D_S≤(n+1)^d by interlacing independent of NEC, and Q_S≥RSS_S. The DIST proof uses a negative determinant moment averaged on containing models on the intermediate sieve; it need not constrain every Gram eigenvalue separately. These are alternatives for proving the inputs, not automatic consequences of MARGIN. In particular a duplicate of an active predictor has zero swap margin. Duplicates among nulls can also destroy a per-coordinate determinant lower bound while leaving deletion margins large. One needs a replacement for the determinant and size arguments to drop NEC entirely.

**Risk and precedent.** Low for the relative transfer under the manuscript's variance-relative SUBG, medium for substituting averaged determinant bounds. Marginal sub-Gaussianity alone does not imply relative concentration in nearly singular directions. SL (22)/(26) concern a different proof; NH14 Condition 4.4 motivates a projection condition. The S2 greedy eigenvalue is an upper bound on the worst sample floor, not an estimate of the required population margin.

## Q5. Sparsity and signal energy

**Verdict and assumptions.** Keep fixed sparsity for the advertised rates. In the interior s≤min(K−1,p/2), manuscript rem:sharp(a) implies δ_post≥(s+1)/(3√n+s+1) deterministically. Thus even δ_post→0 needs s=o(√n) there; s log p=o(n) alone cannot suffice. For fixed s the sharper witness result is design-specific; it does not assert a universal attained √(log p/n) lower bound. With growing s the adjacent prior ratio contributes s+1 to the null sum, suggesting (s+1)√(L/n)→0 for this route. Also account for log binom(s,d) in search over missed sets, growing gamma-function factors in truncated gains, a denominator cost log[1/w(s)]+½log D_T, and a density budget at least of order s log(ep/s) in general. The geometric tail survives but must beat this growing denominator. Q1's finite class argument cannot simply be reused.

**Energy proof step.** With θ_j=β_j sd_sample(x_j), the penalty at the true mean in the slab quadratic is [‖μ_c‖²+‖θ‖²]/(nσ²). The scale envelope requires s_y²/σ²=O_P(1) with suitable tail probability for DIST. A weaker population pair is
βᵀΣβ/σ²≤C,  Σ_j β_j² Var(x_j)/(nσ²)≤C.
Relative SUBG plus column variance concentration bounds the sample analogues and the standardized response; the first bound also gives Q1's ‖y_c‖²/n=O_P(σ²). On the current window NEC implies the second from the first since βᵀΣβ≥(c_ψ/n)Σ_j β_j²Var(x_j). Thus bounded *aggregate* energy is a viable replacement here. For T-SEL-b using prop:dist(i), reissue that input with the energy pair first; using BS verbatim avoids this dependency. For DIST trace the intercept reconstruction as well: its centered prediction error uses the directional mean of xᵀβ*, whose scale is the first energy bound. Growing log-energy is not freely interchangeable: if standardized signal variance is H_n, the true-model slab exponent can cost O(H_n), and the IG envelope ratio depends on H_n. Charge both to the contraction budget, and revisit uniform constants and local odds errors.

**Risk and precedent.** Low for the lower bound and fixed-s recommendation; medium for reissuing DIST under aggregate energy. Manuscript asm:omitted(c) already bounds projected energy. SL (4) is a bounded-support-size regime. NH14 comparability is for sparsity and identifiability, not a derivation of our slab cost.

## Q6. Candidate reruns and misspecification

**Verdict and assumption.** Release (iii) with arbitrary candidate distribution satisfying candidate SUBG/NEC: y=a_C+x_Cᵀβ^C+u, u independent N(0,σ_C²), s_C fixed and feasible. Joint Gaussianity of candidates and hidden predictors is one sufficient construction. The target is the Gaussian KL projection among candidate linear means with constant variance (equivalently the minimizer of expected Gaussian negative log score when finite). Indeed that score minus constants is ½log v + [E u²+E(a+x_Cᵀb−a_C−x_Cᵀβ^C)²]/(2v) for centered orthogonal residuals. Hence the minimizing variance is E u². Apply DIST or Q1 on a fresh candidate-only fit with its own prior and cap, on a prespecified candidate set or using independent selection data. E6's outcome-selected C gives a variance diagnostic only.

**Extensions and proof obligations.** (i) independent centered sub-Gaussian noise excludes the variance heterogeneity in the Grünwald–van Ommen example. It keeps the same KL projection, but fitting a Gaussian conditional density is now misspecified. For proxy noise K_uσ, the directional tail is ≤exp[−t²/(2K_u²σ²)] and quadratic-form concentration typically involves K_u²σ²; the true-variance matched χ² truncation in Q1 fails. For example a bound exp[−z/(2K_u²)] for a squared standardized null score gives a truncated first moment of exp(z/2) up to B of order exp[(1−1/K_u²)B/2] times polynomial factors for K_u>1. This can consume the Occam gain. A new high-dimensional moderate-deviation/tail analysis (e.g. delocalized contrasts, near-Gaussian tails to √log p) and a global size lemma would be needed for the same claims. The DIST numerator argument E_true[m(Y)/f_true(Y)]=1 uses its Gaussian reference denominator when bounding true support; a KL projection cannot simply replace the true density in this identity. Option (i) permits exact pseudo-support selection in special regimes, e.g. fixed p, nonsingular fixed population Gram, bounded fourth moments, fixed identifiable signals. There, underfits lose order n in Gaussian log score by the law of large numbers and each redundant least-squares gain is O_P(1) by fixed-dimensional score variance, versus ½log n per addition. This example does not establish the growing-p theorem. With (ii) the population normal equations can still define the projection with an intercept, but heteroscedasticity and heavy tails persist. It does not exclude the cited failure mechanism or give uniform exponential tests.

**Risk and precedent.** Low for (iii) and the fixed-p special case; high for extending the current coded high-dimensional rates to (i) or (ii). SL Theorem 4 is a fixed-subset result. Distinguish divergence to a projected Gaussian density from divergence to the actual non-Gaussian response law.

## Q8. Engineering sensitivity

**Verdict and assumptions.** Compute adjacent odds before substituting a new exponent. For BB(1,b), per-model w_b(k)=B(k+1,p−k+b)/B(1,b) before truncation. Then
w_b(k+1)/w_b(k)=(k+1)/(p−k+b−1),
Pr_b(k+1)/Pr_b(k)=(p−k)/(p−k+b−1).
With k fixed, b=p^u, 0≤u<1, the prior cost is log p−log(k+1)+o(1), **not** u log p. Multiplication by p−k still cancels the p scale. Relative to b=p, the small-model price falls only about log 2. The substantial change is the size tail: typical untruncated size p/(1+b), and the adjacent size ratio tends to one near zero. For b=1 sizes are uniform before the cap. For b≫p the small-model prior cost is log b−log(k+1)+o(1) and the size prior tightens.

**Proof consequences.**

* For g general the determinant factor in direction λ is 1+gλ/(1+λ); fitted-direction residual weight is (1+λ)/[1+(1+g)λ]. Under local NEC and fixed sizes the determinant price is ½log(1+g)+O(1), while the true slab energy costs (‖μ_c‖²+‖θ‖²)/(gσ²). Thus g≍n changes constants. With g≪n recheck shrinkage as well as the price: even if average signal energy is bounded, the bound O(n/g) on shrinkage loss may swamp the local margin. Fixed g can prevent exact selection already with fixed p and one independent null because the overfit odds retain a nonvanishing determinant factor and the prior ratio is fixed. For g=max(n,p²), shrinkage falls and the denominator cost increases; update the signal boundary, prior concentration, sieve and rate.
* Under the Q1 bounded-size comparison, for an addition the aggregated bound is of order (s+1)p/(p−s+b−1) times √(L/(1+g)) when the bounded shrinkage-error argument holds. In particular one sufficient small-model condition is that this quantity vanish (and analogous powers for bounded m). Price≥(1+ε)log p is not necessary: even for expanding p, an excess over log p that beats ½log L by a diverging amount can suffice here. For u<1 at the coded K, replace the **global** tail proof or constrain the cap; invoking p^{(1−u)m} locally would diagnose the wrong obstacle. For a new g/b use exact size-specific deletion prices and recompute the class-sum term in Q1. The noise search allowance persists.
