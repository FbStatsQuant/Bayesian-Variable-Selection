## Q1 — DISAGREE on closure and on the low–medium risk rating

Fubini supplies the **coarse** cutoff. For the coded response scaling one must first pass to raw centered contrasts and sandwich the outcome-dependent IG rate b=b₀s_y² between fixed endpoints. Conditional on X, E*[m^c_{S,b₋}/f*]=1 for the normalized fixed-endpoint density. This identity cannot be applied directly to the plug-in score. W3-DIST/lemmas/X.md, X4(a), actually uses a denominator lower bound of order D_T^{-1/2} n^{-1/2} exp(−Λ) in noise-normalized units. The extra n^{-1/2} is variance integration. An event of probability 1−o(1) alone does not give the displayed bound on EΠ or an exponentially small exceptional probability. The checked argument uses Markov on the numerator and separately controls that event. Its coarse cutoff, truncated at K, is s₀+⌈[s₀ log(2p)+(s₀+2)log(n+1)/2+3t]/log 2⌉, t the desired negative log tail. This works also for s₀=0.

Put L=log(p∨n), t=M L. At fixed s,M with log p=n^{1/3}, DIM′ holds and q≈L. Isometry needs q log p=O(n^{2/3})≪n. The **claimed testing budget** is nε²=M L=O(n^{1/3}), while log binom(p,q)≈q log(p/q) is order n^{2/3}. Thus §3.1 step 3 asserts a false inequality. Even for polynomial p the delivered theorem uses more than uniform tests on the coarse sieve. Its X4(b,c) cuts down to d_t=s₀+O(t/log n) by fractional BF moments. The factor p^{1−λ}n^{−λ/2} in those moments cannot be controlled by the existing fixed λ for superpolynomial p. Furthermore d_t log p need not be O(t) after this substitution.

Recommend **conjecture, high proof risk at the proposed rate**, not [P]. A proof needs either localization at a testing-compatible size (roughly t/log p), or a prior-weighted/direct predictive error bound that avoids paying full entropy. A cap of order n/log p alone solves the isometry inequality, not the testing inequality. A fixed feasible cap is a distinct, easier case. No impossibility of the uncapped theorem follows from this failed route.

## Q2 — AGREE-WITH-FIX on A-MARGIN-U

Let T=S₀, U=T∪N, h=Pr_n(d), λ̂_S=R̂(S)/σ², v_S=(I−P_S)μ/‖(I−P_S)μ‖. For each D,m define w_Dm(X)=E_g max_N(−⟨v_S,g⟩), g standard Gaussian in the centered observation space. It satisfies w_Dm≤√(2 log binom(p−s₀,m)); with m=0 it equals zero. Use the worst width over D, a deterministic upper bound holding with design probability 1−o(1), or that combinatorial bound outright. Merely choosing a number w_m **below** the combinatorial bound is insufficient. Use a one-sided maximum; the absolute maximum has an extra factor 2 inside the logarithm.

For fixed many classes subtract w_Dm+t_n from √λ̂_S, with t_n→∞ by Gaussian concentration. Put B=h−(m/2)log(n/(C log p))+γ log n. A transparent sufficient condition is
√{(1−δ)R_n(S)/σ²} ≥ w_Dm+t_n+√{2(1+δ)[B]₊}, with 0<δ<1 fixed, γ>0.
The containing-model sum is O_P((C log p/n)^{m/2}) for bounded m (for bounded p use tightness directly). The loss against U is at least σ²(√λ̂_S−w_Dm−t_n)_+² in RSS, with bounded ridge correction in noise units. The deleted prior and determinant refund against U is at most exp(h) by monotonicity of the adjacent deletion price and interlacing. These give O_P(n^{-γ}) per class. For the lead's multiplicative (1+η) form, choose δ small relative to η for both transfer and log-fit conversion, and take t_n≤a_n/3; the factor on a_n also shrinks under transfer. No additional first-order price term is missing.

For γ=0 the displayed rate contains n⁰. Consistency then needs a separate o_P(1) proof, retaining a diverging surplus from a_n even when B≈0. Taking γ>0 avoids this in the headline. For m=0, liminf κ_n>1 permits *some sufficiently small* γ,η>0 since h≳log n; it does not imply a prescribed γ. The simpler squared condition likewise needs spare slack or smaller η,γ. Give M a fixed value and define j₀ by an exact cutoff. My R1 uses the bounded-size event |S|≤s+C_s M, then tests all feasible m up to s+C_s M. If using a smaller j₀, bound the omitted intermediate classes and their contribution at the **claimed rate**, not only o_P(1). With A-BS′ replacing A-BS, first extend the size lemma or retain A-BS. These are dependencies of TS-b at polynomial p too.

## Q3 — AGREE-WITH-FIX for the converse; DISAGREE for the finite-n ceiling

Under regularity **without** the forward margin, limsup κ_n<1 suffices. For the minimizing D, A-NEC on T gives the two-sided determinant refund d log(n+1)/2+O(1), and Q−RSS=O_P(σ²) for both models. Subthreshold R_n/σ²=O(log n); relative sample transfer and the noncentral χ²_d increment give log(F_{T∖D}/F_T)=Pr_n(d)−R_n/(2σ²)+o_P(Pr_n(d))→+∞. A one-sided upper bound on the refund alone cannot prove this converse.

At a single boundary √R_j/σ−√(2Pr_n(1))→c, the probability that **this rival beats T** tends to Φ(−c) (fixed T sets have O_P(n^{-1/2}) relative transfer). Hence liminf P(Π(T)≤1/2)≥Φ(−c). Equality needs control of *all other* odds. For example let a second orthogonal true coordinate have deletion ratio bounded strictly below one. Then Π(T)→0 irrespective of the first coordinate's finite c.

Write K₁ for the number of one-null extensions beating T. P(K₁≥1)≤E K₁; a marginal Beta law specifies no joint union probability. In the ideal χ² score approximation, E K₁≈(p−s₀)2Φ(−√L)≈(s₀+1)/√(2πL(n+1)) when p≫s₀ and L grows. The design can have strongly correlated null directions even under NEC. A union upper bound on this *one kind of failure* gives no upper ceiling on success. In finite samples even the individual coded odds contain a ridge determinant and a data-scaled IG term. Neither the product of correlated deletion probabilities nor the sum involving only deletions and one-null extensions controls all swaps and higher-order rivals. HPM scoring, thresholded marginal PIPs, and the output of a finite sampler are distinct events. Remove the proposition and the attribution of all unexplained failures to this formula; retain a labeled diagnostic. Conditional-on-X bounds should not put a random R̂ on the right of an unconditional probability.

## Q4 — AGREE-WITH-FIX on TM-a0; DISAGREE that SUBG implies SL Condition 3

TM-a0 works for **one fixed i.i.d. law** along a nested sample sequence, σ_C²=E u²>0, finite stated moments, nonsingular Σ_CC, and the coded positive fixed-dimension prior. Allowing arbitrary n-dependent truth makes even fixed-p support selection false (e.g. β_n=1/n). Use raw units and an intercept. The SLLN gives RSS_S/n→σ_S², Q_S−RSS_S=O(1) a.s. eventually, b₀s_y²=O(1) and log D_S=|S|log(n+1)+O(1). Underfits lose (n/2)log(σ_S²/σ_C²)+o(n). Without the mixed fourth moments, each of finitely many containing models has consistent fitted parameters and concentrating conditional posterior, so density consistency follows even if their relative masses do not converge.

For exact support, take Z=(1,x_Cᵀ)ᵀ. E[Zu]=0 and finite coordinate variances give ‖Σ_i Z_i u_i‖=O_a.s.(√(n log log n)). The relevant residualized Gram inverse has norm O_a.s.(1/n), by fixed nonsingular design covariance and SLLN. Residualizing the scores against included columns costs bounded sample regression coefficients. Hence the extra fitted sum of squares is O_a.s.(log log n), losing to (m/2)log n in log odds. The additional assumption is a mixed second moment of the scores, not marginal fourth moments of x and y. For possibly discrete true responses the optimizer is defined by expected Gaussian log score; calling it a literal finite-KL minimizer needs appropriate domination/integrability.

SUBG gives a **conversion inequality**, not residual identifiability. Standard Gaussian duplicates are sub-Gaussian and have R=0. Even distinct Gaussians with correlation 1−1/n have vanishing unit-coordinate residual variance O(1/n), insufficient at scale log n/n. For an affine mean contrast V on S∪T, relative SUBG gives E V⁴≤C_L(E V²)², hence P(V²≥E V²/2)≥1/(4C_L). Gaussian conditional affinity then gives d_H²≥c_L min(1,R/(nσ²)); A-MARGIN-H supplies the independent lower bound on R. Use S from the fixed effective posterior sieve so |S∪T|≤q_n, or enlarge the SUBG window. Add the posterior large-size tail to the contraction tail (absorb the factor 2 in the exponent). This is also the screening argument in ASTRA_R1 Q1.

## Q5 — AGREE-WITH-FIX on the package after the following edits

**Substantive.**

* TS-a's displayed Gaussian free-variance formula is valid: maximizing affinity squared gives 2στ/(σ²+τ²+R/(2n)), optimized at τ²=σ²+R/(2n). Keep the saturation min(1,·) for general SUBG.
* Do not attribute the lead's j₀ summation, the finite-n companion, or full boundary-limit equality to both advisors. Large-size m-dependent Beta bounds require a uniform summation; an O_P bound at each fixed m does not prove one. The null-sum lower bound in rem:sharp(b) is for witness designs. rem:sharp(a) requires s≤min(K−1,p/2). An upper rate s√(log p/n) is not a universal necessity for growing s.
* A-NEC is a sufficient, convenient way to obtain determinant and shrinkage control. Its necessity as stated is unproved. Without a matching lower determinant refund or shrinkage control, the deletion *iff* does not follow from interlacing. The Q1(R1) energy pair uses O(n), whereas A-BS′(b) uses o(n); they are not identical. The new assumption set does not automatically inherit prop:dist(i). Likewise a fixed-floor delivered result on a smaller window does not imply NEC on an arbitrarily larger TD window.
* Comparisons to NH14 as uniformly weaker ignore the positive width term, different model ranges, priors and normalization. Likewise a factor-proxy example showing poor swaps at one (d,m) does not establish global optimality of every constant in A-MARGIN-U for polynomial p. The old unrestricted margin remains open there.
* TD-A needs a change-of-measure inequality for the entire data law, not one-observation Hellinger closeness alone. For Gaussian mean perturbation r(x), E_surrogate[(dP_truth/dP_surrogate)²]=(E exp(r(x)²/σ²))^n; control this by exp(O(c₀t)) and use Cauchy–Schwarz on exceptional events, with small enough c₀ and intercept/mean accounted for.
* A-MIS(ii)'s high-dimensional “iff” and the cap requirements under (i′) are not established. A variance proxy/union argument can suggest a sufficient budget, not a universal necessity. TM-a0 is outside A-GL. A fixed cap ≥s preserves T and removes competitors; K≥s+j₀ is not necessary. Small g can enlarge the slab loss O(n/g), so a smaller nominal price alone does not describe that change. The BB(1,p^u) correction is sound for fixed small k.

**Notation and precision.** Centering costs one residual degree of freedom: RSS_U/RSS_T has Beta((n−1−s₀−m)/2,m/2) law conditional on full rank. Use R̂/σ² for the noncentrality consistently. The Schur complement expresses *linear* projection risk for general finite covariance. A-DIM gives exp(O(log² n)), not polynomially many O(log n)-sets. The exact coded determinant is det[(n+1)G+I]/det[G+I]. A log p-dependent threshold shift from g=n to cn is actually an increment ½log[(1+cn)/(1+n)] at fixed k; a marginal PIP threshold has no universal single-model log-score shift. State TM-a's allowed M-window as in TD and give its a.s. corollary for posterior tails, not for probabilities.
