# ASTRA senior review — TH-SONG-ASSUMPTIONS, Stage 1

**Judgment.** The core model and design assumptions are general. The least defensible item in owner Q&A is the headline mixed-rival margin. A conditional Gaussian width is a legitimate complexity measure, but the condition combines population signal, sample geometry, a posterior localization constant and an unidentified multiplier. The paper can give a population-only sufficient condition and preserve the conditional-width version as a sharper technical result. The sharp deletion converse deserves its own statement.

## 1. Completeness findings

**C-1 · SUBSTANTIVE · Assumptions §3.3 steps 2–3, theorem definitions §4.2, simulation E9b.** At fixed s and M there are finitely many values of m. If a sum is tight after division by (log p/n)^(m/2), it is tight after division by (C log p/n)^(m/2) for *every* fixed C>0. The one-null rate therefore cannot imply C≈121, nor determine which classes pay a finite-sample credit. Set C=1 by convention and carry the multiplicative constant in the tight remainder. E9b at C=121 is a sensitivity calculation. A genuinely calibrated finite-n credit would need an explicit probability and bound on the multiplier. Also distinguish the single-rival nominal price Pr_n(d,m) in definitions §4 from the **summed class** bound B_Dm. They are different quantities even at m=0 when γ>0. The ridge determinant gives a design-dependent adjustment to the nominal price.

**C-2 · SUBSTANTIVE · Simulation E9/E9b and assumptions §1 D4.** The staged generating law uses raw Xβ. In e9_width_mc.py:26,71 and e9b_width_mc_ext.py:41 the simulation uses sample-standardized X times the *raw* coefficients. Centering is appropriate and column rescaling preserves each projection space. But for d≥2 it changes the relative weights of the omitted columns in the projected mean, hence can change the normalized residual direction. For d=1 the positive scaling cancels exactly. Preserve the centered raw mean or multiply the coefficients by their sample column SDs before calculating widths. Recheck multi-deletion numbers before quoting them for the coded target. E9b also selects D by the three lowest residual energies, whereas the width depends on D. These need not minimize the actual surplus. Label them as selected examples. Four designs at zero slack cannot establish a probability tending to one. Amend D4's statement that the margin “holds there.”

**C-3 · SUBSTANTIVE · SL-5/SL-9 map and TS-a/TM-a0.** Two itemwise guarantees are available under weaker hypotheses than the named exact/simultaneous targets. SL Thm 3 (p. 956) protects an individually identifiable true predictor even if another true predictor is indistinguishable. State TS-a first for any j satisfying its own margin and then jointly for those j, using the common TD/size event in supplement_or.tex, prop:dist(i). SL Cor 2 (p. 957) asks only for inclusion of the projected support. TM-a0 step 2 already gives Π(S ⊇ S0^C | data) → 1 a.s. with second moments alone: full rank and finitely many underfits give a strictly positive minimum risk gap whenever underfits exist. The extra score moments enter only for exclusion. Add this as the direct SL-9 counterpart, alongside the stronger exact-selection result. No added proof risk.

**C-4 · SUBSTANTIVE · A-MIS(iii), §3.5 and TM-a.** Independent screening permits conditioning on C, but independence alone does not deliver the displayed unconditional exponential tail if candidate regularity holds only with probability 1−o(1), or its constants deteriorate across possible C. State the result conditional on each admissible C, using candidate dimension in the cap, price, window and prior. For an unconditional result require uniform constants and almost-sure admissibility of C, or add P(C inadmissible) to the bound. For the a.s. corollary the combined exception probabilities must be summable. No issue for deterministic admissible candidates.

**C-5 · SUBSTANTIVE · SL-3/SL-4 map and dossier A-SUBG.** The staged supplement, prop:dist(ii), lines 177–179 of supplement_or.tex, simultaneously removes the eigenfloor **and** reduces SUBG to singleton directions at the slower radius. The catalogue quotes only the floor relief and may leave an owner believing relative tails on sparse contrasts are needed for every growing-p TD guarantee. Include this delivered alternative. Also list thm:dist-param (lines 188–195), a delivered rate analogue under detectable signals and a fixed floor on its own window. Neither recovers SL's growing-p second-moment theorem. These two entries explain precisely what is already available without TD′.

**C-6 · SUBSTANTIVE · E7, lead synthesis and final evidence table.** Across-replicate quantiles of a predictive distance computed from a stored list and posterior-mean coefficients do not measure the within-data posterior mass outside a ball. For instance, errors in opposite directions can cancel in a posterior mean. E7 also uses a different radius. Describe it as a predictive-summary diagnostic. Likewise E1's MPM recovery and restricted κ do not test exact posterior mass or all active mixed classes. E7's selected-MPM variance ratio illustrates variance inflation for a fitted subset. It does not verify a prespecified-candidate theorem. Use the qualifications from the body in the summary table as well.

**C-7 · MINOR · TS-b sieve and §3.3 converse.** Restrict D,m to s0−d+m ≤ min(K,J) and m ≤ p−s0. The current m ≤ J also charges classes already discarded by localization. The containing model U is eventually admissible since J and s are fixed. For the local converse, explicitly record log odds = Pr_n(1) − (√λ̂+Z)²/2 + O_P(1) at λ=R_n/σ²=O(log n). Here V_n/n=1+O_P(n^−1/2) by the centered Gaussian RSS law, the logarithm remainder is O_P(log² n/n), and fixed-support relative transfer gives λ̂−λ=o_P(1). The coarse o_P(Pr_n) expansion in the first converse is insufficient on the boundary's √Pr_n scale. These estimates justify the normal tail without an extra assumption.

**A simple population headline.** For every fixed integer k require min R_n(S)/[σ² log(p∨n)] → ∞ over feasible underfits of size ≤k (empty minimum +∞). This is a familiar uniform projection gap. At fixed M apply it with k=J. The worst-case Gaussian width and the square root of the deletion price are both O(√log n) uniformly on that sieve. Thus the existing mixed-rival path applies with room for positive slack, conditional on completion of its coded-score bounds. The criterion costs boundary signals, including S1 at constant SNR under polynomial growth with log p comparable to log n. S2 alone does not define a growing sequence. Present this as an easily stated sufficient condition, with a finite-ratio alternative for the scientific question about shrinking effects.

**A deterministic finite-ratio alternative.** A more informative population measure is the largest fraction of the deletion residual explainable by m nulls. For A=T∖D and S=A∪N put r=min_N R_n(S)/σ², h=min_N R_n(S)/R_n(A), and a=√[2(1−√h)]. Take admissible classes only. Replace the conditional width in the refined condition by (a+η)√[2 log binom(p−s0,m)] for an arbitrarily small **fixed positive** buffer η, with C=1. Positive residuals on active classes are required as before. This is a sufficient population criterion to derive, with a short geometric argument:
    
- Given X, take the unit residual direction v_A before any nulls are added. Nested projections give ⟨v_S,v_A⟩=√(R̂(S)/R̂(A)). Subtract the common centered Gaussian term along v_A inside the expected maximum. The finite Gaussian maximum bound then costs at most max_N ‖v_S−v_A‖ √[2 log binom(p−s0,m)]. One can take the minimum with the original unit-vector bound.
- At fixed J and polynomial p, sparse relative isometry on T∪N has relative error o_P(1), uniformly (take a vanishing tolerance slowly enough that n times its square dominates (s+J)log(p∨n)). It transfers the residual ratios multiplicatively, so the maximum distance is at most a+η with probability tending to one. There is no leakage-set conjecture in this argument. For m=0 there is one direction and the width remains zero.
- This bound can be conservative when several null sets explain substantial signal in almost the same direction. Its geometric step is low risk; completing TS-b still requires the truncated superset sum and coded mixed odds. Population ratios can be calculated from Schur complements for **any** finite-covariance design with the required invertibility. For S1 the ratio is exactly one when the retained last active column screens all nulls from the omitted signal. The remaining D, active m and positive slack need checking. S2 coverage is unestablished. Neither simulation family occurs in the proposed assumption.

## 3. The set as a whole

The distribution, screening, exact-selection and finite-candidate projection results have distinct jobs. A-GL, fixed sparsity, polynomial growth, relative tails and a sparse correlation floor form a defensible base for the fast TD result. A-MARGIN-H and A-MARGIN-U are alternatives for different conclusions. A-BS′ and A-DIM′ are research options. A-MIS(iii) transfers the well-specified theorem to a reduced experiment. TM-a0 is the result that genuinely tolerates an incorrect conditional response family. For general designs call R/n a **linear projection error**. Its Schur-complement formula holds beyond Gaussian rows. Ordinary conditional variance need not equal this error.

SL provide precedents for tails on sparse contrasts, residual identifiability and a fixed-candidate projection. Their pairwise MAP conclusion is weaker than exact posterior mass concentration. Their Condition 2 analogue in growing dimension and their approximate-sparsity result remain outside the delivered scope here. The catalogue covers these boundaries well. A referee's first question will be whether the exact-selection premise can be stated from the data-generating law before sampling and whether it has actually been verified on the boundary examples. The population alternatives answer the first question. The full answer to the second remains open. Keep all S1/S2 constants and coverage claims in the verification material; §4.2 currently embeds several inside the mathematical statement.

## 4. Top five changes, in order

1. Lead exact selection with population separation, retain a finite-ratio route for shrinking signals, and split out the sharp deletion converse.
2. Remove the unidentified credit multiplier from the asymptotic premise, distinguish class and single-rival prices, and restrict quantification to feasible localized rivals.
3. Correct multi-deletion width construction and qualify the evidence for both posterior mass and mixed-rival separation.
4. Restore the predictorwise SL-5 and moments-only SL-9 inclusion conclusions, and include the delivered alternative TD rates.
5. State the conditioning and uniformity for independent candidate screening and give the boundary-scale odds remainder for the converse.
