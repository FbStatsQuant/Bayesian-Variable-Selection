### SL-5 through SL-7

- **SL-5, line 51 — OK** for the strong and weak sure screening bounds, SL p. 956, with Condition 2 replacing Condition 1 in the latter. The p. 956 notation $\pi(\boldsymbol t\subset\xi_q)\to_P1$ expresses inclusion of all true predictors by the data-dependent thresholded set with probability tending to one for fixed $q\in(0,1)$. **WRONG** if “all choices” includes $j_i=k$. Condition 3 excludes $k$ from its list of at most $\bar r_n$ other predictors, with arbitrary $b_i\in\mathbb R$, SL p. 955. The stated variance criterion is sufficient for jointly Gaussian predictors when it holds uniformly over those choices, pp. 955–956. The residual-variance floor can vanish.
- **SL-5 proof — OK** as a transcription. SLs §1.2 pp. 5–6 gives $d^2(f^*,f)\ge\min\{\delta_n,\beta_t^{*2}\delta_n/(20\sigma^{*2}),1-\sqrt{0.8}\}$, with the final display on p. 6 and SLs' normalized Hellinger convention. **WRONG** as an unqualified consequence of Condition 3 for arbitrary signals or all candidate models. The minimum is $\gg\epsilon_n^2$ if $\beta_t^{*2}/\sigma^{*2}$ is fixed positive, or more generally $\beta_t^{*2}\delta_n/\sigma^{*2}\gg\epsilon_n^2$. Also, the proof's contrast on p. 5 can involve $\bar r_n+\vert t\vert -1$ other predictors from $\xi\cup t$, but Condition 3 covers only $\bar r_n$. Applying the printed bound in that generality needs a condition covering the actual contrasts or an argument reducing their size.
- **SL-6, line 52 — OK** for the sample eigenvalue inequality (22), (23), (24) and both exact precision branches, SLs p. 13. The identity branch requires $nl_n\succ\log l_n'$ and $\log(\sqrt{nl_n}/r_n)\succ\sqrt{\log P_n}$. The Gram-precision branch requires $nl_n\succ l_n'$ and $\log(\sqrt n/r_n)\succ\sqrt{\log P_n}$. It does not imply concentration of total posterior mass. **WRONG** as a complete assumptions list without data from model (1), sparse $t$, non-increasing $l_n$, and non-decreasing $l_n'$.
- **SL-6 proof — OK** for the lower bound $nl_nc^2$ on noncentrality, $c=\min_{i\in t}\vert \beta_i^*\vert $, SLs p. 15. The draft's $nl_nc^2\gg\log\bar r_n+\bar r_n\log P_n$ is an inferred sufficient dominance scale if $c$ is allowed to vary. The proof uses (23) with $c$ treated as fixed. **WRONG** if presented as a separately stated hypothesis or a necessary threshold. The proof's intermediate lower bound on a maximum on p. 15 cannot establish uniform separation as written. The needed bound is on the minimum over underfitted models. SLs p. 13 (25) is the pairwise posterior-odds **inequality**, with Bernoulli factor $(r_n/(P_n-r_n))^{\vert k\vert -\vert t\vert }$, determinant factors $K_2^{\vert t\setminus k\vert }/K_1^{\vert k\setminus t\vert }$, and regularized RSS factor $\{(2b_0+R_t)/(2b_0+R_k)\}^{n/2+a_0}$. The draft assigns no conflicting label to (25).
- **SL-6 eigenvalue remark — OK** for “(22) is hardly satisfied”, SLs p. 15, and for SLs p. 16 attributing the Gaussian argument to Fan & Lv Appendix A.7. **WRONG** if (26) is read as a simultaneous $e^{-nC}$ bound or as guaranteed by arbitrary population bounds at the same endpoints. On SLs p. 15 it bounds $\Pr\{\lambda_{\min}(X_\zeta^\top X_\zeta)<nl_n\text{ or }\lambda_{\max}(X_\zeta^\top X_\zeta)>nl_n'\}$ by $e^{-nC}$ for **each fixed** size-$(\bar r_n+\vert t\vert )$ set. The unnumbered next display gives $e^{-nC/2}$ for failure anywhere by a union bound with $\bar r_n\log P_n=o(n)$. Gaussian concentration needs slack between population and sample thresholds. Even a univariate Gaussian sample second moment falls below its population variance with probability tending to $1/2$ when the threshold is exactly at that variance.
