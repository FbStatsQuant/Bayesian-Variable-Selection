## A. Identifiability / residual-signal margin

Proposed MARGIN assumption (ours): every model that misses a true predictor leaves ‖(I−P_S)Xβ*‖² ≥ cσ²(log p + ½ log n) per missed predictor.

### A1. Chen & Chen (2008), Biometrika 95(3) 759–771 — **NOT VERIFIED (primary)**
- Status: paper not readable (paywall). Do not cite a label or page for it from this file.
- Second-hand, **VERIFIED in Luo & Chen (arXiv:1107.2502v1), p. 7**, which writes: "This condition is slightly different from what is called the asymptotic identifiability condition in [4]" ([4] = Chen & Chen 2008) and "In the case of high dimensional feature space, i.e., p_n = O(n^κ) … and a fixed number of relevant features, [4] showed that if γ > 1 − 1/(2κ) the EBIC is selection consistent" (p. 7). Luo & Chen also say that for fixed p_0 "the sparse Riesz condition implies the consistency condition as shown in [4]" (p. 7).
- SBJ (arXiv:1507.07106v4), p. 14: "Assumption 4 is called the sparse Riesz condition and is also used in Chen and Chen (2008) and Kim et al. (2012)."
- Our expected form "lim inf_n min_{s:|s|≤kp₀, s⊉s₀} Δ_n(s)/(p₀ ln p) = ∞, Δ_n(s)=‖μ−H(s)μ‖²" is exactly the Luo–Chen "Consistency Condition" below (p. 7), which Luo & Chen present as a variant of the Chen–Chen one. Whether Chen–Chen's own condition has the same size restriction ν(s) ≤ k_n is **NOT VERIFIED**.
- To close: someone with journal access (Rice library) should read Chen & Chen §3 (identifiability condition, Theorem 1).

### A4 (used instead of A1 for the exact form). Luo & Chen (2013), "Extended BIC for linear regression models with diverging number of relevant features and high or ultra-high feature spaces", published in J. Statist. Plann. Inference (journal version not read). **VERIFIED in arXiv:1107.2502v1**
- p. 6: Δ_n(s) = ‖μ_n − H_n(s)μ_n‖²₂, μ_n = E y_n = X_n(s_0n)β_n(s_0n); H_n(s) = projection onto X_n(s). EBIC_γ(s) = n ln(‖y−H_n(s)y‖²/n) + ν(s) ln n + 2γ ln τ(S_j).
- p. 7, "Consistency Condition": lim_{n→∞} min{ Δ_n(s)/(p_0n ln p_n) : s_0 ⊄ s, ν(s) ≤ k_n } = ∞, where k_n = k p_0n for any fixed k > 1. They note it "implicitly requires (n/(p_0n ln p_n))^{1/2} min{|β_nj|: j∈s_0n} → ∞" (their display (2)).
- Theorem 1, pp. 8–9: assume model (1), the Consistency Condition, p_0n ln p_n = o(n), ln p_0n/ln p_n → δ ≥ 0; then P{min_{ν(s)≤k_n} EBIC_γ(s) > EBIC_γ(s_0n)} → 1 if γ > (1+δ)/(1−δ) − ln n/(2(1−δ) ln p_n).
- Proposition 1 (p. 8) gives a sufficient condition (3) in terms of ‖(I−H_n(k(s)))X_n({k})‖.
- Difference from our MARGIN: the Luo–Chen/Chen–Chen margin is **total** over the model and scaled by p_0 (Δ_n/(p_0 ln p) → ∞, a "→ ∞" not a constant c). Ours is **per missed predictor** with a constant c.

## B. Necessity of ≈ √(log p / n) signals

### B5. Wainwright (2009), IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 55(12) 5728–5741, arXiv:math/0702301v2 (20 Feb 2007; arXiv pagination 1–… , IEEE pagination not seen). **VERIFIED (page image of p. 4)**
Setup (pp. 2–4): Y_i = x_iᵀβ* + W_i, W_i ∼ N(0,σ²) (w.l.o.g. σ² = 1, stated in the proof set-up, §2.1); rows x_i ∼ N(0, I_p) (**standard Gaussian ensemble**); sparsity s = |S|; M(β*) := min_{i∈S}|β*_i| (display (4)); success = exact 0–1 support recovery; error probability **averaged over a uniformly random support S** and over X, W and "the choice of the entries β*_S" (p. 4).
- **Theorem 1 (Sufficient conditions), p. 4:** "If (n−s)M²(β*) → +∞, then the following condition suffices … for some fixed constant C > 0, n > C max{ s log(p/s), (1/M²(β*)) log(p−s) }." (5)
- **Theorem 2 (Necessary conditions), p. 4:** "Asymptotically reliable recovery is impossible under the following condition: for some fixed constant C′ > 0, n < [C′/(s M²(β*))] s log(p/s)." (6) Equivalent form: n M²(β*) < C′ log(p/s), i.e. reliable recovery needs M²(β*) ≳ log(p/s)/n.
- p. 5: "we identify the critical scaling as M²(β*) = Θ(1/s)" — i.e. sublinear sparsity gives n = Θ(s log(p−s)); footnote 1 states the earlier Lasso result allowed M²(β*) = f(s)/s with f → ∞.
- **Caveats (state when citing):** (i) Gaussian i.i.d. design, not general X; (ii) average-case over random support and random signs/sizes of β_S, not worst case; (iii) constants C, C′ unspecified; (iv) in the stated form, M² is in units of σ² = 1. So B5 supports "a minimum signal of order σ²·log(p/s)/n per coefficient is necessary", not "√(log p/n)" exactly in sup-norm units unless squared: |β_j| ≳ σ√(log(p/s)/n).

### B6. Fletcher, Rangan & Goyal (2009), IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 55(12) 5758–5772, arXiv:0804.1839v1 (11 Apr 2008). **VERIFIED (arXiv v1 pagination; journal version not read)**
Setup (p. 3): y = Ax + d, A ∈ R^{m×n} i.i.d. N(0, 1/m), d i.i.d. unit-variance Gaussian, x k-sparse; SNR = ‖x‖²/m (eq. (4)); MAR = min_j|x_j|²/(‖x‖²/k) (eq. (5)); so MAR·SNR = (k/m) min_j|x_j|² (p. 4).
- **Theorem 1, p. 4:** "Let k = k(n) and m = m(n) vary with n such that lim k(n) = ∞ and m(n) < (2−δ)/(MAR·SNR) · k log(n−k) + k − 1 (6) for some δ > 0. Then even the ML estimator asymptotically cannot detect the sparsity pattern, i.e. lim Pr(Î_ML = I_true) = 0."
- **Derived by me, not stated in the paper:** substituting MAR·SNR = (k/m)min|x_j|² into (6) gives the condition m < (2−δ) m log(n−k)/min_j|x_j|² + k − 1, i.e. (for m ≫ k) failure whenever min_j |x_j|² < (2−δ) log(n−k). In regression units (unit-norm columns in expectation, noise variance 1): **signal energy per active predictor below ≈ 2σ² log p ⇒ ML exact recovery fails**. This has an explicit constant (2), unlike Wainwright's C′, and fits a margin cσ²(log p + ½ log n) with c ≥ 2 on the log p part.
- Also p. 3, Table I: same bound with finite SNR; Theorem 2 (p. 4, not needed) gives a sufficient condition for the maximum-correlation estimator, m > 8(1+SNR)/(MAR·SNR) · k log(n−k).
- Caveats: Gaussian ensemble; **ML decoder** and k → ∞; averaged-error vs. fixed-support nuance not checked in detail.

### D12. Grünwald & van Ommen (2017), Bayesian Analysis 12(4) 1069–1103; **read arXiv:1412.3730v3 (29 Oct 2018 = the 2014 original text; BA labels NOT VERIFIED)**
**What is inconsistent.** It is an **empirical** demonstration; the paper says (p. 5): "we only demonstrate the inconsistency empirically." No theorem proves inconsistency here (the Grünwald–Langford 2004/2007 proof is for a different, artificial classification setup).
- p. 2–4: random design, (X_i,Y_i) i.i.d. from P*; nested linear Gaussian models M_p, p ≤ p_max (allowing p_max = ∞), working model **homoskedastic** N(·, σ²) with standard conjugate priors and a discrete prior on p; truth has **heteroskedastic "in-liers"** (p. 4): "at each i, toss a fair coin. If heads, X_i ∼ U[−1,1] (or N(0,1)-type inputs), Y_i = ε_i; if tails, (X_i,Y_i) = (0,0), so that there is no variance at all." The KL-best P̃ ∈ M₀ (all β = 0, σ̃² = σ₀²/2), yet the posterior mass moves to ever-higher p with predictions far from P̃. Same phenomenon for Bayesian ridge regression (fixed finite p).
- p. 16–17 (§5.1): prior π(p) ∝ 1/((p+2)(log(p+2))²) (also uniform); β|σ² ∼ N(0, σ²Σ₀), Σ₀ = I_{p+1} (informative) or 1000·I; σ² ∼ IG(a₀=1, b₀=1/40). Correct-model experiment: Y = .1(X₁+…+X₄)+ε, σ*²=1/40, X ∼ N(0,I); wrong-model experiment: same with prob. ½ and (0,0) with prob. ½, noise variance σ₀² = 2σ*². Main experiments: p_max = 50 and 100 (Figures 3–6, pp. 22–25); n of order 100 in the plotted region. With **correct** model the same standard Bayes behaves "excellently" (p. 19–20).
- p. 34 (Figure 9 and §6.3): explanation — misspecification is "bad" when the model is **non-convex** and the Bayes predictive P̄ can lie outside M and even beat the best P̃ in M ("hypercompression", Fig. 10 p. 36); if the infimum of KL does not decrease over the convex hull ("benign" misspecification), any η < 1 is fine. p. 38 Theorem 1 is the "no-hypercompression" result valid under correct specification (not an inconsistency theorem).
- **p. 46–47 (§7.2) — what excludes the phenomenon:** "if, for all ε > 0, the model can be covered by a finite number of ε-Hellinger balls, then the Bayesian posterior eventually concentrates … This implies that both in the ridge regression (finite p) and in the model averaging experiments (finite p_max), Bayes eventually 'recovers' … However, if p_max = ∞, then the model has no finite Hellinger cover any more … and indeed the conditions for Theorem 1 of De Blasi and Walker (2013) do not apply any more. Our results show that in such a case we can indeed have inconsistency if the model is incorrect." Also p. 2: the strongest misspecification theorems (Kleijn–van der Vaart 2006, De Blasi–Walker 2013, Ramamoorthi et al. 2015) hold "under regularity conditions substantially stronger than those needed … when the model is correct."
- **Inference for us (mine, flagged):** with p = p_n growing exponentially the model class is the growing analogue of "p_max = ∞", so Kleijn–van der Vaart/De Blasi–Walker (fixed class, i.i.d.) do not apply directly and GvO-type failure (homoskedastic working model, heteroskedastic truth, non-convex union of models) is **not excluded by general theory**. Conditions that **do** exclude their example: (a) homoskedastic or sub-Gaussian-error truth with the working-model second moment correctly specified (the in-lier mechanism removes it), (b) a fixed finite candidate set (Song–Liang Theorem 4 on a fixed subset s), (c) a margin that makes the KL optimum the **only** well-separated model (Rossell 2022, D13), (d) tempering/learning-rate η < 1 (their SafeBayes, p. 41–42). Which of (a)–(d) to state is a decision for the Task Lead.

## What this means for the proposed assumptions (my inference, for the Task Lead)

1. **MARGIN.** Verified precedents state margins either (a) as Δ_n(·) ≥ C·σ²|t|·log p-type totals (NH14 4.4, with |t|; Luo–Chen/Chen–Chen with p₀ and "→ ∞") or (b) as per-coefficient β-min min|β_j|²/σ² ≳ (log p)/n (YWJ (8), Fan–Lv Cond. 3 with exponent κ, CSV15 Thm 5 with √(|S₀| log p), Rossell's table). A per-missed-predictor margin cσ²(log p + ½ log n) is **weaker in the |t| factor** than NH14/Chen–Chen and has the same log-scale as YWJ/NH14. Song–Liang themselves state **no** margin (SBJ also none).
2. **Necessity.** Wainwright (Thm 2) and Fletcher et al. (Thm 1) show that a per-predictor signal energy of order σ² log(p/s) (explicit constant ≈ 2 σ² log p for ML in FRG) is necessary — but only for Gaussian designs and averaged error; cite them as support for the order, not as a universal minimax claim.
3. **Density consistency.** Jiang's Theorem 1/2/4 and Song–Liang's Theorems 1–2 need bounded or sub-Gaussian covariates, Σ|β*_j| < ∞ and a size cap (or the (38)-type tail bound); the cap-free version still has r̄_n ln K_n ≺ nε_n².
4. **Misspecification.** The KL-projection posterior theorems (KvdV, DBW) are for a fixed model and i.i.d. data; Song–Liang apply them on a **fixed** subset s. Grünwald–van Ommen show the growing-class (p_max = ∞) case is not covered and that homoskedastic working models with heteroskedastic truth can fail empirically at moderate n. Rossell (2022) is the only high-dimensional misspecification result found, and it is Gaussian-mean/covariance with p̄ ≲ n.
5. **Dimension.** Verified conventions: log p_n = o(n) (NH14 4.1), log p = O(n^α), α<1 (SBJ A1; Jiang Cor. 1 ξ<1), log p = O(n^ξ), ξ<1−2κ (Fan–Lv), p < n / p = O(n) (JR12), p ≥ n (YWJ), polynomial P_n < n^κ log n (Song–Liang Cor. S1). CSV15 states no explicit log p = o(n).
