## 2. Setting
- State space: a general measurable space (X, B) with dominating measure rho (p.3). No assumption that X is discrete or binary in the theory. Applications: R^d (log-concave mixtures, Gaussian mixtures), and {-1,1}^d (mean-field Ising, Sec 5.3, p.15).
- Target: pi = mu_V. Bridge mu_0,...,mu_V = pi, mu_v = q_v/z_v. A partition A = {A_j : j=1..p} of X is chosen BY THE ANALYST, typically the local modes; p here is the number of partition elements, not a dimension. A is NOT used by the algorithm in practice ("the algorithm given in 2.1 serves as a useful theoretical model", p.3).
- Algorithm analysed (p.3-4): draw X_0^{1:N} iid from mu_0; for v=1..V: (a) multinomial resampling with weights w_v(X_{v-1}^k) = q_v/q_{v-1}; (b) mutate each resampled particle for t steps with the PARTITION-RESTRICTED kernel K_{v|A_j}, equation (1): K_{v|A}(x,B) = K_v(x,B) + K_v(x,A^c) 1_{x in B}, a mu_v-invariant kernel that rejects any move leaving the current partition element. So particles never cross between partition elements during mutation; only resampling reallocates mass among elements. Resampling occurs at EVERY step (no ESS rule). Total N(2V+1) particles.
- Schedule: deterministic, fixed in advance. NOT adaptive (no ESS-triggered resampling, no data-driven temperatures). Tempering is only an example: mu_v proportional to pi^{beta_v} (Sec 5, Sec 6 eq (21)); the main theorem allows any bridge satisfying A1.
- Kernel: any mu_v-invariant kernel K_v, restricted as in (1); the restricted kernel must be ergodic on each element and have a finite 7-warm mixing time.

## 3. Main theorems (faithful statements)

**Assumption A1 (p.3).** For all v, x:
$$\frac{\mu_v(x)}{\mu_{v-1}(x)} = \frac{z_{v-1}}{z_v}\,\frac{q_v(x)}{q_{v-1}(x)} = \frac{z_{v-1}}{z_v}\,w_v(x) \le ZW,$$
with $Z=\max_{v\in\{0..V\}} z_{v-1}/z_v$ and $W=\max_{v}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X} w_v(x)$. (Uniformly bounded density ratios.)

Notation: $\mu^\star := \min_{v=0,\dots,V}\min_{j=1,\dots,p}\mu_v(A_j)$; $M$-warm: $\nu\in\mathcal P_M(\mu)$ iff $\sup_B \nu(B)/\mu(B)\le M$; the $M$-warm mixing time of the restricted kernel
$$\tau_{v,j}(\epsilon,M)=\min\Big\{t:\ \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal P_M(\mu_{v|A_j})}\Big\|\int_{A_j}\eta_{|A_j}(dx)K^t_{v|A_j}(x,\cdot)-\mu_{v|A_j}(\cdot)\Big\|_{TV}\le\epsilon\Big\}\quad(\text{eq. (5)}).$$

**Theorem 1 (p.6).** Suppose $|f|\le 1$ and A1 holds. Let $\epsilon\in(0,1/2)$ and choose
1. $N>\dfrac{1}{\epsilon^2}\max\Big\{3456\Big(\dfrac{VWZ}{\mu^\star}\Big)^2\log\Big(\dfrac{64Vp}{\mu^\star}\Big),\ p^2\log(1024p^2)\Big\}$,
2. $t>\max_{j=1..p}\max_{v=1..V}\tau_{v,j}\Big(\dfrac{\mu^\star}{16NV},\,7\Big)$.

Then with probability at least $3/4$,
$$\Big|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N f(X_V^i)-\frac{1+\epsilon^2/16}{1-\epsilon^2/16}\,\mathbb E_\pi[f]\Big|\le\epsilon .$$
Total work $NVt$ Markov steps. "Probability 3/4" is a fixed constant (proof sets $\delta=1/8$, p.13); no statement of how to boost it (my inference: independent repetition and a median would, but the paper does not say so).

Proof architecture (pp.5-13): bias-variance split $\mathbb E_\pi[f]=\sum_j \mathbb E_{\pi|A_j}[f]\pi(A_j)$ vs $\mathbb E[\hat f]=\sum_j\mathbb E_{\hat\mu_{V|A_j}}[\hat f_j]\hat\mu_V(A_j)$; a coupling $(X_v^i,\bar X_v^i)$ that preserves the algorithm's law but makes $\bar X_v^i$ conditionally $\mu_{v|A_j}$-distributed given its element; events $\mathcal C_v^j(i)$ (weights concentrate around conditional mean), $\mathcal C_v(ii)$ (coupling succeeds), $\mathcal E_v,\mathcal G_v$; Lemma 1 (resampling probability of element j within $\phi^{\pm v}$ of $\mu_v(A_j)$, $\phi=(1+\lambda)/(1-\lambda)$, $\lambda=\epsilon/(24V)$); Lemma 2 ($\sum_j\hat w^j_v\in[\phi^{-v},\phi^{v}]\,z_v/z_{v-1}$); Lemma 3 (resampled law is locally 7-warm w.r.t. $\mu_v$ and $A$ given $\mathcal G_v$, provided $\mathbb P(\mathcal G_v^c)\le\mu^\star/4$); Lemma 4 (weight concentration, needs $N> 2W^2Z^2\phi^{2(v-1)}\log(4p/\delta)/(\lambda^2(\mu^\star)^2)$); Lemma 5 (coupling succeeds if $t>\max\tau(\delta/N,7)$); Lemma 6 ($\mathbb P(\mathcal E_V)\ge 1-\delta\mu^\star$ for $\delta\in(0,1/4)$ and $N>3456(VWZ/(\epsilon\mu^\star))^2\log(8Vp/(\delta\mu^\star))$, $t>\max\tau(\delta\mu^\star/(2NV),7)$); Lemma 7 ($|\sum_j\mathbb E[f(\bar X_V^i)1_{A_j}(\bar X_V^i)|\mathcal F_{V-1}]-\frac{1+\epsilon^2/16}{1-\epsilon^2/16}\mathbb E_\pi f|\le\frac34\epsilon$); Lemma 11 (Hoeffding, $\mathbb P(|\bar f-\mathbb E[\bar f|\mathcal F_{v-1}]|>\lambda)\le 4e^{-N\lambda^2/(2(b-a)^2)}$).

**Proposition 2 (p.16, persistence).** With $\gamma(\mathcal A)=\min_{j}\prod_{v=1}^V\min\{1,\mu_{v-1}(A_j)/\mu_v(A_j)\}$ (from Woodard-Schmidler-Huber 2009b), $\mu^\star>\gamma(\mathcal A)\,\pi^\star$, where $\pi^\star=\min_j\pi(A_j)$.

**Proposition 3 (p.17, overlap).** $\delta(\mathcal A)=\min_{v<V,j}\int_{A_j}\min\{\mu_v,\mu_{v+1}\}dx\big/\max\{\mu_v(A_j),\mu_{v+1}(A_j)\}$ satisfies $\delta(\mathcal A)\ge\min\{1,1/(ZW)\}\gamma(\mathcal A)\pi^\star$.

**Corollary 1 (p.17).** Under A1, $|f|\le1$, $\epsilon\in(0,1/2)$: $N>\epsilon^{-2}\max\{3456(VWZ/(\pi^\star\gamma(\mathcal A)))^2\log(64Vp/(\pi^\star\gamma(\mathcal A))),\ p^2\log(1024p^2)\}$ and $t>\log(288NV/(\gamma(\mathcal A)\pi^\star))\,[\min_{v,j}\mathrm{Gap}(K_{v|A_j})]^{-1}$ give the same conclusion with probability $\ge3/4$ (via the spectral-gap bound (19)).

**Corollary 2 (p.18).** If $\mathrm{Gap}(K_{v|A_j})$, $\mathrm{Gap}(K_0)$, $\gamma(\mathcal A)$, $\pi^\star$, $(ZW)^{-1}$ decay at most polynomially in $d$ and $V=O(\mathrm{poly}(d))$, then (1) parallel and simulated tempering and (2) the SMC algorithm of Sec 2.1 give polynomial-time $(\epsilon,\delta)$-randomised approximations of $\mathbb E_\pi f$. Paper's own caveat: only the "if" direction (PT/ST fast implies SMC fast, up to $(ZW)^{-1}$ vs $\delta(\mathcal A)$); no SMC lower bound (p.18).

**Applications (Sec 5).**
- 5.1 (p.13-14): $\pi\propto w\pi_1 1_H+(1-w)\pi_2 1_{H^c}$ on $\mathbb R^d$, $\pi_j\propto e^{-f_j}$, $f_j$ convex, $H$ a half-space containing $\mathbb E_{\pi_1}X$ and $H^c$ containing $\mathbb E_{\pi_2}X$, equal normalising constants under tempering (eq. (16)). Partition $\{H,H^c\}$, $\mu^\star\ge\max\{w,1-w\}/e$; ball walk/hit-and-run (Lovasz-Vempala) give polynomial 7-warm mixing; conclusion: $\mathcal O(\mathrm{poly}(d,V,\log V,Z,\epsilon^{-1}))$ cost.
- 5.2 (p.14-15): Gaussian mixture $\tfrac12N(-\mathbf 1_d,\sigma^2I)+\tfrac12N(\mathbf 1_d,\sigma^2I)$ truncated by $H=\{x^\top\mathbf 1_d>0\}$ (Woodard et al.), $\beta_v=d^{-1}(1+1/d)^v$, $V=\lceil d\log d\rceil$; $Z\le 2e$, $\mu^\star\ge1/4$; cost $\mathcal O^\star(\mathrm{poly}(d))$. Contrast with PT/ST torpid mixing for this case (Woodard et al. 2009b).
- 5.3 (p.15-16), THE ONLY BINARY-SPACE RESULT: $\mathcal X=\{-1,1\}^d$, mean-field Ising $\pi(x)\propto\exp\{\frac{\alpha}{2d}(\sum_ix_i)^2\}$, $\alpha\in\mathbb R$, $d$ odd; kernel $K_v$ = single-site (coordinate uniformly chosen, sign flipped) Metropolis-Hastings chain with limit $\pi^{\beta_v}$, $\beta_v=v/d$, $V=d$; partition $A_1=\{\sum x_i\ge0\}$, $A_2=\{\sum x_i<0\}$ (the two magnetisation lobes); by symmetry $\mu_v(A_1)=\mu_v(A_2)=1/2$ so $\mu^\star=1/2$; Woodard et al. give $\mu_v/\mu_{v-1}\in[e^{-\alpha/2},e^{\alpha/2}]$ so $ZW\le e^{\alpha/2}$; Madras-Zheng give a polynomial lower bound on the restricted gap. Result: $N=\mathcal O^\star(d^2)$, $t=\mathcal O^\star(\mathrm{poly}(d))$, $NVt$ polynomial. The paper does not state that the single-flip chain at $\pi$ itself mixes slowly for $\alpha>1$ (that is a known fact (from memory, not checked) due to the bottleneck between the two lobes). The paper only says tempering helps.

## 4. Does any result cover ...
- (i) Binary spaces {0,1}^p: only via the mean-field Ising example (a symmetric, permutation-invariant 2-mode target on {-1,1}^d). The general theorem is state-space agnostic, so formally it applies to any finite space PROVIDED a partition, restricted-kernel warm mixing times, $\mu^\star$ and $ZW$ can be computed. No general binary-space theory.
- (ii) BVS / spike-and-slab posterior: NO. No statistics example, no regression, no model space. Nothing about posterior consistency.
- (iii) Adaptive tempering: NO. Deterministic, fixed schedule; the Conclusion defers adaptive path selection to Marion-Mathews-Schmidler (2022, "L2 bounds ... adaptive path selection", under revision) (p.19, ref [25]) .
- (iv) Frozen schedules: YES in the sense that the whole analysis is for a schedule fixed in advance; it says nothing about a schedule estimated by a pilot run (the theorem would apply conditionally on the schedule only if A1 and the mixing quantities are verified for that realised schedule; not discussed).
- (v) Evidence / normalising constants: NOT a theorem. Lemma 2 (p.8) shows that on the event $\mathcal G_v$, $\sum_j\hat w_v^j\in[\phi^{-v},\phi^v]z_v/z_{v-1}$, and the text immediately after Remark 1 notes that the algorithm "can be used to approximate $z_v$ ... when the initial $z_0$ is known". No unbiasedness, no variance bound, no theorem for $\hat Z$.

## 5. What the result needs and what it costs (relevant to BVS; all (my inference) unless stated)
1. Bounded density ratio (A1). For a geometric bridge with increments $e^{\delta\xi\,\Delta(S)}$, $ZW\le\exp\{\delta\xi\,(\sup\Delta-\inf\Delta)\}$, so one needs $V\gtrsim$ range of $\Delta$ over the (size-capped) model space. For BVS that range is order $n$ times SNR for models near the truth, so $V$ polynomial in $n$ is plausible. Not proved here.
2. $\mu^\star=\min_{v,j}\mu_v(A_j)$ with the partition fixed across steps. In a multimodal BVS posterior that is consistent, the mass of every competing mode at $v=V$ is $\mathrm{e}^{-cn}$-small or smaller. Then $\mu^\star$ is exponentially small and the bound on $N$ is $\propto(\mu^\star)^{-2}$, i.e. exponential in $n$, as stated. The paper itself remarks that one may disregard negligible elements because their contribution to $\mathbb E_\pi f$ is negligible (p.13), but the theorem as stated has no such truncation. A coarser partition that merges the trap basin with the true-model basin brings back a restricted kernel that mixes slowly (my inference). So Theorem 1 does not give a polynomial bound for the trap design family at final-time, and does not give an exponential LOWER bound either (no lower bound anywhere).
3. Local warmness of resampled population (Lemma 3) is the mechanism: resampling keeps every element's mass in the right ratio, so a restricted kernel only needs local mixing. This is the theoretical counterpart of our "independent particles + resampling + local moves" design, but our moves (Gibbs, uniform swap, correlation-neighbour swap, independence move from $\pi_0$) are NOT restricted to a partition. The independence move from $\pi_0$ is the global move that, in their language, jumps between elements, violating their assumption that the kernel does not cross elements. Their theory therefore covers a stricter algorithm than ours (my inference).
4. The estimator analysed is $N^{-1}\sum_if(X_V^i)$ with multinomial resampling at every step, N particles in one system. No Rao-Blackwellisation, no islands, no list renormalisation, no "best model reported". The error is to $\mathbb E_\pi f$ for bounded $f$, i.e. a guarantee about approximating posterior expectations (PIPs as expectations of $1\{j\in S\}$). It is a Monte Carlo statement conditional on the target, never a statement about data events.

## 6. Relation to us
- Prop 5 (unbiased evidence conditional on frozen calibration): NOT supplied by this paper (no unbiasedness result). Classical sources, from memory and NOT verified against the texts: Del Moral (2004), Feynman-Kac Formulae, Ch. 7 (unbiasedness of the unnormalised particle measure / normalising-constant estimator for fixed Feynman-Kac models), Del Moral, Doucet and Jasra (2006, JRSSB) for the SMC-sampler form with product of mean incremental weights, Andrieu-Doucet-Holenstein (2010, JRSSB, PMCMC, Thm 4) as a standard citation for unbiased likelihood estimates, Pitt et al. (2012) for the same. Unbiasedness for a deterministic schedule is a direct martingale/tower-property argument. For adaptive schedules the estimator is not exactly unbiased (Beskos-Jasra-Kantas-Thiery 2016 prove the CLT/LLN with adaptation); our "conditional on frozen calibration" statement is the clean way to recover unbiasedness (the final islands use randomness independent of the pilot).
- Thm 3 (every run reports the truth on high-probability data events): NO overlap. This paper proves a Monte Carlo approximation bound to $\mathbb E_\pi f$ with probability 3/4 for a fixed target; it contains no data, no true model, no probability over data. It would be complementary: (data-level concentration of $\pi$ on $S_0$) + (SMC approximates $\pi$ well) would give "SMC output near $S_0$" through a different route than ours, but only if the Theorem 1 hypotheses were verified, which this paper does not do for BVS. Our Thm 3 holds for every seed and every calibration outcome without any mixing/warmness assumption (it uses a visibility/leakage assumption), which is a different and (for the reported model) stronger kind of statement.
- P1 (trap theorem): SUPPORTS in spirit, does not prove it. (a) It is the only paper here where a binary-space target (mean-field Ising) has a proven poly-time SMC with tempering where single-flip Metropolis is torpid at low temperature (the torpidity is outside this paper). That justifies the story "single-flip local dynamics fail, SMC with a bridge succeeds on bimodal binary targets", and it is the natural comparator to cite. (b) It contains NO lower bound for single-flip chains in BVS, nothing about Zanella-Roberts weighted tempered Gibbs, and it explicitly leaves lower bounds for SMC open (p.18-19). (c) It does NOT compete with P1: P1 is about slow mixing of MCMC on a BVS multimodal design family; this paper's positive SMC bound hinges on $\mu^\star$, $ZW$ and restricted-kernel mixing, none of which is computed for BVS, and for a consistent posterior with vanishing competing mode mass its bound is exponentially large. P1 should say: "our family shows single-flip/ZR chains mix slowly although the posterior concentrates; known SMC complexity results (Mathews-Schmidler) do not directly give a positive polynomial guarantee for our sampler on this family, and any such statement would require the restricted-kernel framework".
- Risk of being scooped or contradicted: low. One caution: if a referee reads Corollary 2 ("SMC is polynomial whenever PT/ST are"), they may ask whether our sampler on the trap family should be polynomial too. Answer: Corollary 2 requires $\mu^\star$, $\gamma(\mathcal A)$ polynomial; in a design family with vanishing mode mass $\gamma(\mathcal A)\pi^\star$ is exponentially small.
