## 2. Model and prior
- Likelihood: Gaussian linear regression, Y = 1_n alpha + X_gamma beta_gamma + eps, noise precision phi (p.2); "directly applicable to GLMs" via Laplace-approximated BFs (p.2, p.10 Remark III). No GLM experiment.
- Model space: all of {0,1}^p, Omega_M (p.2). No size cap in the theory (pFS runs up to p steps). In Ex 3.4 (p=1000) the symmetric prior puts equal mass on each size 0..100 (p.30), i.e. an effective size cap of 100 (n=700).
- Model prior: arbitrary, specified through the pFS pair (rho, lambda) (pp.11-14). Used: "Beta-Binomial(1,1)" = uniform on size, uniform within size (p.22, 24, 27); h(s)=1/(p+1-s) gives uniform size prior (p.12). Also priors with annotation weights, pathways, interactions, blocks, dilution clusters (pp.12-13, S3).
- Coefficient prior: Zellner g-prior with g = n in all experiments (p.22), p(alpha,phi) proportional to 1/phi, beta | phi, gamma ~ N(beta0, g (X'X)^{-1}/phi) (S2, p.46); hyper-g Beta(1, a/2-1) also given (p.46-47). No unit ridge; X_gamma'X_gamma must be invertible, so n > p+1-type issues are avoided only because sizes stay below n.
- Variance prior: improper 1/phi. Marginal likelihood closed form: BF_0(gamma) = (1+g)^{(n-1-|gamma|)/2} / (1+g(1-R^2_gamma))^{(n-1)/2} (p.46). Incremental BF(gamma_t, gamma_{t-1}) = BF_0(gamma_t)/BF_0(gamma_{t-1}) (p.47).

## 3. Sampler design
**Is LIPS an SMC / sequential importance sampler over models? Answer: a sequential importance sampler on a path space; "SMC" only in the weak sense (SIS, no resampling in the algorithm).**
- Particle: a whole pFS path gamma^i_(0) -> ... -> gamma^i_(p) of nested models, each step adds at most one variable; after t steps the model has size <= t (p.16). Equivalent to a randomised forward-stepwise run. A particle that stops early (S_t=1) stays frozen, and its remaining weight increments are 1 (my inference from Eq. on p.17, 18).
- Initial law: deterministic null model, w_0^i=1 (Algorithm 1, p.21). Not the prior, not data-driven beyond the BFs.
- Sequence of targets: pi_{0:t}(.|D), t=0..p: the posterior under the prior pi_{0:t} obtained by forcing the pFS prior to stop at step t+1, i.e. the prior truncated at model size t with all mass of size >= t collapsed onto size t (p.15-16). pi_{0:p}=pi(.|D). So the "path" is a growing MODEL SIZE, not a growing set of variables and NOT a tempering of the likelihood: all p predictors are available at every step; no inverse-temperature, no bridge. Ratios among models of size <= t-1 agree with the full posterior (p.16).
- Schedule: fixed, t=1..p (always p steps; stopped particles idle).
- Proposal / "local information propagation": the exact optimal proposal is the posterior pFS kernel (rho(.|D), lambda(.|D)) of Theorem 3, built from phi(gamma) = E_prior[BF_0(final) | path passes through gamma], which needs an enumeration of all supermodels (exponential). LIP truncates this recursion to k levels below each current model: phi_zeta(gamma) = BF_0(gamma) if |gamma| >= |zeta|+k or |gamma|=p; otherwise rho BF_0 + (1-rho) sum_j lambda_j phi_zeta(gamma^{+j}); then phihat(gamma)=phi_gamma(gamma), and rhohat, lambdahat are given by the Theorem 3 formulas with phihat in place of phi (p.17). Exact when k=p; k=1 is greedy/main-effect; stated to be a special case of the k-step look-ahead proposals of Lin, Chen, Liu 2013 (p.18). Rule of thumb: k=3 for p <= 200-500, k=2 beyond (p.20). Cost is not stated formally; paper says one run at p=1000, k=2, N=20,000 takes about 30 hours on one 3.6 GHz core with 400 MB (p.30). (My inference: each step needs O(p^k) BF evaluations per particle.)
- Weight update: w^i_(t) = w^i_(t-1) * [pi_{t|t-1}(gamma_(t)|gamma_(t-1)) / q_{t|t-1}(gamma_(t)|gamma_(t-1), D)] * BF(gamma_(t), gamma_(t-1)) (p.18, Algorithm 1 p.21). pi_{t|t-1} is the prior pFS transition (Thm 1). Telescoping BFs gives final weight = (prior path probability / proposal path probability) * BF_0(gamma_(p)) (my inference). The paper derives it via pi_{0:t}(gamma_(t))/pi_{0:t-1}(gamma_{t-1}) "= the prior transition probability" (p.18); strictly this identity holds for path probabilities, not for model marginals (a model has many orderings), so the weights are correct as path-space IS but the displayed derivation is loose (my reading; minor).
- Resampling: NONE in Algorithm 1 (p.21). The only mention is in the Discussion: "incorporating a resampling step (Liu 2001) can help remedy (though not eliminate)" accumulating Monte Carlo error when the true model is large (p.33). No ESS, no adaptivity. Algorithm 1 initialises normalised weights w~=1/N (p.21) but they are never used.
- MCMC moves: NONE. Explicitly contrasts itself with MCMC: independent short chains, no convergence needed (pp.32-33). Future work: bidirectional pFS, birth-death link, LASSO-based baseline start, and LIP-based MCMC proposals (pp.33-34).
- Parallel/islands: yes. N particles split into L islands of N/L, each giving an H-T estimate; islanded estimate = mean of L, variance = sum (delta_l - mean)^2 / (L(L-1)) (p.19). Used for variance estimation and parallelism; in the experiments "200 repeats" of LIPS are treated as 200 islands (p.22-24, 27, 30).
- Outputs: PIP_j = Horvitz-Thompson (self-normalised importance sampling) ratio delta_HT = sum_i w^i_(p) E(Delta|gamma^i_(p),D) / sum_i w^i_(p) with Delta = 1(gamma_j=1) (pp.18-20). Same form for predictions E(Y_new|D). Delta-method variance estimator (p.19). No Rao-Blackwellisation (E(Delta|gamma,D) is just the indicator for PIPs; for prediction it is the conditional posterior mean), no list renormalisation over visited models, no median-model rule computed (only suggested, p.34), no reported/best model, no local search, no certification. No evidence estimate is reported (my inference: mean of unnormalised final weights is unbiased for p(D)/p(D|0) = phi(0); the paper does not say this).
- Sizes of experiments (largest p and n): p=15 (n not stated in text), p=88 (n=96: 90 train + 6 test), p=100 (n=350; 200 train / 150 test for prediction), p=1000 (n=700; 600/100). Largest p = 1,000. Particles: 5,000 (k=4), 50,000 (k=3), 50,000 (k=3), 20,000 (k=2); 20,000 particles (and even 200) for prediction at p=1000 (p.32).
- Competitors: MC3 (add/delete/swap; R package BMS; 2e7 iterations, 1e7 burn-in for the "truth" proxy at p=88, 100; at p=1000 it "fails to produce meaningful estimates" after 1e8 iterations, 5e7 burn-in, ~5 days, p.30-31), BAS (1e6 draws; cannot run for p >= n or p >= 1000, p.31), iBMA (keeps <= 30 predictors), LASSO (10-fold CV). The paper acknowledges a better-tuned/population MCMC (Liang-Wong 2000; Jasra et al 2007; Bottolo-Richardson 2010) could do better (p.31); no comparison with Schafer-Chopin 2013, Bottolo-Richardson ESS, or any SMC competitor.
- Empirical results: Ex 3.1 (exact PIPs by enumeration): H-T estimates unbiased in 200 repeats, average over repeats recovers truth (p.23). Ex 3.2/3.3: islanded LIPS PIPs match the MC3 proxy; BAS is biased toward the middle (p.24, 27-28). Prediction ASE: Ex 3.2 LIPS ~ BAS, both better than iBMA (-0.076 se 0.02 for LIPS-iBMA) and LASSO (-0.3 se 0.08) (Fig 3, p.26). Ex 3.3 all similar, LIPS-LASSO -0.21 (se 0.3) (Fig 5, p.29). Ex 3.4 LIPS beats LASSO (-2.4, se 0.4) and iBMA (-6.2, se 0.5) (Fig 7, p.31). PIPs in Ex 3.4 are shown as sampling distributions with NO ground truth (MCMC failed), so correctness at p=1000 is not validated (p.30). Even 200 particles predict well but PIP estimates are "unreliable" (p.32).

## 4. What they prove
Only three results, all exact finite-p statements about the data-augmentation representation. No asymptotics (no n to infinity, no p growing), no probabilistic error bounds.
1. **Theorem 1 (Markov property, p.8; proof S1 p.40).** The pFS tentative models {gamma_(t)}_{t=0}^p form a Markov chain with transition probabilities pi_{t|t-1}(gamma_(t)|gamma_(t-1)) = 1 if |gamma_(t-1)| < t-1 and gamma_(t)=gamma_(t-1) (already stopped); = rho(gamma_(t-1)) if |gamma_(t-1)| = t-1 and gamma_(t)=gamma_(t-1) (stops now); = (1-rho(gamma_(t-1))) lambda_j(gamma_(t-1)) if |gamma_(t-1)|=t-1 and gamma_(t)=gamma_(t-1)^{+j}; = 0 otherwise. Type: structural identity; no randomness-of-algorithm content.
2. **Theorem 2 (Generality, p.9; proof S1 pp.40-42).** Every probability measure pi on Omega_M = {0,1}^p has a pFS representation (rho, lambda) whose final model gamma_(p) has law pi. Proof by induction on p: merge gamma and gamma^{+p} into pi'(gamma)=pi(gamma)+pi(gamma^{+p}) on {0,1}^{p-1}, take its pFS (rho',lambda'), then make X_p always the last variable added, with rho = rho' pi/pi', etc. Type: representation/existence. Consequence stated: any model prior can be put in pFS form; explicit simple recipe for symmetric priors h(0)=pi(0), h(t)=pi(gamma) C(p,t) / prod_{s<t}(1-h(s)), lambda_j=1(gamma_j=0)/(p-|gamma|) (p.14, "one can verify").
3. **Theorem 3 (Information propagation, p.10; proof S1 pp.42-45).** If the prior has pFS (rho,lambda), the posterior given D has pFS (rho(.|D), lambda(.|D)): for rho(gamma)<1, rho(gamma|D) = rho(gamma) BF_0(gamma)/phi(gamma), lambda_j(gamma|D) = lambda_j(gamma)(1-rho(gamma)) phi(gamma^{+j}) / (phi(gamma) - rho(gamma) BF_0(gamma)); for rho(gamma)=1: rho(.|D)=1, lambda(.|D)=lambda. Here phi(gamma)=BF_0(gamma) if |gamma|=p, else rho BF_0 + (1-rho) sum_{j:gamma_j=0} lambda_j phi(gamma^{+j}). Proof: define Phi(gamma)=E[p(D|gamma_(p)) | U_gamma], where U_gamma = {path passes through gamma at step |gamma|} (the paper words this event as "gamma is a submodel of the final model", S1 p.4, which is slightly inaccurate since the first |gamma| picks must be exactly gamma), get the recursion by the tower property, read the posteriors of the latent decision variables off Bayes (HMM). Type: exact algorithm identity (forward summation / backward sampling); the exact scheme costs an enumeration of the model space. Note (my inference): phi(0)=E_prior[BF_0] is the Bayes-factor normaliser.

Not proved / only asserted:
- Consistency and "approximate unbiasedness" of the H-T estimator: asserted by citation (Horvitz-Thompson 1952; Clyde-Ghosh 2012), p.18; no theorem, no CLT. The variance formula is the standard delta-method self-normalised-IS variance (Liu 2001 p.35), p.19. In reality the ratio estimator is consistent in N and biased O(1/N) (my inference); "unbiased" in the abstract/title is loose (the paper itself says "approximately", p.4, 18).
- No proof that the LIP proposal dominates the target (absolute continuity), no bound on weight variance, no dependence of N on p, n, k or sparsity. Only informal remarks: N needed depends critically on proposal quality (p.33); errors accumulate stepwise so large true models are hard (p.33); k>=2 recommended (p.20).
- No posterior consistency, selection consistency, contraction rates, or any guarantee about a reported model. No complexity/mixing result.
Classification: (i) Monte Carlo convergence: none stated (only informal); (ii) complexity: none; (iii) statistical consistency: none; (iv) output guarantees: none. The three theorems are exact algebraic facts about the augmentation, of type "design validity".

Theory vs ours:
- Our Thm 1 (posterior concentration, HPM=MPM=S0 w.p.->1, PIP rates), Thm 2 (Hellinger contraction), Thm 4 (certified screening), Thm 6 (sharpness): no counterpart in Ma (2015); he proves no statistical consistency.
- Our Thm 3 (reported model equals truth for every admissible run, delivery): no counterpart; LIPS has no reported model.
- Our Prop 5 (unbiased evidence under frozen calibration): not claimed by Ma; the same structural fact (mean importance weight unbiased for the normalising constant, no calibration since the schedule is fixed) is implicit in LIPS weights (my inference), but a pure-SIS evidence unbiasedness is textbook and no resampling/adaptation is involved, so this does not pre-empt a statement for an adaptively calibrated, frozen-grid tempered SMC with resampling.
- Our planned P1 (trap theorem for single-flip chains and Zanella-Roberts): LIPS discusses MC3 failure empirically at p=1000 (p.30-31) but no theorem; no overlap. P2-P5: none.
- Contradicts: nothing. Mildly relevant statements: (1) Ma's claim that SIS/LIPS gives "unbiased" BMA is only approximate; (2) his remark that Monte Carlo error accumulates over stepwise inclusion when the true model is large and resampling would help (p.33) is a heuristic that supports our use of resampling.

## 6. Verdict
Novelty risk to us: **PARTIAL OVERLAP (low) on method framing; NONE on theory.**
Justification: LIPS is a non-resampling sequential importance sampler over forward-stepwise inclusion paths (growing model size), with islands and H-T PIPs, tested to p=1000 against MC3/BAS/iBMA/LASSO. It has no likelihood tempering, no MCMC moves, no resampling in the algorithm, no calibration, no evidence estimate, no reported model, and its only theorems are exact representation identities (Thms 1-3), with consistency of the IS estimator merely asserted. Our tempered, adaptive-then-frozen, resample-move SMC with block start law, RB/list PIPs, local-search certification, p up to 10^6, and Theorems 1-6 are not touched.
Must cite: Ma (2015) as an earlier "scalable unbiased BMA via sequential importance sampling over model-space paths with k-step look-ahead proposals", together with Schafer and Chopin (2013) (SMC on binary spaces with geometric bridge) as the two precedents for particle methods over models.
One-sentence differentiation: "Ma (2015) propagates weighted forward-stepwise paths by importance sampling without resampling, tempering or MCMC moves and proves only that the augmentation is exact, whereas our sampler is a tempered resample-move SMC over inclusion vectors with a frozen calibrated schedule, and we prove posterior/selection consistency and guarantees on the reported model."
