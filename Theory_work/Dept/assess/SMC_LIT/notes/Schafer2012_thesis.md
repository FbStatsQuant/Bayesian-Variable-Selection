## 2. Model and prior
- Ch.4 (normal linear model): y | alpha, beta_gamma, sigma^2 ~ N(...) with centred columns (p.75-78); hierarchical prior beta ~ N(0, sigma^2 tau Sigma_gamma), sigma^2 ~ IG(a/2, ab/2); Sigma = I ("independent prior", used in experiments) or Zellner g-prior (Sigma=(Z'Z)^-1, tau=n, tau=d^2, local EB mentioned; mixtures of g priors cited as "ensuring posterior probabilities are consistent", p.79-80, no result). Experiments use the independent prior with George-McCulloch hyper-parameters a=4, b=sigma_hat_1^2, tau=10/b (p.84-85) -- same as the 2013 paper. Model space: all 2^d subsets (no size cap in experiments).
- Model priors (p.80-81, NEW vs the 2013 paper's description): Bernoulli(m) inclusion; Beta-hyperprior (Nott-Kohn); "uniform prior conditional on size with a binomial hyper-prior on size k ~ B(m, d*)", d* <= d wedge n the largest admissible model, m = dbar/d* (i.e. a size-truncated size prior, p.80-81); main-effect (heredity) prior (p.81). The Ch.4 experiments do not say which model prior was used (p.84-85 only fix the coefficient hyperparameters); Ch.5 explicitly uses the 4.3.1 size prior with expected size dbar and d* = 2 dbar (p.104-105). No beta-binomial / Scott-Berger prior.
- Ch.5 (logistic/probit): normal prior N(0, tau Sigma) with tau = n, Sigma = tau I; marginal likelihood not closed form: (i) Laplace approximation (p.101), (ii) pseudo-marginal importance-sampling estimate with Student-t proposal around the penalised MLE (p.102, eq. 5.4), ESS target eta_IS growing with rho_t (p.102), (iii) "corrected Laplace sampler": SMC on the Laplace posterior followed by IS correction weights (p.103).

## 3. Sampler design
- Particles / population: binary vectors, one population (no islands). Ch.4 and Ch.5 do not state n for the SMC runs (only the stopping budget in evaluations of pi, ~1.2-1.4e6 Boston/Concrete, 6.1e5 Protein, p.90-95). Ch.6: n = 1.2e4 (CE), 0.8e4 (SMC) (p.127). No island structure; parallelism = distributing evaluations of pi in the move step (p.89, 108, 134-135).
- Start law: still the uniform law / prior on model space. p.82: "numerical experiments taught us that premature adjustment of p, for example using MCMC pilot runs, leads to faster but less robust algorithms. For Bayesian variable selection, we recommend to use the prior on the model space as initial distribution." p.122: for UQBO they say one could start from a fitted logistic-conditionals family instead of uniform, "we did not exploit this option". Alternatives listed in 4.4.1 (p.82): data-partition (Chopin 2002) path, and a data-orthogonalisation path (Ghosh-Clyde 2011 augmentation) that would give an independent-component start pi_0(gamma | y_o, Z_o) for uniform prior, "obviously more involved" -- stated as a possibility, not implemented. So: no data-driven non-uniform start in any experiment.
- Path: geometric bridge pi_rho ~ p^(1-rho) pi^rho (p.36), mixture path mentioned; adaptive ESS bisection eta* = 0.92 for d ~ 100, "closer to one" as d grows (p.38). Ch.6: tempered family exp(rho f) and level-set family 1_{L+}; ESS replaced by quantile truncation (p.115-116).
- Resampling: systematic at every step (p.39). Move: independence MH with logistic-conditionals proposal fitted on weighted particles (Ch.2-3); repeated until diversity zeta >= 0.95 or improvement < 1e-2 (p.40); p.40: local kernels "less suitable ... since they mix rather slowly. However, batches of local moves can be alternated with independent proposals" -- NEW nuance vs the 2013 paper's "practically impossible"; Ch.6 actually runs an "SMC symmetric" variant (single-flip symmetric kernel, batches of 10 local moves between diversity checks, p.127) and finds it good on easy/weakly multimodal UQBO (Fig 6.6-6.8) and says "it seems advisable to alternate global and local moves" (p.128). Still NO Gibbs/swap-neighbour move in the BVS experiments.
- Particle aggregation: not aggregated; augment-resample (Fearnhead-Clifford) variant tested, no gain ("the Rao-Blackwellization effect ... does not seem to justify the extra computational effort", p.42). Same as 2013 paper.
- Outputs for BVS: weighted particle mean sum_k w_k x_k (2.1, p.35); PIPs only (box-plots of 200 runs, p.88). NEW in Ch.1 (p.24, eq. 1.6): "normalized estimator" sum_{gamma in V} f(gamma) pi~(gamma)/sum_{V} pi~ over the set V of visited vectors (Clyde et al. 2011, Garcia-Donato & Martinez-Beneito 2011), "although biased, ... might be more efficient due to a Rao-Blackwellization effect" and "Bayesian adaptive sampling ... rationale to produce a unique collection V of most likely models ... stochastic search" (p.33, Hans et al. 2007). This is the list-renormalised idea, but it is a review of others' estimators; it is NOT used in any SMC experiment (Ch.4 uses raw weighted means) and there is no theory.
- Evidence: not computed; p.89: "SMC methods are more suitable than MCMC to approximate the evidence ... compare generalized regression models with different link functions" (qualitative). Pseudo-marginal consistency in the number of IS samples is stated by citation (Andrieu-Roberts 2009; Chopin-Jacob-Papaspiliopoulos SMC^2) (p.102), no proof.
- Best model / optimisation outputs: for BVS none. Ch.6: SMC-for-optimisation returns argmax over particles ever present (Alg. 12, p.116), stopping when diversity < delta or q_theta degenerates; p.115: "we either return the maximizer within the particle system or solve the subproblem of dimension d* by brute-force enumeration ... or perform some final local moves"; no certification, no optimality guarantee, no theory. Cross-entropy method = SMC with kernel replaced by its proposal (p.116); lag parameter tau for CE (p.117).
- Largest dimensions in experiments: BVS d <= 104 (Boston, n=506), Concrete 79/1030, Protein 88/96 (p.85-87), GLM d = 34 (n=690), 29 (n=198), 95 (n=476) (p.105-107); UQBO d = 250 (p.126). Scaling claim (informal, NOT shown): p.76 "examples with 1500 predictors" and p.134 "plant-genetics association studies ... with 2000 predictors on a 64-CPU cluster ... as reliable as for the test problem with about 100 predictors ... part of an on-going comparison study ... results ... premature and therefore not included". (Internal inconsistency: 1500 on p.76 vs 2000 on p.134.) p.134 also: in high dimension "reliability ... hardly improved by using more particles but mostly depends on the number of resample-move steps ... more efficient to use fewer particles but more intermediate steps" (informal heuristic, no proof).
- Competitors: Ch.4 same as 2013 (metropolised Gibbs k*=2, Nott-Kohn AMCMC) (p.88); Ch.5 Green's automatic generic sampler (reversible-jump; p.104-105); Ch.6 cross-entropy, simulated annealing, 1-opt local search (p.127). Not BAS, ESS, shotgun stochastic search (explicitly "beyond the scope", p.85).

## 4. What they prove
No theorem on SMC convergence, stability, complexity, or on statistical (selection) consistency anywhere in the thesis. The only SMC-theory statement (p.36): "Under mild conditions, Chopin (2004) shows that the estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal" -- a citation, applied to the fixed-schedule algorithm; the fully adaptive ESS schedule and refitted proposal are outside that setting and the thesis does not discuss this. Consistency of the pseudo-marginal / adaptive variants is likewise by citation (p.102). Statistical consistency of BVS: only the remark that mixtures-of-g priors give consistent posterior probabilities (p.79-80, cites Zellner-Siow, Liang et al.). Type (i) MC CLT/unbiasedness: none proved; (ii) complexity/mixing: only Ch.1/Ch.3 formulae for the independence sampler (below); (iii) selection consistency: none; (iv) output guarantees: none.

Everything proved in the thesis is Chapter 3 (and elementary Ch.1/6 facts), about binary distributions and proposal families. Exact list (statements faithful, my wording of the maths):
- Prop 3.2.1 (p.46): sharp (Frechet-Hoeffding) bounds max{sum_{i in I} m_i - |I| + 1, 0} <= m_I <= min{m_K : K subset I} for cross-moments m_I = E prod_{i in I} X_i. Proof: two lines.
- Def 3.2.2-3.2.3 (p.47): cross-moment matrix M (M - diag(M)diag(M)' positive definite and (3.1) for all pairs); generalised correlation c_I. Remark: C is a correlation matrix of a binary law with mean m iff M = C.ss' + mm' is a valid cross-moment matrix (p.47).
- Prop 3.2.2 (p.47): for d=2 uncorrelated iff independent; for d>=3 pairwise uncorrelated does not imply independent (explicit counterexample).
- Prop 3.2.3 (p.48): every f: B^d -> R admits a multi-linear representation f = tau[sum_I a_I prod_{i in I} gamma_i] (bijective tau).
- Prop 3.2.4 (p.48): Bahadur/Lazarsfeld expansion pi(gamma) = q^prod_m(gamma) [sum_{I} c_I^pi u_I^pi(gamma)] (u_I orthonormal basis in L^2(q^prod_m)).
- Prop 3.2.5 (p.49): for pi, omega with the same mean m and p >= 1, sum_gamma |pi - omega|^p <= sum_I 2^{(1-min{p,2})|I|} |c_I^pi - c_I^omega|^p <= (1+r)^d - dr - 1, r = 2^{1-min{p,2}} max_I |c_I^pi - c_I^omega|^{p/|I|}. Cor 3.2.6: TV distance <= (1/2) sum_I |c_I^pi - c_I^q|. Prop 3.2.7: with the same cross-moment matrix M, sum |pi - q|^p <= (1+r)^d - (1/2)d(d-1)r^2 - dr - 1 (gain from matching second-order structure).
- Prop 3.2.8 (p.49-50): for the independence MH kernel kappa with proposal q and target pi, both with mean m, the lag-1 autocovariance E_{kappa pi}(X_2 X_1') - mm' = (1/2)(M^pi - M^q) + R, |r_ij| <= sum_gamma |pi - q|. Interpretation (p.49): a product proposal matching only the mean leaves the autocovariance governed by the second cross-moments => low acceptance under strong correlations. (Ch.1 p.29 also states, without proof, that average acceptance >= 1 - 2||q - pi||_TV and lambda_2 = 1 - min q/pi, citing Liu 1996 / Diaconis-Hanlon.)
- Prop 3.3.1 (p.51): mu-conditionals family with A = diag[mu^{-1}(m)] equals the product family.
- THEOREM 3.3.2 (p.51; proof p.52-53 via Lemma 3.3.3 (positive-definite bordered matrix) and Lemma 3.3.4 (a differentiable-bijection/Jacobian argument), induction over d): Let mu: R-bar -> [0,1] be an increasing differentiable bijection (logistic, probit, arctan, complementary log-log all qualify) and M a d x d cross-moment matrix. Then there is a UNIQUE real lower-triangular matrix A with sum_gamma q^mu_A(gamma) gamma gamma' = M. I.e. the chain-rule "mu-conditionals" (logistic-conditionals) family can reproduce ANY feasible mean/correlation structure. (Existence/uniqueness in the abstract; p.71 admits "we cannot entirely turn this into practice" because of numerical accuracy.) Proof is by induction: add one dimension via the equation f(a) = M(d+1)_{.,d+1}, solved in a large ball by the Jacobian lower bound. Note the induction proves surjectivity onto the cross-moments; uniqueness follows from the (stated) injectivity of f.
- Prop 3.5.1 (p.59): maximum entropy law with prescribed cross-moments is exp(nu + sum a_I prod gamma_i). Prop 3.5.2-3.5.5 (p.59-60): exponential quadratic family (Ising-type): marginal, logistic conditional, constant conditional log-odds ratios omega_ij = a_ij. Prop 3.5.6 (p.61): the logistic conditionals family equals exp(gamma'A gamma - sum_i log[1 + exp(a_ii + sum_{j<i} a_ij gamma_j)]). Prop 3.5.7 (p.61-62): second-order log-cosh approximation giving approximate marginals of the exponential quadratic family. Def 3.5.3, Prop 3.5.8, Lemmas 3.5.9-3.5.10, Cor 3.5.11, Prop 3.5.12 (p.63-66): additive linear family q(gamma) = nu(a_0 + gamma'A gamma) with closed-form cross-moments, normalising constant and marginals (so sampling is exact); a_0 defined through an NP-hard minimisation (p.63).
- Def 6.1.1-6.1.2 (p.113): tempered and level-set families (no results).
So Chapter 3's mathematics is: representation / approximation / feasibility facts for binary families and the single existence-uniqueness theorem for logistic(-type) conditionals. None is a statement about SMC, about BVS posteriors, or about the algorithm's output.

Theory vs our Theorems 1-6 and planned P1-P5:
- Thm 1-4, 6 (posterior concentration, Hellinger, reported-model, certified screening, sharpness): no overlap. Thesis has no consistency statement, no reported-model guarantee.
- Prop 5 (unbiased evidence): no overlap, no contradiction.
- P1 (trap theorem for single-flip / weighted tempered Gibbs): thesis Prop 3.2.8 and Ch.1 p.29 give independence-sampler autocovariance and spectral formulae, and the empirical Figs 4.4-4.9 and 6.6-6.8 show single-flip MCMC and AMCMC trapped on multimodal BVS/UQBO; no theorem about slow mixing of single-flip chains. Citable as empirical motivation only. Weighted tempered Gibbs (Zanella-Roberts) did not exist yet.
- Ch.3 theorems overlap with nothing of ours; they could matter only if we ever use logistic-conditional independence proposals (Theorem 3.3.2 would justify expressiveness of the family; the autocovariance bound Prop 3.2.8 explains why product proposals fail). We do not use them.
- P2-P5: none.
- Tension (not logical conflict): p.82 "premature adjustment of p ... faster but less robust algorithms" and p.40 "locally operating Markov kernels ... less suitable for SMC ... mix rather slowly" can be quoted against our data-adapted block-product start and local move set; our regime (p >> n, exact block tables, islands, frozen grid) differs and no thesis result contradicts ours.

## 6. Verdict
- Novelty risk: THEORY: NONE (the only theorem about SMC-related objects is Thm 3.3.2 on binary proposal families; no SMC convergence/stability/complexity theorem, no selection-consistency result, no output guarantee; SMC CLT is cited from Chopin 2004). ALGORITHM: PARTIAL OVERLAP, LOW-TO-MODERATE, same as the 2013 paper (tempered adaptive resample-move SMC on model space with uniform/prior start, now also for GLM variable selection and for binary optimisation). New relative to 2013 but still below ours: GLM extension (pseudo-marginal, corrected Laplace) at d <= 95; optimisation chapter with SMC/cross-entropy and argmax-of-particles output; size-truncated model prior; a review mention of normalised (visited-set) estimators; informal, unreported 1500/2000-predictor scaling claim (p.76, 134).
- Must cite: Schafer (2012) thesis alongside Schafer-Chopin (2013): as the SMC-on-binary-spaces precedent including GLM and optimisation versions, for the Theorem 3.3.2 / logistic-conditional proposal toolbox, and for the p.24/33 normalised-estimator remark if we discuss list-renormalised PIPs; also mention the unreported 2000-predictor genetics run as prior informal evidence of p ~ 10^3.
- One-sentence differentiation: "Schafer (2012) develops uniform-start, fully adaptive tempered SMC with learned logistic-conditional independence moves for BVS up to a few hundred variables and for pseudo-Boolean optimisation, with no theory beyond a representability theorem for the proposal family; we start from an exact data-adapted block-product law, use Gibbs/swap moves on frozen schedules with independent islands, and prove posterior consistency, a reported-model guarantee and unbiased evidence estimates for p >> n up to 10^6."
