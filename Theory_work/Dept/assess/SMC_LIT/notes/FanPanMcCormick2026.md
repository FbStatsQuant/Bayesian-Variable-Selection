## 2. Model and prior
- NOT variable selection. The "models" are permissible PARTITIONS Pi of the K cells of a factorial design (pooling cells with identical means), M=2 or 3 ordered/categorical features; the model space is the set P* of permissible partitions (p. 7, App. A3 p. 19-22). The authors only remark (p. 3) that for variable selection d could be the Hamming distance between inclusion vectors, and (p. 12) that trees and DAGs could be accommodated; no variable-selection experiment.
- Likelihood (eq. 6, p. 7): Gaussian linear model y | gamma, Pi, sigma^2 ~ N(X~_Pi gamma, sigma^2 I_n), pool means gamma; sigma^2 treated as known (estimated separately from the saturated model, p. 7). Since X~_Pi'X~_Pi = diag(n_1..n_H) (Prop. A1, p. 22), everything is conjugate.
- Coefficient prior: Zellner g-prior gamma | Pi, sigma^2 ~ N(0, g sigma^2 (X~'X~)^{-1}) (value of g not stated in the text I read). Model prior: p(Pi) proportional to exp{-lambda |Pi|} (an l0 penalty on the number of pools; flat given |Pi|).
- Closed form (App. A4.2, p. 23): p(D|Pi, sigma^2) proportional to (1+g)^{-|Pi|/2} exp{-g SSE_Pi / (2 sigma^2 (1+g))}, so p(Pi|D) is available in closed form (unnormalised) and the MAP partition solves min SSE_Pi/n + lambda~|Pi| (eq. 8, p. 8). Posterior of cell means given Pi is Gaussian (eq. 7), so the final posterior of beta is a Gaussian mixture weighted by the AIS weights.

## 3. Sampler design
- State: a single model (partition), identified with its cut-incidence matrix Sigma (bijection with P*). No parameters sampled (collapsed). Annealed IMPORTANCE sampling, J independent trajectories, NO resampling, NO particle interaction (Algorithm 1, p. 5).
- INITIAL DISTRIBUTION (the point of the paper; eq. (3), p. 4): given a seed set S subset M (here S = Rashomon Partition Set R_eps, enumerated beforehand by the algorithm of Venkateswaran et al. 2025), the unnormalised initial density is
  q_0(M;S) = alpha_1 * p~(M|D)/sum_{M' in S} p~(M'|D) * 1_S(M) + alpha_2^+ * 1_{L_1(S)}(M)/|L_1(S)| + (1 - alpha_1 - alpha_2^+),
  with alpha_1, alpha_2 > 0, alpha_1 + alpha_2 < 1, alpha_2^+ = alpha_2 1{L_1(S) nonempty}, L_1(S) = the unit-distance (one cut-swap) level set of S. Sampling recipe (p. 4): with probability alpha_1 draw from S proportional to p~, with probability alpha_2 draw uniformly from L_1(S), otherwise uniformly from M. So it is a data-driven MIXTURE of (i) the posterior restricted to an externally enumerated seed set, (ii) uniform on its one-step neighbourhood, (iii) uniform on the whole space (guaranteeing full support). The values of alpha_1, alpha_2 are NOT reported anywhere in the text I read (I grepped).
  - Note on a notational inconsistency I found (p. 4): the last term of eq. (3) is the constant (1 - alpha_1 - alpha_2^+), but the sampling recipe draws M uniformly with probability (1 - alpha_1 - alpha_2^+), i.e. the third component has density (1 - alpha_1 - alpha_2^+)/|M|. If f_0 = q_0 is used in the weights exactly as printed, then the initial draws are not distributed as f_0/C_0 and the unit initial weight w=1 would be wrong. It is probably a typo (the 1/|M| factor missing; they also call the uniform "unnormalised"), but I cannot confirm because the code was not accessible.
- PATH (eq. 4, p. 4): geometric bridge f_t(M) = q_0(M;S)^{1-b_t} p~(M|D)^{b_t}, 0 = b_0 < ... < b_T = 1. Same functional form as our bridge pi_xi proportional to gamma_0 e^{xi Delta} = gamma_0^{1-xi} pi^{xi}; here gamma_0 = q_0 (non-prior start), exactly like our gamma_0 = pi_0.
- SCHEDULE: recommended log-equispaced ladder, or an ADAPTIVE PILOT run (App. A2.2, p. 18-19): K pilot models, halve Delta b until ESS(Delta b)/K >= tau (0.7-0.9), a few MH moves, repeat; "After the ladder is fixed, the full AIS run with J independent chains is performed using this schedule" - i.e. adaptive pilot, then FROZEN ladder, same design as ours. No values of T, L or the ladder size are given in the main text for the simulations.
- Kernel (p. 4): symmetric MH proposal phi supported on the unit neighbourhood N_1 of the current model (single entry flip of Sigma, "cut-swap"); L MH steps per temperature (Algorithm 1 line 6; L not reported). No Gibbs, no multi-site moves, no informed or independence moves.
- Weights: w_j <- w_j f_t(M^{(t-1)})/f_{t-1}(M^{(t-1)}) (Algorithm 1 line 8, Neal's AIS ordering). Output weighted sample (M_j, w_j), J independent. No resampling.
- Outputs: self-normalised weights; posterior summaries of the cell means (Gaussian mixture mean, quantiles by Brent root-finding of the mixture cdf, App. A2.1), credible intervals, "confidence measure" over effect-size intervals (Sec. 5). No PIPs (not variable selection), NO evidence/normalising-constant estimate (they note the constant C = C_T/C_0 cancels in self-normalised estimators, p. 5, p. 18), no reported model, no local search; the RPS itself is a deterministic enumeration of near-optimal models (done before and separately).
- Experiments: Scenario 1: M=2 features with R=(4,3) levels, K=12 cells, exact posterior by enumeration; Scenario 2: M=3, R=(4,3,3), K=36 cells, reference = a 10^6-iteration MCMC (single-flip MH over Sigma); 50 replications, RPS mean sizes 41.9-150 (Scenario 2, Fig. 2, p. 9) and 13.7-27.6 (Scenario 1, Fig. A1). Competitors: RPS-truncated inference, a PAC-Bayes estimator (greedy search for a localised set), and the MCMC. Real data: NHANES telomere (n not stated in what I read), RPS of 2,435 models, AIS with 300 trajectories; charitable donation (Karlan-List), RPS 123 models, 300 trajectories (p. 11-12, Table 1). Largest model space: 36-cell factorial partitions (the cardinality of P* is not stated). Nothing near p >> n or 10^4+ predictors.
- Reported performance (p. 9-12, Table 1): AIS L1 error to the MCMC reference about 1.4 vs RPS 15 and PB 11 in Scenario 2 (Fig. 2); IoU of credible intervals about 1.0; runtime about 140 s (Fig. 3B) vs about 510 s MCMC. Inconsistencies in the paper: text says AIS "approximately 180 seconds" and PB "over 800 seconds" (p. 10) while Fig. 3B shows about 141 s and about 620 s; in Table 1, the NHANES AIS row has L1 error 0.000 with ESS ratio 0.129 (about 39 effective of 300), which looks implausibly exact, and the "MCMC" reference has n_states 3000 and is itself an approximation. AIS runtime in Table 1 (1,199 s, 2,915 s) is not smaller than half of the MCMC time, so the "70% reduction" claim holds only for Scenario 2.

## 4. What they prove
All proofs are in App. A1 (p. 16-18). Faithful statements (p. 4-5):
- Setup: Q = law of one trajectory's (model, weight) pair; trajectories i.i.d., 𝐐 = product measure; C = C_T/C_0 with C_T = sum_M p~(M|D), C_0 = sum_M q_0(M;S).
- Unbiasedness of weights (standard AIS, cited to Neal 2001 and Gelman-Meng 1998, p. 4, A1.1 p. 16): for bounded measurable zeta: M -> R^p, E_Q[w zeta(M)] = C E_{M|D}[zeta(M)], in particular E_Q[w] = C. NOT proved in the paper, only cited as "a standard property".
- Theorem 1 (consistency of AIS estimators, p. 5): Assume E_Q[w] < infinity and E_Q[w ||zeta(M)||] < infinity. Then psi_hat_J = sum_j w_j zeta(M_j)/sum_j w_j -> psi = E_{M|D}[zeta(M)] almost surely under 𝐐 as J -> infinity. Proof (p. 16): Kolmogorov SLLN on A_J = (1/J) sum w_j zeta(M_j) and B_J = (1/J) sum w_j, continuous mapping, C > 0.
- Corollary 1: for scalar parameter theta, hat P_J(theta) = sum w_j P(theta|D,M_j)/sum w_j -> P(theta|D) a.s. Corollary 2: sup_theta |hat P_J - P(theta|D)| -> 0 a.s.; and if P(theta|D) is continuous and strictly increasing near its alpha-quantile q_alpha, then the empirical quantile hat q_{alpha,J} -> q_alpha a.s. (proofs p. 17: total variation bound plus finiteness of M).
- Lemma A1 (quantile bracketing, p. 18): min_j q_{alpha,j} <= q_alpha <= max_j q_{alpha,j} for the quantile of a mixture of component cdfs (an elementary numerical-root-finding tool).
- Lemma A2, Props A1-A2 (p. 22): algebraic identities for the design matrix of pools; App. A4: closed-form posterior and marginal posterior of partitions; eq. (8) MAP characterisation (these are model derivations, not sampler theory).
- Regime: finite model space M, fixed design (data fixed), FIXED temperature ladder, fixed number L of MH steps; limit J -> infinity of the number of independent AIS trajectories. Type: (i) Monte Carlo consistency of a self-normalised importance-sampling estimator for a fixed data set. Nothing of types (ii), (iii), (iv).
- What is NOT proved: no CLT, no variance or ESS bound, no dependence on the seed quality (the claim that a seed near the target "requires fewer temperatures T and updates L" is informal, p. 5), no statement that the AIS correction removes the bias of the truncation beyond the SLLN, no mixing or complexity result, no posterior or selection consistency in n, no guarantee on a reported model (they report none). The proofs "do not rely on any special property of the seed set S" (p. 5), which is honest: the theorem is purely the generic AIS SLLN for any full-support initial law. On a finite space with q_0 > 0 the moment assumptions E[w] < infinity, E[w||zeta||] < infinity hold automatically (weights are bounded products of ratios of finite positive numbers; my observation, they do not state it). They also state (Remark A1, p. 19) that the guarantees "hold for any temperature ladder that preserves the unbiasedness of the weights", but do not treat the dependence of the pilot-run ladder on data (the final run's independence from the pilot is not stated explicitly, though it is implied by the description).

## 5. Comparison with ours (METHOD_SUMMARY.md)
| Feature | Fan-Pan-McCormick | Ours | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) SMC over models | Annealed IMPORTANCE sampling (no resampling) over a discrete model space (partitions), J independent trajectories | SMC with resampling over subsets | SIMILAR (annealed population over discrete models; AIS = SMC with N=1, no resampling) |
| (b) path/bridge | Geometric bridge from a non-prior start law q_0 to the posterior: f_t = q_0^{1-b} p~^b | Geometric bridge from pi_0 to posterior via Delta: gamma_0 e^{xi Delta} | SAME form (non-prior start, geometric path) |
| (c) adaptive vs frozen | Adaptive ESS pilot, then FROZEN ladder for the final J chains (App. A2.2) | Adaptive pilot, frozen grid | SAME design |
| (d) move set | Single-entry-flip symmetric MH on unit neighbourhood | Gibbs-all-j, uniform swap, neighbour swap, independence from pi_0 | DIFFERENT (only a local single-flip) |
| (e) independent islands | J independent AIS trajectories, no communication | 4 / 16 islands with N=2,500 particles each | SIMILAR (non-communicating) but each unit is a single trajectory |
| (f) PIP estimator | None; self-normalised weights for cell-mean functionals; no RB, no list | RB PIPs + list-renormalised PIPs | DIFFERENT |
| (g) evidence estimate | Weights unbiased for C = C_T/C_0 but never used or reported as evidence | Unbiased island evidence, mean of islands | SIMILAR property (cited to Neal), not reported by them |
| (h) block-structured start law | Data-driven mixture: posterior on externally enumerated Rashomon seed + one-step neighbours + uniform; needs the seed set and a membership/distance test; not a product law | Exactly samplable block-product law of exact local marginal-likelihood tables, no external seed | DIFFERENT construction, SAME purpose (a data-informed start with full support) |
| (i) reported model + certification | No reported model (the RPS is a separate deterministic enumeration) | Best-scoring model, local-search certificate, list T | DIFFERENT |
- Start-distribution comparison in detail: Both are non-prior, data-driven, full-support, exactly samplable initial laws plugged into a geometric tempering bridge so that the bridge starts near the posterior (they: "an initial distribution near the target requires fewer temperatures and updates", p. 5; ours: the discrepancy Delta only carries the cross-block coupling). Their law is built from a global enumeration of high-posterior models (Rashomon set, which requires an exhaustive-type search that is feasible only in small structured spaces: 12-36 cells, seed sets of 14-2,435 models) plus a uniform backstop with arbitrary weights alpha_1, alpha_2 (not reported); its mass outside the seed is essentially uniform on a huge space so the bridge must do almost all the work off the seed. Ours is a product over correlation blocks of EXACT block marginal-likelihood tables, computed by local enumeration (blocks of at most 16 predictors), so pi_0 captures all within-block structure including multimodality, scales to p = 10^6, needs no seed-set enumeration, and the SMC weights depend only on the discrepancy. Neither the conceptual idea nor the formula (annealing from any full-support initial law) is new in either paper (Neal 2001; Del Moral, Doucet, Jasra 2006 allow arbitrary pi_0).
- Theory vs our Theorems 1-6 and P1-P5:
  - Their Theorem 1 / Cor. 1-2 (J -> infinity SLLN consistency of the self-normalised AIS estimator, fixed ladder, fixed data, finite model space): type (i). It is the generic AIS argument. It overlaps (loosely) with what is implicit behind our Prop. 5 only in that both rely on the standard unbiasedness of AIS/SMC weights (Neal 2001, Del Moral 2004), but our Prop. 5 is the statement for resampling SMC islands conditional on the frozen calibration and for every data set; theirs is not stronger and not a consistency-in-n result. We should cite Neal/Del Moral for the unbiasedness mechanism and, if useful, Fan et al. as another instance.
  - Our Theorems 1, 2, 4, 6 (posterior concentration, Hellinger contraction, certified screening, sharpness; type iii) and Thm 3 (output equals S0; type iv), and planned P1-P5: NO overlap, nothing implied, nothing contradicted. They have no statistical asymptotics (n or p growing), no rates, no mixing/trap results, no output guarantees.

## 6. Verdict
- Novelty risk to us: PARTIAL OVERLAP on the algorithmic IDEA (data-driven non-prior start law + geometric bridge + adaptive-pilot-then-frozen ladder + independent annealing runs on a discrete model space for BMA); NONE on theory (their only result is the generic SLLN for AIS; no PIPs, no evidence, no reported-model or consistency results). Different model space (partitions of factorial cells, 12-36 cells, not variable selection; no p >> n).
- We must cite it: Fan, Pan and McCormick (2026) as the closest prior use of a non-prior, data-driven start law in annealed importance sampling for model averaging over a discrete space, together with Neal (2001) for the unbiased weights and Del Moral-Doucet-Jasra (2006) for arbitrary pi_0.
- Differentiation sentence: "Fan et al. (2026) seed annealed importance sampling with a mixture of the posterior restricted to an exhaustively enumerated Rashomon set, its one-step neighbours and a uniform backstop, and prove only the generic almost-sure consistency of self-normalised AIS estimators as the number of independent trajectories grows; we instead start from an exactly samplable block-product law built from exact local marginal-likelihood tables (no external seed enumeration, p up to 10^6), run resampling SMC with Gibbs, swap and independence moves on a frozen calibrated grid, and prove posterior consistency, unbiased evidence and guarantees on the reported model."
- Caveats: code not inspected (anonymous repository page unreadable), alpha_1, alpha_2, T, L, J (in simulations) not stated in the PDF text; eq. (3) normalisation looks inconsistent with the sampling recipe (see Sec. 3).
