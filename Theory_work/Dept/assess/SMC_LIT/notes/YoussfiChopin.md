## 2. Model and prior
- Likelihood (p. 2-3, eq. 1-2): binary regression, P(Y_i = 1 | beta, theta) = F( sum_j eta_j beta^x_j x_ij + sum_k gamma_k beta^u_k u_ik + sum_l beta^z_l z_il ), F a link (they take F = Phi, probit, in experiments p. 7). Stated for "concreteness", "could easily be generalised to other GLMs" (p. 2). The full model is thus a probit GLM only; no other GLM is run.
- Data: n individuals; X in R^{n x p} (individual variables, subject to selection), U in R^{n x q} (group variables, subject to selection), Z in R^{n x r} (always included, e.g. intercept/demographics). Each of the p variables belongs to exactly one of q groups, g(j) = group of j (p. 3).
- Model space (p. 3): theta = (gamma, eta), gamma in {0,1}^q, eta in {0,1}^p, with hierarchical constraint eta_j = 0 whenever gamma_{g(j)} = 0 (strong heredity group -> variable, "bi-level"). No size cap on the model; no sparsity constraint beyond the prior.
- Model prior (p. 3, eq. 3): p(gamma) a product of Bernoulli(p_k^gamma); P(eta_j = 1 | gamma) = p_j^eta if gamma_{g(j)} = 1, else 0. The values/hyperparameters p^gamma_k, p^eta_j are NOT given anywhere in the paper (I grepped the text); no beta-binomial/multiplicity correction is mentioned.
- Coefficient prior (p. 3): independent Gaussians, p(beta^z) = N(0_r, sigma^2 I_r), p(beta^u) = N(0_q, sigma^2 I_q), p(beta^x) = N(0_p, sigma^2 I_p). Value of sigma^2 not stated (grep confirms nowhere specified). No g-prior; no variance prior (sigma^2 is a fixed constant, as far as the text says; probit has no noise variance anyway). Only the active coordinates of beta enter (beta_theta = components with theta_i = 1).
- Marginal likelihood L(theta) = integral of L(beta, theta) p(beta) d beta (p. 4): intractable for probit. Two approximations (p. 6):
  - Laplace (LA): log L(theta) ~ -h_theta(beta_hat) + (d_theta/2) log 2pi - (1/2) log |H_hat_theta|, with h_theta = -log{L(beta,theta)p(beta)}, beta_hat the MAP given theta (Newton-Raphson per model), d_theta = dim beta_theta. Cost O(n) in sample size and O(d_theta^3).
  - Approximate Laplace (ALA, Rossell, Abril, Bhattacharya 2021): one Taylor expansion about beta = 0: log L(theta) ~ -h_theta(0) + (1/2) g_theta' H_theta^{-1} g_theta + (d_theta/2) log 2pi - (1/2) log |H_theta|, with g, H the gradient and Hessian of h at 0; computed once for the FULL model and sub-indexed for each theta. Cost O(1) in n after precomputation, still cubic in d_theta (p. 6).
  - ALA is "not a consistent (in n) approximation" and "tends to be biased against truly active variables" (p. 7, attributing to Rossell et al.). The target of the ALA sampler is therefore an approximate posterior, not the exact one; no bound or theory on this (only the empirical comparison in 4.1 and a citation of Schafer 2012 thesis showing LA error is negligible in a different, one-level, setting).

## 3. Sampler design
- Particle: theta = (gamma, eta), a pair of binary vectors satisfying the bi-level constraint. N = 25,000 particles = M x P with M = 125 resampled ancestors, P = 200 (p. 7).
- Initial distribution (p. 4): the PRIOR p(theta) (lambda_0 = 0; Algorithm 1 lines 2-4, theta_0^n ~ p(theta)). Not data-driven; no block/correlation-based start.
- Targets (p. 4): geometric tempering bridge pi_t(theta) = p(theta) L(theta)^{lambda_t} / Z_t, 0 = lambda_0 < ... < lambda_T = 1 (L being LA, ALA, or exact integrated likelihood).
- Schedule (p. 4): "it is recommended to set lambda_t automatically, by choosing the next lambda_t so that the ESS equals a certain threshold"; no threshold value is stated, no search procedure described, no frozen second pass. So: adaptive ESS, no discussion of the evidence-estimate bias that adaptation induces.
- Waste-free structure (p. 4-5, Algorithm 1; Dau-Chopin 2022): at t >= 1, resample M ancestors from the N weighted particles (multinomial: "draw IID variables with P(A = n) = W^n_{t-1}"), run each through P-1 applications of kernel K_t (invariant for pi_{t-1}), keep ALL P states of each chain as the new N-sample, weight w_t^n = L(theta_t^n)^{lambda_t - lambda_{t-1}}, normalise. Note the weights are computed after the moves, as in waste-free SMC. Final estimate: E_pi phi ~ sum_n W_T^n phi(theta_T^n) (p. 4).
- MCMC kernel (Section 3.2, p. 4-6, Algorithms 2): ONE kernel only, an independent Metropolis-Hastings step with a global proposal fitted to the current weighted particle sample (adapted from Schafer-Chopin 2013). Proposal q(theta) = q(gamma, eta) = q_1(gamma_1) prod_{k=2}^q q_k(gamma_k | gamma_{1:k-1}) prod_{j=1}^p q_j(eta_j | gamma_{g(j)}) (eq. 5), where q_k(gamma_k = 1 | gamma_{1:k-1}) = logistic(b_kk + sum_{i<k} b_ki gamma_i) (eq. 4; nested logistic regressions, parameters b by maximum-likelihood fit to the current weighted particle sample), and q_j(eta_j = 1 | gamma_{g(j)}) = c_j if gamma_{g(j)} = 1 else 0, with c_j in [0,1] also by MLE on the sample (p. 5). Accept with probability min{1, pi_{t-1}(theta') q(theta) / (pi_{t-1}(theta) q(theta'))} (Algorithm 2). Respects the bi-level constraint by construction. They note (p. 6) one could correlate the eta_j in a group through a nested logistic regression, but "did not observe much benefit". Gibbs / one-flip Metropolis kernels are mentioned only as the standard alternative that "tend to mix poorly, and get stuck in local modes" (p. 4-5); no swaps, no neighbour moves, no mixture of kernels, no Rao-Blackwellisation.
- Parallelism (p. 4): the likelihood evaluation of the N particles can be parallelised (8 cores used). No independent islands/non-communicating populations; the 10 repeats of the whole sampler (p. 7, p. 11) are only used to measure variability.
- Outputs: marginal posterior inclusion probabilities of groups and variables as weighted particle averages (raw, sum W_T^n 1{theta_T^n includes j}), p. 4, p. 7-11. Whether a variable/group is "selected" in 4.2 is shown as a result of PIPs (Fig. 5 clusters at roughly 0.7-1.0 selected, below 0.3 not selected) but the selection rule/threshold is not stated. Evidence estimate Z_T / marginal likelihood of the model: Z_t is defined as the normalising constant (p. 4) but it is NEVER estimated, reported or discussed. No reported best model, no MAP model, no local search, no certification.
- Experiments (Section 4):
  - Simulated (p. 7-8): probit data from the model; g = 5 groups, r = 5 always-included covariates; rows of X, U, Z independent N(0, Sigma) with Sigma_ii = 1, Sigma_ij = 0.5 (equicorrelated); beta^z = (0,0,1,1,1), beta^u = (0,0,1,1,1), beta^x all zero except the last variable of each active group set to 1. Scenario 1: p = 50, n from 100 to 2,500. Scenario 2: n = 1,500, p from 10 to 250 (the Fig. 2 and 3 captions say "10 to 25" but the axes and the text give p up to 250; typo in the preprint). 10 runs for confidence bands. Only 5 groups, so ~50 variables per group at p = 250.
  - Real (p. 9-12): French SNDS case-control data on sudden cardiac death: n = 47,916 (23,958 cases + 23,958 matched controls), q = 36 groups, p = 337 binary variables (group sizes 2 to 27), r = 0. ALA-based sampler selected 16 of 36 groups and 55 of 337 variables; three clusters (4 groups selected with no variable, 20 groups not selected, 12 groups selected with >= 1 variable). 10 runs: mean 61.8 hours (7 days total CPU) for ALA; the LA-based version had not finished after 30 days. Stability reported as the density of IQR of PIPs across 10 runs (Fig. 6, IQR mostly below about 0.1 for many variables, a tail up to 1; no numeric summary).
  - Largest p: 337 binary variables (q = 36 groups) in the real data; p = 250 in simulation. Largest n: 47,916. Nothing near p >> n (all n > p), nothing near 10^4-10^6.
  - Competitors: none. The only comparison is LA vs ALA inside their own sampler (p. 7-8). No MCMC competitor, no Gibbs sampler, no other SMC, no BIVAS or group-bridge benchmark, in spite of the claim of superiority over MCMC (which is only by citation of Schafer-Chopin 2013, p. 2).
  - Timing claims vs abstract: abstract says "within a few minutes"; sim runs take 16 min (n = 2,500, p = 50) to 39-42 min (p = 250, n = 1,500) for ALA (Fig. 3, p. 8); real data 61.8 h. The text says LA at p = 250 needs "330 min", while Fig. 3 right curve ends near 235 min (read off the figure); inconsistency in v1.

## 4. What they prove
- NO THEORY. The paper has no theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary or proof (grep for theorem/proposition/consisten*/converge found none; the intro says section 4 "assesses (statistically and numerically)", p. 2, but the assessment is only numerical). The words about theory are all inherited by citation:
  - "We refer to Dau and Chopin (2022) for a more thorough discussion of the advantages of SMC samplers over MCMC, and the extra advantage brought by waste-free SMC (relative to standard SMC), in particular the greater robustness relative to the choice of tuning parameters such as P and M" (p. 4). No statement that a CLT, consistency or variance bound from Dau-Chopin applies here; and indeed the setting here (adaptive lambda_t via ESS; kernel parameters b, c re-estimated from the current particle sample) is outside the fixed-kernel/fixed-schedule conditions of the standard waste-free SMC results, so no consistency is even implied. (Statement about what Dau-Chopin proves: I have not read that paper in this task; my claim is only that Youssfi-Chopin invoke no result from it.)
  - Informal claims: ALA "is not a consistent (in n) approximation" (p. 7, from Rossell et al.); Schafer (2012) found LA error negligible (p. 6); waste-free SMC "tends to outperform" standard SMC (p. 2).
- Type of results by category: (i) Monte Carlo convergence of sampler: none stated; (ii) complexity/mixing: none (only empirical run-times and the remark that ALA is O(1) in n and O(d^3) in model dimension, p. 6); (iii) posterior/selection consistency: none; (iv) guarantees on the output: none.

## 6. Verdict
Novelty risk to us: NONE on the theory side; PARTIAL (low) on the method side. The paper is a short applied note (13 pages) that combines three existing ingredients (Schafer-Chopin 2013 tempering SMC with a learned logistic independence proposal, Dau-Chopin 2022 waste-free SMC, Rossell et al. 2021 ALA) for probit bi-level selection with p <= 337; it has no theorem, no evidence estimate, no islands, no Rao-Blackwellisation, no best-model/local-search output, no p >> n, and no competitor comparison. The overlap with our method is the generic "tempered SMC over inclusion vectors with an independent logistic-type proposal", which we already attribute to Schafer-Chopin 2013.
What to cite: Youssfi and Chopin (2023/FoDS 2024) as the waste-free tempered SMC for bi-level / GLM selection, alongside Schafer-Chopin 2013 and Dau-Chopin 2022. One-sentence differentiation for our paper: "Youssfi and Chopin (2024) run a prior-started, ESS-adaptive waste-free tempered SMC with a single learned independence proposal for probit bi-level selection with p <= 337 and report no theory, whereas we start from a block-exact data-driven law, freeze the grid, use Gibbs/swap/independence moves on independent islands, and prove posterior consistency, correctness of the reported model, and unbiased evidence for p up to 10^6."
Caveat: the FoDS journal full text was not read (only landing-page metadata); if the published version adds a theorem it would have been noted on the page abstract, which it was not, but this is unverified.
