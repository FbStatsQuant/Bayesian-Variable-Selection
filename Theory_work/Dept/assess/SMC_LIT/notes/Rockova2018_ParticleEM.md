## 2. Model and prior
Gaussian linear regression, Y centred (intercept dropped), standardised X. Model space: all gamma in {0,1}^p (no size cap). Continuous spike-and-slab (Gaussian mixture, George-McCulloch SSVS-type) prior beta_i | gamma_i ~ gamma_i N(0,v1) + (1-gamma_i) N(0,v0), v0 < v1 (v1=100; v0 = 0.1, 0.08, grids down to 0.001). NOT the spike-and-slab LASSO (it is mentioned only as a possible extension, p13-14; the LASSO prior needs an approximate E-step). sigma^2 ~ IG(eta/2, eta nu/2) used non-conjugately (enters only likelihood); gamma_i | theta iid Bern(theta), theta ~ Beta(a,b), with a=1,b=p in experiments. Marginal posterior pi(gamma, sigma^2|Y) closed form up to a constant (26) (George-McCulloch 1997). Note: this "model probability" is the posterior under the continuous mixture prior with beta integrated out, a different object than the point-mass g-prior posterior; closed form, no closed form for g-prior (they say point-mass requires approximate E-step, p13).

## 3. Sampler design
Particle EM (PEM, main method) is NOT a sampler: a deterministic ensemble optimiser. Particles = K binary vectors gamma_k (models; copies allowed), with weights w_k. Maximises F_lambda(Gamma,w,sigma^2) (6) = variational Bayes lower bound (lambda=1) with the point-mass mixture q_PEM (5), via a nested EM: E-step computes ridge regression moments for the K* unique particles; M-step does coordinate-wise one-site binary updates gamma_ik = 1{pi_ik>0.5} (23) where pi_ik includes the repulsion factor Rep (22) = ratio of entropies of the particle system with gamma_ik=0 vs 1 (the "mutually repulsive" term); weights update (27) proportional to pi(gamma|Y)/n_k (inverse of copy count), i.e. posterior-probability weights normalised over the particle set (the particle approximation is "a normalised restriction of pi to the particle set", p7). lambda=0 gives "parallel EM" (K independent EMVS-like hill climbs); lambda=1 default (balance fit/diversity); lambda>1 worse. No resampling in the deterministic algorithm; no tempering of the likelihood; initialisation arbitrary (zero matrix, or Bernoulli(0.1) random binary matrix, K=10..200; they initialised at zeros in p=200).
Dynamic PEM (3.3): sequence of spike variances v0 (the "temperature") decreasing, particles proliferated backward from large to small v0 with warm starts (deterministic annealing-like; cf. Ueda-Nakano, Yoshida-West). Not a likelihood tempering path; the target changes through the prior spike variance.
Stochastic variant (Sec 5, Table 2, "repulsive particle filter" / SMC): K particles, weights w_k=1/K at start v0^0 large (initial sample from pi_{v0^0}), reweight by ratio pi_{v0^m}(gamma_k|Y)/pi_{v0^{m-1}}(gamma_k|Y) (34) (a tempering-like sequence indexed by the spike variance), resample multinomially when Var(w) > epsilon (epsilon=1e-4), then rejuvenate with a repulsive MCMC kernel (30)-(32): Metropolis-Hastings with a one-site proposal tilted by exp{lambda H} (pinball-sampler of Robert-Mengersen 2003) and a correction step. Fixed schedule {0.01+k*0.01}, k=0..50 (no adaptivity/ESS but a weight-variance resampling trigger). Invariant distribution = pi_{v0}(gamma|Y) (Lemma 1 of Robert-Mengersen). This is the only SMC-like piece; presented as a secondary variant. Single population, no independent islands.
Outputs: PEM reconstruction pi_hat(gamma|Y)=sum_k w_hat_k 1{gamma=gamma_k} (5); MIP P_hat(gamma_i=1|Y)=sum_k w_hat_k gamma_hat_ki (35) = particle-set-renormalised inclusion probability (renormalised weights w_hat = normalised posterior model probabilities at the unique particle locations) (p11). No Rao-Blackwellisation; no evidence estimate (though F_lambda is an ELBO-type objective, not claimed as evidence estimate and not a lower bound when lambda != 1, p4); no reported/best model beyond "global mode found" counts; no certificate. Experiment sizes: Sec 6.1 p=12, n=50 (full enumeration of 4096 models), K=10..100, 100 repetitions; Sec 6.2 p=200, n=100 (20 blocks of 10 near-perfectly collinear, rho=0.99, q=4 true), K=50..200, benchmarks SSVS MCMC with T=100,000. Supplement: US crime p=15, K=500, timings p up to 200. LARGEST p = 200 (n=100). Competitors: EMVS, parallel EM (lambda=0), SSVS (George-McCulloch) MCMC; SMC variant (K=5000) compared to PEM visually. Performance metrics: number of unique modes, % of posterior mass captured (exactly in p=12; relative to an MCMC benchmark in p=200), times the global/most frequent MCMC model is found. Table 4 (p=200, v0=0.08, K=200, lambda=1): 171.9 unique models, 90.52% posterior mass, global mode found 98/100; lambda=0: 24.11 models, 54.64%, 62/100.

## 4. What they prove
NO theorems, propositions, lemmas or corollaries in the main paper or supplement (all 15 + 5 pages read; Sec 3-5 are derivations; Lemma 1 of Robert & Mengersen is only cited, p9). Supplement S2 (p2-3) gives only a heuristic, no formal statement:
- TV bound (unnumbered): ||pi(.|Y) - pi_T(.|Y)||_TV <= 2 sum_{gamma not in S} pi(gamma|Y), where pi_T is the renormalised restriction of the posterior to the discovered particle set S (assuming no replicates, K=|S|). (Elementary; type (iv)-ish but trivial and for a given dataset.)
- A "natural guide": choose smallest K with pi(gamma not in S|Y) < eps; "asymptotically ... smallest K with E_{beta0} pi(gamma in S|Y) -> 1 as n,p -> infinity"; cites Castillo-Schmidt-Hieber-van der Vaart (2015), Rockova-George (2016) (sparse-support / posterior concentration results for the posterior itself) and Narisetty-He (2014) for selection consistency. These are statements about the posterior, not about PEM output; no claim that PEM finds such an S. The abstract's "supported by asymptotic considerations, which indicate that the requisite number of particles need not be large in the presence of sparsity (p>n)" refers to this. 
Convergence of the EM iteration: stated only as practical stopping ("no binary switch in two consecutive iterations", Table 1); there is no convergence theorem, no monotone-ascent proof written out (the minorise-maximise construction is described, p6), no claim about reaching global/true modes. No consistency of PEM, no guarantee that a reported model is the truth, no Monte Carlo CLT for the stochastic variant.

## 6. Verdict
Novelty risk: NONE to PARTIAL OVERLAP at most (conceptual only: a particle set of models with particle-set-renormalised inclusion probabilities and an annealing-in-the-prior path; no theory, p<=200, optimiser not a sampler, no evidence/Rao-Blackwell/islands/start law). Not a direct overlap with any of Thm 1-6.
Must cite: as an ensemble/population mode-finding precursor for continuous spike-and-slab posteriors and for the practice of reporting a posterior reconstruction renormalised over a discovered model set (TV bound 2*sum_{gamma not in S} pi), alongside EMVS (Rockova-George 2014a) and Shi-Dunson/Schafer-Chopin for SMC.
Differentiation sentence: "Rockova (2018) builds a deterministic, repulsion-regularised ensemble of EM mode-finders (with an SMC-style variant over a prior-spike-variance path) for continuous spike-and-slab posteriors with p up to 200 and no consistency or output guarantee, whereas we run a tempered SMC over the exact g-prior model posterior and prove posterior and output (reported-model) consistency with certified local search."
