## 2. Model and prior
- Gaussian linear regression y = X beta + eps, eps iid normal (p.4), first column may be the intercept; p may exceed n (p.5), but the subset size p_s must be < n (p.6). Problem (2) (p.5): argmin_beta ||y - X beta||_2^2 s.t. ||beta||_0 <= p_s <= p, i.e. best subset of a FIXED size p_s; constraint "turned into an equality" (p.6). The objective is the residual sum of squares of the OLS fit on the subset, equivalently in-sample R^2 for fixed size (my inference: with intercept/centring; R^2 = 1 - RSS/TSS).
- NOT a Bayesian posterior. No model prior, no coefficient prior, no variance prior, no marginal likelihood. The author explicitly distances the method from spike-and-slab (Mitchell-Beauchamp, George-McCulloch, Polson-Sun) and from Schafer-Chopin adaptive Bernoulli sampling (pp.2-3): "does not make any additional distributional assumption and solves the combinatory problem in its original form". The "density" is a device: f(U; y,X) proportional to exp{-||y - X_U beta_hat(U)||_2^2} (eq (4), p.7), "up to a norming constant", used for optimisation. The slides say it outright: "The density-tempered SMC can serve as an optimization method without any statistical inference purpose" (slides p.10; Bayesian reading: slide p.9-10, setting p(theta)=1 gives maximum likelihood).
- The exponent uses the raw, unscaled RSS (no 1/(2 sigma^2), no 1/n). The final target at gamma = 1 is exp(-RSS), so its peakedness depends on the units of y (my inference; data scaling is not discussed). Models are fixed-size; the size p_s is then chosen outside the sampler (Section 3.4).

## 3. Sampler design
- Particles: permutations U = (U_1,...,U_{p_s}) of p_s distinct regressor indices (ordered tuples without replacement), set P(p_s) (p.6). Reason given (p.3, pp.6-7): permutations are easier to sample and evaluate; the permutation law is the combination law scaled by p_s!, "the SMC sample representing the distribution function over combinations is exactly the same as the one over permutations after collapsing". Different permutations of the same set share the same objective value (p.8).
- Start law I(U; q_bar): each regressor i gets probability q_bar_i = R_i^2 / sum_j R_j^2 where R_i^2 is the single-regressor R^2 (p.7); a permutation is drawn sequentially without replacement, the next element chosen with probability q_j/(1 - q_i) etc. (p.7); q_bar_i = 0 trims regressor i from P. Data-driven (marginal-R^2 weighted product-of-categoricals), not the prior; arbitrary otherwise, its density is absorbed into the weight (p.3, p.7).
- Path: geometric bridge between start law and target (eq (5), p.8): f_gamma(U) proportional to (exp{-RSS(U)} / I(U; q_bar))^gamma * I(U; q_bar), gamma from 0 to 1; "density-tempered" after Del Moral-Doucet-Jasra (2006) and Duan-Fulop (2015). The author notes density-tempering vs data-expanding (slides p.5).
- Schedule: adaptive, ESS-based at every step: choose gamma* so that ESS of the incremental weights w = (exp{-RSS}/I)^{gamma - gamma_j} is no less than eta*M, eta = 1/2 (p.8); "need not be exactly at eta M". No frozen grid, no pilot run.
- Resampling: "use the incremental importance weight to resample to obtain an equally-weighted sample" (p.8); scheme (multinomial/systematic) not stated.
- Moves ("support boosting", pp.8-10): the MH kernel's only purpose is to restore diversity after resampling (also slides p.14). Proposal: pick a random subset A~ of positions of the permutation, remove them, and re-draw the removed elements from the remaining pool (standard sequential permutation draw) with probabilities from a mixture h^(omega) = omega * h(.; Q_U(gamma)) + (1-omega) * I(.; q_bar_i), omega = 50% (p.9). Q_U(gamma) = counts-based inclusion frequencies q_i(gamma) = c_i / sum_j c_j computed from the CURRENT particle population (p.9), so the proposal is population-adaptive, independence-type on the replaced block; the mixture with q_bar protects against a population that has lost the optimal variables (p.9). Acceptance (6) (p.10): min{1, exp(-gamma(RSS* - RSS)) (I*/I)^{1-gamma} h(U|...)/h(U*|...)}. Moves are repeated "until the cumulative realised acceptance rate has reached a target level, say, 100%" (p.9) and later "the cumulative average acceptance rate over rounds has reached the target level, and for which we set it at 500%" (p.10); the slides say 500% (slides p.13). The distribution of the size |A~| is not specified (p.9: "a random subject of elements"). No Gibbs inclusion update, no uniform or neighbour swap, no explicit add/delete (size is fixed), no hill-climb. The author contrasts this with the single best replacement algorithm of Soussen-Idier-Duan-Brie (2015) (p.9).
- k-fold duplication (Duan-Zhang 2016) (pp.10-11): after gamma = 1 with M particles, copy the sample k times to get kM and run MH support boosting to diversify; skips the tempering for the extra (k-1)M points. Used once: 1,000 -> 2,000 particles (p.11).
- Adding/trimming regressors (p.11, eqs (7)-(8)): warm-start for a new size p_s' from the solution U_hat_{p_s}: mixture initialiser omega' * T(.; U_hat) + (1-omega') * I, omega' = 0.1; T removes p_s - p_s' variables at random or adds variables from the remainder by q_bar; acceptance (8) adjusted.
- Choosing p_s (p.12): m-fold CV (out-of-sample SSE vs p_s, cubic spline smoothing with knots at 2, 9, 20), or BIC (9)/AIC (10) computed at U_hat_{p_s}; "BIC or AIC tends to select more regressors vs cross-validation".
- Parallel/independent runs or islands: none in the method. (The 500 + 100 independent re-runs in Section 4 are an experimental device.) Parallelism is only mentioned via "multi-core desktop" (p.1).
- Outputs: the reported subset is the final-sample point with the highest objective (p.10); however R2bar(p_s), "the best result recorded throughout the whole sequential updating process", can exceed the final-sample maximum (p.13). Reported: the best subset and its OLS fit; NO frequencies, NO PIPs, NO evidence/marginal likelihood estimate. The inclusion frequencies c_i(gamma) are used only inside the proposal.
- Size of experiments: largest p = 900 in all experiments (pp.3, 14-16) ("1,000" only in the abstract/intro claim, p.1, p.2); largest subset size p_s = 18 in Table 1, 20 in Tables 3-5 (and 1-20 swept, pp.16-17); n = 200 or 1,000 (p.15); M = 1,000, doubled to 2,000 (p.11; p.4: 2,000 particles, 12 of 900, about one minute on a desktop; 10,000 particles possible in a couple of hours, p.4). Search-space sizes quoted: 1.026e21 (9 of 900) and 1.986e37 (18 of 900) (p.3).
- Competitors: adaptive Lasso of Zou (2006) only (Table 2, p.21), plus the benchmark "estimated true model" (OLS refit on the true support at the same size) in-sample R^2 (Table 1). No comparison with stepwise, Soussen et al. homotopy, mixed-integer optimisation, spike-and-slab MCMC, or Schafer-Chopin.

## 4. What they prove
NO THEORY: no numbered or unnumbered theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary or proof anywhere in the 24 pages or the 30 slides. The only statements with mathematical content are informal, listed here faithfully with their type.

(I1) Convergence to the global optimum as M grows (informal; p.3 and p.4; p.10).
- p.3: "The zero-SMC algorithm can in theory find the right solution with a probability of one when one increases the number of particle to infinity. Other than a theoretical interest, that would be no better than the brute force approach of exhausting all possible combinations."
- p.4: "Theoretically, this stochastic solution can be made arbitrarily close to the true solution through the argument of the maximum order statistic."
- p.10: "if M is made arbitrarily large, the SMC solution will be arbitrarily close to the true solution in probability, or may even be the true solution simply because the underlying distribution is discrete with a large but finite number of combinations."
- Formalised as the author's evident intent (my formalisation, NOT stated in the paper): with $\hat U_M$ the reported subset from $M$ particles, $\mathbb P(\mathrm{RSS}(\hat U_M)=\min_{U\in\mathbf P(p_s)}\mathrm{RSS}(U))\to 1$ as $M\to\infty$. Type: SMC/annealing convergence to the global optimum as the sample size grows (not as temperature grows; the final gamma is 1, the temperature is not sent to infinity). Assumptions: none given. No proof or reference to an SMC convergence theorem (Del Moral 2004 is not cited). The argument offered is a counting one: a large enough population covers the finite support (which is trivial, brute force) (p.3).

(I2) Invariance of the MH support-boosting kernel (informal; p.10): "The acceptance probability defines a Markov kernel, and the target intermediate distribution in (5) is, by the standard argument, the stationary solution to the Markov kernel." Type: Monte Carlo correctness of a move. Caveat (my inference): the proposal depends on the whole current population through $Q_U(\gamma)$, so the kernel is not a fixed MH kernel for a single particle; invariance of the population law is argued only by "standard argument" and is not proved. Slides p.13-14 make the same claim ("proposal is independent because $\mathcal M_\theta$ reflects the whole sample").

(I3) Extreme-value (Weibull) approximation to the maximal R^2 (pp.13-14, eqs (11)-(12)); the only displayed "result", presented as a heuristic.
- Setting: $\bar R^2(p_s)$ = $R^2$ of the reported subset; $R^2(p_s;M)=\max\{R_i^2(p_s):i=1,\dots,M\}$ over the final sample; $R^2_{\max}(p_s)$ = the true maximum over all $p_s$-subsets, so that $R^2(p_s;M)\le \bar R^2(p_s)\le R^2_{\max}(p_s)$ (p.13). $R^2(p_s)$ is "a random variable resulting from random $p_s$-variable combinations" with unknown discrete distribution function $G$ (p.13; the sampling measure that defines $G$ is not specified, my inference).
- Statement (p.13, "one can easily show that $G(x)$ can be approximated by $(R^2_{\max}-x)^{\alpha}L(1/(R^2_{\max}-x))$", $L$ slowly varying; the text says $L$ is "converging to zero at infinity", which is not the usual definition of slow variation, my remark):
$$\mathrm{Prob}\Big\{\frac{R^2(p_s,M')-R^2_{\max}(p_s)}{R^2_{\max}(p_s)-G^{\leftarrow}(1-1/M')}\le x\Big\}\cong \exp(-|x|^{\alpha}),\quad x\le 0,\tag{11}$$
$$F_{R^2(p_s,M')}(z)=\exp\Big[-\Big(\frac{R^2_{\max}(p_s)-z}{\eta}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big],\quad z\le R^2_{\max}(p_s),\ \ \eta=R^2_{\max}(p_s)-G^{\leftarrow}(1-1/M'),\tag{12}$$
with $G^{\leftarrow}(x)=\inf\{y:G(y)>x\}$ and "for large $M'<M$". Parameters $(R^2_{\max},\alpha,\eta)$ (or $(R^2_{\max},\alpha)$ with $G^\leftarrow(1-1/M')$ read from the empirical distribution of the final sample) are estimated by randomly splitting the $M$=2,000 final particles into 20 subsamples of $M'$=100, taking the 20 subsample maxima, and minimising the $l_2$ distance to their empirical CDF (pp.13-14); degenerate cases (1 distinct value: treated as already optimal; 2 values: two-parameter fit) in footnote 4 (p.14). Outputs used: the estimated $R^2_{\max}$ (room for improvement) and the probability of exceeding $\bar R^2$ (p.13).
- Type: extreme-value statement about the maximal R^2 (statistical heuristic about the optimisation gap), not selection consistency and not a guarantee on the output. Assumptions: those of the Fisher-Tippett-Gnedenko theorem for iid maxima from a distribution in the Weibull domain of attraction; none is verified. Gaps (my inference): (a) the final SMC particles are resampled, dependent, with duplicates, and drawn from a tempered law, not iid from $G$; (b) $G$ is a discrete law on a finite support with an atom at its right endpoint (the global optimum), for which the maximum equals the endpoint with probability tending to 1 and there is no non-degenerate limit (the regular-variation condition at the endpoint fails); the author acknowledges discreteness but waives it ("approximated", p.13); (c) estimating $R^2_{\max}$ by fitting an upper endpoint to 20 maxima of near-ties is statistically fragile (Table 5 shows a minimum error of -0.047, i.e. an over-prediction of 4.7 R^2 points at $p_s$=20, while the text on p.17 says "the maximum gap [is] just over 0.5%", which refers to the Max column only).
- Empirical support (pp.16-17, Tables 3-5): one fixed n=200 data set (12-variable truth, p=900, R^2 = 80%); 500 randomised SMC runs per $p_s$=1..20; grand maximum over 600 runs used as proxy for the true maximum; average predicted exceedance probability at most 0.039 (Table 3); agreement rate (predicted exceedance probability strictly positive and grand max strictly above the run's $\bar R^2$) between 0.882 and 1 (Table 3); mean improvement potential at $p_s$=20 is 0.357% (Table 4, Max 0.5462%); prediction error of $R^2_{\max}$ mean at most 0.0026 in absolute value, Max up to 0.0051, Min down to -0.047 (Table 5).

(I4) Selection consistency / rates: nothing. The paper says the l_0 problem "can be solved", never states that the solution recovers the true support under any design or signal condition; simulations only. Table 1 uses the metric "R^2 of the selected model strictly greater than that of the estimated true model" (an optimisation-success proxy), which is 100% at n=200 and 95.8%/94.6% at n=1,000 (p=900, 9/18 variables, R^2=80%); in the n=1,000 rows "strictly greater" and "greater or equal" coincide, so in 4.2%/5.4% of repetitions the SMC output has a LOWER in-sample R^2 than the true support, i.e. the global search failed (my reading of Table 1, p.20).

(I5) Output guarantee: none (no certified or verifiable statement about the reported subset; the EVT probability is the only reliability device and is heuristic).

Theory vs ours:
- Thm 1/2 (posterior concentration, Hellinger contraction): no overlap; Duan has no statistical consistency result at all.
- Thm 3 (every run reports the true model on high-probability data events; delivery): no overlap and no contradiction. Duan's only claim of this flavour is I1 (global optimum as M -> infinity for one fixed data set), which is Monte Carlo convergence of the optimiser to the empirical best subset, not to the true support, is only stated in words, and is about the number of particles rather than a statement for every admissible run at fixed computational budget. Nothing in Duan identifies the empirical best fixed-size subset with the true model: Table 1 shows hit ratios near 0 for weak coefficients (0.1) and 0.06-0.6 in the 0.8-correlation group even when the SMC beats the true model's R^2 (pp.20-21), so even a perfect optimiser would not report the truth.
- Thm 4 (certified screening, fixed design, no beta-min; a certified reported model of size <= k_n misses a true predictor w.p. -> 0): no overlap. Duan's reliability tool (I3) estimates the probability of improving the objective, it is not a certificate about the reported set, has no coverage statement about true predictors, and rests on an unproved EVT approximation. The only common ground is the notion of a "reliability/optimality assessment" attached to a best-subset search; ours is a theorem with a verifiable flag.
- Prop 5 (unbiased evidence), Thm 6 (sharpness), P1 (trap theorem for single-flip chains): none. Duan has no negative/mixing result; the author motivates SMC over MCMC informally (slides p.14: independence of particles, no burn-in).

## 6. Verdict
Novelty risk to us: PARTIAL OVERLAP on the method family only (low); NONE on theory.
Justification: Duan (2019) is an early "density-tempered SMC over subsets" optimiser (geometric bridge from a data-driven start law, ESS-adaptive gamma, population-adaptive independence-type block moves, best-ever reported subset), used for fixed-size l_0 best-subset search, not for a Bayesian posterior; it reports no PIPs, no evidence, no islands, no swap/Gibbs moves, and proves nothing (only informal "M -> infinity" remarks and a heuristic Weibull/EVT approximation of the maximal R^2). Largest experiment p = 900, p_s <= 20, n in {200,1000}, against adaptive Lasso only.
Must cite: as prior art for tempered SMC applied to l_0 best-subset selection with an adaptive ESS schedule and a best-ever reported subset, and for the EVT-based "how close is the reported R^2 to the true max" idea; also cite alongside Schafer-Chopin (2013) and Schafer (2012) as SMC for binary/subset search.
One-sentence differentiation for the paper: "Duan (2019) uses a density-tempered SMC as a heuristic optimiser of the in-sample R^2 for subsets of a fixed size (p <= 900, no theory beyond an informal extreme-value approximation of the optimisation gap), whereas we target the full Bayesian posterior over models of all sizes with a block-structured start law and prove that the reported model equals the true model on high-probability data events (Thm 3) and that a certified reported model misses no true predictor asymptotically (Thm 4)."

## 7. Slides (2018) vs paper (2019): differences
- Slides (30 pp., Sept 2018): generic density-tempered SMC tutorial (lasso vs zero norm pp.2-4; SMC, importance sampling, ESS, resampling pp.5-8; density-tempered SMC with arbitrary initialisation sampler I(theta) and the remark that p(theta)=1 gives MLE and that it "can serve as an optimization method without any statistical inference purpose" pp.9-10; four algorithmic steps pp.11-13; advantages over MCMC and gradient methods pp.14-15; k-fold duplication p.16), then an application, proxy CDS curves (pp.17-30): 29 explanatory variables expanded to 442 regressors, 24 selected on a 3,000-observation subsample (R^2 81.96%, BIC -3,889.73), refitted on 141,918 observations (R^2 80.89%), R^2 by subcategory (p.30), using "proprietary zero-norm penalty regression software developed by CriAT" (p.19).
- Slides state the zero-norm objective as argmax exp{-||y - X_U beta_hat(U)||^2} over permutations (p.3) but give NO permutation sampler, NO initialisation q_bar_i ∝ R_i^2, NO block-replacement proposal or mixture, NO add/trim mechanism, NO CV/BIC size selection, NO extreme-value/Weibull reliability assessment, NO simulation study, NO adaptive-Lasso comparison, NO p=900 experiment. The slides' support boosting is generic ("MH moves to randomly sampled sub-blocks", cumulative acceptance at least 500%, independent proposal from the sample statistics M_theta, pp.13-14).
- The paper adds (relative to the slides): permutation-sampler details, mixture proposal omega=50%, adding/trimming with omega'=0.1, CV with spline smoothing, BIC/AIC, EVT section, simulation tables; and drops the CDS application. No theorem in either version.
- Text inconsistencies in the paper (my reading, for faithfulness): acceptance target 100% on p.9 vs 500% on p.10 (slides: 500%); Table 2 hit-ratio row labelled 0.004 for SMC vs 0.1 for adaptive Lasso, and caption within-group correlations 0/0.4/0.8 vs text 0.1/0.4/0.8 (p.15, p.21); the Table 1 coefficient description on p.15 ("0.1 for 100 variables, 0.5 for the second batch of 100, 1 for the remainder" among 300 per group) is incompatible with selecting 9 or 18 variables and appears to describe the sampling of coefficient levels only loosely; I could not reconstruct the exact design from the text.
