## 2. Simulation designs

One generative model, eq (21), p.19, is used for ALL simulations (Secs 2.3, 4.2, 5, 6, Figs S2-S4):

    w_i ~ iid N_p(0, Sigma);  z_i ~ N(w_i' beta, 4);  beta_j = 2^{-(j/4 - 9/4)} = 2^{(9-j)/4} for j < 24 (j = 1..23), beta_j = 0 for j > 23.

- Response noise: N(., 4). Fig S2 (p.x) states the true residual standard deviation sigma is 2, so the 4 is a variance (sigma^2 = 4, sigma = 2). Note: p.24 text says intervals for "sigma^2 ... cover the true value of 2" while Fig S2 plots sigma with true value 2; minor wording inconsistency in the paper.
- s0 = 23 non-zero coefficients, all positive, located at the first 23 positions (j = 1..23), the rest (p-23) exactly zero. Values decay geometrically by factor 2^{-1/4} per index: beta_1 = 4, beta_5 = 2, beta_9 = 1, beta_14 ~ 0.42, beta_23 = 2^{-3.5} ~ 0.088. 18 of 23 are below sigma = 2 (p.19 says "smaller than the residual variance"; by my count the threshold that gives 18 is sd = 2, beta_6 = 2^{0.75} ~ 1.68 is the largest of those 18). There is no minimum-signal gap; signals decay continuously into the noise level on purpose (p.19: "in contrast to typical simulation studies for shrinkage priors, in which signals are either zero or large"). They state this makes the posterior marginals bimodal for some beta_j.
- Sigma (two cases, p.19): (a) identity (independent design); (b) Sigma_ij = phi^{|i-j|} with phi = 0.9 ("dependent design"). The paper then says this is "the covariance matrix for an AR(1) model with autoregressive coefficient phi and stationary variance (1-phi^2)^{-1}". The displayed formula gives unit variance; a stationary variance of 1/(1-phi^2) = 5.26 would instead imply Sigma_ij = phi^{|i-j|}/(1-phi^2). AMBIGUOUS in the paper; literal formula = unit-variance AR(1) Toeplitz. All non-zero betas sit in the first 23 columns, which are therefore a contiguous highly correlated block (their stated intent, p.19). No column scaling/standardization is stated. No block structure, no factor model, no binary X in simulations.
- Implied signal strength (MY computation, not in the paper): with Sigma = I, Var(x'beta) = sum beta_j^2 = 54.61, so SNR = 54.61/4 = 13.65 and R^2 = 0.932. With unit-variance AR(1) phi = 0.9, Var(x'beta) = 391.9, R^2 = 0.990. (If the 1/(1-phi^2) reading were used, Var = 2062.5.) The paper itself never reports SNR, R^2 or PVE.
- Prior hyper-parameters: horseshoe (half-Cauchy on local and global scales), sigma^2 ~ InvGamma(omega/2, omega/2); the numeric omega used in the experiments is not stated. The truncations of the prior in eq (54) are only for the ergodicity proof (p.12, S2); experiments use the untruncated horseshoe (as far as the text says).
- Algorithms run: "old" (Polson et al. 2014 sampler with Bhattacharya et al. 2016 beta update), "new" exact blocked sampler, "approximate" sampler with threshold delta (default 1e-4 from Sec 4, rule delta = 1/p proposed on p.19; Fig 6 uses delta = 2 p^{-1} = 1e-4 at p = 20000).

- Name/source: maize inbred lines of the USDA Ames seed bank (Romay et al. 2013, Genome Biology 14:R55); previously analysed by Liu et al. - Genotype coding, imputation, centring/scaling, covariates/population structure: NOT stated.

## 4. Metrics, competitors, hardware, code

Statistical:
- Estimation error: MSE of beta from MCMC time averages (Fig 5 left; p^{-1}||beta - beta_hat||^2 in Fig 6). Reported median: old ~2x the MSE of new/approximate (Fig 5, p.24).
- Coverage of 95% equal-tailed credible intervals for beta from time-averaged quantiles (Fig 5 right): median ~90% (old, only one simulation reaches 95%), ~93% (new and approximate, never below 90%) over 20 simulations; the authors cite van der Pas et al. (2017b) that intermediate-size signals cannot reach nominal coverage.
- sigma recovery: 95% intervals cover sigma = 2 in all but 2 of 20 (Fig S2).
- Posterior marginal shape (bimodality) for beta_j, Figs 2, 8, S3, S4; Lasso vs horseshoe threshold-count comparison (Fig 9).
- NOT reported: TPR/FDR/FDP, MCC, F1, exact recovery, PIPs or PIP calibration (the horseshoe has no inclusion indicators), AUC, prediction error, credible-set size.

Computational:
- ESS: n_e = var(phi) n / tau^2_phi (eq 23); tau^2 estimated with overlapping batch means, batch size n^{1/3}, via R package mcmcse (Flegal & Jones 2010), burn-in 5,000 (or last 15,000 iterations for D4). Functions phi: coordinate projections of (beta_{1:100}, eta_{1:100}, log xi, -2 log sigma).
- ESS per second (Figs 1 right and 7 right); autocorrelation of log(xi) (Fig 1 left); median ESS/s: old 0.42, new 0.22, approx 11.7 at N=2000, p=20000 (about 50x for approx vs exact, text p.4 and Fig 1 labels).
- Runtime: log t on log N + log p regression (Table 2): N exponent old 1.6478, new 1.6847, approx 0.5449; p exponent old 0.7204, new 0.6065, approx 0.3392 (20 sims; 12 threads, so exponents are below theoretical O(N^2 p) for dense algorithms). Authors note the approximate cost is non-constant because the early iterations before the active set is found dominate; suggest warm-starting from CV-Lasso.
- Mixing scaling: regression of -log(n_e) (more exactly log of tau^2_hat) on log N and log p (Tables 3-4): independent: intercept -7.65, logN -0.17 (SE 0.03), logp -0.03 (0.02); dependent: -7.56, -0.15 (0.03), -0.06 (0.04); i.e. no deterioration with N, p.
- Approximation error: sup-TV bound sqrt(delta) * ||W|| * sqrt(4 + (N+omega)/omega + N||z||^2/(2 omega)) + O(delta) (Thm 6, App C); W2 bound ~ ||W|| ||z|| delta (Sec 4.1, App F); empirical KS statistic between exact and approximate marginals (Fig 3), correlation of pathwise means/variances (Table 1: delta = 1e-2: 0.98/0.39; 1e-3: 1.00/0.78; 1e-4: 1.00/0.99; 1e-5: 1.00/1.00; exact 1.00/1.00).
- Cost per step: exact O(N^2 p) dominated by W D W'; approximate O((s_delta^2 v p) N) with s_delta the thresholded active-set size.

Competitors: only the authors' own sampler variants ("old" = Polson-Scott-Windle 2014 with Bhattacharya-Chakraborty-Mallick 2016 fast beta update; "new" exact; "approximate") plus Lasso (cross-validated, glmnet is implied by the Friedman et al. 2010 citation but not named) in the maize data. Hahn et al. (2018), Makalic & Schmidt (2016, monomvn), Narisetty et al. (2018, skinny Gibbs) and Nishimura & Suchard (2018) are discussed but NOT run. No spike-and-slab, SuSiE, or screening comparators.

Hardware: "multicore hardware with 12 threads" (Sec 5.2, p.22) is the only hardware statement; no CPU model, memory, or language (R is implied by mcmcse; no mention of C++/Rcpp/BLAS).
Code/data availability: no statement and no URL in the PDF; the JMLR page lists none. Simulation data are generated by eq (21) (reproducible in principle, but several parameters unspecified, see above). Maize data not linked.

## 5. What a standard design looks like here (to be comparable at large p)

1. Model: linear, z = W beta + eps, rows w_i ~ N_p(0, Sigma), eps ~ N(0, sigma^2) with sigma = 2 (sigma^2 = 4); no column standardisation stated.
2. Sigma: two cases only, I and Toeplitz phi^{|i-j|} with phi = 0.9 (specify unit variance explicitly; the paper is ambiguous).
3. Truth: s0 = 23, beta_j = 2^{(9-j)/4} for j = 1..23 (4 down to 0.088), all positive, placed in the first 23 columns (so contiguous and, under AR(1), highly correlated); rest exactly 0. Implied R^2 ~ 0.93 (Sigma = I) and ~0.99 (AR(1)).
4. Size: largest simulation N = 5,000, p = 50,000 (Fig S4); random (N, p) with N in [1e3, 5e3], p in [5e3, 5e4] for the scaling study; p = 20,000 for the N-sweep (200 to 2,000). Nothing above p = 5e4 in simulation; the real data goes to p = 98,385. They do NOT reach 1e6: a p = 1e6 suite extends this paper by a factor 10-20 in p.
5. Replication: 20 random (N, p) data sets per setting (10 per N for the MSE-vs-N sweep); single large runs for the biggest N, p.
6. Run length: 20,000 iterations, burn-in 5,000 (21,000 / 1,000 in the MSE sweep, 30,000 / 5,000 on real data).
7. Statistical metrics: MSE of beta from posterior mean (p^{-1}||beta - beta_hat||^2), coverage of 95% equal-tailed credible intervals over replicates (median about 93%), posterior marginals for the first 25 coefficients; sigma coverage.
8. Computational metrics: ESS and ESS/second via mcmcse overlapping-batch-means (batch n^{1/3}) on coordinate projections of beta_{1:100}, eta_{1:100}, log xi, -2 log sigma; total wall-clock; regression exponents of log time on log N, log p; autocorrelation of log xi.
9. Real data: maize GWAS N = 2,267, p = 98,385 (computing efficiency plus Lasso comparison; no prediction/selection score); for comparability one needs the same SNP matrix (not publicly linked in the paper).
10. Hardware to match/report: multicore with 12 threads; they do not give CPU/RAM. Gaps to fill ourselves for a selection benchmark: no TPR/FDR/PIP/exact-recovery metrics, no competitors outside their own samplers, no block/factor/genotype X, no signal-strength sweep, no code release.

Caveats about comparability: several parameters are unspecified (Sigma used in D1-D3, D5, D7-D9; omega; starting values; maize preprocessing; which coordinates enter the coverage average), and the Sec 5.3 p range in the text (10,000-50,000) conflicts with the realised p in Figs 4 and S2 (down to 5,114).
