## 1. Recommendation and the object being proved

Build the paper around **statistical concentration plus discovery**, with a separate, stronger-assumption theorem for numerical posterior summaries. The reported HPM is the best model ever scored, not the most frequent terminal particle. That distinction creates a much easier route to a theorem about actual output: a rejected independence proposal can discover the global HPM. LS0 supplies another route that needs no successful random exploration. Neither route, by itself, establishes accuracy of PIPs or evidence.

My principal statistical target is fixed true support size, polynomial ambient dimension, a sparse identifiability condition, and a quantitative signal condition. Fixed support is a substantive but conventional starting point: Song–Liang's strict sparsity assumption (4) also bounds the true support size. Permit every fixed exponent (A) in \(p\le n^A\); constants may depend on (A). Do not organize statements by covariance families. Growing support should be a subsequent extension after the large-model posterior tail is resolved.

Below, “candidate theorem” means a precise statement proposed for proof, **not an established result**. Some statements have short complete arguments; the main statistical candidate has a serious unresolved lemma, identified explicitly. Conditional computational theorems are useful interfaces, but do not become mild-design results merely by assuming their difficult premises.

Let \(t\) be the true support, (s=|t|), (K=\min\(p,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\)), and \(\Omega=\{S:|S|\le K\}\). Use the exact coded score

\[
F(S)=\log\varpi(S)+\log m(S),\quad
\varpi(S)\propto\binom{2p-1}{|S|}^{-1},
\]

with (g=n), ridge one, (a_0=b_0=.01), and the coded standardization. Write \(\Pi\) for its normalized model distribution, \(S^\star\) for its prescribed HPM, and \(\widehat S\) for the ledger output. Distinguish \(Z\), the working standardized-data evidence, from a raw-data likelihood normalizer.

For data-generation assumptions, take centered, column-standardized fixed (X), raw centered response generated by \(Y=\mu+\varepsilon\), \(\mu=X_t\beta_t\), and Gaussian errors before centering. Analyze the score after applying the actual response standardization. Do not treat the resulting standardized errors as independent Gaussians. Probability statements are over the original errors; random-design results follow by conditioning on a high-probability design event.

### Implementation facts that determine the statements

The permitted current core is more recent in relevant respects than the implementation account in the method section. Direct code is decisive here.

* `v3_run.R::v3_run_settings` enables LS0 by default. Its default cap is \(\ell+|\widetilde S_0|\), where \(\ell=\lceil\log_2(n+p+1)\rceil\). `cpp/v3_core.cpp`, around lines 465–480, computes the start mode and executes this search after the final local search.
* `cpp/v3_start.h` constructs the exact capped start distribution by size convolution and computes its mode with a tie-aware fallback. No assumption about recovering a latent block partition is required to define either object.
* `cpp/v3_engine.h::step_indep` evaluates a fresh \(\pi_0\) proposal **before** acceptance. `eval` sends its score to the ledger. Gibbs alternatives and local-search neighbors are also scored.
* `cpp/v3_ledger.h::event` keeps a running HPM; cache hits need not re-offer a model because its first evaluation already did so.
* Four final islands each use \(N_e=4^e\max(2500,n,p)\), followed by \(L=\max(4,\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil)\) burn-in rounds and four measured rounds. Every round has one independence proposal per particle.
* `v3_run.R::v3_assemble_final` reports the **equal-weight** mean of island RB PIPs as `pip_primary`; evidence is the arithmetic mean of island evidence estimates, computed on the log scale. A weighted-PIP theorem would describe a different estimator unless the reporting code changes.
* `v3_calib.R` freezes settings before final streams and has a default tier-1 fallback to a valid grid. Failure of restricted-grid construction is therefore not automatically failure to execute the final run.

All stochastic algorithm arguments below use exact arithmetic and ideal independent random variates with the coded sequence of operations. A fixed finite-state PRNG does not literally provide an unlimited iid sequence. LS0-based discovery avoids that probabilistic idealization, but still needs score-comparison fidelity. These qualifications must accompany, rather than silently replace, the claim about the implementation.

## 2. Proposed numbered theorem package

### Theorem 1 — model concentration, with predictive consistency as a corollary

**Candidate statement.** Fix \(A<\infty\), \(s_0<\infty\), and positive constants \(c,C\). Suppose:

1. \(p\le n^A\), \(s\le s_0\), and the Gaussian error variance lies in \([c,C]\). Also \(\|\mu\|^2/n\le C\). These are sparse Gaussian regression and bounded signal-to-noise assumptions, not restrictions to a particular design family.
2. For (m_n=\min\{K,\lceil D\log(n+p)\rceil\}\), all nonempty submatrices with at most \(m_n+s_0\) columns have eigenvalues of \(X_U^TX_U/n\) in \([c,C]\). Here \(D\) is a sufficiently large fixed constant depending on \(A,s_0,c,C\). In the eventually fixed-\(p\) case, interpret the condition only for existing subsets. This is a sparse eigenvalue assumption on a logarithmic-size sieve, not invertibility of the full \(p\)-column design.
3. Uniformly over \(S\) with \(|S|\le m_n\) and \(t\not\subseteq S\),
   \[
   \|(I-P_S)\mu\|^2\big/\{\sigma^2m_n\log(n+p)\}\longrightarrow\infty.
   \]
   This conservative separation condition is implied by condition 2 and \(n\beta_{\min}^2\gg m_n\log(n+p)\). It is stronger than the anticipated sharp detection threshold. It buys a tractable first proof and allows shrinking signals.

Then the intended conclusion is \(\Pi\(t\mid Y\)\to1\) in probability for the **unmodified** capped target. Consequently \(P\(S^\star=t\)\to1\),
\[
\max_{j\le p}|\Pi\(j\in S\mid Y\)-1\{j\in t\}|\to0,
\]
and the associated coefficient/variance working posterior is consistent in empirical prediction loss, on the response's standardized scale. In the latter conclusion one can first aim for any diverging multiple of \(n^{-1/2}\) when \(s\) is bounded and model selection has occurred; a sharp unconditional contraction rate is not needed for the first paper.

**Status and indispensable gate.** I do **not** assert that assumptions 1–3 already have a complete proof of this conclusion. The decisive unresolved claim is
\[
\sum_{m_n<|S|\le K}\exp\{F(S)-F(t)\}=o_P(1). \tag{Tail}
\]
If this lemma requires additional design assumptions, the theorem must state them. It is unacceptable to relabel (Tail) as a “mild design assumption” and declare the work done. Conditional on (Tail), the candidate becomes a considerably lower-risk theorem on a logarithmic sieve. The first proving milestone should determine whether the stated primitive assumptions suffice, or exhibit the obstruction.

**Why this is a reasonable target.** Song–Liang Conditions 1 and 2 regulate random-design tails; conditioning on \(X\) avoids those assumptions for fixed-design empirical conclusions. Random-design population prediction still needs moment conditions. Their Condition 3 rules out mimicking an active predictor. Our sparse eigenvalue/separation assumptions have the same purpose, with a quantitative margin, but are stronger in requiring control of inactive sparse subsets too. We must say so. Exact duplicate active/inactive columns violate support identifiability even though the ridge score remains finite.

**Proof plan.** Establish four lemmas: standardization stability, uniform ridge-versus-projection score comparison on the sieve, elimination of underfits, and summation of overfit odds; then prove (Tail). On well-conditioned small models,
\[
\log\{\det((n+1)G_S+I)/\det(G_S+I)\}
=|S|\log n+O(|S|/n).
\]
Control the difference between \(Q_S\) and projection RSS at the scale required by score comparisons, not only pointwise. Use Gaussian projection/noncentral chi-square bounds for underfits. For overfits, retain the **exact** prior ratio
\[
\varpi\(S\cup\{j\}\)/\varpi(S)
=(|S|+1)/(2p-1-|S|).
\]
It supplies multiplicity correction in addition to the \(n^{-1/2}\) determinant penalty. Avoid summing expectations of untruncated likelihood-ratio exponentials at their critical moment; use truncation or carefully tuned fractional moments. A fixed fractional power wastes a factor \(p^{1-\alpha}\), so uniformity over every polynomial exponent requires care.

For (Tail), exploit the geometric size tail of BB(1,p), prior mass near the sparse truth, and integrated-likelihood testing. The cap is \(n/2\), not (O\(\log n\)\); the sieve must be justified, not substituted into the algorithm. Rank-deficient large models and nearly redundant columns are the stress cases: a unit ridge ensures propriety but does not generate a full \(k\log n\) Occam penalty when rank is deficient. The response-dependent standardization also blocks a careless direct use of a prior-predictive identity. One promising route bounds the raw-data transformed scale hyperparameter on a compact interval with probability tending to one and compares the resulting integrated scores uniformly.

**Risk: high overall; medium after (Tail).** Failure would mean that the large-model mass is not controlled by the proposed primitive assumptions, or the overfit summation loses too much multiplicity correction. What it buys is the statistical spine of the paper, including the distinction between a correct posterior target and a correct algorithmic answer.

### Theorem 2 — sure screening under weaker statistical conclusions

**Precise modular statement.** Suppose there is a sieve \(\mathcal M_n\) and losses \(d_n(S)\ge0\) such that
\[
\Pi\(\mathcal M_n^c\mid Y\)=o_P(1),\qquad
\Pi\{d_n(S)>\epsilon_n\mid Y\}=o_P(1),
\]
and every model in \(\mathcal M_n\) missing an active predictor has \(d_n(S)>\epsilon_n\). Then \(\Pi\(t\subseteq S\mid Y\)\to1\), and for any fixed (q\in(0,1)\), the target PIP set \(\{j:q_j>q\}\) contains \(t\) with probability tending to one. One can take (d_n(S)=\|(I-P_S)\mu\|/\sqrt n\), provided the preceding posterior approximation theorem is actually established for this loss.

**Assumptions and meaning.** Approximation consistency plus separation is the same logical structure as Song–Liang Theorem 3 and Condition 3. It does not require exclusion of redundant variables, so it remains available when exact-selection proof obligations are harder. This is a target-PIP result. It transfers to the reported PIPs if \(\max_j|\widehat q_j-q_j|=o_P(1)\), which is a separate algorithm theorem, not an implication of screening.

**Proof and risk.** Missing an active variable implies membership in the union of the two exceptional posterior sets. Thus all active PIPs simultaneously tend to one; a coordinate-by-coordinate union bound is unnecessary. **Low** risk for the implication, **medium/high** for deriving its statistical premises for the coded prior. It buys an honest weaker result if exact overfit exclusion is delayed. It does not justify calling the reported HPM a sure screener without a discovery argument for a suitable score-dominating screening model.

### Theorem 3 — discovery consistency of the actual reported HPM

**Statement with two alternative mechanisms.** Let \(\mathcal C\) denote frozen calibration, \(T\) its final grid length, and
\[
D_n=4N_e(1+T+L+4),\qquad a_n=\pi_0(t).
\]
On the SMC branch, \(D_n\) counts initial draws plus independence proposals across the four final islands. Given data and \(\mathcal C\), these are fresh \(\pi_0\) draws in the ideal-randomness model. Let \(G_n\) be the event that LS0 scores \(t\). If \(P\(S^\star=t\)\to1\), then the potential completed run satisfies
\[
P\(\widehat S\ne t\)
\le P\(S^\star\ne t\)
 + E[1\{G_n^c\}(1-a_n)^{D_n}], \tag{D}
\]
with the second term taken as zero on the exact branch. In particular, either \(P(G_n)\to1\) **or** \(D_na_n\to\infty\) in probability is sufficient. The latter can be conservatively replaced by \(4N_e(L+5)a_n\to\infty\), independently of the realized grid length.

**Proof.** Every independent proposal is scored, whether accepted or rejected. On \(S^\star=t\), scoring \(t\) fixes the ledger's final answer forever. LS0 is determined by data and its default settings, independently of the final proposals. Condition on data/calibration and multiply the probabilities of avoiding \(t\). Tables and inherited calibration scores can only improve this bound. This is a discovery proof, not an MCMC mixing proof. If numerical scores are approximate, require their errors to preserve the relevant global order; Theorem 1 alone does not establish that fact.

**Assumptions and comparison.** The overlap condition (D_n\pi_0(t)\to\infty\) is substantially weaker than \(\pi_0(t)\to1\), but it is an algorithm-specific additional premise, absent from Song–Liang's target-posterior results. It is not implied by sparse eigenvalues. A start distribution may spread mass over many proxy models, making its mass at the truth exponentially small even when the true posterior concentrates. Correlation clustering with maximum block size 16 does not eliminate that possibility.

**Risk: low for (D), high for a broad primitive overlap theorem.** This result buys a direct guarantee about the reported model with the precise existing particle schedule and no mixing-time theorem. It is worth proving even if the main corollary ultimately uses LS0. It also quantifies exactly what a proposed budget-growth change would need to overcome, without silently changing the shipped budgets.

### Theorem 4 — deterministic LS0 recovery from a reachable basin

**Statement.** Write \(M_0=\widetilde S_0\), \(b_n=\ell+|M_0|\), and let \(\mathcal B_n\) be a collection of feasible models containing \(M_0\) and \(t\). Suppose there is an integer potential \(V\ge0\) on this collection with (V(t)=0\), (V(S)>0\) otherwise, and (V(M_0)\le b_n\). At every \(S\ne t\) in \(\mathcal B_n\), the exact best flip-or-swap neighbor selected under the **implemented** tie rule strictly improves \(F\), remains in \(\mathcal B_n\), and decreases \(V\) by at least one. Then LS0 scores \(t\) before exhausting its cap. Together with \(S^\star=t\), this yields \(\widehat S=t\). If these events have probability tending to one, reported-model consistency follows without an overlap or mixing assumption.

**Proof.** Induct along the actual best-improvement path. There are at most (V(M_0)\) accepted moves before reaching potential zero. The code also scores the neighbors in its extra terminal scan, but this theorem does not need that extra allowance. The requirement concerns the chosen best move, not merely the existence of some improving move: an existence argument alone is insufficient for the implemented greedy search.

**Primitive research target, explicitly speculative.** Seek a basin consisting of sparse models with a controlled number of false inclusions and omissions. First establish that the actual max-plus start mode belongs to that basin. Then use conditional residual signal margins and a restricted incoherence condition to show that the best addition/deletion/swap reduces a weighted error count. This should be presented as a stronger computational regularity condition, not as another name for statistical identifiability. Signal cancellation can make the start mode omit active variables; correlated proxies can create local optima despite a unique global HPM.

For the block initializer, the relevant coefficients are projections of the **whole** mean onto each block. A nonzero true coefficient need not remain nonzero after projection. Song–Liang Condition 5 is marginal faithfulness; it supports their iterative screening argument, not a theorem about our start-mode DP or greedy search. Moreover, our cap couples blocks through a global size prior: independent statements about each block's favorite subset do not automatically determine the global start mode.

**Risk: low for the deterministic theorem, high for the design-to-basin theorem.** It buys a seed-independent path to reported selection and uses LS0 as actually coded. I would fund a narrowly stated primitive basin corollary, but would not make broad SMC-BVS consistency depend on proving that sparse eigenvalues alone force greedy recovery.

### Theorem 5 — accuracy of coded PIPs and evidence under explicit stability

This theorem deliberately has stronger assumptions than HPM discovery. A correct selected model does not prove a correct posterior average or normalizer.

**PIP statement.** Let
\[
M_n=\sup_{S\in\Omega}\Pi(S)/\pi_0(S).
\]
For the final-temperature independence Metropolis kernel, \(K_I\(S,\cdot\)\ge M_n^{-1}\Pi\(\cdot\)\). All the other elementary kernels preserve \(\Pi\), so every full random-order round has the same minorization. Conditional on the bridge-end population and calibration, the subsequent particle chains use independent randomness. If
\[
L/M_n\to\infty,\qquad \log(p+1)/N_e\to0
\]
in probability, then the **equal-weight RB PIPs actually reported** satisfy \(\max_j|\widehat q_j-q_j|\to0\) in probability. With coded budgets the second condition holds for polynomial \(p\); the first holds, for example, if \(M_n=o\(\log n\)\). This is a strong global overlap assumption.

**Proof.** Couple each terminal chain to stationarity through the independence step, giving a bias bound \((1-1/M_n)^L\). At stationarity the Gibbs conditional inclusion probability has expectation \(q_j\), including when evaluated at its random position inside the round. Kernel invariance controls the pre-coordinate distribution, so the same bias bound applies to the measured RB functional. Average each particle's four-round bounded functional; conditional independence across particles yields concentration at order \(\sqrt{\log(p+1)/N_e}\). The four rounds themselves need not be independent. A union bound over coordinates and the fixed four islands completes the argument. This route avoids any false assumption of independent resampled particles at the bridge endpoint.

**Evidence statement.** Conditional on any valid frozen calibration and data, the coded product-of-mean-weights normalizer has the correct conditional expectation under ideal arithmetic/randomness. To obtain **relative consistency** with four islands, a sufficient, deliberately conservative condition is
\[
T\exp\{2\operatorname{osc}(d)\}/N_e\to0,
\qquad \operatorname{osc}(d)=\max_S d(S)-\min_S d(S).
\]
Under this condition, \(\widehat Z/Z\to1\) and \(\log\widehat Z-\log Z\to0\) in probability. Here \(d\) is the coded log-likelihood discrepancy between target and start. This is a sufficient theorem target, not a claim that the condition holds generally.

**Proof strategy.** Apply the multinomial Feynman–Kac normalizer second-moment expansion conditional on the frozen grid. Each future product has oscillation bounded by \(\operatorname{osc}(d)\), because the remaining temperature increments sum to at most one. Bound each collision contribution conservatively by \(\exp(2\operatorname{osc}(d))\); the product bound then tends to one under the displayed condition. Check the exact indexing and normalizations against the reweight/resample/move order. This supplies an elementary, unattractive sufficient condition; a useful refinement would replace the global oscillation by transported second-moment bounds on posterior-relevant states, with explicit control of excursions outside them.

**Assumptions and risk.** These are computational stability assumptions, not Song–Liang Conditions 1–5. Calibration ESS on a restricted visited set does not establish either one. **Low/medium** risk for the minorization/RB argument and normalizer identity; **medium** for formalizing the conservative second-moment bound; **high** for a broadly useful design-based stability corollary. The paper should not hide that global overlap may be much harder than discovery. Unbiasedness of \(\widehat Z\) is not relative consistency, and says nothing equivalent about unbiasedness of \(\log\widehat Z\).

### Theorem 6 — misspecification through projected support, not original labels

**First, a precise modest statement.** Consider a fixed set \(U\) of \(d\le16\) candidate predictors from iid jointly Gaussian predictors and a Gaussian linear response, with nonsingular population covariance for \(X_U\) and positive residual variance. Let
\[
b_U=\Sigma_{UU}^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}(X_U,Y),\qquad
u=\operatorname{supp}(b_U).
\]
For the working posterior restricted to subsets of \(U\), using the same \(g\), ridge, scale prior and either the ambient coded size weights or their restriction to \(U\), suppose nonzero coordinates of \(b_U\) are bounded away from zero, covariance eigenvalues are bounded, and \(p\le n^A\). Then \(\Pi_U\(u\mid Y\)\to1\). Thus it screens the projected active set \(u\); it screens \(t\cap U\) only if \(t\cap U\subseteq u\).

**Proof and relation to Song–Liang.** Gaussian projection gives an exactly linear conditional model with independent Gaussian residual, though original predictors have been omitted. For finitely many subsets, underfit log-likelihood losses are order \(n\); the ambient prior cost is only order \(\log p\). Overfits incur a diverging determinant/prior penalty against bounded-dimensional noise gains. Standardization must again be retained. This is a specialized, transparent counterpart to Song–Liang Theorem 4, Condition 4 and Corollary 2; it does not claim their general misspecification result has been proved for our data-dependent prior.

**Use and limit.** This theorem explains exactly what block likelihood tables are learning under omission, but the tables themselves contain Bayes factors, not these independently normalized posteriors. For a growing collection of data-selected blocks, a uniform result requires additional concentration arguments. Even if it holds for every block, it does not establish concentration of the cap-coupled \(\pi_0\), nor preservation of original support labels. This is a genuine misspecification result of **medium** proof risk, not a complete misspecified-global-output theorem.

**Optional extension, high risk.** For nonlinear or non-Gaussian truths, formulate concentration around minimum Gaussian KL-risk distributions, and label support selection only when a unique sparse risk minimizer exists with a quantitative risk gap exceeding model penalties and fluctuations. Do not promise recovery of the original coefficient labels under arbitrary omitted-variable bias. That extension is lower priority than the coded Gaussian target and its output guarantee.

## 3. How the package fits the Song–Liang story

Song and Liang (2015), *A split-and-merge Bayesian variable selection approach for ultrahigh dimensional regression*, JRSSB **77**(5), 947–972, supplies the narrative, not a black-box theorem for this implementation. Their Theorems 1–2 concern density posterior consistency; Theorem 3 concerns sure screening; the PDF points to supplementary Theorem S for MAP consistency; Theorem 4 and Corollary 2 concern projection under misspecification. I did not read the supplementary material and do not attribute unverified MAP assumptions to it.

The proposed correspondence is: Theorems 1–2 supply posterior concentration/screening; Theorems 3–4 add the missing sampler/search-to-output connection; Theorem 5 separately addresses reported numerical summaries; Theorem 6 supplies a controlled misspecification result. This is a stronger output story without pretending every conclusion shares the same assumptions.

Condition 1 is an exponential-tail condition on covariate combinations; Condition 2 weakens this to second moments and obtains weaker probabilistic convergence. Neither is needed to formulate conditional fixed-design empirical consistency. Condition 3 supplies identifiability, which is indispensable for exact labels. Condition 4 is the moment condition in their fixed-subset misspecification theorem. Condition 5 is nonzero marginal covariance for active predictors and underpins their iterative screening. It must not be imported as if it guaranteed our greedy search. The polynomial-dimension restriction makes \(\log p=O\(\log n\)\), but does not eliminate statistical-computational separation.

Proof tools worth using, without claiming they already prove our statements: Jiang (2007), “Bayesian variable selection for high dimensional generalized linear models: convergence rates of the fitted densities,” *Annals of Statistics* **35**, 1487–1511, for testing/sieve architecture; Del Moral (2004), *Feynman–Kac Formulae: Genealogical and Interacting Particle Systems with Applications*, Springer, for normalizer identities and collision expansions; Mengersen and Tweedie (1996), “Rates of convergence of the Hastings and Metropolis algorithms,” *Annals of Statistics* **24**, 101–121, for independence-MH domination arguments. These are known proof frameworks, not newly consulted sources or claimed verbatim applicability. The elementary discovery and LS0 arguments should be proved directly from the code.

## 4. Honest scope, completion, and proof priorities

Do not claim that ridge regularization solves identifiability, that a high posterior mass implies the sampler discovers the mode, that successful LS0 makes PIPs accurate, or that unchanged ESS/diagnostics establish mixing. Do not claim block independence merely because \(\pi_0\) is assembled from block tables; the size prior couples blocks. Do not replace the coded cap with a small sieve or let the number of islands diverge in a theorem advertised for four islands. Do not promote optional weighted PIPs or unimplemented diagnostics to current outputs.

Operational output needs an explicit probability statement. Let \(\mathcal O_n\) be successful delivery. A theorem for the potential uninterrupted run gives \(P\(\mathcal O_n\cap\{\widehat S\ne t\}\)\to0\), without conditioning on completion. To assert \(P\(\mathcal O_n,\widehat S=t\)\to1\), also prove or assume \(P\(\mathcal O_n\)\to1\) under a declared growing resource allocation and negligible numerical failures. A fixed 8-GiB allocation cannot support unrestricted \(p\to\infty\) when the code stores a dense Gram matrix. The tier-1 fallback helps establish a valid final schedule, but does not prove resource delivery or numerical stability. These are implementation qualifications to an asymptotic statement, not reasons to demand a finite-sample theory.

The statistical candidate's first acceptance test is (Tail), followed by sharp enough overfit summation for arbitrary fixed \(A\). The computational candidate's acceptance test is a **primitive** LS0 basin or overlap condition with real scope. A paper consisting only of “if the search reaches the truth, it returns the truth” would not meet the brief. Conversely, spending weeks on global tempering stability before deciding whether the mild-design statistical target is true is a poor allocation of effort.

I would allocate the multi-week proving budget first to Theorem 1 and its large-model gate, while writing the short, code-faithful discovery and LS0 interface proofs. Next, prove one explicit design-agnostic basin corollary with clearly stronger computational assumptions; use independent-proposal discovery as an alternative corollary wherever start mass can be controlled. Add sure screening and fixed-subset misspecification to match the Song–Liang narrative. Keep PIP/evidence consistency as a separately labeled stability theorem, with no claim that posterior concentration alone verifies its premises. This package offers a credible path to a substantive paper about the coded reported HPM while exposing, rather than burying, the two hardest obligations: posterior mass above the logarithmic sieve and computational access to the concentrating model.
