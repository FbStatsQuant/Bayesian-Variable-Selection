2. **Replace the false “argmin-retained out” swap rule in PLAN.md:18.** With γ = γ_k and τ_j = ξ_j−x_T, the null swap objective is

   V(j,l) = (1−γ)τ_l² + 2γτ_lτ_j − (1+γ)τ_j².

   For a fixed incoming l, the optimal retained null is nearest to γτ_l/(1+γ) in τ-coordinates, equivalently nearest to x_T + γ(ξ_l−x_T)/(1+γ) in ξ-coordinates. It need not be nearest to x_T. Checking the two outside extremes is valid by convexity; fixing the outgoing index to the best-deletion index is not. This is already contradicted by the saved N2 diagnostics: sampled swaps with out_is_nearest=false are 12/14 in N2_0.5_20000_19000_0.json, 6/8 in N2_0.5_20000_15000_1.json, and 8/12 in N2_0.5_6000_4500_2.json. These are counts among saved swap rows, not among all moves. The displacement is O(√log n/n) at k=K, larger than typical central spacing O(1/n), so “asymptotically nearest” cannot silently restore the exact rule. Correct L3 before using it to prove no reinsertion, W ≤ p−K, or a band invariant. P3 itself does not assert this erroneous argmin rule and can remain a conjectural target.

3. **State the actual transition and deletion invariants, with quantitative margins.** PLAN.md:21–25 treats an outside-addition threshold as also establishing that every swap loses to the best deletion. That implication needs a separate comparison: the exact identity is G_sw(j,l;T) = G_del(j;T) + G_add(l;T\{j}), involving another centre and multiplier. A bound on additions at T alone is insufficient. Moreover, an inequality containing an unspecified (1+o(1)) factor cannot support exact move selection near a vanishing threshold margin. Give an exact invariant or errors demonstrably smaller than the available margin, uniformly over the visited centres. Prove that swapped-in indices cannot leave during the swap phase before concluding W ≤ p−K; strict increase of F alone does not establish that property. For the deletion phase, formulate the uniform empirical regularity lemma before relying on it: block independence before optimisation does not automatically persist after a global, data-dependent water level and an adaptive deletion path. This is the most likely positive-proof failure point. If exact phase separation fails, a bounded-total-move theorem sufficient for discovery would still answer the cap question.

4. **Make the P1 proof uniform, but do not discard its top-by-ξ conclusion on the suggested quadratic-term objection.** That objection does not produce a counterexample. For a fixed-size truth-free block subset R∪{j}, |R|=u−1, exchanging j for l changes α·quad by

   (z_l−z_j)[(1−γ_u)(z_l+z_j)−2γ_u Σ_{r∈R}z_r].

   Substituting z_j=κ_c+κ_1ξ_j gives bracket 2κ_c(1−uγ_u)+O(κ_1 max|ξ|). For u≤16, θ̄>0 fixed and p≤n, κ_c/κ_1 is of order √n, while max|ξ|=O_P(√log n). Thus the bracket is uniformly positive across all blocks and pairs. The ξ gap factors out exactly, however small it is. Write this argument, rather than comparing absolute O(n) and O(n^{3/2}) errors without the gap factor. Separately establish a strict population discrete-concavity gap for the block *log-table* objective and a uniform perturbation bound before asserting counts differ by at most one. Conditional on that count statement, the no-full-block deduction is correct: there are J_f=p/16−O(1) full truth-free blocks, and one count 16 would force all their counts ≥15, hence K≥15J_f=15p/16−O(1), contradicting (PK).
