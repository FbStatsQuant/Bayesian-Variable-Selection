## Reasons for continuing

- **(P1): the start argument is uniform over maximisers.** Saturation uses an available truth-free block and a uniformly positive population increment, with the count justified by p ≥ K + 16s₀ + 1. It needs neither truth keeping nor the stronger block hypothesis. The within-block exchange has a positive order-n factor uniformly dominating its noise term. For full truth-free blocks, strict population concavity dominates the uniform O(√(n log n)) perturbation, forcing kept counts to differ by at most one. The 16/15 condition then excludes a fully retained regular block. No cancellation condition on proper subsets of the truth has slipped into these arguments.

- **(P2): the negative mechanism survives arbitrary path histories.** The size-K bounds apply to every size-K set. Missing truths supply an order-n improving swap; otherwise an excluded sufficiently negative null supplies a swap beating every deletion. The deterministic partition makes the regular-block minima independent, and the number below the chosen threshold is n^{1/2−o(1)} with high probability. Each move can introduce at most one of these initially excluded minima. This proves the initial swap run without assuming that the start contains S₀ or that subsequent swaps have a particular form. The final counting argument correctly accounts for every scanned neighbour: centres of size at least s₀ + 2 cannot score S₀ in one scan. The conclusion concerns the deterministic LS0 route, with no lower bound on D_n or claim that the full method fails.

- **(P3): the static bounds address the difficult uniformity issue.** The residual floor in E4 is valid even at p = n − 1: the fixed-set Gaussian projection bound and the union bound over at most 2^n sets give a simultaneous floor. Independence across those sets is unnecessary. Thus the FACB p ≤ n/2 bulk multiplier bound is not being reused outside its hypotheses. The imported small-energy refinement does extend as claimed. The centring identity and tail-count argument control arbitrary retained sets, including one-sided sets; the small-m split also handles the potentially tight two-sided case when s₀ = 1 and ρ is close to one. All constants may depend on the fixed model parameters, as permitted here.

- **The endpoint and gain budget can be ordered without circularity.** At size K, a centre missing a truth cannot terminate or delete. Once all truths are present, positive-gain moves cannot remove a truth. Every strict superset of S₀ has an improving deletion, while S₀ is a strict local maximum. Finiteness and strict increase therefore establish the endpoint independently of any path-length bound. Only then is F(S₀) a valid upper budget. The budget also correctly allows additions and later returns above m_e: after the first small-m entry, the remaining potential is small and the global positive-gain lower bound still applies. The O(log n) initial phase and O(log² n) remainder are negligible relative to K, yielding every fixed multiplicative slack δ > 0.

- **(P4)–(P5): assembly respects the statistical and operational distinctions.** The anchored eigenvalue hypotheses of T03♯ hold for every principal submatrix, including the weak-eigenvalue cutoff; fixed coefficients give beta-min, and p ≤ n − 1 gives A = 1. Bounded posterior error converging in probability also converges in expectation. Scoring S₀ and posterior concentration imply reported-model consistency through the stated pathwise inclusion. Delivery additionally uses the total fallback, the recharged resource envelopes and inherited audit/machine premises. The revised L4 explicitly separates the scan-dependent factor of at most two from the more conservative delivery-envelope increments. Neither an optimal allowance nor a two-phase path theorem is asserted.

## Numbered issues and fixes

1. **MAJOR for original v3; resolved in the observed v3.1 — evaluation cost of an arbitrary measurable cap.**
   **Location:** original STATEMENT (P4)(iii); current STATEMENT lines 73–84 and L4_assembly.md lines 24–28.
   A deterministic bound on the *value* of a(data) does not bound the work or memory needed to evaluate that rule. An arbitrary measurable rule may lack an implementable evaluation, and a computable rule can take arbitrarily more than the declared allocation. Consequently the original v3 delivery assertion did not follow for its whole quantified class. The statistical claims for an abstract cap parameter are unaffected. **Fix:** retain the explicit evaluation-cost hypothesis now present in v3.1 and L4, and preserve the version/narrowing qualification in the final theorem package. The three proposed elementary rules satisfy this restriction. With that correction, I see no remaining obstruction here.

2. **MINOR, presentation — put endpoint existence before the endpoint budget.**
   **Location:** L3_static.md lines 94–108.
   The phase-A paragraph uses F(S₀) as an upper budget before the following paragraph proves the endpoint, expressly saying “once we know (i)”. This is a valid forward reference, not a circular proof: the endpoint argument uses no numerical bound on A_n or m*. **Fix:** first give the size-K/invariance/finite-ascent argument establishing (i), then bound A_n and the subsequent phases. This makes the logical dependency immediately reviewable.

3. **MINOR, exposition — spell out the exponent slack behind the P5 failure wording.**
   **Location:** STATEMENT (P5), lines 85–86; L2.4's cap threshold.
   P2 directly excludes caps at most K − s₀ − 2 + ⌊n^β⌋, whereas P5 describes allowances over K of size at most n^β. The stronger-sounding wording is nevertheless correct asymptotically: for any fixed β < 1/2, choose β′ strictly between max(β, 0) and 1/2. Then K + n^β ≤ K − s₀ − 2 + ⌊n^{β′}⌋ eventually, and P2 applies with β′. **Fix:** add this one-sentence inference. No change of claim or proof strategy is needed.

The remaining frontiers are accurately disclosed: the small p − K window, the negative result below the 16/15 threshold, non-exact-Gram/p ≫ n designs, the unresolved optimal allowance, and numerical P6. None is an objection to continuing toward the stated narrowed claims.
