## Independent mathematical checks

**Metric toolkit and UNI-0.** In L1_metric the shell beginning at m = 0 is essential and is present. It accounts for distinct indices at distance zero and positive distances below one. The conjugation error has both row and column bounds, so its operator-norm estimate is valid even though the conjugated matrix is not symmetric. Applying the inverse-decay argument to the appropriate principal precision matrix gives conditional covariance decay uniformly in the conditioning set. For a non-unit matrix the regression denominator is bounded below by 1/C, as required; using 1 here would have been incorrect.

For U = T intersect B(l,r), V = T minus U, the regression normal equations give beta_U - beta_{T,U} = Psi_UU^{-1} Psi_UV beta_{T,V}. Each column of Psi_UU^{-1} Psi_UV is a regression coefficient vector with uniformly bounded l1 norm. Thus the displayed operator l1 bound and the vanishing tail bound are legitimate, independent of |T|. Orthogonality gives the exact variance identity psi_{l|U} = psi_{l|T} + Delta' Psi Delta, hence the variance-ratio comparison used later. Empty U and V cause no gap.

L2_k0's replacements preserve the load-bearing steps of LOC2 L4 / APP L3 / GROW L6: fixed-size population and sample regularity; uniform truth visibility; selection removal; a reference count with log cost O(log log n); the conditional spherical-tail bound; and the size ladder, including near the model cap. The conditional spherical argument fixes the conditioning design and noise and uses the isotropic innovation law; it does not condition that law on the global sample-covariance event. Uniform bounds on the standardisation factors justify inserting the random rescaled coefficients into the deterministic inequalities. The relevance-set enlargement preserves polylogarithmic size and gives the required feasibility dichotomy eventually. For NA-R, the references are deterministic row functionals, the atoms have bounded variances, their total coefficient norm is uniformly bounded over a polylogarithmic atom set, and the variance ratio tends to one. LOC2's quasi-polynomial toolkit supplies TL under the fixed beta-min actually assumed here.

**U-LOC and UNI-1.** The residual-factor decomposition in L3.1 holds for general positive definite Psi: the orthogonal complement of span(gamma_T) inside span(xi_0,zeta_T) is one-dimensional, generated by xi_0 - lambda_T' Psi_TT^{-1} zeta_T. The idiosyncratic innovation is independent of that entire larger space. Neither claim needs block diagonality. The deterministic inequality in L3.2 follows with the stated normalisation; T = empty is separately covered, including the s0 = 1 truth-loss use.

With r_n = ceil((log log n)^2), the local reference family has log cardinality at most log p + O((log log n)^(2d0)). LOCL controls the coefficient difference against the maximum of all p coordinate atoms, and its variance identity controls the change of standardisation. Consequently the leading idiosyncratic term remains sqrt(2 log p), with a deterministic o(sqrt(log n)) remainder. This is the crucial improvement over charging a bounded atom weight against all p coordinates.

For the factor term, choosing h occupied groups and a nonempty subset in each gives the bound binomial(G,h)(2^gammabar-1)^h. The extra 2 log(h+1) makes the failure probabilities summable over h. Each F_T is marginally standard normal; joint independence is unnecessary. Although c_G may tend to zero, its inverse square root appears multiplied by kappa', giving precisely kappa. The separate kappa' bound follows from unit diagonal, the Psi floor and RB_M. Thus allowing a vanishing common loading scale does not conceal a divergent remainder.

FAC-A U(d) uses the correlation diagonal and the spectral floor, not a bounded upper spectrum of Sigma. Its coupling and coefficient perturbation therefore apply to this extension. The error is bounded by a constant times delta_n sqrt(p log log n). In the random-row case the entrywise correlation event gives delta_n of order p sqrt(log n/n); the resulting p^(3/2) sqrt(log n log log n/n) tends to zero under the strict exponent assumed here.

The FAC-A NA proof consumes only the leading two square-root terms and an o(sqrt(log n)) remainder. Its optimisation gives max over 0 <= s <= 1 of ((1+kappabar s)^2-s^2) = kappabar(kappabar+2), hence alpha_0 = 1/[2 kappabar(kappabar+2)]. The strict inequality supplies a genuine order-log-n margin. TL uses the resulting uniform O(sqrt(log n)) bound, including empty conditioning sets, against order-n signal losses. The block sandwich needs only the floor and TV on the actual blocks, or TV-pop on the specified covering family. The declaration that all subsets of size at most 16 can serve as that family under PTV is valid. The random-design conditioning/uniformity argument and the fixed-design posterior input are not interchanged.
