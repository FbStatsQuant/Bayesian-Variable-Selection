LOCK-WITH-CHANGES

The applicability-map target is right. POST and CT look viable; LOC is a plausible new theorem, not an established consequence of CT. The blanket negative family classifications and the MIS claim are false as written. Do not lock v1 unchanged. References below use FIN, GROW, GEN and PACK as defined in STATEMENT.md. This is a statement/plan review, not verification of the proposed new proofs.

1. Location: STATEMENT.md:40–47 (POST); PLAN.md step 2/L1_POST.
   Problem: LSF is sufficient, including p >> n and unbounded upper spectrum, but the cutoff/envelope and rate transfer must be made explicit. No upper-spectrum hypothesis should be added. Unit diagonal gives uniform Gaussian entrywise concentration; multiplying its error by O(log n) gives an o(1) operator error on every logarithmic-size submatrix. Energy is bounded deterministically by ||X theta||^2 <= (n-1)(s0 theta_hi)^2.
   Concrete fix: give an explicit cutoff, for example B = [A s0 + (s0+2)/2 + 1]/log(3/2), m_sharp <= min(K, ceil(B log n)) eventually, and C_m = B+s0+j0+2. Use R_m <= 3(2p)^s0(2/3)^(m+1) for p>=2; handle p=1 separately. Define measurable design events by a fixed bound max|Rhat-Sigma| <= D_A sqrt(log n/n), and state that EVERY deterministic sequence in these events has the same phi0^2=phi^2/2, g0=1, M, and deterministic BM envelope. For the joint O_P rate use the diagonal TIGHTNESS argument, not just conditional convergence. GROW L6 §7 explicitly establishes convergence; FIN s3's random-design paragraph points to PACK I4 for the rate transfer. State SL1(b)'s restriction on moving q_n; arbitrary thresholds approaching 0 or 1 are not covered automatically. "Idiosyncratic floor" must mean a fixed floor after population correlation normalisation.

2. Location: STATEMENT.md:49–56 (CT and covariance/precision alternatives).
   Problem: the conditional-decay consequence needs two inverse-decay applications; conditioning does not preserve entrywise covariance bounds merely by subtraction. It is a population statement, not a simultaneous bound on arbitrary sample conditional covariances (which may be singular for p>n).
   Concrete fix: make constants dimension- and U-independent. One valid CT construction chooses 0<t<a so 2K sum_{d>=1} exp(-ad)(exp(td)-1) <= alpha/2. Exponential conjugation then gives the claimed inverse bound. For covariance decay, first obtain decay of Theta=Sigma^{-1}; for V=[p]\U, use Cov(W_V|W_U)=(Theta_VV)^{-1}, interlacing, and CT again on the inherited integer positions. Do not compress gaps when taking a principal submatrix. For the precision-decay alternative start at the second step. Record the resulting changed constants; the two alternatives are equivalent up to constants, not with identical (K,a).

3. Location: STATEMENT.md:54–63; PLAN.md L3_LOC.
   Problem: "replace P1–P4 by L2_CT" is not a complete delta-proof. P1 is supplied by the spectrum assumption; P3 by the Schur-complement floor; P4 by CT; P2 (exact nearest-neighbour regression) is absent. L6.6 uses P2 for an innovation independent of ALL W_T and for only p(q+1)^2 reference innovations. CT alone does not give either exact fact. Directly repeating the conditional chi-square assertion for a window innovation and a larger T would be false.
   Concrete fix: make a separate quantitative NA lemma the gate for LOC. For T0=T intersect {i:|pi(i)-pi(l)|<=R}, R=B log log n, prove uniform decay of population regression coefficients/tails, then control the effect of the omitted predictors and of empirical projection. There are at most p 2^(2R+1)=p exp(O(log log n)) reference innovations. Require an explicit uniform bound max_{T,l}|Z_T,l-Zref_T0,l|=o_P(sqrt(log n)) (a stronger o_P(1) is welcome), sufficient to obtain max Z_T,l^2 <= 2 log p+o_P(log n). Choose B from the proved decay constants. Track the mean/correlation error from far predictors as well as the sample error; do not assume independence of a window innovation from W_(T\T0). The window proposal is plausible, but unproved in the supplied sources.

4. Location: STATEMENT.md:57–61; PLAN.md L3_LOC beyond NA.
   Problem: PTV is a sufficient replacement for separation for arbitrary selected blocks, but locality alone does not imply it. Also the random-design sandwich is not GROW L3 under automatically verified NEG. GROW L6 explicitly disclaims fixed-realisation NEG and exact G4-dir.
   Concrete fix: retain PTV as a separately labelled signal hypothesis, with a0 fixed uniformly in n and deterministic support/permutation sequences. Show uniform sample TV over ALL |U|<=15, taking account of raw coefficients theta_j/t_j. Specify the other required replacements: L6.3's O(log n) neighbourhood of BOTH l and S0, its selection-uniform reduction, L6.4's spherical tail for the entire RSS-normalised statistic, L6.5's global size ladder, and L6.7's conditional TL transfer with common constants. Use the three joint-law events directly and the pathwise assembly; do not claim literal verification of (G3c)/(G4-dir). Exhibit the deterministic envelopes for posterior/TL transfer. Define Q^0 first and enlarge based on its size, and define L={T:S0 subseteq T subseteq Q}. A sufficient checkable PTV condition is |theta_j|c - K2 sum_{k in S0\{j}}|theta_k|exp(-a2|pi(j)-pi(k)|) >= a0. PTV is stronger than necessary for the actual partition; failure of PTV is not a method impossibility.

5. Location: STATEMENT.md:65–71 (VN).
   Problem: Y in §1 is an n-vector using random sample scales, so Corr(W_l,Y|W_U) is not a defined population scalar correlation. The consequence has two distinct growth requirements; neither "many loaded variables" nor a nonzero covariance alone verifies them.
   Concrete fix: define V=theta^T W+e for a single population row with independent e~N(0,sigma^2), and impose the floor on Corr(W_l,V|W_U)^2, or explicitly define the equivalent covariance formula. Set N_n subseteq [p]\S0 deterministically; impose the floor uniformly in n,l,U. Show uniform sample partial-correlation error O_P(sqrt(log n/n)) over the bounded-size families, including sample-scale perturbations. The sparse floor on size s0+16 and n*kappa_n/log n -> infinity make this feasible. Prove the bound by: if a maximiser has k<K, every excluded visible null in a truth-free block has a strictly improving add; truth-containing blocks account for at most 16s0 exceptions. If k=K, use k-s0. The stated minimum bound then looks sound. Keep the GEN conclusion as obstruction to a bounded centre/certificate, and the GROW conclusion only when min(K,|N_n|)/log n -> infinity. No conclusion about failure of the method follows.

6. Location: STATEMENT.md:82–85 (growing equicorrelated blocks/factors).
   Problem: these classifications are false without signal and rate conditions. A growing block need not contain any truth; coefficients can cancel its common signal; correlations can vanish with n. "g_n=O(log n) not covered (open)" is also false as a blanket claim. Counterexample: a single equicorrelated block p_n=g_n=ceil(sqrt(log n)), fixed 0<rho<1, one fixed truth. Take Q=[p]. Uniform Gram floors and TV hold with high probability, NEG is vacuous, and |P|=o(log n) satisfies G4-chi. Existing GROW applies by the usual common-envelope transfer.
   Concrete fix: apply VN only to an explicitly exhibited visible subset in signal-connected blocks, with uniformly controlled correlations, noncancellation, and the two separate size/rate conditions from item 5. Include the already covered small-logarithmic regime. For larger O(log n) blocks distinguish coefficients satisfying G4-chi from cases requiring a new NA argument; do not call the entire regime open.

7. Location: STATEMENT.md:84–85 (strong factor classification).
   Problem: b^T theta != 0 and a growing loaded set do not imply VN, even for a strong one-factor model with fixed idiosyncratic floor. Example: p=n^2, W_1=n^{-1}F+sqrt(1-n^{-2})e_1, W_l=(1/2)F+sqrt(3/4)e_l for l>=2, independent standard Gaussian latent variables, S0={1}, theta_1=1. The factor eigenvalue is order p, the correlation matrix has floor >=3/4, all coordinates are loaded, and b^T theta=n^{-1} !=0. Yet Corr(W_l,V)^2=O(n^{-2}), already at U=empty, so VN cannot be invoked. Even with fixed nonzero loadings, mere growth of the visible set does not imply the superlogarithmic size needed to obstruct GROW.
   Concrete fix: require a quantified visibility floor on a sufficiently large null subset, for example uniformly nonvanishing effective signal/loading and bounded-conditioning residual signal. For multiple factors, nonzero common signal vector alone is insufficient: loadings may be orthogonal to it. Record cancellation, weak exposure, bounded visible subsets, and logarithmic visible subsets separately. Use FAC status verbatim without turning a failed VN hypothesis into a negative theorem.

8. Location: STATEMENT.md:74–81 (positive families, STAR and masking).
   Problem: family membership is being conflated with full theorem applicability. Bandedness needs uniformly bounded bandwidth to imply LOC with fixed constants. Uniform ARMA/spectral/decay constants must survive n; a generic summability assumption supplies no stated spectral floor for POST's LSF proof. Bounded-block covariance matrices do satisfy LOC after concatenating blocks, including g>16, but do not guarantee PTV for multiple truths. Example: a two-variable block with rho=1/2 and theta=(1,-2) has Cov(W_1,theta^T W)=0, violating PTV at U=empty. "GEN/PXN masking" is not an automatic replacement for arbitrary such blocks. Whole-group recovery is not a hypothesis of abstract GEN; SEL uses whole-group containment in actual blocks.
   Concrete fix: list fixed bandwidth, uniform spectral floor and decay constants explicitly; retain PTV beside every LOC applicability entry. Cite GEN/PXN only after their actual centre/partition/visibility/leakage assumptions are checked; distinguish containment from equality. Supply an actual STAR matrix and growth regime. For example Sigma=[[1,a1_m^T/sqrt(m)],[a1_m/sqrt(m),I_m]], 0<a<1, truth at the hub, m=ceil(sqrt(n)), has spectrum in [1-a,1+a]; its leaf partial signal given <=15 other leaves has squared correlation asymptotic to a constant/m, so VN applies to m leaves. This proves a bounded-spectrum counterexample to the routes, not that all nonlocal bounded-spectrum designs fail. For p>>n one can append independent coordinates. Put the required iid, bounded-spectrum, AR(1), Toeplitz, banded, bounded-block, growing-block and factor families in separate rows with positive, conditional, obstructed and unproved entries.

9. Location: STATEMENT.md:86–87 (MIS), read with §1's signal convention.
   Problem: full precision bandedness does not imply fixed projected support for arbitrary candidate sets. Prop. P uses the precision of the MARGINAL vector (W_C,W_k), not automatically the full precision. Eliminating excluded null variables can create fill-in. Example: a uniformly positive pentadiagonal precision with negative off-diagonals at distances 1 and 2 (a strictly diagonally dominant M-matrix), candidate odd coordinates, and one active omitted even coordinate. The excluded even coordinates form a connected chain. Marginalising the other evens connects the active omitted coordinate to arbitrarily many odd candidates, giving growing proxy support. Bounded blocks and AR(1) have useful special structure, but general AR(k) is not settled by the stated neighbour rule. Conversely factor signal cancellation can eliminate all proxies, so "not for factor designs" is not universal.
   Concrete fix: state the rule with marginal precision and a bounded neighbourhood assumption after marginalisation; give candidate-set restrictions or derive sparsity separately for each family. Keep fixed projected support SIZE distinct from fixed support POSITIONS. Retain MIS's other premises: nonempty fixed projected sparsity, projected beta-min, common energy/eigenvalue envelopes, and fixed tau_n^2≡tau^2. Also do not import MIS(iii) directly into §1's sample-standardised signal model: raw slopes theta_j/t_j depend on omitted columns, so the jointly Gaussian raw-linear GR representation is not automatic. Use a separately flagged fixed-raw-slope model or prove an extension.
