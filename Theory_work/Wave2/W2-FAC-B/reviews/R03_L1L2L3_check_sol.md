Verdicts: **L1 VERIFIED-WITH-AMENDMENTS; L2 VERIFIED-WITH-AMENDMENTS; L3 VERIFIED-WITH-AMENDMENTS.** These verdicts concern the asymptotic conclusions under the locked STATEMENT hypotheses. Several displayed proof steps are false as written. The repairs below establish the conclusions without changing the locked target; no counterexample to those conclusions was found.

Line references below are to the reviewed lemma files. I checked their full contents against STATEMENT.md, the score/prior/block/start/local-search definitions in 02_method.tex, and 04_theory.tex:60–90.

## Numbered issues and repairs

1. **L1:100 — reversed inequality in the offset bound.** Put d = 1−ρ. The displayed chain asserts
   (d+1/a)/(d+kρ) ≤ (d+1/a)/(ρ(k+κ_n)).
   This is false: the right denominator is d+kρ+1/a, strictly larger than the left denominator. In fact the last expression equals the original α/(α+kβ). **Fix:** replace the whole chain by the exact identity
   α/(α+kβ) = (d+1/a)/(ρ(k+κ_n)).
   The claimed C_ρ(|η|+1)/(k+κ_n) bound then follows. The deterministic contribution after multiplication by k+κ_n is √(n−1)θ̄/(σ√d·a), which tends to zero, so allowing n₀ to depend on θ̄ and σ does justify a constant depending only on ρ.

2. **L1:113–114 — the final weakened inverse bound does not imply the stated RSS upper bound.** The preceding inverse expansion is correct, but line 114 drops a factor 1/(n+1) inside the correction. Its displayed lower bound only gives an additive Q−RSS/s² bound of approximately 1+1/d, rather than 1+1/((n−1)d). **Fix:** retain the factor and write, with h = z_TᵀG_T⁻¹z_T,
   n z_TᵀH_T⁻¹z_T ≥ [n/(n+1)]h[1−1/((n+1)(n−1)d)].
   Consequently
   0 ≤ Q_T−RSS_T/s² ≤ (n−1)/(n+1) + n/((n+1)²d) ≤ 1+1/((n−1)d).
   Thus the asserted Q bound is correct; its written final inference needs repair. L1.5(c) then follows with the stated constants for sufficiently large n, uniformly in T. In particular n(n−1)d/α ∈ [1−2/n,1] is an eventual bound with a threshold depending on ρ, not a bound for every admissible finite n.

3. **L2:23–35 — explicitly handle U = ∅.** The addition argument includes u=0, whereas the justification “γ_u ≤ 1/u” divides by zero there. The empty-matrix regression expression also needs the usual empty-vector convention. **Fix:** state B_∅=0 and t_{∅,l}=z_l. For u=0 the noise expression is g_l and the population expression is (Σθ*)_l; the displayed c(∅,l) formula gives exactly this value. For u≥1, γ_u u≤1 and the noise expression is bounded by 2 max|g_j|, hence certainly by the asserted 16 max|g_j|. No extra hypothesis is needed. This is an endpoint omission, not a failure of the start-mode claim.

4. **L3:13,19,30 — η denotes both a Gaussian input and a fixed balance constant.** Line 13 redefines the η already used for N(0,1), making E1 and the offset reference ambiguous. **Fix:** rename the fixed 1/50 constant, for example ε_bal, and define K₁=100/ε_bal². Retain η exclusively for the Gaussian factor. All calculations below use this intended interpretation.

5. **L3:21 — the E3 parenthetical is false in the included bounded-p regime.** The bound H̄−H≤L_n/H is valid, but L_n/H≤δ_e need not hold. For m₀=1 and L_n=10000, H̄−H≈139.766 while δ_e≈30.470; hence even H̄−H≤δ_e fails. Such bounded-m₀ sequences are allowed. **Fix:** restrict “L_n/H≤δ_e” to regime (L), for sufficiently large n. There H≥0.1√log n and 2 log(25m₁H)≥L_n eventually. E3 itself still has probability tending to one in both regimes, and the uses of the stronger band in (c2) are already restricted to (L). This does not invalidate P(E_n)→1.

6. **L3:10–11,21,44–47 — missing empty-null-sample conventions.** The target allows m₀=0, for example p=s₀, or a single truth-complete block. Then max over 𝒩* and log₂m₀ in the proof are undefined. **Fix:** define max_{j∈∅}|ξ_j|=0; E4 and all its indexed statements are vacuous for m₀=0; skip the E4 proof in that case. Treat E5–E7 as the sure event outside regime (L). For E6 in regime (L), define its assertion to include the existence of at least m₁ observations of each sign, or assign nonexistent order statistics value −∞. The binomial argument proves this existence as part of the event. These conventions cover bounded, divergent and oscillating p_n without a subsequence restriction.

7. **L3:54–55 — the written numerical justification for E6 is invalid, although 25 suffices asymptotically.** The proof invokes a factor 3 and then informally absorbs it into 25, but 4√(2π)·3≈30.080>25. Also 2m₀+2≤3m₀ requires m₀≥2, not m₀≥1. **Fix:** for m₀≥5 use
   4√(2π)(2+2/m₀) ≤ 4√(2π)·2.4 <25.
   Since t₀≤H, this gives exactly
   2 log m₀−2 log(4√(2π)m₁t₀) ≥ H²−2 log(25m₁H).
   Here t₀≥1 eventually because 2m₁/m₀→0; and t₀≤H follows from Φ̄(H)≤e^(−H²/2)=1/(2m₀+2)<2m₁/m₀. Thus the stated H−δ_e lower bound and both Chernoff bounds are valid with the original δ_e. No larger constant or different event is required.

8. **L3:57–61 — make the E7 grid, endpoint treatment and vanishing union bound explicit.** An unspecified cover by intervals of length L_n/4 does not alone establish the claimed containment property. **Fix:** put b=H−δ_e, h=L_n/4, and use the full grid cells [rh,(r+1)h] lying in [0,b]. Every length-2h subinterval of [0,b] contains a full cell; the leftover terminal segment need not itself be covered. There are at most b/h such cells. Eventually b≥1, so φ(b)≥Φ̄(b); by the repaired E6 calculation b≤t₀. Each signed empty-cell probability is at most exp(−L_nm₁/2). Their total is bounded by 2(4H/L_n+1)exp(−L_nm₁/2), which tends to zero because log H≤L_n/2+O(1) and m₁≥K₁L_n. Reflection handles the negative sample. This supplies the omitted construction and confirms that the probability bound does go to zero.

## Checks supporting the verdicts

**L1.** Independently expanded the determinant, inverse, deletion, addition and swap expressions, including k=0 additions and k=1 deletions. The closed form of F, both γ identities, all three neighbour gains, the sign of the centre offset and the k-free offset in STATEMENT §3 agree. The size-only increment formula also works at u=1 with L₀=0. The prior ratios are those of the shipped BB(1,p) prior. An independent NumPy calculation over all subsets of five variables for 12 (n,ρ) settings checked 384 subsets: largest absolute errors were 3.16·10⁻¹⁴ for quad, 6.08·10⁻¹⁵ for deletions, 1.59·10⁻¹⁵ for additions, 7.22·10⁻¹⁶ for swaps, 1.50·10⁻¹² for the centre identity, and 3.69·10⁻¹¹ for deletion F-gains. The RSS and eventual multiplier bounds passed these checks. These numerical checks supplement the algebra; they are not asymptotic proofs.

**L2.** The finite c_FS minimum is independent of p and the partition under fixed ϑ, fixed s₀ and FS. The global maximum bound costs at most e^(−L_n); the signal projection and χ² failures vanish. Zero pairs occur only in the single block containing all truths; their number is uniformly bounded, and the variance bound ≤4 gives the asserted vanishing finite-union bound. Their population remainder is O(n⁻²) before multiplication by (n−1)/s, hence O(n⁻¹) in t. The resulting block log-increment is −½log n+O(L_n), uniformly over zero pairs. For positive pairs it is at least a positive constant times n minus O(log n). The prior cannot overturn either improvement: its addition cost is at most log(2p), and its deletion ratio is ≥1 for k≤p. The uniqueness argument correctly treats missing truths before removing N₀, so it never incorrectly applies the zero-pair bound to a truth-incomplete block. Independent exhaustive enumeration at n=10⁷, p=6 verified unique starts for truth-complete, truth-separated, and single-block partitions, with the predicted N₀ exclusions. The full n4_start.py sweep was not rerun.

**L3.** E1 and all three parts of E2 have vanishing failure probabilities; independence between their events is unnecessary. E3's probability bound is valid after the empty-sample convention. E4's dyadic Markov union is O(1/log n), including bounded positive m₀. E5 correctly conditions on magnitudes; the weighted-sign and sign-count inequalities cost O(e^(−L_n)), and the sign totals have exponentially small imbalance probabilities in regime (L). E6 and E7 are valid with issues 7–8 repaired. For (c2), E7 bounds interior gaps and the two smallest signed magnitudes; E6/E3 bound the upper band by 2δ_e. A gap crossing b is at most L_n/2+2δ_e, which is ≤L_n+δ_e eventually since δ_e=o(L_n). Thus the claimed global g_max bound follows, including the gap across zero. Finally H≥0.1√log n and H=O(√log n) in regime (L), so δ_e=O(L_n/H), m₁δ_e=O(L_n²/H)→0 and Hδ_e=O(L_n). The offset consequence (c1) follows directly from the corrected L1 offset identity and E1–E2.
