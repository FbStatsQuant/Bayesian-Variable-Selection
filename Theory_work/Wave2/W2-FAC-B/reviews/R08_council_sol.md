The bulk induction can be ordered B1, B2, B3, B4 without circularity. The outward gap after deleting a boundary point is a consecutive original-sample gap. The corrected Young inequalities leave strictly positive deletion margins and strictly negative addition margins in both bulk subcases.

In the end phase only retained null magnitudes are replaced by Hbar. Outside coordinates enter through their envelope and convexity of the swap quadratic. The majority-side rule decreases the imbalance until it oscillates between zero and one; whenever its magnitude is at least two, its ratio to k+kappa cannot increase. This makes the swap upper bound smaller than the deletion lower bound. Reflection changes both xi and the offset varrho, as required. The proof correctly claims domination of swaps by the chosen deletion, without claiming that every swap has a negative log-n gain relative to its centre.

The post-lock masked-truth extension is valid. Under FS-prime, the additional problematic pair is an empty block with a missing marginally masked truth. That block contains a null, whose empty-set population increment is rho times the nonzero total truth coefficient. Adding that null strictly improves the start objective. Once the block is nonempty, the retained u >= 1 conditions apply. Thus no such competing set can maximise the start objective. The finite positive-increment minimum can still be taken over the nonzero values, and L3–L5 need no FS condition beyond the resulting start conclusion. The block condition in FS-prime is retained; marginal masking alone is not asserted to suffice.
