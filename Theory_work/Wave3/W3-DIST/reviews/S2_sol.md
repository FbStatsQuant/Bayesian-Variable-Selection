## Part 1: findings

### S2-01 — MAJOR — Theorem B, (A6)(b), B3: the beta-min envelope has no positivity requirement

STATEMENT lines 194–200 require a lower bound by b_n and divergence of b_n² n/log n, but never require b_n > 0. These two statements do not imply a diverging lower bound on the squared signal, as B3 uses.

Here is a counterexample to the literal theorem. Take p = 2, s = A = K = c_ψ = b̄ = ψ = 1 (p itself remains 2), x ~ N_2(0,I), independent ε ~ N(0,1), α* = 0, and β* = (a_n,0), where a_n = (log n)^(1/4)/sqrt(n). For all sufficiently large n, (A1)–(A5) and (A6)(a) hold, q_n = p, and there is exactly one nonzero coefficient. Set b_n = −1. Then min |β*_j| sd(x_j)/σ* ≥ b_n and b_n² n/log n → infinity, so the written (A6)(b) holds as well.

Nevertheless the coded posterior selects the empty model with probability tending to one. From the permitted W3-POST score formula, for a singleton versus the empty model, log posterior odds are

    −(1/2) log n + (1/2) n a_n² + O_P(sqrt(n) a_n + 1)
    = −(1/2) log n + (1/2) sqrt(log n) + O_P((log n)^(1/4) + 1) → −infinity.

For the null singleton the fit gain is O_P(1); the two-variable model has penalty −log n and the same leading signal gain. The prior odds are fixed positive constants because p = 2. These facts follow directly by expanding D_S and the ratio of (0.02 + Q_S) powers in W3-POST §0; for a standardized singleton D_S = n exactly. Thus π(empty) → 1. Under the empty model the original-scale variance posterior concentrates at 1 + o(1), and the fitted mean is constant in x. Its joint Hellinger distance from the truth is bounded below by a constant times a_n, uniformly over its intercept and over variances in [1/2,2]. Indeed, Gaussian integration of the mean affinity gives an upper bound (1 + a_n²/[2(v+1)])^(−1/2) on the affinity, so squared distance is at least a constant times a_n² on that variance interval. For M_n = (log n)^(1/8), M_n/sqrt(n) = o(a_n). The posterior probability in Theorem B's display therefore tends to one, rather than zero.

Minimal repair: require b_n > 0 eventually, or replace (A6)(b) by the direct condition n min_j(|β*_j| sd(x_j)/σ*)²/log n → infinity. Apply the same correction in TARGET §6 and the notation table. B3's squared-envelope inference then becomes valid. This is a small repair, but the uncorrected Theorem B is FALSE-AS-STATED.

### S2-02 — MAJOR — B2 and B3: the asserted window inequalities fail when the window saturates at p

STATEMENT line 360 asserts q_n ≥ m_n(M log n) + s. This is false for allowed fixed small p. Take independent Gaussian predictors, p = 2, s = s₀ = 1, a bounded nonzero signal, and sufficiently large n. Then the coded cap is 2, m_n(M log n) = 2, and q_n = 2, for every M ≥ 1. The displayed assertion is 2 ≥ 3. All reader assumptions can hold. Likewise B3's assertion m♯_n + s ≤ q_n fails: at p = 2, s₀ = 1, the remaining size-tail ratio at m = 1 is a positive constant, so m♯_n = 2 eventually. A bound s larger than p, also allowed, makes the line “m ≤ q − s” particularly unsuitable.

This does not refute the intended (ND) implication. Only unions S₀ ∪ J that exist inside [p] need control. The correct sufficient condition is

    q_n ≥ s₀ + min{m_n(t), p − s₀}
        = min{p, s₀ + m_n(t)}.

For B3 replace m_n(t) by m♯_n. When q_n = p these conditions are automatic. When q_n < p, the stated C_q has ample slack to dominate the cutoffs, uniformly over s₀ ≤ s and M ≥ 1. For example the leading coefficient in m_n(M log n) is at most [sA + (s+2)/2 + 3M]/log 2, whereas C_q M = [2s(A+2)+8]M/log 2. The fixed additive terms can be absorbed by one n₀. For m♯_n use W3-POST's explicit log-2 cutoff bound, again capped at p on union sizes.

Minimal repair: state the effective union-size conditions above and split the saturated and unsaturated cases. The eigenvalue and determinant argument itself is sound; the false integer inequalities must not remain in its application to Theorems A and B.

### S2-03 — MAJOR — reader Setup and Corollary C: p is raw in the setup but is used as retained in the coded prior

In §1R, p is the dimension of the observed vector x. After dropping sample-constant columns, the method description still specifies BB(1,p) and min(p,floor(n/2)), with no retained-dimension definition. The consumed W3-POST §0 explicitly uses p_ret for both quantities. This is an actual difference of posteriors on positive-probability data sets, not just a zero-probability issue for continuous predictors.

For example let the three raw predictors be independent Rademacher variables and let β* = 0. This satisfies (A1)–(A5), with K = 1 and identity population correlation, for large n. The event that only the third sample column is constant has positive probability 2^(1−n)(1−2^(1−n))². There are then two retained predictors. The coded prior odds of a particular retained singleton against the empty model are 1/3; the prior described using raw p = 3 gives 1/5. (These follow from w(1)/w(0) = 1/(2p−1).) B1 bounds the probability of this event but does not make the two posterior definitions equal on it.

Minimal repair: retain p for the raw dimension and introduce r = p_ret(X); use BB(1,r), cap min(r,floor(n/2)), and padded original-coordinate coefficients with zeros on dropped columns. Specify how the posterior is defined when r < 2, or explicitly supply a convention and charge that event to the remainder. Corollary C's list has capacity 40r+1, hence at most 40p+1; the latter upper bound is valid. Theorems A and C can absorb the dropped-column event at their stated probability scale, so this finding is a definition/faithfulness repair rather than a counterexample to the intended contraction displays.

### S2-06 — MINOR — Corollary C: final-run scope and returned log scores

The permitted v3_run.R assembles the output of a fresh final production run using raw$ledger and returns led$top as top_store. The permitted v3_ledger.h keeps the best 40p_ret+1 distinct models offered to that ledger. It does not establish a list of the best models over all calibration and final computations of an entire fit unless those models were offered to the final ledger. §2 states the correct final-run scope; §1R should carry it explicitly instead of the ambiguous “all models the run evaluated.”

The list stores score values F, i.e. log unnormalized weights, rather than literal unnormalized weights. “Weighted in proportion to their returned weights” should therefore say “in proportion to exp(F(S)).” The R file also returns PIP estimates, an MPM, normalizing-constant estimates and diagnostics; the reader text says the output contains a list, not that the list is the only output, so that omission is not a contradiction. Minimal repair: name the final SMC run/ledger and exponentiate the returned log scores. Retention of an offered model with π(S₀) > 1/2 is correct: it is the unique global score maximizer and cannot be evicted by TopStore's ordering.

### S2-07 — MINOR — TARGET §6 necessity lines and the “no identifiability condition” wording

A4 is not a fixed positive population floor, but it does impose sparse identifiability. For large n with q_n ≥ 2s, two distinct s-sparse coefficient vectors cannot have equal linear predictor almost surely: their difference is supported on at most 2s coordinates and has strictly positive variance by A4. Thus the unqualified statement “no identifiability condition” is misleading. Replace it by “no beta-min or fixed positive eigenvalue floor; no Song–Liang Condition 3 is imposed,” as appropriate. This distinguishes a weak floor from absence of any injectivity restriction.

The A3 necessity line is justified as a statement about the master's interface formulation, not as a proved primitive replacement preserving every bound of reader Theorem A. Keep that qualification prominent. Its assertion about simulations with infinite fourth moments cannot be checked from this packet: the relevant experiment outputs are not permitted inputs. The A2 line should similarly avoid a blanket necessity claim without a uniform class qualification: sparsity is not necessary for prediction over every particular degenerate design law. The A4 line correctly leaves necessity for the sharp rate open, and the no-A4 consistency result follows from the given slower bound. The A5 line concerns failure of a proof interface, not an established necessity theorem for distribution recovery; its wording should continue to distinguish those assertions. The A6 explanation is correct after S2-01.
