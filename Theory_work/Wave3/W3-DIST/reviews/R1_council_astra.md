## Additional mathematical checks

- **H and T.** The normal affinity separates variance mismatch from the bounded mean loss. The fitted intercept yields the exact empirical mean-error identity, with the sample noise mean accounted for separately. In T, the swap argument has sufficient slack (48/169>1/4), and the countable construction followed by dominated convergence covers arbitrary atomic laws. It does not require a moment-generating function of x.
- **S and V.** S's residual term is controlled by the fixed, design-measurable reference cost; its endpoint predictive numerator has expectation one without conditioning on the observed response scale. The denominator is integrated over an interval of length σ²/√n with a positive IG lower bound. The admissible cutoff is an up-set, so the claimed simultaneous-in-m event follows from its first element. V's order comparison and Gaussian quadratic-form determinant formula remain valid on covariance nullspaces. No eigenvalue lower bound is needed for those identities.
- **Bridges and reader assembly.** B2-core whitens only on the covariance range; population-null directions vanish almost surely. Its affine-coordinate construction handles the intercept. B1 supplies the no-drop/sample-scale event. B2 then transfers the population floor to the Gram and determinant ratios at the needed union support sizes. B3 has the larger fixed window required by selection, with the no-drop exceptional event treated separately. The final reader theorem absorbs all exceptional events on a common data event; it does not infer independence among them. The no-(A4) version pays its stated logarithm and enlarged size bound.
- **DIST-3.** Conditional on the true model, bounded ridge loss controls the center, Gaussian and IG tails control the posterior spread, and TR₀ supplies the parametric population transfer on the raw true support. Mixing back over models adds δ_n. The passage to every diverging M_n follows by a deterministic tail level and the explicit selection premise; a sparse VC transfer at order log n/n is not substituted for TR₀.
- **NEC.** I followed the near-copy construction, its determinant scale, posterior normalization and the many high-noise subsets. The lower mass c(k)/n on errors of order k log n, on an event with probability bounded below, contradicts any proposed fixed exponential constants by first choosing t=T log n and then fixed k large enough. The construction violates ND; it is not a random-design Hellinger counterexample and does not establish failure of in-probability consistency. The current statement and TARGET preserve these distinctions.
