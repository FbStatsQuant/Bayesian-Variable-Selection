## D. Misspecification decision and proof

**Recommended main statement.** Let A8 hold, and let \(f_0\) be the Gaussian conditional law with least-squares affine mean on the candidates and variance equal to its residual mean square. Let \(T_{\mathcal C}=\operatorname{supp}\beta^{\mathcal C}\). Along the iid sample, almost surely for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \Pi(d_H(f,f_0)>\epsilon\mid D_n^{\mathcal C})\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \Pi(S\supseteq T_{\mathcal C}\mid D_n^{\mathcal C})\longrightarrow1 .
\]
If also \(\mathbb E[x_j^2\varepsilon_{\mathcal C}^2]<\infty\) for every candidate \(j\), then \(\Pi(T_{\mathcal C}\mid D_n^{\mathcal C})\to1\) almost surely. The first two clauses give the Song–Liang counterparts. The exact-support clause is an additional corollary. Under fixed dimension empty pseudo-support is allowed. The Hellinger comparison uses the common predictor law and Lebesgue response measure for the two *Gaussian working densities*. For a discrete response define the target by expected Gaussian log score; call it a KL projection only when finite KL exists.

1. **Finite model space and population gaps.** Set \(d=|\mathcal C|\) and \(Z=(1,x_{\mathcal C}^\top)^\top\). For \(n\ge2d\) the cap admits every subset. The SLLN applies entrywise to \(ZZ^\top,ZY,Y^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. Positive definiteness makes all sample subset Grams eventually invertible, and positive response variance ensures eventual preprocessing success. Uniformly over this finite family, \(RSS_S/n\to v_S=\inf_b E(Y-Z_S^\top b)^2\) almost surely. If \(S\) misses a projected active, positive definiteness gives \(v_S>v_T=\sigma_{\mathcal C}^2>0\) by the projection normal equations. Empty classes require no minimum-gap assumption.
2. **Exact working scores.** In raw centred contrasts, \(Q_S^c-RSS_S=O_{\rm a.s.}(1)\): eventually the nonzero Gram eigenvalues are of order \(n\), in-span residual shrinkage is \(O(1/n)\), and response energy is \(O_{\rm a.s.}(n)\). Also \(b_0s_y^2=O_{\rm a.s.}(1)\) and \(\log D_S=|S|\log(n+1)+O(1)\). The fixed BB prior odds are positive constants. Thus \(n^{-1}\log\{\Pi(S)/\Pi(T)\}\to-\tfrac12\log(v_S/v_T)<0\) for each underfit. Finite summation gives almost-sure inclusion, strictly strengthening the draft mode without extra moments.
3. **Population density.** On each containing support, the raw conditional coefficient centre tends to the padded projection vector by the sample normal equations and vanishing shrinkage. The conditional variance draw concentrates at \(v_T\) by the inverse-gamma moments (shape \(a_0+n/2\), raw rate \(b_0s_y^2+Q_S^c/2\)); the conditional raw coefficient covariance is \(O(1/n)\) when variance is bounded. The mapped intercept \(\bar Y-\bar x^\top\beta\) also concentrates at \(\alpha_{\mathcal C}\). Gaussian affinity is continuous in these parameters after averaging over \(P_x\) by bounded convergence. Finite mixtures and step 2 finish the proof even if posterior weights among supersets fluctuate. Intersect events at rational radii for simultaneous \(\epsilon>0\).
4. **Exact exclusion under finite score variances.** Write \(u=Y-Z^\top b_{\mathcal C}\). Then \(E[Zu]=0\); the intercept score has finite variance by A8. The additional hypothesis gives the coordinatewise LIL, \(\|\sum_i Z_i u_i\|=O_{\rm a.s.}(\sqrt{n\log\log n})\). In full raw intercept coordinates every subset Gram inverse is eventually \(O(1/n)\). For any \(S\supsetneq T\), the reduction in RSS relative to \(T\) is bounded by the gain relative to the *true projection coefficients* in the larger model, hence by \(O_{\rm a.s.}(\log\log n)\). The likelihood gain in log odds is of the same order since \(RSS_S/n\to v_T>0\). The determinant cost is \((|S|-|T|)\log n/2+O(1)\). This proves almost-sure exact selection. With A8 alone products \(x_j u\) can have infinite variance; do not claim this exclusion rate without a new argument.
