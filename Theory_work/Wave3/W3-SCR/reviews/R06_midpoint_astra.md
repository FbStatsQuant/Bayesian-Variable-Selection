## 1. Reconstruction of the key result

**Score and one-step algebra.** Write lambda = 1/(n+1), H_S = (n+1)(G_S+lambda I). The variational formula gives R_S positive definite, R_S <= I, R_S^2 <= R_S and
q_S(v) = ||v||^2/(n+1) + n v'R_Sv/(n+1).
No rank assumption on X_S is needed. For an addition t, block inversion gives
R_{S+t} = R_S - R_S x_t x_t' R_S/a,
a = x_t'R_Sx_t+lambda.
Thus q_S(v)-q_{S+t}(v) = n(x_t'R_Sv)^2/((n+1)a). Schur complements give the two determinant increments (n+1)a and a_1. The variational characterisation of a_lambda gives 1 <= D=(n+1)a/a_1 <= n+1, and a_1<=n gives D >= (n+1)a/n. The prior increment is exactly -log((2p-1-k)/(k+1)). This verifies L1.2–L1.4, including the n/(n+1) factor and response rescaling.

Put B=log((2p-1-k)/(k+1))+(1/2)log D, A_S=0.02*tauhat^2+q_S(y_c), and u=n*g_{y_c}/((n+1)A_S). Then
F(S+t)-F(S) = -B - nu log(1-u), 0<=u<1.
For standardised y, Q_S<=n-1. Since nu>n/2 and -log(1-u)>=u, g_y>=T_n yields a STRICT improvement: the likelihood contribution exceeds n*g_y/(2(n+1)), whereas B<=n*T_n/(2(n+1)). This checks L1.5, including equality at the sufficient threshold. For L1.5°, g_{y_c}>=rhobar*(n+1)A_S/n implies u>=rhobar>0, and -log(1-u)>u gives the strict improvement. Also rhobar*(n-1)<=T_n. Both derivations are valid.

**Events and deterministic witness.** Choose the smallest-index missing truth maximising gamma before observing the errors. Because R_Sx_t is centred,
x_t'R_SC epsilon is Gaussian with variance sigma^2 x_t'R_S^2x_t <= sigma^2 a.
One scalar tail per S suffices; no extra s0 or p multiplicity is needed. Summing over at most N_q sets proves E1's e^{-x} failure bound. E4 follows from variance <=sigma^2 m_S^2. The chi-square dimension of C epsilon is n-1. The upper and lower scale bounds in E2 follow by expansion of ||mu+C epsilon||^2 and 2 sigma||mu||<=sqrt(n-1)*tau_n^2. The constants 1+2a_x and 1-4a_x are conservative and valid. E3a uses contractions of rank at most k, including in augmented space; E3b sums exp(-m*x') over excess-set sizes. Their probabilities are correct and need no independence between events.

**Screening.** On E1 intersect E2+, simultaneously for every missing model of size <=q,
sqrt(g_{y_c}) >= sqrt(Gamma_n(q))-sigma*r_n > taubar*sqrt(T_n) >= tauhat*sqrt(T_n).
Since q<K the addition is a coded neighbour. Consequently no such model is LO. The certificate-to-LO implication then gives exactly the two probability inequalities in SCR-A, with failure <=3e^{-x}. This is an unconditional bad-event bound, not a conditional coverage statement given certification. It holds on the same data event for every seed and every search path. The empty-family intersection convention is harmless.

**Self-normalisation.** Expanding y_c'R_Sy_c and using E4 gives the upper bound by m_S^2+2 sigma m_S r_n+sigma^2(n-1)(1+2a_x+2a_x^2). The AM–GM inequality 2 sigma m_S<=sigma_S^2 sqrt(n-1), together with the remaining <=1.02 taubar^2 term, yields precisely L3's denominator bound. L1.5° applies under MC°. The extra E4 costs one e^{-x}, giving 4e^{-x}.

**Master gap.** The signed d_S controls only the adverse noise direction; it has norm <=1. The e_S vector is R_S mu/m_S, not necessarily a unit vector, and also has norm <=1. The maximum plus zero is 1-Lipschitz. Both the union-bound threshold and the Gaussian-width threshold separately have failure <=e^{-x}. Taking their deterministic minimum does NOT require doubling this failure probability: one of these two fixed thresholds is the smaller. Intersect the two noise events and E2+ to obtain 4e^{-x}. On that event the numerator is at least G_S and the denominator at most both U_S and (n-0.98)taubar^2. Monotonicity of -log(1-u) proves the stated delta_S. The u_S>=1 convention is consistent: the constructed event would then be empty; a positive lower probability bound rules out that parameter situation. No false finite-score inequality is asserted on a nonempty event.

I could verify every mathematical step of SCR-A, SCR-A°, and SCR-M. The exceptions are the unqualified statement of M3 (finding 1) and the implementation dependencies outside the permitted excerpts (finding 4).

## 2. Stated versus used hypotheses

The central proofs use exactly fixed centred, sample-standardised X; centred Gaussian homoscedastic errors with sigma>0; deterministic theta and deterministic family/witness; q<=K-1; and exact scores when mapping flags to F. Singular designs and duplicated noise columns cause no algebraic problem because of ridge augmentation. No independent noise across model scores, independence of tauhat and numerators, search initialisation assumption, calibration success probability, or uniform random-number premise is used.

Random X is covered conditionally only if the specified conditional error law holds (independence from X is sufficient). A response-selected family/witness cannot be substituted without a new argument. Data-adaptive reported models are allowed because the event is simultaneous over the deterministic family. All asymptotic constants called fixed really must be uniform along the triangular array.

Neither s0 fixed nor polynomial p nor fixed sigma is hidden in A/A° or M. A1 DOES imply bounded s0 through s0*b<=||theta||_1<=M, and explicitly fixes sigma; that is a consequence of its stated assumptions. A1° need not have fixed sigma. SCR-C explicitly needs bounded s0 and its coefficient bound; both are used. The cap is essential for improving additions and the swap argument in M5. The numerical 0.02 is consistently retained; 1.02 and n-0.98 arise from it, rather than being asymptotic substitutions. EX-A is a genuine idealisation and is not a floating-point certificate.

The implementation-only dependencies (cache coherence, HPM offering, successful enumeration, accurate scorer) should remain separate from the statistical hypotheses. See question 4 for what is and is not independently checked.
