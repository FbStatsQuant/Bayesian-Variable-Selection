# L01 — blind statement-lock review (Sol)

Scope: W3-MISR/STATEMENT.md v1.0. Only the brief and its permitted inputs were read. No new MIS-R proof is assumed. The conclusions below are conditional on the declared POST2 inputs, rather than an independent certification of those inputs.

## Findings

1. **MAJOR — Remark P leaves Theorem F's BM-infinity hypothesis unnormalised; the resulting extension is false.**

   Evidence: `Theory_work/Wave3/W3-MISR/STATEMENT.md:158-160` specifies arbitrary positive sigma_n^2 and changes the energy condition, but does not replace Theorem F's BM-infinity condition. That condition is explicitly `theta_min^2 n phi_0^2 / log n -> infinity`, without a variance denominator, at `Theory_work/Wave1C/C-MIS/STATEMENT.md:132-133`. Fixed variance made the omission harmless in MIS1. It is not harmless here. The signal-to-noise instruction at MISR lines 154-157 is attached to Remark SNR and its listed W3P/POST2 results, and does not explicitly repair the separate Remark P / Theorem F clause.

   Counterexample to the literal extension: take p=2, centred orthogonal standardised columns with Gram (n-1)I, projected coefficient theta=(1,0), exact projection residual r=0, and sigma_n^2=n^2. Set m_n=K=2 for all sufficiently large n, so TAIL is identically zero. All required floors hold, s=1, and the new energy bound holds with M=1. The original BM-infinity condition holds since n/log n diverges. Nevertheless, after dividing the response by n, its mean is X_1/n and its noise variance is one; the squared mean norm is (n-1)/n^2 -> 0. This experiment approaches pure noise in total variation. With fixed p=2 and the orthogonal design, under pure noise each nonempty-model posterior odds against the empty model is O_P(n^{-|S|/2}) times a tight likelihood factor, and the empty-model mass tends to one. Thus pi({1}) tends to zero, contradicting the claimed Theorem F conclusion.

   **Required change:** explicitly replace F's BM-infinity condition by `theta_min^2 n phi_0^2 / (sigma_n^2 log n) -> infinity` (equivalently use sigma_{C,n}^2, since their ratio is uniformly bounded under the new energy condition). State that EP coefficients and residuals are divided by sigma_n, TAIL is imposed for the same invariant posterior, and the BM(c) and inclusion thresholds use sigma_{C,n}^2. The original unnormalised BM-infinity alternative must not remain available. This repairs the scaling argument without changing the main MIS-R theorem.

2. **MINOR — The floating-point assertion in R'(a'') is not a consequence of target invariance and is false without restrictions.**

   Evidence: `Theory_work/Wave3/W3-MISR/STATEMENT.md:70` says the floating-point outputs agree up to rounding, after a lemma quantified over every real shift and nonzero scale (`:57-58`). The code checks exact response constancy before scoring (`Scripts/SMC_BVS/core/v3_preprocess.R:105-115`); the subsequent stable centring cannot recover distinctions already lost in the supplied doubles (`:37-64`). For y=(-1,0,1), adding 2^55 in binary64 rounds all entries to the same number. The original input is valid and the transformed input has status y_constant, rather than a nearby posterior output. Overflow supplies another obstruction. This does not refute real-arithmetic R'(a).

   **Required change:** delete the blanket floating-point output assertion and retain the real-arithmetic target statement. If a numerical observation is wanted, label it as a test restricted to a specified input range, with a concrete source; the bare `numerics N0` is not a defined result or an identified file in this statement.

3. **MINOR — Corollary H's reference-law interpretation needs an explicit centring operation and KL experiment.**

   Evidence: `Theory_work/Wave3/W3-MISR/STATEMENT.md:139,146-148` identifies N(mu_i,v) with the '(centred) conditional law of y_i' and the KL projection of the full model. Under MIS1 GR, the raw conditional law has mean alpha+a+(x beta)_i (`Theory_work/Wave1C/C-MIS/STATEMENT.md:54-58`). The actual sample-centred response Cy has conditional covariance vC, hence coordinate variance v(1-1/n), not v. The displayed reference N((Cx beta)_i,v) is instead the law obtained by subtracting the *conditional mean's* sample average, leaving the noise uncentred. Furthermore, a population/marginal KL projection, averaging the omitted rows, differs from the fixed-realised-full-design KL projection; MIS1 explicitly distinguishes the latter sample-projection target (`Theory_work/Wave1C/C-MIS/lemmas/L1_reduction.md:69-74`). No choice of experiment is made in the current sentence.

   **Required change:** define the reference as the unit-noise comparison law transported to response units, or explicitly as the conditional law of `y_i - [alpha+a+mean(x beta)]` under GR. Say that it is not the marginal law of (Cy)_i. Specify which variables are averaged and conditioned on in the KL claim; restrict the familiar population KL interpretation to the stated Gaussian-row/intercept setting if that is the intended meaning. The contraction inequalities themselves do not require a KL interpretation.

4. **MINOR — The OS implication in the per-n hypothesis list suppresses its necessary onset.**

   Evidence: the list is headed 'Hypotheses at a given n' (`Theory_work/Wave3/W3-MISR/STATEMENT.md:35`), and its OS entry says it is implied by F_w, DG, ADG (`:39-40`). The cited implication explicitly holds only for n >= n_0 (`Theory_work/Wave3/W3-POST2/STATEMENT.md:46-47`; `lemmas/A_occam.md:14-15,25-27`). ADG bounds a geometric series with ratio proportional to sqrt(log n/n); establishing the chosen OS constant requires a uniform onset. The finite-n classes must use the actual OS inequalities at that n.

   **Required change:** append 'for n >= n_OS(s,A,g_0) (respectively n_OS(s,A,c_D)), with C_OS chosen as in Lemma A0'; distinguish that bridge from direct membership in the per-n configuration class. Section 9 already includes the appropriate 'for large n' qualification (`:178`).

## Answers to the eleven questions

1. **Well-posedness.** Apart from findings 1-4, the main objects can be recovered from the explicitly imported definitions. In particular M_S is the contrast smoother `n X_{c,S} H_S^{-1} X_{c,S}^T`, not an orthogonal projection (permitted locked input `Theory_work/Old_Projects/New_BVS_Headquarters/branch_snapshots/main@c4ef0dc/Docs/Theory/proofs/T01_T02_contrast_dimension/PROOF_v3.tex:315`). All constants in the main bounds have declared dependence. The per-n claim uses actual per-n size and OS inequalities, not an asymptotic property of a single configuration. For full formality in section 4, equip the configuration space with the Borel structure on the countable disjoint union over p of finite-dimensional valid-input spaces, fix a deterministic U at each n, and take measurable HPM tie rules. These natural conventions make p, support, all classes, and all displayed random quantities measurable; they require no selection of an unidentified beta from the data. The chosen beta is already part of kappa. For section 2 consequence 3, use the usual extension pi(T)=0 for infeasible T, as expressly stated in MIS1 L1 at line 68. For H(a), use the zero-padding convention imported from POST2 Claim D at lines 109-110.

2. **Lemma R'.** (a) is true: `s_y(a1+cy)=|c|s_y(y)`, so standardised y changes by sign(c), and both quadratic score arguments are unchanged (`W3-POST/STATEMENT.md:20-28`). Constant design columns do not create a response-dependent dropping rule; x is already the retained design. A constant response is explicitly outside the deterministic domain and has probability zero under GR^c with v>0. (a') is true for the stated c>0; posterior variance maps by c^2, coefficient mean by c, and coefficient covariance by c^2. It could also be extended to c<0 with the signed coefficient map, but that is not required. (a'') correctly traces the target: standardisation at `v3_preprocess.R:123-127`, `pre$y` passed at `v3_fit.R:118`, context forwarding at `v3_setup.R:7-10`, fixed rate and quadratic inputs at `cpp/v3_score.h:14,55-60,217-223`. Raw scale metadata occurs only at `v3_preprocess.R:126-127`; no other core file consumes those fields. Budgets depend on n,p (`v3_preprocess.R:19-32`, `v3_fit.R:104-118`). The pins are accurate; the numerical extension is finding 2. (b) is true for both posterior laws: Cy^r=U[mu_c+sqrt(v)W] has the specified law, and division by sqrt(v), followed by (a)/(a'), yields the comparison experiment. Arbitrary dependence of the intercept disappears pointwise. Consequently the law of pi is identical for every GR^c law at the same kappa, as asserted at MISR lines 52-53.

3. **Hypothesis translation.** mu and theta divide by sqrt(v); support, p, K, j_0, m-sharp, all Gram matrices and smoothers stay fixed. Thus E^sigma becomes E with variance one and energy M; F_0, OS, F_lambda stay unchanged; L_S divides by v; BM^sigma becomes BM(c) at variance one (`MISR:36-49`; `POST2/STATEMENT.md:25,29-48,85-89,128-130`). SZ carries p>=2, A>=1, fixed size slice s, and s<=K. All are imposed at the actual n. No additional fixed lower/upper bound on v is needed. See finding 4 for the onset of a sufficient bridge to OS.

4. **MIS-R (i).** Follows exactly from R'(b) and Claim C cases iii/ii (`POST2/STATEMENT.md:85-94`). n_0,C are evaluated at unit variance; taking the supremum over kappa does not introduce a dependence on v or beta. Consequence 1 follows from the deterministic missing/wrong-coordinate mass bound. HPM/MPM error implies delta>=1/2; threshold-q error implies delta>=min(q,1-q), including the possible boundary ties. Markov gives the factors in consequence 2. Proxy/cancellation claims in consequence 3 follow from beta=beta_C+gamma and these deterministic bounds, provided the configuration is in the stated class; infeasible full-support intersections receive zero mass.

5. **MIS-R (i').** The expectation upgrade is derivable; it is not an inference from O_P alone. Lemma C2 supplies expected tail O(n^{-1/2}) using E,F_0 only (`C_expectation.md:31-42`). For (a), the assembly in `A_occam.md:85-86` bounds the remaining mass on the good event by `exp(rho_n) Upsilon'_n`; (C5')(b) bounds its expectation by OS (`:64-75`), and the complement has probability <=4/n (`:53`). For (b), restrict E2 to U_j; Lemma E1(b) applies to each restricted bounded-excess underfit using only the coordinate margin (`E_visibility.md:26-54,67-68`). The same counting/union comparison as E2 gives an expectation bounded by a constant times `n^{-1/2} + n^{-2}(1+C_OS r_n) + C_OS r_n + 5/n`, by (C5')(b) and both OS inequalities (`:61-66`; `C_expectation.md:49-54`). The uniform thresholds depend only on the listed constants. A union bound over at most s strong coordinates gives the stated s factor. For (c), F_lambda gives `Delta_S >= (n-1)phi_0^2 theta_j^2`, and L_S>=Delta_S, so c''>=cn/(n-1) is precisely sufficient (`E_visibility.md:76-77`). No extra global margin or floor away from S_0 is required for (a),(b).

6. **MIS-R (ii).** Independence and the standard Gaussian law of W_n are sufficient. Given the Borel conventions in answer 1, delta is a measurable function of (kappa_n,W_n); integrating over the fixed Gaussian measure gives a version of the conditional expectation. No regular conditional law of the arbitrary intercept is needed. A random p poses no problem on the countable disjoint union, and support is measurable coordinatewise. Validity of inputs and v>0 hold because kappa_n is a configuration. Part (b) uses finitely many fixed size slices, hence the maxima work. For (c), for every eta and large n, `P(delta_n>Lr_n) <= C_eta/L + P((A_n^eta)^c)`. Taking limsup, then L large and eta small, proves O_P(r_n); replacing Lr_n by 1/2 gives selection consistency. Only the limsup bad-event condition is needed, and no expectation O(r_n) follows from it alone (none is claimed).

7. **Corollary G.** (a) holds at each n, including sigma^2=0, as long as sigma_C^2>0. Gaussian conditioning in `L2_gaussian.md:40-45` and removal of its constant intercept give the required contrasts; no across-n fixed-parameter argument is used. (b) works with variable sigma_C^2 by normalising W beta^C by sigma_C. The SD event is unchanged (`L2_gaussian.md:91-93`); the sparse eigenvalue argument is unchanged (`:94-100`); the energy bound becomes `||W beta^C||^2 <= 2N M_0 sigma_C^2` (`:101-102`); the sample BM ratio is at least half the stated population SNR times phi_0^2 (`:103`). Take, for example, phi_0^2=c_pop/(8C_0), g_0=1, M=2M_0+1, then the fixed F_w-to-OS bridge gives a common C_OS after its onset. For any fixed c>c_0 the divergent population SNR eventually ensures BM^sigma(c), with an onset allowed to depend on that sequence. The original probability bound `2n^{-2}+2k_n n^{-2}+n^{-1}` (`:105`), with k_n=O(log n), is O(1/n)=o(r_n), enough for the expectation conclusion. If 'growing p' means p->infinity, p>=2 holds eventually; the corollary should explicitly retain p>=2 if it is also intended to cover arbitrary bounded-p sequences through the same class argument. Sigma_C^2>0 is inherited from (a) and is necessary for the displayed ratios. These are interpretation conventions, not evidence of a failed Gaussian argument.

8. **Corollary H.** The contraction inequalities are correctly transported from POST2 D (`STATEMENT.md:101-119`, `D_hellinger.md:32-79`). The no-margin constant is C_H(s,A,1) at unit variance, hence depends only on s,A; mean-squared thresholds multiply by v_n and coefficient thresholds by sqrt(v_n). The relative-variance and Hellinger quantities are invariant. The coefficient vector is zero-padded. For the no-margin Hellinger consequence choose M_n=sqrt(log n) in the variance bound; this gives the displayed `1+C_H/4` constant. Arbitrary v_n approaching zero or infinity does not change the argument. The SL analogy is limited to the explicitly described in-sample, in-probability contraction around the comparison law; the interpretation of that law is finding 3, and the statement does not establish SL's integrated or almost-sure conclusion.

For the principal theorem: p=2 and s=K cause no assembly failure (empty superset sums are zero, while OS still includes its defined comparison unions); rank deficiency away from S_0 is allowed by rank-based events and ridge losses; F_0 excludes rank deficiency on S_0 itself; multiple beta representations are handled as chosen configurations, with U ruling out incompatible well-separated supports at the stated threshold; v->0 or infinity is removed exactly by scaling; constant responses have probability zero under GR^c; and negative c in R'(a) only flips the standardised response.
