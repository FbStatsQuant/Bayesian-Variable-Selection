## W3-POST2:C — Proposition ER (E δ_n ≤ C r_n)

**Summary.** The upper rate √(log n/n) cannot be improved, but the claim as packaged can be strengthened cheaply. A matching lower bound of order r_n looks provable in about half a page, which would close the "between n^{−1/2} and √(log n/n)" gap. Several hypotheses and one by-product are also looser than the proof needs. The lower-bound argument below is my own sketch from the ticket's formulas; I have not seen [T03R]/[P3], so one step is flagged for checking.

### (a) Hypotheses

- **(OS), unweighted part: necessary.** A2 is deterministic, so E δ_n = O(r_n) forces 𝒪⁰_n = O(r_n) (STATEMENT.md:58-60).
- **(OS), the √(log n) weight on generic j = 1 terms: not an artefact for this claim.** For a null column with d_J = 1 and determinant ratio ≍ n, the sketch in (b) shows the expected mass really carries √(log n). So ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:13 ("the weights … are proof envelopes") is too pessimistic for the expectation statement.
  - What is slack is the truncation level. The proof truncates at τ_j = C_A j log n with C_A = 8 + 3A (A_occam.md:71-74). The natural level is where the single-model ratio reaches 1, about 2 log(1/c_J) with c_J = prior ratio × (D_{S₀}/D_{S_J})^{1/2}.
  - This matters for near-collinear J with d_J = 1 but determinant ratio O(1): the true weight is √(log p), not √(log n).
  - It is the already-recorded A+ (REPORT.md:184), and it only changes constants or special designs.
- **(F₀) in the size tail: stronger than needed, easy to weaken.** Lemma C1 uses (F₀) only to get a deterministic ρ ≤ ρ̄ in Q^c_{S₀} ≤ σ*²E′ + ρ (C_expectation.md:21-22). That is the exact-reference cost (‖μ‖² + ‖θ*‖²)/n. So C1, C2 and the by-product at STATEMENT.md:94 need only ‖θ*‖² ≤ nM′, which is the [L1a′] condition already quoted at B_tail_approx.md:45. This allows λ_min(R_{S₀}) as small as order 1/n.
- **(F₀) in the superset and underfit parts: not removable cheaply.** It enters through η_n in (C4′) (A_occam.md:59-60) and R_n in E1 (E_visibility.md:39-42). The naive replacement costs ‖P_{S₀}e‖² in the exponent, which is O_P(1) but has no finite exponential moment at the required level, so the expectation bound breaks. N8 (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:32) suggests the condition is not necessary. I rate this medium-hard, in line with REPORT.md:186.
- **Margin constants c > c₀ (cases i–ii) and c > c₁ (case iii): artefacts, partly removable by retuning.** Three slack points in E1:
  - The target ℓ(S)/ℓ(S′) ≤ n^{−2} (E_visibility.md:27, 49-54) is more than Claim C needs; κ̄_n = O(r_n) suffices at C_expectation.md:51-53. So "+2" can become "+½".
  - log(1+u) ≥ (log 2)·min(u,1) (E_visibility.md:48) can be replaced by log(1+u) ≥ u/(1+ε) on u ≤ ε, since D/B′ = O(log n/n) in the binding case. That turns 2/log 2 into 2 + ε.
  - C_x bounds s₀ + log(j₀+1) + log(2n) by multiples of log n (E_visibility.md:34); asymptotically C_x = Aj₀ + 1 + ε. The prefactor n^{s₀(A+1)} can be n^{|S₀∖S|(A+½)} (E_visibility.md:37-38).
  - By my arithmetic this takes c₁ from about 161 to about 116 at s₀ = A = 1; R03b claims about 54. The true threshold (about 2A + 2 per the ticket's own obstruction, ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:28) needs new work.
- **Gaussian noise (G): probably close to necessary for this rate in expectation.** The bound rests on E[e^{χ²_d/2}1{χ²_d ≤ τ}], which is exactly at the edge of integrability. A noise law whose projections have slightly heavier moderate-deviation tails at level √(log n) would inflate E δ_n by a power of n. I am unsure whether a delocalised-design, Cramér-type extension works; it is outside the ticket.
- **s₀ fixed, p ≤ n^A: scope restrictions relative to the literature.** Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart, Narisetty–He and Yang–Wainwright–Jordan all allow growing s₀ and log p = o(n), to the best of my recollection. Here 2^{s₀}, (2p)^{s₀}(n+1)^{s₀/2}, j₀ = ⌈(2A+1)s₀⌉+1 and K_{s₀,·} are all exponential in s₀ or tied to A. Extending either is new work.
- **Per-n, uniform-over-designs form.** This is already the right form; nothing to weaken.

### (b) Conclusion

**The upper rate is sharp; the stated lower bound is not.** STATEMENT.md:92-93 and C_expectation.md:60-62 leave a √(log n) gap, and REPORT.md:190 lists it as open. I believe the √(log n) is genuine.

- For d = 1 the truncated moment is exact: E[e^{χ²_1/2}1{χ²_1 ≤ τ}] = √(2τ/π).
- For a single null j, write R_j = π(S₀∪{j})/π(S₀). A2 discards the factor ((2b₀+Q_{S₀})/(2b₀+Q_S))^ν ≥ 1 (A_occam.md:93-94). That factor is ≈ e^{χ²_j/2}.
- Then E[R/(1+R)] with R = a·e^{χ²_1/2} and a ≍ n^{−1/2} is of order a·√(log(1/a)), both from above and below. This holds already at fixed p.

So I expect E δ_n ≍ r_n on LB's domain for every design in cases (i)–(ii), where (F_λ) supplies the floor on S₀∪{j}. Case (iii) carries no floor away from S₀, so there the lower bound holds only for designs having such a floor on a non-negligible share of null columns; that is enough to show the uniform rate cannot be improved there either.

**Expectation versus in-probability.** At fixed p, e^{χ²/2} is tight, so δ_n is plausibly O_P(n^{−1/2}) while E δ_n ≍ √(log n/n). The expectation is driven by the tail event χ²_j ≈ log n. So Claim C is not a lossy version of a better in-probability rate; it is the correct order for the mean.

**Corollaries are lossy.** P(HPM ≠ S₀) ≤ 2Eδ_n and P(MPM ≠ S₀) ≤ 2Eδ_n (STATEMENT.md:90-91) go through Markov at level ½.

- The one-superset heuristic gives P(R_j ≥ 1) ≍ n^{−1/2}(log n)^{−1/2}, so the Markov route loses about a factor log n.
- A direct bound on P(δ_n ≥ ½) (truncate each term at level 1, union bound on the exceedances, second moment on the rest) recovers this at fixed p or for weakly dependent null columns.
- For p = n^A with arbitrary dependence among the χ²_J I do not know whether the improvement holds.

**By-product C2.** The additive (8 + 24M/σ*²)/(n−1) at C_expectation.md:34 comes from a Chebyshev-type bound on the scale event 𝓑_n. If 𝓑_n is the two-sided event on s_y², as B_tail_approx.md:19 suggests, Gaussian concentration should make this term exponentially small. That matters for deep cutoffs m where R_m√(D_{S₀})√n ≪ 1/n.

**Literature context.** The n^{−1/2} floor is a property of the coded target (unit-information slab, O(1) prior odds for one extra variable), and A2 shows it deterministically.

- Yang–Wainwright–Jordan (g ≍ p^{2α}, p^{−κ} size prior) and Narisetty–He (diffusing slab) get faster superset decay because the prior differs.
- Johnson–Rossell non-local priors get faster decay by construction, for p ≤ n.
- None of these gives, to my knowledge, a two-sided expectation rate. I am not certain of the exact form of the expectation statements in Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart.
- A two-sided E δ_n ≍ r_n would therefore be a distinctive result.

### (c) Cheapest route to a stronger version

1. **Matching lower bound (easy, about half a page).**
   - For null j keep the Q-factor: R_j ≥ c_j·exp{νD_j/(2b+Q^c_{S₀})}, with c_j = [w(s₀+1)/w(s₀)](D_{S₀}/D_{S₀∪{j}})^{1/2} and D_j the Q-gain.
   - On 𝒢^sup_n: 2b + Q^c_{S₀} ≤ Nσ*²(1+δ″_n) (E_visibility.md:39-42) and ν ≥ n/2.
   - Under a floor on S₀∪{j}: D_j ≥ σ*²χ²_j − C. This is the reverse direction of (B3) as used at A_occam.md:59-60; ridge shrinkage is O(1/(nφ₀²)) relative and ‖y_c‖² ≤ 4n(M+σ*²). **This is the one step to check against [T03R].**
   - Deterministically δ_n ≥ ΣR_j/(1+ΣR_j) ≥ ½ Σ_j R̲_j·1{R̲_j ≤ 1/(p−s₀)}, where R̲_j is the lower bound above. No independence across j is needed.
   - Use D_{S₀∪{j}}/D_{S₀} ≤ n+1 and LB's prior-multiplicity bound, then the exact moment √(2τ/π) with τ = log n + O(1). The factor e^{−δ″_nτ/2} → 1, and removing 𝒢^sup_n costs at most 4/n.
   - Result: E δ_n ≥ c·r_n − 4/n.
2. **(F₀) → ‖θ*‖² ≤ nM′ in C1/C2 and the by-product:** a one-line change at C_expectation.md:21-22.
3. **Retune c₁** as in (a): easy.
4. **Exponential bound for P(𝓑_nᶜ) in C2:** easy, if 𝓑_n is as assumed.
5. **Direct P(HPM ≠ S₀) bound:** easy at fixed p; unclear for dependent growing p.
6. **Hard items:** dropping (F₀) in the superset and underfit parts; the sharp margin constant; growing s₀; log p = o(n); non-Gaussian noise.
