## W2-APP:CT — Lemma CT (L2_CT.md:9-13, proof :15-28)

I checked every step of the proof and found no problem.

- **Constant (i), :15-18.** The series sums as stated: S(t) = 2K[Σ_{d≥1}e^{−(a−t)d} − Σ_{d≥1}e^{−ad}] = 2K[1/(e^{a−t}−1) − 1/(e^a−1)] for 0 ≤ t < a. Solving S(t) = α/2 gives e^{a−t} = 1+u with 1/u = α/(4K) + 1/(e^a−1), which is t = t*. Since 1/u > 1/(e^a−1), u < e^a−1 and t* > 0; u > 0 gives t* < a. So S(t*) = α/2 exactly and is finite.
- **Conjugation (ii), :19-21.** (DAD⁻¹)_ij = A_ij e^{t(x_i−x_j)} and the diagonal of E is zero. The inequality |e^s − 1| ≤ e^{|s|} − 1 holds for s < 0 because e^{|s|} + e^{−|s|} ≥ 2. With |t(x_i−x_j)| = t*d_ij the bound on |E_ij| holds for both signs of t.
- **Schur test (iii), :22-24.** Injectivity into ℤ gives at most two j at each distance d ≥ 1 (x_j = x_i ± d). Row sums are therefore ≤ Σ_{d≥1} 2K e^{−ad}(e^{t*d} − 1) = S(t*) = α/2, and column sums likewise since the entry bound is symmetric. ‖E‖₂ ≤ √(‖E‖₁‖E‖_∞) is valid for the non-symmetric E.
- **Inverse (iv), :25-28.** ‖A⁻¹‖₂ ≤ 1/α by symmetry and λ_min ≥ α. The Neumann series gives ‖(I + A⁻¹E)⁻¹‖ ≤ 2, so ‖A_t⁻¹‖ ≤ 2/α. A_t⁻¹ = DA⁻¹D⁻¹, and each entry is bounded by the spectral norm. Choosing the sign of t per pair (i, j) is legitimate because the norm bound was proved for both t = ±t*. The case i = j is covered trivially.
- **Uniformity.** β, |I| and the particular positions are never used, as Remark (1)-(2) says.

Sanity check on AR(1) with ρ = ½ (K = 1, a = log 2, α = 1/3): u = 12/13, t* = log(26/25) > 0, and the bound 6e^{−t*d} dominates the true tridiagonal precision.

## W2-APP:CD — Lemma CD (L2_CT.md:41-50, premises :36-39, proof :52-59)

I checked each part against the LOC definition and against Lemma CT's hypotheses, and found no problem.

- **Precision bound, :52-55.** Θ has spectrum in [1/C, 1/c].
  - Under (LOC-Σ), CT applies to Σ: symmetric, λ_min ≥ c, off-diagonal decay (K, a), positions π injective into ℤ. It gives |Θ_jk| ≤ (2/c)e^{−t₁d} with t₁ = t*(c, K, a). This is the second half of (c), with constants as stated.
  - Under (LOC-Θ), the off-diagonal bound is the hypothesis, so K₁ = K and t₁ = a. CT applied to Θ with α = 1/C gives |Σ_jk| ≤ 2C e^{−t*(1/C,K,a)d}. This is the first half of (c), with constants as stated.
- **(a), :55-57.** Cov(W_V | W_U) = Σ_VV − Σ_VUΣ_UU⁻¹Σ_UV = (Θ_VV)⁻¹ for V = [p]∖U is the standard block-inverse identity, valid since Σ ≻ 0. Θ_VV is a principal submatrix, so it is symmetric with λ_min ≥ 1/C. Its off-diagonal entries inherit |Θ_jk| ≤ K₁e^{−t₁d(j,k)} on the original, uncompressed positions π|_V, which remain injective. CT's hypotheses therefore hold with (α, K, a) = (1/C, K₁, t₁), K₁ > 0, t₁ > 0, giving |((Θ_VV)⁻¹)_jk| ≤ 2C e^{−t₂d(j,k)}. This matches K₂ = 2C and t₂ = t*(1/C, K₁, t₁).
- **Quantifiers in (a).** The bound holds for every U (any size, including ∅ and V = {j, k}), because CT is uniform in |I| and in the positions.
- **(b), :57-59.** The spectrum of (Θ_VV)⁻¹ lies in [c, C], so its diagonal entries are ≥ c. Var(W_j | W_U) ≤ Var(W_j) = 1 by unit diagonal. Principal submatrices give the spectrum claim for any V′ ⊆ V.
- **Dependence of constants, :50.** (K₂, t₂) are explicit functions of (c, C, K, a), given which of the two alternatives is used, and do not involve p, U, j, k or π. A sequence that switches between (LOC-Σ) and (LOC-Θ) is harmless: there are only two candidate values of t₂ and one can take the smaller.
