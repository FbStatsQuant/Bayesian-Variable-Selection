## W3-MISR:Cor-G(b) — Gaussian rows, growing p (STATEMENT.md:171-174; proof G_corollaries.md:36-65)

The improvement arguments below are my own heuristic calculations unless a file line is cited; none is checked against the upstream proofs.

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **Beta-min "→ ∞" is stronger than the proof uses (STATEMENT.md:173).** Step 4 (G_corollaries.md:53-55) only needs ½φ₀²ρ_n^{BM} ≥ c for one fixed c > c₀(s, A). So liminf ρ_n^{BM} > 2c₀(s, A)/φ₀² = 16·C₀·c₀(s, A)/c_pop suffices.
  - The order σ²_𝒞 log n/n cannot be lowered for this method. The unit-information slab gives an Occam factor n^{-1/2}, so a coordinate with nΣ_jjβ_j²/σ²_𝒞 below a small multiple of log n is dropped; N2 (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:20) shows this. For p polynomial in n it also matches the known necessary order σ² log(p − s)/n for exact support recovery (Wainwright 2009).
  - Only the divergence and the constant are slack; c₀ is a proof artefact (STATEMENT.md:266).

- **The covariance conditions are not scale-free, although the method is (STATEMENT.md:171-172).** The pair "λ_min(Σ_TT) ≥ c_pop, max_j Σ_jj ≤ C₀" enters only through the ratio φ₀² = c_pop/(8C₀) (G_corollaries.md:40, 47-48). The posterior is invariant to column rescaling, and the energy and beta-min conditions are already written scale-free.
  - The weaker condition λ_min(Corr(Σ)_TT) ≥ c_corr suffices. Apply step 2 to the columns rescaled by Σ_jj^{-1/2}; step 1 is already a ratio statement.
  - It is strictly weaker, since λ_min(Corr_TT) ≥ c_pop/C₀, and it admits column variances that vary arbitrarily with n and j. That fits the claim's "everything may depend on n".

- **Eigenvalue floor over all T with |T| ≤ k_n.** Step 2 uses only T = S₀ ∪ J (G_corollaries.md:48-49). Restricting to T ⊇ supp β^𝒞 is a small gain: by interlacing it only frees sets of size between max(j₀, m♯_n)+1 and k_n that miss part of S₀.
  - The floor is a sufficient route to (OS) through (F_w; 1) (G_corollaries.md:41), not a necessary one. The statement says "sufficient", so this is acceptable.

- **Joint Gaussianity of the rows.**
  - G.1 (G_corollaries.md:26-31) needs only that ξ + ε is N(0, σ²_𝒞 I) and independent of X^r_𝒞. That is, the omitted part is linear in the candidates plus an independent Gaussian remainder.
  - The candidate rows themselves are used only for concentration in steps 1–3 (G_corollaries.md:43-52), which have standard sub-Gaussian analogues.
  - Gaussian candidate rows are therefore not needed. This is moderate work: the [L2] steps must be redone. It is an "example" corollary, so low priority.

- **Bounded population signal-to-noise, β^𝒞ᵀΣβ^𝒞 ≤ M₀σ²_𝒞.** Some energy control is needed (N5, ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:17), but a bounded constant is probably not.
  - It excludes near-noiseless regimes (σ²_𝒞 → 0 with fixed signal), which sits oddly with "σ² ≥ 0, any n-dependence".
  - M enters the cited bounds as (8 + 24M)/(n − 1) and ρ_n = 8(M + 1)/(Nφ₀²) (M_misr.md:61, 65). Tracking M there suggests slowly growing M_n is admissible.
  - If I recall correctly, Yang–Wainwright–Jordan's g-prior condition is ‖Xβ*‖² ≤ gσ² log p. With g ↔ n that corresponds to M ≍ log n.
  - This is upstream work in W3-POST2, so hard from this ticket.

- **s fixed, p ≤ n^A.** These are inherited. The design steps here would tolerate k_n log p = o(n). This is the main gap to Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart and Narisetty–He (growing s₀, log p = o(n)), and it is hard.
  - "s fixed" can trivially become "s_n ≤ s̄" by taking maxima of constants as in MIS-R (ii)(b) (STATEMENT.md:155-156).

**(b) Conclusion**

- **E δ_n = O(r_n): I believe the order is sharp for this prior, including the √log n.**
  - Heuristic: a one-variable superset has posterior odds ≈ ((s+1)/(2p))·n^{-1/2}·e^{χ²/2}. Its truncated mean over p − s nulls is of order √(log(np²)/n).
  - The file records only the lower bound (1 + √(n+1))^{-1} (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:27), so a √log n gap is documented. I think it closes on the lower-bound side, not the upper.
  - The rate belongs to the method (unit-information slab, prior ratio ≍ 1/p). Faster superset decay would need a different prior, for example the non-local priors of Johnson–Rossell.

- **The conclusion is stated as a sequence-dependent O(·), but the proof gives more.** G_corollaries.md:57-61 yields E δ_n ≤ C r_n + 2(1 + k_n)n^{-2} + n^{-1} for n ≥ n*. With a constant-level beta-min, this is uniform over the whole population class (m, Σ, β, σ²).
  - MPM, ξ_q and PIP rates follow from MIS-R (ii)(a) (STATEMENT.md:151-153) and are not harvested.

- **P(HPM ≠ supp β^𝒞) = O(r_n) is probably loose by a factor of about log n.**
  - It comes from Markov, P(δ ≥ ½) ≤ 2Eδ (M_misr.md:40, 43).
  - A direct union bound over one-variable supersets gives P(χ²₁ > log n + 2 log(2p/(s+1)))·p ≍ (n log n)^{-1/2}. Larger supersets contribute n^{-j/2}, and the underfit and bad-event terms are O(1/n).
  - This needs tail versions, not expectation versions, of the upstream superset bounds. It is new but routine work.

**(c) Cheapest route**

Restate with three changes, all inside G.2 with no new lemma:
1. Beta-min as liminf ρ_n^{BM} > c_BM(s, A, c_corr).
2. A correlation-matrix eigenvalue floor on T ⊇ supp β^𝒞.
3. The explicit uniform bound of G_corollaries.md:57-61.

Next, and harder: the (n log n)^{-1/2} HPM rate by a direct tail argument, and a growing M₀ by tracking constants upstream.

## W3-MISR:Cor-H — in-sample Hellinger contraction (STATEMENT.md:178-197; proof G_corollaries.md:67-93)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **Clause (a) asks for more than its proof uses (STATEMENT.md:184).** It requires the margin class 𝒦_n with c > c₁, or 𝒦^λ_n with c > c₀, on top of 𝒦⁰_n.
  - The proof (G_corollaries.md:83-84) uses the margin only to get δ_n → 0 in probability. It then invokes W3-POST2 Claim D(a) under "R1, G, (E), (F₀)".
  - So (a) holds for any array with (SZ), (E^σ), (F₀) and δ_n →_P 0. Neither the artefact constants c₁, c₀ nor (OS) are needed as such.
  - This covers selection consistency obtained by any other route, for example Remark P's range. It rests on the file's description of D(a)'s hypotheses; I cannot see D itself.

- **(F₀)** is needed for the θ-clause, since it is what identifies θ on S₀ from the mean.

- **(E^σ; M) with bounded M** is probably weakenable for this corollary. The ridge bias in the fitted mean is about ‖μ‖²/n² ≤ Mv/n, and the relative variance inflation is O(M/n), against thresholds v log n and n^{-1/2}. The binding use of M is in the model-space inputs, so this is upstream and hard.

- **GR^c cannot be relaxed to (PR) here.** The parameter posterior depends on s_y (R_reduction.md:89-90). That restriction is genuine.

- **Fixed designs, sequences.** Both are relaxable; see (b).

**(b) Conclusion**

- **Rate in clause (a) is sharp.** M_n√(v/n) for θ, M_n n^{-1/2} for v′/v and for h̄_n are parametric. The conditional posterior given S₀ is an explicit Student-type law with exactly this spread, so M_n → ∞ cannot become a constant with posterior mass → 0.

- **Rate in clause (b) is sharp in order for this method.** A coordinate with nθ_j²/v equal to a small multiple of log n is excluded with posterior probability → 1. That leaves ‖μ̂ − μ‖² ≍ v log n; it is the BIC-type log n risk inflation, present even at fixed p.
  - For p = n^a it also agrees with the minimax order s log(p/s).
  - The constant C_H(s, A) is opaque. If it is linear in s(1 + A), the bound could be written as s log p, which is the form in Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart. I cannot tell from the files.
  - The "(1 + C_H/4)" in the Hellinger display (STATEMENT.md:183) is constant slack only.

- **Divergence.** The primary displays control ‖μ̂ − μ‖²/v and v′/v. The same displays therefore give in-sample average Kullback–Leibler (and Rényi) bounds at the same rates. Hellinger is the weakest reading of what is proved.

- **Mode of convergence: weaker than the rest of the ticket, and partly for free.**
  1. **Uniformity.** The statement holds along every sequence in the class and D's constants depend only on (s, A). So sup_{κ∈𝒦⁰_n} P_κ(Π(B_n) > ε) → 0 follows by a subsequence argument. It is not stated.
  2. **Random designs.** With (1), the freezing argument of M_misr.md:143-149 gives the conditional version, with P(𝒜_nᶜ) added. That makes Cor-H available in the Corollary G setting, where only its KL reading is currently discussed (STATEMENT.md:191-195).
  3. **Expectation with a rate** (the file's own BT1-A2, ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:34-36). The model-space half is already MIS-R (i′)(d): E π(Ω∖𝒩_n) ≤ C′r_n (STATEMENT.md:136-138).
     - What remains is the within-model conjugate tail for S ∈ 𝒩_n. On the event {‖P_Sε‖² ≤ C log n for all |S| < s + j₀}, a union over at most p^{s+j₀} models with C = C(s, A), each such model has bias < c·v·log n and the posterior tail is explicit.
     - A bound sup_κ E_κ Π(‖μ̂ − μ‖² > C_H′v log n) ≤ C r_n therefore looks closer than "redo D in expectation" suggests. It is moderate work.
  4. **Oracle form.** (b) is the worst case of a bound of the type ‖μ̂ − μ‖² ≲ M_n v + max{L_S : S ∈ 𝒩_n with mass}, which would interpolate between (a) and (b). Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart have, I believe, an oracle-type contraction result of this kind. This is hard.
  5. **Bernstein–von Mises for (a).** Given selection and exact conjugacy, total-variation convergence of the θ-posterior to N(θ̂, v(X_{S₀}ᵀX_{S₀})⁻¹) looks within reach. The ridge bias relative to the spread is of order √(M/n). This is moderate work.
  6. **Out-of-sample or almost-sure statements, as in Song–Liang Theorem 4.** Out-of-sample needs intercept handling and a sparse Σ̂-to-Σ transfer (BT1-B2). Almost-sure is out of reach with bad-event probabilities of order 1/n. Both are hard.
  7. **Growing s, p > n^A.** Inherited and hard.

**(c) Cheapest route**

Three restatements with no new mathematics:
1. Clause (a) under "(SZ), (E^σ), (F₀) and δ_n →_P 0".
2. Uniform-over-class and conditional random-design forms.
3. State the KL consequence beside Hellinger.
