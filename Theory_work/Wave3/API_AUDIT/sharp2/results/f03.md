## 1. W3-RM2:RM2-F (STATEMENT.md:206-214)

### (a) Hypotheses

**(W) is stated more strongly than the proof needs.** `L2_width.md:77-86` uses (W) only to get the event {Φ⁰_n(ε/σ) ≤ −r} for some r with r·√log(n+1) ≥ 4κ_E+2. On that event |Z| ≤ a − r gives Z² ≤ a² − ar ≤ c_n(|T|).
- So the quantile form suffices: P_g(Φ⁰_n(g) ≤ −(4κ_E+2)/√log(n+1)) → 1. It is still a deterministic condition on (X, S₀, Q).
- The converse `L2_width.md:100-109` equally needs only P_g(Φ⁰_n ≥ C/√log n) → 1.
- Whole-lattice no-addition is therefore characterised up to a window of width O(1/√log n) in Φ⁰_n. The mean-gap dichotomy "→ −∞ / → +∞" leaves an O(1) window.
- Much of the "bounded gap undetermined" item (O5, SHARPNESS.md:55-56) is an artefact of stating the condition through the mean plus Lipschitz concentration. Where the lattice maximum superconcentrates (orthogonal-type outside columns, fluctuations O(1/√log p)), a bounded negative gap already gives no-addition.

**(BM-W) (STATEMENT.md:137) asks a diverging ratio; the proof needs an additive margin.** `L2_width.md:211-217` needs only √λ_n ≥ d_n + C(A, M, σ)·√log n, with the constant coming from C_R and (A+1) log n.
- When d_n² ≫ log n this reads λ_n ≥ (1+o(1))·d_n², not λ_n/d_n² → ∞.
- A weighted form is also available at the same step: compare |Z_{A,i}| with θ_i‖(I−P_A)x_i‖/σ pair by pair, instead of the uniform floor (n−1)φ₀².
- Some floor at the scale d_n looks route-necessary for whole-lattice truth removal: by (4.1), a direction with Z_{A,i} ≈ −√λ cancels the square. This is heuristic and says nothing about the conclusion.

**(V) with M_n → ∞ (STATEMENT.md:130-131) is stronger than used.** `L1_sandF_b.md:126-130` needs only ½M_n log n − σ²z_n² > 2C_R(log 2p + ½ log(n+1) + 2κ_E). A fixed large constant M* (depending on A, M, σ²) suffices. This is the scheduled rider T4.

**(FL-Q) with fixed φ₀ is stronger than used.**
- The toolkit needs only |A|/((n−1)φ²) bounded (`L0_toolkit_b.md:98-110`, and n₀ at :45), i.e. φ_n² ≳ q/n.
- In truth removal the floor enters only through the product λ_n (`L2_width.md:211`).
- A decaying floor on Q ∪ {l} is therefore admissible, with (BM-W) read on the product. I did not re-run every constant.

**b < β(η, A) is sharp for the sandwich under a uniform leak, but the uniform quantifier is not needed.**
- β(η, A) is exactly the first-moment threshold of the junk-exclusion step: p·exp(−½(√((1+2A−2b) log n) − √(η log n))²) → 0 ⇔ b < β(η, A). So the "counting form" does not decouple b and η when all p outside columns leak at level η.
- What is improvable is the quantifier. `L1_sandF_b.md:133-148` takes a max over all outside columns. A leak-profile sum Σ_{l∉Q} exp(−½(√t_n − max_U δ_{U,l}/τ_U)₊²) → 0 gives a strictly larger region when only n^c ≪ p columns leak. This is O2, and Form R's (4.3) already argues this way.
- A can be replaced by log p/log n at `L1_sandF_b.md:139-145`. This is trivial.

**b < ½ at η = 0.**
- It is the level where the fixed prior's penalty log(p/k) + ½ log n meets the best of p noise gains.
- For the sandwich in a deterministic Q it looks necessary whenever the start mode truly has about n^b members (expected junk count k/√n). This is heuristic only, as the files say.
- Necessity for the conclusion is not supported: probe B1 held in 48/48 runs for b up to 0.75.

**Inherited restrictions.**
- s₀ fixed, p ≤ n^A and BM-∞ come from Theorem 1.
- The ticket's own lemmas use s₀ only through k₀ = |Q| + 16s₀ and the lattice definition, so the restriction is not created here.
- The literature is broader on these axes. Yang–Wainwright–Jordan, Narisetty–He and Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart allow growing s₀ and log p = o(n), with beta-min at a constant multiple of σ²log p/n rather than "≫ log n/n".
- Those results use p-dependent or tunable priors, and none covers a coded search with a polynomial set of visible nulls. No dominance either way; I am not certain of their exact constants.

### (b) Conclusion

- **Mode.** "One event, every admissible run" is as strong as one can ask.
- **Rate.** None is claimed, although `L3_assemblyF.md:36-38` already gives an explicit bound for the search event. Its 1/log n term is removable (`L1_sandF_b.md:154-156`).
- **Exponent.** exp(−s²/8) at `L2_width.md:79` improves to exp(−(s − O(1/√log n))²/2), by cutting at −r_n instead of −s/2. c* improves by a factor of about 4.
- **Reported-model rate.** This needs a tail bound for π(S₀) from Theorem 1, which is not in the files.
- **Headline barriers.** No lattice condition, and b ≥ ½, both need path-level control of LS0 with cap accounting (O1).

### (c) Cheapest route

One revision can bundle the following; each is a few lines at the cited steps.
1. Quantile form of (W), with the O(1/√log n) dichotomy in (C3).
2. Additive (BM-W).
3. Constant-level (V).
4. The harvested search-event rate with exponent s²/2.
5. Leak-profile form of junk exclusion. This is moderate: a rewrite of `L1_sandF_b.md` Step 2(b) as a count.

Removing (W) or passing b = ½ is hard.

## 2. W3-RM2:RM2-R (STATEMENT.md:216-218)

### (a) Hypotheses

**(L-gain-R) is per-truth (STATEMENT.md:180-181); only the aggregated leak is used.**
- In Lemma 2, s_ly and S(l, V_β | U) are the aggregates Σ_j β_j Cov(W_l, W_j | ·) (`L4_sandR_b.md:112-120`).
- In Lemma 3 the mean shift enters h only through mᵀv, whose population value is the same aggregate (`L4_sandR_b.md:150-160, 169-171`).
- It appears to suffice that |Cov(W_l, Σ_kθ*_kW_k | W_U)| = o(√(log n/n)) and each individual covariance is o(1). The second part gives ‖α‖ → 0, keeps ‖m‖ = o(√n), and makes the Box/standardisation correction o(h_n).
- This matches Form F's (L-gain), which is on δ_{U,l} = uᵀμ and allows cancellation between truths. The bound on mᵀv should be placed on the 𝒢-measurable part of E_cov. I checked the two entry points, not every constant.

**Leak scale o(√(log n/n)) is strictly narrower than Form F's η > 0.**
- `L4_sandR_b.md:173-176` already has the un-linearised tail exp(−(√t − κM_n)₊²/2). It gives a frontier at ϖ_n = κ√(log n/n) immediately, with a crude constant (O3′).
- The sharp analogue of β(η, A) needs the shift expressed as a partial correlation with the response rather than (2s₀/c)√n·ϖ_n.

**(L-loc-R) with r_n → 0.**
- The only use is the sample error r_n·h_n of the cross term (`L4_sandR_b.md:126-133`).
- A small fixed r₀ would cost O(r₀) log n on the thresholds, giving a frontier 15ρ + b + C·r₀ < ½.
- Some localisation is needed by this route: without it the reference count is p^{15}.

**15ρ + b < ½.**
- n^{15ρ} is the union over reference sets (`L4_sandR_b.md:139-141, 194-196`).
- Since the partition may be any X-measurable one, it can pick the 15 block-mates in H_l adversarially. I do not see a cheap way to beat the count in that generality.
- A better exponent probably needs a property of the coded partition. I am unsure whether 15 is improvable.
- It bites only when H_l must be polynomially large. For decaying designs the budget is b < ½.

**Signals.**
- This ticket's lemmas do not need fixed PTV(a₀) or fixed θ_lo.
- `L4_sandR_b.md:85-89, 229-234` work with a_n ≥ C√(log n/n) for a large C.
- `L5_formR.md:24-26` needs only θ_lo² ≫ n^{b−1} + log n/n.
- The binding input for "θ*_min → 0 is a non-claim" is therefore the posterior premise W2-APP:POST, not this route.

**b < ½ for sample floors.**
- `L5_formR.md:20-23` uses (q+1) × the entrywise error. A Wishart operator-norm bound would give floors for every b < 1.
- This is immaterial while junk exclusion and P9a Step 4 (`L5_formR.md:82-85`) cap b at ½.

**(W-R).** The quantile remark of claim 1 applies conditionally on X, at `L5_formR.md:39-40`.

### (b) Conclusion

Same as RM2-F: no rate is claimed, and the headline barriers (b ≥ ½, no lattice condition) are path-level. Compared with the parent, the gains (α > 2 instead of 17/2, no localisation rate) are real.

### (c) Cheapest route

- **Easy:** the aggregated leak, a non-sharp κ-frontier and r₀-frontier (both from inequalities already written), and decaying a_n and θ_lo at lemma level.
- **Moderate:** the sharp Form R frontier.
- **Hard or unclear:** replacing 15ρ, and O1.

## 3. W3-RM2:Ex-Proxy (STATEMENT.md:265-267; L6_examples.md:330-384)

### (a) Hypotheses of the example

**Fixed θ is not needed.**
- The truth-removal directions here are u = (s·e₁ − rΣ_A e_j)/√(s² + tr²), with s = √(1−r²) and t = |A|.
- The maximum of |uᵀg| is at most |g₁| + r√q·‖g_Q‖/s, which is |g₁| + O(rq) with rq = √(C₀ log n)·n^{b−½} → 0.
- So d_n = O(1), and (BM-W) is just BM-∞. The example holds for θ_n with nθ_n²/log n → ∞; (V) then has M_n = θ_n²(n−1)/(2 log n) → ∞.
- For log n ≪ nθ_n² ≪ n^b the parent's (BM-Q) fails. This would be the only witness in the table that (BM-W) is strictly weaker at polynomial |Q|. As written (`L6_examples.md:373`) the example goes through BM-Q and shows no such gain.

**r² = C₀ log n/n is not needed.**
- The (W-loc) identity (`L6_examples.md:375-381`) holds for every r.
- The floor on Q is at least (1−r²)/(2 + r²q): the block singular values are 1−r² and those of [[1, r√m], [0, s]].
- So any r_n with sup r_n² q_n < ∞ and sup r_n < 1 works, e.g. r² ≍ n^{−b}. Then (BM-W) is back at scale q.
- With such proxies they certainly enter the start mode. With r² = C₀ log n/n and small C₀θ²/σ² most proxies need not enter, so the current example witnesses the hypotheses at polynomial |Q| but not necessarily a polynomial-size start mode.

**p = ⌊n/3⌋ is not needed, at least for the hypotheses I can see.**
- If the outside columns lie in span(X_Q)^⊥ ∩ 1^⊥, they need not be mutually orthogonal.
- Then u_{T,l} = x_l/‖x_l‖ for every T, so (W-unif) holds with p directions.
- δ_{U,l} = 0 for every U, so η = 0.
- This allows p up to n^A, provided the outside block satisfies Theorem 1's sparse-eigenvalue clauses. I cannot see those clauses in full, so that part is unverified.

### (b) Conclusion

The range "every b < ½" is the full range of RM2-F at η = 0, so it is sharp relative to the theorem.

### (c) Route

All three generalisations are a paragraph each in `L6_examples.md` E4. The decaying-signal version is the most valuable.

## 4. W3-RM2:Ex-Equi (STATEMENT.md:267-270; L6_examples.md:385-474)

### (a) and (b) The range b < 3/10 is an artefact of bound (5.1)

(5.1) at `L6_examples.md:427` pairs the worst projection, 2·max|g_j| (attained only at small |T|), with the lowest level, √((3−2b) log n) (attained at |T| = q).

A size-by-size comparison gives the true threshold:
- At |T| = t = n^τ the maximal projection is at most max|g_l| + (tA_t)·(mean of the t largest |g_j|) + O(|g₀|), about (√(2b) + √(2(b−τ)))·√log n.
- With p = n^{A′} the level is √((1 + 2A′ − 2τ) log n).
- The difference (level minus projection) is increasing in τ, so the binding size is τ = 0.
- (W) therefore holds iff, up to the boundary, 8b < 1 + 2A′. With p ≍ n this is b < 3/8.

For b > 3/8, take a large fixed t₀, T the t₀ most negative g_j and l the argmax. The gap is then at least (2√(2b)(1−ε) − √3)·√log n → +∞, so (C3)(b) applies and the route cannot cover (3/8, ½) at p ≍ n. I re-derived both halves at sketch level; they agree with the two reviewer sketches (SHARPNESS.md:66-69).

Two consequences:
- The withdrawn "heuristic range 8b < 2A+1" (`L6_examples.md:465-466`) is in fact the correct threshold of (W) for p = n^A. The "−2b" objection applies only to the crude bound, not to the design.
- If the outside block is placed in span(X_Q)^⊥ with p = n^{A′} and A′ ≥ 3/2, the example would cover every b < ½. This is subject to the same Theorem 1 caveat as in claim 3.

Fixed a ∈ (0, 1), θ ≠ 0 and η = 0 are fine. Q cannot be chosen smaller, because every group member leaks at order n.

### (c) Route

- Bound E S_t ≤ t(√(2 log(q/t)) + O(1)) for the top-t sum.
- Use dyadic blocks in t: the top-t average is monotone and the level is monotone, so only log₂ q Lipschitz maxima are needed, at a cost of O(√log log n).
- Verify (W) directly rather than through (W-unif).

This is about one page. The matching lower bound is half a page and would make the example a proved instance where the lattice route fails (O1/O4′).
