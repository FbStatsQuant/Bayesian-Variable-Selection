## W3-PSHARP:A — Proposition A (√(log p) factor attained)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **Gaussian noise (G) under (W-sph): stronger than the proof needs.** The law of the scores ζ_j is fixed by the sphere, conditionally on ε, and "does not depend on ε" (`lemmas/A_log_factor.md:82-85`). Gaussianity enters only through the χ² laws of E, E₁, E₂ at `A_log_factor.md:92-98`, used via Chebyshev/Markov.
  - Sufficient instead: ε independent of the null directions, with ‖ε‖² = O_P(n), ‖(C−P₀)ε‖² ≥ cn w.h.p., and ‖P₀ε‖²·log N = o_P(n). Mean-zero iid noise with a finite fourth moment gives this.
  - The constant 1/√π is then a property of the null-block geometry, not of the noise. `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:11` ("the constant is noise-specific") and the registry tag `A:GAUSS-NOISE = NECESSARY-FOR-ROUTE` (`REPORT.md:149`) are correct only for (W-orth).
- **Gaussian noise under (W-orth): needed for this route.** The scores u_jᵀε must be exactly iid N(0,1) (`A_log_factor.md:53-54`). A non-Gaussian version would need moderate-deviation control at level √(2 log N) plus decorrelation, which is new work.
- **Exact orthogonality in (W-orth): stronger than needed.** Independence is used only for Var(T_N) ≤ N·E W₁² (`A_log_factor.md:19`). For jointly Gaussian scores with correlation ρ, my calculation gives E[W_jW_k] ≤ (2τ/π)·e^{ρτ/(1−ρ²)}/√(1−ρ²).
  - So null columns orthogonal to col X_{S₀} with mutual coherence max_{j≠k}|x_jᵀx_k|/(n−1) = o(1/log N) suffice.
  - This removes the cap N ≤ n−1−r₀ (`STATEMENT.md:55`) and gives a fixed-design witness with p ≫ n. It also partly answers open item O2 (`REPORT.md:122`): the ρ = 0.5 equicorrelated block is far outside this class.
- **(E), (C) fixed:** weakenable to (M+M′) log N = o(n). Already recorded at `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:12`; I agree.
- **N_n → ∞:** necessary for the in-probability log factor, since a bounded number of e^{χ²/2} terms is O_P(1). It is not needed for the expectation statement (see (b)).
- **(log N)² = o(n) and (r₀+1) log N = o(n):** harmless. The second matters only where Λ_n → ∞ anyway. It disappears if the nonzero eigenvalues of G_{S₀} are ≥ 1, since then e_i(1−e_i) ≤ 1/((n+1)λ_i) can replace the bound ¼ at `A_log_factor.md:90`.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **In-probability constant, fixed s₀ (`STATEMENT.md:72`): I judge it sharp.** W = e^{χ²₁/2} has P(W > xN) ≈ 1/(xN√(π log N)), so no term exceeds εN w.h.p. The one-step sum therefore equals (1+o_P(1))·2N√(log N/π), and j-step supersets contribute O(Λ_n^j). This agrees with the numerics (ratio 1.003–1.007, `REPORT.md:89-90`). The matching upper constant is open (O3).
- **Expectation bound (`STATEMENT.md:72`, proof `A_log_factor.md:119`): not sharp; the constant is improvable.** The proof passes through the in-probability event and so truncates at W ≤ N. Truncate instead at T = ε/c_n, where c_n = (s₀+1)/((2p−1−s₀)√n).
  - Put X = c_n Σ_j W_j·1{W_j ≤ T}. Then E X = N c_n·2√(log T/π) exactly (the computation at `A_log_factor.md:23`).
  - Since W² ≤ T·W on the truncation event and the scores are independent, E X² ≤ ε·E X + (E X)².
  - Since x/(1+x) ≥ x − x², E δ_n ≥ E X·(1 − ε − E X) − P(bad κ event).
  - Result: **liminf E δ_n / [(s₀+1)·(2N/(2p−1−s₀))·√(log(p√n/(s₀+1))/(πn))] ≥ 1.**
  - For p = n^a this gives the constant √((a+½)/π), against the stated √(a/π) — a factor √(1+1/(2a)), which is 1.22 at a = 1.
  - It holds for every N ≥ 1, bounded N included. So E δ_n ≳ √(log n/n) even for fixed p or log p = o(log n): the √(log n) in E δ_n is a tail effect of e^{χ²/2}, not a multiplicity effect.
  - Consequences for the reading: the expectation rate r_n is exact on the witnesses for every p, not only when log p ≍ log n (`STATEMENT.md:84-87`). Open item O1 (`REPORT.md:121`) can hold only in probability. The exemption "does not apply when log p = o(log n)" (`STATEMENT.md:109`) fails for expectation-rate targets.
  - This is my own derivation; I know of no published statement of it.
- **Corollary A2 at the critical scale (`STATEMENT.md:75-77`): improvable.** It keeps only one-step supersets, giving odds ≥ (1−o_P(1))Λ_n even when Λ_n does not tend to 0.
  - On (W-orth), D_{S₀∪J}/D_{S₀} = n^{|J|} and the Q-decrements add, so the j-step term is ≥ [w(s₀+j)/w(s₀)]·n^{−j/2}·e_j(W).
  - Since Σ W_j²/(Σ W_j)² → 0 in probability, e_j = (1+o_P(1))·e₁^j/j!.
  - Hence for s₀ → ∞, s₀/p → 0 and Λ_n → λ: liminf odds ≥ e^λ − 1, i.e. limsup π(S₀) ≤ e^{−λ}, against the stated 1/(1+λ).
  - This is the analogue of K01-P1, which was adopted for Claim B but not for A.
- **Literature.** As far as I can recall, Yang–Wainwright–Jordan, Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart and Narisetty–He all make the per-variable penalty grow polynomially in p (through the prior or the slab scale). That is not the coded prior with g = n, so nothing there dominates or contradicts this lower bound. I know of no published lower bound with this constant.

**(c) Cheapest routes**

1. Expectation constant on (W-orth): about ten lines, replacing `A_log_factor.md:119`. Bound the bad event with a χ² exponential tail instead of Chebyshev at `:64-68`. Easy. On (W-sph) the Markov term 1/x_n at `:102` must also be replaced by a χ² tail, and the sphere comparison extended to t² ≤ 2 log T. Easy to moderate.
2. Drop G under (W-sph): restate `:92-98` with second-moment or LLN bounds. Easy.
3. e^λ − 1 at the critical scale on (W-orth): easy. On (W-sph) it needs multi-step fit control. Moderate.
4. Coherence witness replacing exact orthogonality: add a covariance term to Lemma W. Easy to moderate.

## W3-PSHARP:B — Corollary B (sparsity obstruction on every design)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- There are essentially none. s₀ ≤ K−1 is necessary because otherwise Ω contains no superset. s₀ ≤ p/2 only fixes the constant 1/3; c(p,s₀) is stated exactly (`STATEMENT.md:94-96`).

**(b) Conclusion**

- **The inequality is sharp as a uniform design-free, data-free bound.** Equality in D_{S∪j}/D_S ≤ n holds iff x_j ⟂ col X_S, the prior ratio is exact, and the fit factor is exactly 1 when x_j ⟂ ρ̃.
- **The total odds, not only the superset odds, attain the bound asymptotically.** Take an orthogonal design with p ≤ n−1 and y ∈ col X_{S₀} with equal shares, s₀ = o(n).
  - Then ρ̃_{S₀} ∈ col X_{S₀}, so every superset has fit factor 1 and D-ratio n^j, and the superset odds equal 𝒪^{lb}_n.
  - Each non-superset pays a fit factor of order (s₀/n)^{n/2}, which beats the 2^p model count.
  - This is a better tightness witness than Remark 2 (`lemmas/B_sparsity.md:53-54`). With y ⟂ col X only the superset part matches; the total odds are much larger.
- **B1 critical cap e^{−λ/2}:** exact as a worst case, by the same witness.
- **B2 "s₀ = O(√log n) on every design":** sharp for a data-free statement, by the same witness at s₀ ≍ √log n.
- **No uniform √(log p) gain under G in probability.** With duplicated null columns the one-step sum is N·e^{ζ/2} with a single ζ, which has no log factor.
- **Unharvested readings of the same inequality (cosmetic):**
  - Supercritical regime: the full sum gives π(S₀) ≤ exp(−(1−o(1))·s₀/(2√n)) when s₀/√n → ∞, s₀/p → 0 and K − s₀ ≫ s₀/√n. The stated consequence is only "→ 0" with the polynomial cap 1/(1+λ/3) (`STATEMENT.md:100`).
  - s₀/p → θ ∈ [0,1): limsup π(S₀) ≤ exp(−λ(1−θ)/(2−θ)). The report already has the θ = ½ case.
  - B2/B3 in expectation: by the improved expectation bound under Claim A, E δ_n = O(r_n) forces s₀ = O(1) on (W-orth) witnesses for every p. The parenthesis at `STATEMENT.md:109` should be limited to in-probability rates.

**(c) Route**

- No stronger design-free inequality exists. The items above are one-paragraph additions to `B_sparsity.md:32-40`.
