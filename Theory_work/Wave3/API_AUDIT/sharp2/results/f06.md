## W3-START:START-R — Theorem (T3)(b), Gaussian rows (`STATEMENT.md:306-314`; premises `:160-167`)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **(V-R) with a fixed a_V is stronger than the proof uses.** `L9_BR.md:53-55` needs only two things: a_V ≥ 2s₀θ_hi·C_S·h′_n, and N·a_V² ≥ 4c_V(σ²+Δ_hi)·log n. Both hold for a_{V,n} ≥ C_*√(log n/n) with an explicit C_*. This is the level that (N3)/(C2)(ii) show is necessary in order (`STATEMENT.md:325-327`). The constant C_* is a route artefact (the Schur constant C_S) and is not the sharp 1+2A. The choice c_V = 2A+4 (`L9_BR.md:158`) is also more than needed: any c_V > 2+2A gives κ_V > ½.
- **Fixed θ_lo > 0 (Setting R, `STATEMENT.md:70-71`) is a route assumption.** It enters through Δ_lo, ā < 1, L_a and c_d (`L9_BR.md:16-17, 67-70, 81`). The fixed-design theorem and example F-2 work at θ* ≍ √(log n/n), and the posterior literature (Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart; Yang–Wainwright–Jordan) also works at beta-min ≍ √(log p/n). Shrinking signals should make leakage smaller (δ = √Δ_U·g), but ā → 1 breaks the (F2) Lipschitz step as written. This needs re-parametrising L9.3–L9.4 and is not free.
- **(L-gain-R) is a uniform (sup over l) condition at scale C_L·n^{−1/2}.** This is weaker than achievable in two ways.
  - *Scale.* (C2)(i) puts the real threshold at population partial covariance ≍ √(log n/n). With d̄ = c√log n in `L9_BR.md:115-123`, Φ_n becomes n^{C(c)}, and G_n stays O(√log n), so `:131-135` survive. That gives a polynomial rate n^{−1/2+C(c)}. This is open item O1 (`REPORT.md:163`); it is easy, though the exponent from the loose 2/a̲ and 1/a̲ factors would not be sharp.
  - *Uniformity.* The selling point of (T1) is an averaged leakage condition, but (L-gain-R) reverts to a sup. Lemma L9.5 is per pair (l, U₀), so one can carry a per-column C_{L,l}, hence d̄_l, and use E Ψ_n ≤ p⁻¹Σ_l N_ref·Φ_n(d̄_l) at `L9_BR.md:138`. An averaged population condition then falls out with no new argument.
- **(L-loc-R)** is needed only because the partition-free functional maximises over all 15-sets (`L9_BR.md:178-179`). I see no cheap way round it, since the coded partition depends on X and conditioning on it destroys the Gaussian law.
- **(FL-R16) with constant c_Σ** is stronger than what the fixed-design side needs: (GF-16) allows near-twin columns (F-3), while the bridge excludes them. Relaxing it touches C_S, τ_e² and ‖γ²‖ and is not cheap.
- **s₀ fixed, p ≤ n^A.** The literature above allows growing s₀ and log p = o(n). Here s₀ growth is open item O3. I am unsure how far p can go; from the n^{1/3} choice in `L2_TK.md:25-34` I would guess log p = o(n^{1/3}) with modest work.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **Exponent ½** is optimal by (T0). It is a property of the scorer (g ≈ n), not of the proof. Yang–Wainwright–Jordan-type rates of order 1 − c/p (as I recall their Theorem 1) come from g ≍ p^{2α} and are not a fair target.
- **C_L = 0, Q = S₀: the factor (log n)^{15κ_Q+1/2} is an artefact.** `L9_BR.md:162-173` runs the lattice form L8.2(i) with ϱ_n(Q), even when Q = S₀. The Q = S₀ form (T3)(a)/(L8.2(ii)) has leading functional 𝔏⁰_n, whose maximum is over U ⊊ S₀ only (`STATEMENT.md:112-116`). There are at most 2^{s₀} such sets, all inside H_l, so no localisation and no N_ref factor arise. L9.5 then gives 𝔏⁰_n = O_P(√log n), and the second-order term n^{−1}𝔏_n(S₀)² = n^{−1}(log n)^{30κ_Q+1} is negligible. The result is **o₀ = O_P(√(log n/n))** for every design satisfying (L-R) with Q = S₀ and C_L = 0. The ticket notes this only for example G-1 (`L11_EXAMPLES.md:85-86`) and attributes it to bounded |I_l|; it is general.
- **The true order for Gaussian rows is probably √(log p/n), not n^{−1/2}.** Jointly in (X, ε), √(1−a)·g + √a·Z is N(0,1) at the critical exponent, so p⁻¹Σ_l exp(·²/2) ≈ √(2/π)·√(2 log p). A matching lower bound is unclaimed (`STATEMENT.md:380`) and would need (f1) plus a sum-of-heavy-tails argument. A distributional limit for √n·o₀/√log p in G-1 looks reachable but is new work.
- **Same route, general C_L.** Only the conditioning sets U ⊊ S₀ need the n^{−1/2} leakage scale. All other sets enter through n^{−1}𝔏_n(S₀)² and (AL), so they only need 𝔏_n(S₀) = o(n^{1/4}). That is a genuine weakening of (L-gain-R) once O1 is done.
- **The form exp(C√log n) for C_L > 0 looks unimprovable uniformly over the class.** Take W_l = c·n^{−1/2}·W_j + noise for all nulls; this is admissible under (L-loc-R). The largest of p shifted Gaussians then contributes exp(d·√(2 log p))/p·p. This is a heuristic; I have not checked the block composition. The constant C_R = 2d̄√(2A+1)/a̲ + 2 is loose. `REPORT.md:146` ("not known to be sharp") can probably be upgraded to "form sharp, constant not".
- **Mode of convergence.** The leading term has tail index 1, so P(T₁ > λ·rate) ≍ 1/λ. Probability 1 − n^{−c} at the stated rate is impossible. The O_P form for C_L = 0 is therefore natural; a fixed-constant bound with probability → 1 at √(log p/n) needs concentration across columns (`REPORT.md:166`).
- **Lattice case Q ≠ S₀.** N_ref enters by bounding E max by a sum (`L9_BR.md:136-138`). A maximal inequality for index-1/(1−a) tails would lower the polylog power; this is minor.

**(c) Cheapest route**

1. Add a Q = S₀ clause via 𝔏⁰_n and L8.2(ii). This is about half a page using existing lemmas.
2. State (V-R) with a_{V,n} ≥ C_*√(log n/n).
3. Use per-column C_{L,l} with an averaged condition.
4. Do O1.

All four are easy. Shrinking θ_lo, growing s₀ and the √(log p/n) lower bound are hard or moderate.

## W3-START:IF-START — (T5), P-START with a rate (`STATEMENT.md:334-338`; premises `:135-157`)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **The partition-free (AL) and visibility are stronger than this corollary needs.** IF-START is an interface about the coded start on its actual partition. The proof bounds everything by the block-relative quantities and relaxes only in its last line (`L5_ASM_STARTF.md:51-52, 65-67`); (C1)(b) is the block-relative statement (`STATEMENT.md:217`). So IF-START holds verbatim with:
  - premise (|Q|+1)·n^{−1/2}·𝔏^b_n(S₀;h) → 0 and block instances of visibility;
  - rate n^{−1/2}𝔏^{b,0}_n(h) + n^{−1}𝔏^b_n(S₀;h)² + n^{−κ}.

  This is strictly more general. It covers the `leakall`, η = 0 design, which is outside partition-free (AL) (`ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:57-58`).
- **Necessity.** T₁ → 0 is necessary, and Ξ → 0 is necessary when s₀+16 ≤ K (AGG-b). The 1+2A visibility level is necessary ((C2)(ii)). The energy bound, (GF-16) and Gaussian noise are route assumptions with no counterexample. The event 𝒜 plays no role (`L10_INTERFACES.md:13-15`), so nothing is lost there.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **Gaussian-row rate.** The corollary quotes r_n = n^{−1/2}exp(C_R√log n) only (`L10_INTERFACES.md:16-18`). IF-START is an O_P statement, so the C_L = 0 clause of (T3)(b), which is itself O_P, plugs in directly and gives r_n = n^{−1/2}(log n)^{15κ_Q+1/2}. With the 𝔏⁰_n route above it gives r_n = √(log n/n).
- **Fixed-design rate.**
  - The exponent κ < κ_V is sharp at exponent level when log p/log n → A: deleting a truth at gain exactly c_V costs n^{−κ_V+o(1)} by the computation of (C2)(ii). When A̲ < A it can be stated with log p/log n in place of A.
  - The leading term is of the right order in the two extreme examples: n^{−1/2} in F2, and √(log p/n) when τ = σ (for example s₀ = 0). Constants (e^{2κ_E+1}) are not sharp.
  - A probability → 1 version costs log² n (`L6_COROLLARIES.md:19-26`) and is available but not stated.

**(c) Cheapest route.** Restate with block-relative functionals as the primary form and the partition-free form as a corollary; add the C_L = 0 and Q = S₀ Gaussian-row rates. All of this is easy, with no new proof.

## W3-START:OVL — Proposition OVL (`STATEMENT.md:339-344`; premises `:135-157`)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **(V-c) is not needed; [VIS(κ;Q)] suffices, so (V-m) works.** `STATEMENT.md:381` lists "OVL under (V-m)" as not claimed, but it is reachable.
  - The proof uses (AGG-c) with e_n (`L10_INTERFACES.md:34-36, 43`). Instead take (AGG-a) in its proved form Σ_Ω γ₀ ≤ F·Σ_{T∈𝓛(Q)} γ₀(T) (`L1_AGG_FLOOR.md:64-67`).
  - Bound the lattice sum by (P3) with D = 0 and (CH): Σ_{T∈𝓛(Q)} γ₀(T)/γ₀(S₀) ≤ ∏_{l∈Q∖S₀}(1 + |Q|·m_l/p).
  - This gives 1/π₀(S₀) ≤ ∏_b(1+Ξ_b(Q))·(1−U_Q)^{−(c_Q+1)}·∏_{l∈Q∖S₀}(1+|Q|·m_l/p).
  - Ξ(Q) ≤ n^{−κ} follows from L4.1 under (V-m) and [TK] alone (`L4_VIS.md:24-27`). Step (iii) of the OVL proof (`L10_INTERFACES.md:44-50`) is unchanged, and so is E_n.

  This matters because OVL is advertised for the case "start mode is not S₀", where (V-c) is awkward: it demands the truth gain given every 15-set, including the truth's close correlates.
- **Uniform ā.** It is used only to keep x → 0 for (f3). It is harmless for the consumer, since P-OVL needs E_n < max(1, log p/log n) anyway.
- **|Q∖S₀| ≤ q̄ fixed.** Polylog growth, as in (T3)(b), looks reachable with per-column slack bookkeeping; this is modest work but not free.
- **Gaussian rows.** No bridge delivers the levels a_{n,l}. With fixed θ and constant population leakage the gains are of order n. OVL therefore lives in the local regime δ² ≍ log n; this is a scope fact, not a defect.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **The slack n^{−ε} is improvable to exp(−M_n√log n) for any M_n → ∞.** Expand (δ+σZ)² ≤ δ² + 2|δ|σ|Z| + σ²Z² directly on {|Z| ≤ M_n}, instead of Young's inequality with fixed ε₁ on {Z² ≤ 2 log log n} (`L10_INTERFACES.md:38, 44-49`). The (1+ϑ̄)(1+x̄) correction is O(n^{−1/3}log n). The result is π₀(S₀) ≥ n^{−E_n}·e^{−O_P(√log n)}. This form is exact: the Bayes factor carries a factor exp(√(a·log n)·N(0,1)). It parallels PXN's "e^{O_P(1)}" law.
- **The exponent E_n is worst-case sharp but not instance-sharp.**
  - Worst case: (C2)(i)-type lower bounds match it when the leaking columns' gains are additive and attained at U*_b.
  - Instance: it sums per-column worst-case levels. When leaking columns are substitutes (two nulls proxying the same off-block truth), the true exponent is max over N ⊆ Q∖S₀ of [joint gain/(2τ² log n) − |N|(½ + log p/log n)]₊. This can be much smaller.
  - A joint-gain hypothesis gives it directly from (TK-d)/(TK-e) for nested sets, since the lattice has at most 2^{q̄} elements.

**(c) Cheapest route.** Replace (AGG-c) by (AGG-a) plus the lattice sum, state OVL under [VIS(κ;Q)] (either visibility form), and sharpen the slack. Both are easy, about a page. The joint-gain exponent needs a modest reformulation of the hypothesis.

## W3-START:MIS — Corollary MIS (T6) (`STATEMENT.md:346-357`; premises `:135-157`)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **(GR) makes the candidate model exactly well-specified.** It assumes an exact linear-Gaussian homoscedastic residual, so the corollary is a relabelling (`L10_INTERFACES.md:71-73`). It is correct, but it is not a misspecification or "sandwich" result in the sense the brief asks for (`BRIEF.md:19`).
- **The fixed-design machinery does not need μ ∈ span X.**
  - Identity (2.1), (TK-a), (TK-b) and (TK-e) hold for any deterministic μ ∈ 1^⊥ with ‖μ‖² ≤ Mn (`L2_TK.md:20-21, 47-55, 78-81`).
  - Lemma AGG holds for any reference model (`STATEMENT.md:197-198`).
  - μ = X_{S₀}θ* is used only in (B1)(iii) (δ_{S₀,l} = 0, τ_{S₀} = σ; `L3_LEAK.md:36-40`) and in F2.

  So for a **deterministic** omitted part ω, with no (GR), (T1) should hold with μ := C(X_𝒞β_𝒞 + ω) and any reference support satisfying visibility and (AL). The only change is that U = S₀ joins the maximum in 𝔏⁰_n.
  - If ω ⟂ span X_𝒞, the target is S₀ ∩ 𝒞 itself and τ² inflates by ‖ω‖²/(n−1). Then τ > σ, (J1) applies, and the √log factor disappears.
  - The same extension fixes the brittle requirement that β^𝒞 = β_𝒞 + γ be exactly s₀-sparse with every nonzero entry visible. A dense weak γ, which is typical under omitted variables, currently sends π₀(S₀^𝒞) → 0 by (C2)(ii). With a general μ, the weak part is absorbed into Λ and the leakage functional.
- **Gaussian ξ.** Relaxing to non-Gaussian or heteroscedastic ξ is hard: the critical exponential moment is sensitive to tails at the √(2 log p) scale. Conditioning on ξ and using the deterministic-μ version through (T3)(a) is a plausible route; I am unsure about it.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **(T3)(b) and (T7) are not on the list of transferred claims** (`STATEMENT.md:349-350`). Yet (GR) holds exactly in the one natural case: jointly Gaussian candidate and omitted rows. Then ξ is independent of W_𝒞 and τ² = β_𝓜ᵀCov(W_𝓜|W_𝒞)β_𝓜. The headline "candidate-run start concentrates on S₀^𝒞 at n^{−1/2}e^{C√log n} under population conditions on (Σ_𝒞𝒞, β^𝒞)" is missing.
  - It needs Setting R with a deterministic raw-scale β, so θ* = D_xβ is random. L9.5 already conditions on W_D, which fixes these scales (`L9_BR.md:103-104, 125-126`), so the adaptation is small.
- **Cost of misspecification.** Under the reduction, σ² → σ²+τ² at visibility level 1+2A_𝒞. This is necessary on the actual partition by the substituted (C2)(ii). Rates are inherited, so the START-R, IF-START and OVL improvements above carry over.

**(c) Cheapest route**

1. Add the Gaussian-row instance. This is easy.
2. Restate (T1) for a general deterministic mean and reference model, and derive MIS without (GR) for fixed (candidate, omitted) designs. This is a re-read of L2–L5 with the two changes named above; I judge it modest, but it needs a careful pass.

Non-Gaussian ξ is hard.
