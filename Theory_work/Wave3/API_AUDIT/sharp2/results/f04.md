## W3-START:FLOOR — `STATEMENT.md:241-243`

**(a) Hypotheses.** There is nothing to weaken.
- The proof uses no rank condition and no model (`lemmas/L1_AGG_FLOOR.md:113-127`).
- The restriction |S| < K is necessary: a saturated model has no one-variable additions inside Ω, and its mass can be exponentially close to 1 (`STATEMENT.md:207-209`).

**(b) Conclusion.** As a statement for every data set, it is tight including the leading constant (my computation, not in the ticket).
- Take a response orthogonal to every null column after residualising on S∩C_b, with nulls not nearly collinear.
- Each one-step ratio is then (n+1)^{-1/2}(1+O(1/n)): in `L1_AGG_FLOOR.md:121-125`, s(1/(n+1))/s(1) = 1−O(1/n), and Q is unchanged.
- So the sum of single additions equals f_n(k)(1+o(1)), and multi-null terms are O(f_n²). This works for p ≫ n too.

The displays ⅓(n+1)^{-1/2} and ½(n+1)^{-1/2} drop the factor (|S|+1), but f_n is stated exactly, so nothing is lost.

The exponent ½ is the classical rate at which a local, unit-information prior accumulates evidence for a true smaller model (Johnson–Rossell 2010). Results with faster rates use a different prior, not a sharper analysis:
- Yang–Wainwright–Jordan: g ≍ p^{2α} with a p^{-κ|γ|} model prior, giving p^{-c}-type mass bounds.
- Narisetty–He: diffusing slab.
- Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart: complexity prior.
- Johnson–Rossell 2012: non-local priors.

With the coded w(k), the total prior odds of adding some variable are about (k+1)/2, so no design condition helps. The floor belongs to the method.

**(c) Stronger version.** None for the stated inequality. Two adjacent items exist but neither sharpens this claim:
- The same lines give the floor for the full posterior (`L1_AGG_FLOOR.md:143-144`, `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:50-51`); this is free.
- The in-probability lower bound of order √(log p/n) when τ = σ (`STATEMENT.md:380`) belongs to START-F.

## W3-START:F1 — `STATEMENT.md:268-271`

**(a) Hypotheses.**
- The bound is taken over all 15-sets, as in item 6 above.
- The threshold η_A comes from (J2) (`L3_LEAK.md:18-19`), which sets a = σ²/τ² to 1.

**(b) Conclusion.** The exponent η/2 + √(2Aη) is sharp in the worst case of the class, and loose elsewhere.
- Worst case (my computation, not in the ticket): τ ≈ σ, all nulls leaking at level η, near-independent projections. The largest term alone gives T₁ ≥ n^{-1/2+η/2+√(2Aη)−o(1)}. The same holds for √(log n/n) at η = 0.
- Whenever the unexplained signal is non-negligible (a ≤ ā < 1, the fixed-signal regime), the bound is loose.
- The integrand at `L3_LEAK.md:10` is exp(d²/(2t²) + dσz/t² − (1−a)z²/2)/√(2π). With d² = ηt² log n, the exact Laplace exponent is E(η, ā, A) = η/2 + max over 0 ≤ x ≤ √(2A) of [√(ηā)·x − (1−ā)x²/2].
- This is increasing in ā, equals (J2) at ā = 1, and equals (J1), namely η/(2(1−ā)), when the maximiser is interior.
- Example: A = 1, ā = ½ admits η < ½, against η_A ≈ 0.10.
- A should also be replaced by log p/log n, since z_n depends on the actual p (`lemmas/L6_COROLLARIES.md:32-34`).

**(c) Route.** Add the hypothesis σ²/τ²_U ≤ ā on pairs with δ_{U,l} ≠ 0. Sets U ⊇ S₀ keep J = √(2/π)z_n. Replace (J2) by the exact exponent in L6.1(i). This is a one-lemma change that unifies F1 and F2 and enlarges the admissible leakage range. Easy.
