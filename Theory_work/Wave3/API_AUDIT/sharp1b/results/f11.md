## W2-FAC-PK:Thm-NEG (STATEMENT.md:63-68)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **(PK) liminf p/K > 16/15 and (PB)** (STATEMENT.md:51-54) are stronger than the mechanism needs.
  - They enter only through L1(c),(d) (L1_start.md:61-67), i.e. the count "K ≥ 15|𝔅|", which forces every regular block's minimum out of the start; that feeds O* ∩ C₀ = ∅ at L2_neg.md:54.
  - What L2.3 actually uses is "at least n^β excluded nulls with ξ ≤ −t_n".
  - Under (PK′) alone, L1(b) (L1_start.md:54-60) already gives at least (p − K − 16s₀)/16 truth-free blocks whose minimum is excluded. What is missing is which blocks these are.
  - The cross-block exchange at L1_start.md:62-64 has exactly cancelling population parts when counts differ by one. The decision is then made by the O(√n) linear term of N_U (L1_start.md:26-27), roughly ξ_min − Σ_others/(15 + κ). Blocks with the lowest such statistic are the non-full ones, and a block with minimum ≤ −t_n has a low statistic unless its other members are atypical.
  - So NEG should extend to liminf p/K > 1, and plausibly to p − K ≥ n^γ with swap count about min(p − K, n^{β}). The ticket lists this as F2 (STATEMENT.md:110, REPORT.md:87). I judge it moderate work: one new selection lemma, with ties at scale log n/√n to control.
  - Some growth of p − K is necessary for this mechanism: with p − K = O(1) only O(1) nulls are excluded, so polynomially many swaps cannot come from it.
- **θ̄ ≠ 0** (STATEMENT.md:48) is necessary for this mechanism. Saturation in L1(a) (L1_start.md:48-53) needs c(U,l) ∝ θ̄ > 0; without factor signal the start does not fill to K.
- **Fixed s₀ and ϑ** are stronger than the proof needs. L2.2(ii) (L2_neg.md:34-40) only needs the missing-truth swap gain to exceed Δ_K. That is roughly nϑ²_min ≳ log n, plus nθ̄² ≳ log n for saturation. Relaxing is routine but requires re-tracking every constant that depends on ϑ; it is not free.
- **p ≤ n − 1** is used at L2_neg.md:33 and :42. The p ≫ n case is open (F1) and hard.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **The exponent ½ is not sharp.** It is an artefact of M_T ≥ ½(1 − 3n^{−1/3}) at L2_neg.md:45, which comes from the crude bound RSS_T ≤ ‖e‖². The ticket says as much at L2_neg.md:79-81.
- At size K ≈ n/2 the fit removes a positive fraction of ‖e‖² uniformly over T:
  - Σ_TT⁻¹ ⪰ (1 − ρ)⁻¹(I − 11ᵀ/k), so Φ(T) ≥ Σ_{j∈T}(ξ_j − ξ̄_T)².
  - Any K-subset of p ≤ 2K + 1 iid N(0,1) variables has Σ_T(ξ − ξ̄_T)² ≥ vK with v > 0 w.p. → 1 (DKW: no interval of length 2a holds more than p(2Φ(a) − 1) + o(p) points).
  - My computation gives v ≈ 0.14 at p ≈ 2K (central-half truncated second moment), and larger v as p/K falls. The value assumes the optimal centre is 0; positivity does not.
  - With L0.5(c) (L0_imports.md:36-37) this gives M_T ≥ M_* := ½/(1 − v/2) − o(1) > ½ for every size-K set T ⊇ S₀.
- Re-running L2.2(iii) with threshold t_n′ = 2 + (log n/(2M_*) + C)^{1/2} gives N_n ≥ n^{1 − 1/(4M_*) − o(1)}.
  - This exponent is strictly above ½: about 0.535 at p ≈ 2K, and about 0.68 near p/K = 16/15, by my arithmetic.
  - The theorem then holds for every β < β* := 1 − 1/(4M_*), under the same hypotheses.
- A further gain is available along the path. Swaps at fixed size decrease ψ, so M_{C_i} ≥ M_{C_0}, and M_{C_0} can be computed from the "top-u of 16" structure of L1(b),(c).
- The conjectured truth is W ≈ p·n^{−1/(4M_W)} (L2_neg.md:79-80). Matching it needs an upper bound on M along the path, which is hard (F4).
- The "− s₀ − 2" in the cap bound is combinatorially exact (L2_neg.md:65-67).
- "W.p. → 1" carries no rate. The proof gives about 1/log n from (S1) (L1_start.md:18). This is minor.
- No lower bound on D_n and no full-method statement is given (STATEMENT.md:66-68). That is a hard, different problem.
- I know of no result in the Bayesian variable selection literature that addresses greedy paths from a saturated size-n/2 start. Yang–Wainwright–Jordan-type mixing results cap model size at a level where s·log p ≲ n, so they are not comparable.

**(c) Cheapest route**

- Add one event "Σ_T(ξ − ξ̄_T)² ≥ vK for all |T| = K" to E^N_n (L2_neg.md:16-17).
- Replace the M_T bound at L2_neg.md:45 and the threshold at L2_neg.md:15.
- L2.3 and L2.4 are unchanged. This is about half a page and easy.
- The F2 extension (dropping 16/15 and (PB)) is the next step and is moderate.

## W2-FAC-PK:Thm-PATH (STATEMENT.md:69-72)

**(a) Hypotheses**

- **(PK′) lower end p ≥ K + 16s₀ + 1** (STATEMENT.md:50) is used only for saturation, L1(a) (L1_start.md:48-49). Saturation feeds phase A of L3.4 (L3_static.md:95-100).
  - The static Lemma L3(a)–(d) (L3_static.md:38-42) holds for all T ∈ 𝒯 with no lower condition on p.
  - Removing it needs L3(e) for sets missing a truth at sizes below K (missing-truth addition or swap gains order n). When the start is unsaturated its size is at least p − 16s₀ ≍ n/2, so t_l is of order n as at L3_static.md:44-45.
  - This looks moderate and is flagged as F2′.
  - The same remark covers p ≤ K (L3_static.md:129-130).
- **θ̄ ≠ 0** is likewise used only for saturation. In L3, ϱ and κ_c tolerate θ̄ = 0.
  - The endpoint claim plausibly survives without it, given the phase-A extension.
  - The count "≤ (1 + δ)|S̃₀|" would need an additive term, because the start may be small. I am not certain of the details.
- **Blocks ≤ 16**: any bounded block size works (L1_start.md:51). This is not a real restriction.
- **Fixed ϑ and s₀** are much stronger than L3 needs.
  - L3(a) needs λ_θ|ϑ|_min/8 ≥ 2H̄ + CL_n (L3_static.md:50-52), i.e. |ϑ|_min ≥ Cσ√(log p/(n(1 − ρ))).
  - L3(e) needs nϑ² ≳ log n.
  - This is the standard √(log p/n) beta-min scale of the selection-consistency literature (Narisetty–He and Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart type conditions, and the Yang–Wainwright–Jordan mixing condition, up to s-dependent factors). The theorem is stated at constant signal.
  - Extending is plausible, but all constants (s²_∞, A, c_κ, c₃) must be re-tracked, and saturation needs nθ̄² ≫ log n. This is medium to hard.
- **p ≤ n − 1** is essential to the present proof of (E4) (L3_static.md:24-28, a union over 2^n sets). REPORT.md:101-105 records a p ≫ n design where the residual floor fails. So this is hard, and not removable without design structure.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **The leading constant 1 is sharp.** Trivially m* ≥ |S̃₀| − s₀.
- **The fixed δ is not sharp.** The proof gives δ_n → 0 at an explicit rate with a two-layer version of the device already used at L3_static.md:104-113.
  - Put M₁ := ⌈K/log n⌉.
  - For T ∈ 𝒯 with m > M₁, Step 3 of (d) (L3_static.md:78-81) gives Φ̄(a)(6 + 2a) ≥ m/(2p) ≥ 1/(5 log n). Hence d² ≤ a² ≤ 2 log log n + O(log log log n).
  - With (M1) the best deletion then gains at least ½log n − 5C_M log log n. The case d ≤ 1 gains at least ½log n − 2C_M (L3_static.md:58-59).
  - So the moves before first entry into {m ≤ M₁} number at most K(½log n + 2.4)/(½log n − C log log n) = K(1 + O(log log n/log n)).
  - After first entry the remaining budget is at most M₁(½log n + O(log log n)), and every move gains at least (c_D − ε)log n. That adds only O(K/log n) moves.
  - Hence m* ≤ |S̃₀|(1 + C log log n/log n) w.p. → 1.
  - Any cap ⌈(1 + δ_n)|S̃₀|⌉ with δ_n·log n/log log n → ∞ then suffices, for example δ_n = (log n)^{−1/2}.
- This narrows the (P5) gap from [n^β, δK] to [n^β, CK log log n/log n], without new events or hypotheses.
- Closing the gap to a polynomial allowance n^γ·polylog, to match NEG and the heuristic W ≈ p·n^{−1/(4M_W)}, needs path-restricted control. Either the swap phase must be described, or F(S₀) − F(T) must be bounded below layer by layer. This is hard (F4, REPORT.md:91).
- No probability rate is given. The true failure probability of "S₀ is a strict local maximum" is polynomial, about n^{−1/2} up to logs, by a tail count at L3_static.md:53-57. The proof's L_n = log log n events give only about 1/log n. This is minor and fixable by retuning H̄.
- Literature: the static "every non-true model has an improving neighbour" device is the same as the canonical-path argument of Yang–Wainwright–Jordan. Those results assume model-size caps far below n/2, so they neither imply nor improve this claim. I am not confident enough to say more about Zhou et al. than that.

**(c) Cheapest route**

- Insert the intermediate index (first entry into m ≤ K/log n) into L3.4(ii) and restate with δ_n. This is a few lines and easy.
- Next, in order of difficulty: F2′/θ̄ (phase A at sizes below K, moderate); beta-min scaling (medium to hard); polynomial allowance and p ≫ n (hard).
