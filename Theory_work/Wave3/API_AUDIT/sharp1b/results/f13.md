## 1. Per-claim assessment

### W2-LOC2:PLR (STATEMENT.md:61-65; proof L5_assembly.md:13-33, engine L2_NAR.md, L1_toolkit.md)

**(a) Hypotheses.** Three are stronger than the proof uses, and one is redundant.

- **|Q| ≤ (log n)^κ is not needed; |Q| ≤ n^b with fixed b < 1/2 suffices.** The ticket proves this itself for one instance, in L6_poly.md §3, §6 and §7 (ledger items 3, 7, 8 at L6_poly.md:443-458). The size-sensitive steps are:
  - Gram floors need q·√(log n/n) → 0 (L1_toolkit.md:48-49; L6_poly.md:176-179).
  - The toolkit needs (q + log n)·log n/n → 0 (L1_toolkit.md:39-41; (P4) at L6_poly.md:203-204).
  - The sample-vs-population error needs ϵ_n = O((q + log n)/√n) → 0 (L2_NAR.md:99-100; (P18)).
  - The threshold keeps slack c_n − 2 log p ≥ (1 − 2b) log n − O(1) (L6_poly.md:415-416).
  - The assembly "has no polylog-size use once these events and feasibility hold" (L6_poly.md:429-431).

  (R2) as locked already reads L_n² log(|𝒜_n| + 1) = o(log n), which is the right condition for polynomial q. So PLR as stated does not even imply the ticket's own (D3); L6 has to re-run it.

- **The o(log n) margins in (R1)–(R3) are stronger than Step 5 needs.** Step 5 (L2_NAR.md:102-110) compares ζ_n² ≤ 2 log p + o(log n) with c_n ≥ 2 log p + log n − O(log log n). A full log n of slack is unused. The proof only needs limsup (ζ_n² − 2 log p)/log n < 1 − 2·limsup(log q/log n). So the following fixed-fraction version suffices, with the constants small enough jointly:
  - log N_n ≤ log p + ν log n;
  - η_n ≤ η (it enters as 2η log p);
  - L_n² log(|𝒜_n| + 1) ≤ γ log n (it enters through B_n² ≈ 8γ log n/c and the cross term 2√(x′_n)·B_n).

  Remark 2 (L2_NAR.md:124-125) only says the bound "could exceed c_n" at order log n; it does not show the o(·) is needed.

- **Fixed θ_lo is stronger than TL needs.** The TL step (L1_toolkit.md:33-36) uses Λ_A ≥ C(h_n² + t_n) and L′_n/(8C_R) > (A + 1) log n + O(1). That is nθ²_min ≥ C(|Q| + log n) for a large constant C. POST already runs under BM-∞ (consumed W2-APP:POST (a)). Since Sand_n is a premise of PLR, PLR could be stated at beta-min level θ²_min ≫ (|Q| + log n)/n. This is still above the √(log p/n) level of Narisetty–He, Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart and Yang–Wainwright–Jordan when |Q| ≫ log n. Whether the |Q| term from the 2^{|Q|} lattice union can be removed is unclear to me.

- **The feasibility dichotomy (STATEMENT.md:62) is not used in PLR's proof.** L5_assembly.md:22-29 uses only |Q| ≤ K_n. The dichotomy belongs to the proof of Sand_n, which PLR assumes. It is harmless but redundant.

- **Necessary or structural hypotheses.** The local floor (N1) is an identifiability condition: without it, lattice competitors are near-collinear with S₀. Gaussian rows are used essentially, for the exact independence of e_{T,l} from W_T and the χ² laws (L2_NAR.md:63-71). Removing Gaussianity looks hard. Growing s₀ is blocked by κ_E ≍ M ≍ s₀² entering c_n (consumed C-GROW:L0.1); only s₀ = o(√log n) looks reachable without new work.

**(b) Conclusion.** The rate 1 − π(S₀) = O_P(√(log n/n)) looks sharp for this method when p is polynomial in n. This is my own heuristic, not a proof:

- The prior odds for one extra variable are ≈ (k + 1)/p (L1_toolkit.md:37-38).
- The marginal-likelihood factor is (n + 1)^{−1/2}·e^{Z_l²/2}.
- Summing over ≈ p near-independent nulls gives Σ_l e^{Z_l²/2} ≍ p√(log p).
- Hence 1 − π(S₀) ≍ √(log p/n).

The p^{−c} concentration in Yang–Wainwright–Jordan comes from their stronger p^{−κ|γ|} prior, which would be a method change. The claim gives no rate for D_n → 0. The slowest terms are artefacts of threshold choices: E_d fails at O(1/log n) (L2_NAR.md:74-78) and E_e at e^{−b_n} (L2_NAR.md:79-84). With the unused log n slack, thresholds x′_n = 2 log N_n + δ log n would give polynomial rates for the NA part. I cannot judge whether the external Sand and POST inputs support the same.

**(c) Cheapest route.** Restate PLR with |Q| ≤ n^b (b < 1/2), fixed-fraction versions of (R1)–(R3), and no dichotomy. The proof is L2_NAR Steps 1–5 verbatim plus L6_poly.md §3, §6 and §7, which are already checked. Difficulty is low, mostly editorial. It would make (D3) and REPORT.md:83-84 item 2 genuine corollaries.

### W2-LOC2:Thm-Poly (STATEMENT.md:87-93; proof L6_poly.md)

**(a) Hypotheses.** The cutoff α > 17/2 is an artefact, as the file itself says (L6_poly.md:10-13). I believe it can be lowered to α > 2 by a local change to §4.

- **Where 17/2 comes from.** It is forced by (P6) (L6_poly.md:233-238): ρ > 1/(4α) and 15ρ + 1/(2α) < 1/2. The requirement ρ > 1/(4α) comes from the crude bound |z_U − z_0| ≤ H₃v_n² with v_n = R_n^{−α} + √(log n/n) (L6_poly.md:255-258). That bound treats the cross term aᵀR⁻¹b as ‖a‖·‖b‖ ≈ R_n^{−2α}.

- **What the crude bound misses.** For l ∉ Q the product structure is already available from (P5) (L6_poly.md:213-220). The population partial correlation of W_l with Y is O(n^{−1/2}) given any set of size ≤ 15, so given both U₀ and U. The exact population recursion then forces a_popᵀR_pop⁻¹b_pop = O(n^{−1/2}), whatever the radius.

- **Sharper version.** Bound the cross term entrywise by |a_v|·|(R⁻¹)_{vw}|·|b_w| ≲ d(l,v)^{−α}·(1 + d(v,w))^{−α}·d(w,k)^{−α}. Use d(l,v) + d(v,w) + d(w,k) ≥ d(l,k) > D·n^{1/(2α)}. The decay of the 15×15 inverse follows from the cofactor argument of (P1). This gives a population cross term O(n^{−1/2}·r₀^{−α}) when every removed v is farther than r₀ from {l} ∪ S₀.

- **Resulting error with r₀ = ⌈log n⌉.** Redoing (P8)–(P9), the remaining terms are:
  - population × sampling: n|z_0|·r₀^{−α}√(log n/n) = r₀^{−α} log n;
  - denominators: h_0·r₀^{−2α} ≲ r₀^{−2α} log n;
  - cross: √(log n)·r₀^{−α}.

  All are o(1) for any α > 1. Then |H_l| = O(log n), |I_l| = O((log n)^{15}) = n^{o(1)}, and λ = 0 in (P7), (P11), (P12), (P14).

- **New cutoff.** The remaining constraints are (P13) with λ = 0, i.e. ½ < τ < 1 − 2b, which is b < ¼, and Lemma IR's α > 2 (L6_poly.md:120, 145). Both give α > 2.

- **Confidence.** I have checked this against the displayed steps only. The interface with the external GROW (6.10) and L6.3–L6.4 needs re-checking. Nothing in (P10) depends on H_l.

- **Minor redundancy.** The upper spectral bound C is implied by the decay for α > 1 (Schur row sums ≤ 1 + 2K_dec·ζ(α)). The lower bound c and PTV are genuine. PTV failure is masking and is declared out of scope (STATEMENT.md:106).

**(b) Conclusion.**

- **Posterior rate.** Same as PLR: sharp for this prior.

- **Size of Q.** The radius n^{1/(2α)} is probably near-necessary up to logs when the envelope is saturated. A null at distance d from a truth in another block has partial correlation ≍ d^{−α}, and is visible to the start mode once n·d^{−2α} ≳ log n. This is the same mechanism as the visible-nulls lemma (STATEMENT.md:82-83). I have no proof of a lower bound.

- **Below α = 2.** The barrier is IR's exponent loss, α − 1 with a log factor (L6_poly.md:122-123). Jaffard's theorem says inverses of polynomially off-diagonal-decaying, boundedly invertible matrices keep the same exponent α for α > 1 in dimension one. I am confident of the theorem. I am less sure that the constants are uniform over principal submatrices; I believe Gröchenig–Klotz norm-controlled inversion gives this. If so, ‖β_T(l)‖₁ and its tail are summable for all α > 1, and the remaining constraints (b < ½, and (P13) without the τ > ½ range restriction) point to α > 1. That is new work.

- **Long memory and d ≥ 2.** Long memory (α ≤ 1) stays out of reach of this route. Polynomial decay in d ≥ 2 is not claimed (STATEMENT.md:108). With n^{o(1)} references the natural requirements would be q = n^{d/(2α)} = o(√n) and summable regression tails.

**(c) Cheapest route.** Replace (P6)–(P9) by the triangle-inequality cross-term bound with radius log n. This is about one page plus a re-check of the (P11)–(P15) arithmetic with λ = 0. It is the "sharper selection removal" that REPORT.md:80-82 lists as open, and it looks modest. Going from α > 2 to α > 1 via Jaffard is harder.

### W2-LOC2:F-LOC2 (STATEMENT.md:100-102; proof L7_delivery.md:17-35)

**(a) Hypotheses.**

- **The restriction to classes (D1)–(D3) is stronger than Step 3 uses.** Step 3 (L7_delivery.md:29-35) uses only P(Sand_n ∩ NA(𝓛) ∩ TL(𝓛)) → 1 and E[1 − π(S₀)] → 0. These are exactly the PLR premises (C-i) and (C-ii). The proposition could be stated once for any design sequence satisfying PLR, including its polynomial-|Q| form.

- **Step 1 does not need the global floor.** L7_delivery.md:21-23 invokes λ_min(Σ_n) ≥ c only to get non-constant columns. Unit diagonal already gives that. The floor is needed only through LSF.

- **The worst-case allocation is necessary for the exact claim.** The budget of ℓ_n + K + 1 scans (L7_delivery.md:11, 26-27) is what makes P(no output) = 0 hold for every n. A budget of order |Q| scans would give only P(no output) → 0. That is a trade, not an improvement.

- **Inherited scope.** The (D3) scope inherits the 17/2 cutoff. It would drop to α > 2 with the Thm-Poly fix above.

**(b) Conclusion.** P(no output) = 0 is unimprovable. P(Ŝ⁺ = S₀) → 1 is the strongest selection mode. No rate is given: the bound 2E[1 − π(S₀)] + P(𝒦_nᶜ) is at best O(1/log n) as constructed, an artefact of the E_d/E_e thresholds (see PLR (b)). "Along each sequence" is equivalent to uniformity over the constant-indexed class if θ* values may vary in [θ_lo, θ_hi], since the proofs use only the class constants. Stating it uniformly costs nothing.

**(c) Cheapest route.** Restate as "under PLR (C-i)+(C-ii) and the delivery premises", with (D1)–(D3) as instances. The proof is unchanged. A polynomial rate would need retuned thresholds and a check of the external POST and Sand failure rates; I cannot judge that from the material.
