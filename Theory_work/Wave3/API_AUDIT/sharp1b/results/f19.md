## W3-RM:RM-0 — Theorem RM-0 (core)

**(a) Hypotheses.** Three are stronger than the proof uses.

- **Posterior concentration.** The event {π(S₀) > ½} is used only to get F(S₀) > F(S) for all S ≠ S₀ (`lemmas/L0_core.md:53-55`). The step needs only "S₀ is the unique F-maximiser on Ω" (or among scored models). The ticket concedes this (`SHARPNESS.md:151-153`, `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:19`) but STATEMENT.md:169-170 still states the bound through 2E[1 − π(S₀)].
  - This is a real weakening. Pairwise (posterior-mode) consistency is known to hold where strong selection consistency fails: Johnson–Rossell make exactly this distinction for growing p.
  - The Markov factor 2 is a further avoidable loss.
- **Whole-lattice NA/TL.** NA(𝓛) and TL(𝓛) are asked on all of [S₀, Q]. GROW L1.2 is deterministic and "no bound on |Q| enters" (`lemmas/L0_core.md:9-14`). Applied pathwise with Q := S̃₀, it gives the same conclusion on {S₀ ⊆ S̃₀} ∩ NA([S₀, S̃₀]) ∩ TL([S₀, S̃₀]).
  - This event contains Sand(Q) ∩ NA(𝓛(Q)) ∩ TL(𝓛(Q)) for every Q. The upper half of the sandwich and the deterministic Q are bookkeeping for later union bounds, not hypotheses of the core.
  - Smaller still: only the visited sets and their deletion children matter (`SHARPNESS.md:130-132`).
- **Cap.** S₀ is already scored when the cap is ≥ |S̃₀ ∖ S₀| − 1 (`lemmas/L0_core.md:11-12`). So (a) holds for any cap ℓ on the extra event {|S̃₀ ∖ S₀| ≤ ℓ + 1}; STATEMENT.md:184-185 records this only under (N-χ).
- **Not necessary.** The ticket's own record shows this: GEN/PXN (Sand fails, conclusion proved; `SHARPNESS.md:30-32`), FAC-PK PATH (non-deletion path reaches S₀; `SHARPNESS.md:133-136`), and probe N1 (NA violated in 90/180 runs, S₀ reached in 178/180; `SHARPNESS.md:168`).

**(b) Conclusion.** The pathwise inclusion and the single data event are the right mode of statement. Two limits remain:
- No delivery: P(U_n) → 0 is not asserted (STATEMENT.md:172), so "= 1 − P(U_n) − o(1)" has content only with a separate delivery theorem.
- No rate is stated, although the inequality is finite-n.

**(c) Cheapest route.** Restate (a) on {S₀ ⊆ S̃₀} ∩ NA([S₀, S̃₀]) ∩ TL([S₀, S̃₀]) and (b) with {S₀ not the unique argmax_Ω F}. Both are one-line changes to `lemmas/L0_core.md:33-37` and `:53-57`.
- The payoff is downstream: a bound |S̃₀ ∖ S₀| ≤ m_n would then need NA only on sets with at most m_n extras, about |Q|^{m_n} sets instead of 2^{|Q∖S₀|}. That count is my own estimate, not worked through.

## W3-RM:RM-F — Theorem RM-F (fixed design)

**(a) Hypotheses.**

- **Exponent in η_A.** STATEMENT.md:37 and :85-86 fix A ≥ 1, so η_A ≤ 0.101 always. The sandwich uses p ≤ n^A only in the ladder count and in the term √(2Aη) (`lemmas/L1_fixed.md:53-61`, `lemmas/L1b_rss.md:53-57`).
  - Both steps work with any a ≥ limsup log p/log n, giving η < η_a = (√(2a+1) − √(2a))². For example η_{1/2} ≈ 0.17, and η → 1 (the BIC level) for p = n^{o(1)}.
  - The ticket notes the mismatch only for the numerics (`SHARPNESS.md:178`). T1-F can keep its own A.
  - Caveat: GROW L3 is visible to me only through the deltas.
- **Tightness of η_a within the sandwich.** Optimising ε₁ reproduces the direct threshold of the heuristic at `SHARPNESS.md:45-48`. The constant is therefore sharp for the sandwich with that Q in a worst-case profile, but not for the conclusion: probe L2 shows entering proxies being deleted (`SHARPNESS.md:167`).
  - (L-gain) is a sup over all l ∉ Q, whereas the ladder consumes only counts. A counting or profile version would be weaker; it is recorded only as a separate ticket (`ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:55-56`).
- **Visibility level.** M_n → ∞ in (V) (STATEMENT.md:95-96) is not needed; the ticket says so at `SHARPNESS.md:27-29`.
  - Truth inclusion compares (δ + σZ)²/(2τ²) with ½ log n + log(2p). In fixed design only s₀·2^{15} pairs (U, j) arise, so Z is negligible.
  - My reading is that δ²_{U,j} ≥ (1 + ε)(log n + 2 log p)τ²_U suffices. This is a constant multiple of log n, the order of the known necessary beta-min for support recovery (Wainwright). The step itself (GROW L3:78-84) is not in the packet, so the exact constant is unverified.
- **Every truth versus some truth.** (V) asks every truth to be visible given any 15 columns. Yang–Wainwright–Jordan need only that some missing truth is visible at each stage, which follows from a restricted eigenvalue. The recorded (JV_b) (`SHARPNESS.md:35-37`, "LOW") is a step in that direction and was not adopted.
- **Size of Q.** The sandwich needs only log|Q| = o(log n) (`lemmas/L1_fixed.md:58-62`). NA-F loses 2 log(q+1) against a slack of (1 − 2h) log n (`lemmas/L2_NAF.md:46-49`). The polylog cap comes from the L0′ toolkit (`lemmas/L2_NAF.md:51-53`, `lemmas/L1_fixed.md:75-76`). R02 P5 (|Q| ≤ n^b; `SHARPNESS.md:100-103`) is the known route.
- **Lattice condition (N).**
  - The constant 1 in (N-χ) is tight for the common-χ² bound. Heuristic N1 puts failure of whole-lattice NA near 2.5 log n (`SHARPNESS.md:121-129`), so the route is within a constant of its own limit.
  - h < ½ is tight for a union bound over near-orthogonal directions.
  - Necessity for the conclusion is unsupported: no counterexample exists and N1 points the other way.
  - The Gram-floor remark at `SHARPNESS.md:137-140` can be made uniform: a union over the C(q, e) sets with e extras gives min_e D_e ≲ σ² log q/φ₀² ≪ log n at every lattice set. So an improving deletion always exists; only best-move control (swaps and additions) is missing. This is my own extension of that remark.
- **(BM-Q).** The term |Q| is the price of a 2^{|Q|} union (`lemmas/L1_fixed.md:78-88`), a route artefact of the same kind.
- **Inherited from T03♯ (STATEMENT.md:91-92).** These are narrower than the literature:
  - fixed s₀, whereas Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart, Narisetty–He and Yang–Wainwright–Jordan allow growing sparsity;
  - p ≤ n^A, whereas those results reach log p = o(n);
  - BM-∞ with a diverging factor, against constant-level beta-min.

**(b) Conclusion.** Consistency of the reported model with no rate. In NA-F the 1/log n failure probability is an artefact of the log log n slack (`lemmas/L2_NAF.md:34-40`); a polynomial rate is available there. The overall rate is limited by [POST], which is given only as O_P.

**(c) Routes.**
- Easy: η_a with the actual exponent; constant-level (V); (JV_b) in the block-relative variant.
- Medium: polynomial Q (R02 P5, estimated at 3–5 proof days); counting form of (L).
- Hard: dropping (N). The natural template is the "no local mode except S₀" argument of the dimension-free-mixing paper that extends Yang–Wainwright–Jordan. As I recall it relies on a prior penalty of κ log p per variable with κ large; I am not certain of the details. The shipped ½ log n slack does not supply that penalty, which is exactly why the lattice must stay small.
- Hard, upstream: growing s₀ and super-polynomial p.

## W3-RM:RM-R — Theorem RM-R (Gaussian rows)

**(a) Hypotheses.**

- **(D-R) is required even for small lattices** (STATEMENT.md:146-156, :188). NA(𝓛), TL(𝓛) and [POST] do not involve the partition, so for them Form R is Form F conditional on X. Lemma B8b already argues this way for TL (`lemmas/L4_formR.md:43-46`).
  - Lemma B4 applied conditionally on X ∈ A_X gives NA under (N-χ) with no direction condition. The inputs are the sample floor c/2 on Q ∪ {l} and the sure energy bound (`lemmas/L4_formR.md:10-13`).
  - Likewise NA-F applies with an X-measurable 𝒰_n, since ε is independent of X.
  - So "(D-R) or (N-χ) or sample (N-D)(i) with probability → 1" suffices. As stated, (D-loc) reaches only ν_n = o(log n) and frames p·n^{o(1)}, against (1 − δ₀) log n and h < ½ in Form F. The h < ½ analogue is recorded as LOW (`REPORT.md:145-146`).
- **(L-gain-R).** The leak C_L n^{−1/2} (STATEMENT.md:140) can be any o(√(log n/n)) with the same proof (`SHARPNESS.md:52-53`, R04 F6): ‖m‖ = o(√log n) costs n^{o(1)} against a slack of n^{−1/2+o(1)} (`lemmas/L3_SANDR.md:235-237`). This was not adopted.
  - It is still a factor log n short of Form F in gain (n·leak² = O(1) versus η log n). The η-budget version (R02 P3b) is medium.
- **(L-loc-R).** The rate o((n log n)^{−1/4}) can be o((log n/n)^{1/4}) by carrying e^{ω_n/2} = n^{o(1)} (`SHARPNESS.md:109`, `lemmas/L3b_rate.md:29-32`). Not adopted.
  - The clause pays for "any X-measurable partition", which is more than the method's single clustering rule needs. Removing it is hard (`SHARPNESS.md:110-111`).
- **Signal.** Setting R fixes θ_lo > 0 and (V-R) asks PTV(a₀) with a₀ fixed (STATEMENT.md:43, :135-136). That gives δ² ≍ n, where Form F needs only M_n log n.
  - The O(h_n) sample comparison (`lemmas/L3_SANDR.md:134-139`) needs only a_n√(n/log n) → ∞.
  - TL can use Lemma B3 with (BM-Q) conditionally on X.
  - Whether POST-R (APP L1_POST(b)) survives θ_min → 0 I cannot judge from the packet.
- **Gaussian rows.** They are essential to the spherical-tail step (`lemmas/L3_SANDR.md:214-222`). A sub-Gaussian version would be new work.
- **Fixed s₀ and p ≤ n^A.** Same remarks as for RM-F.

**(b) Conclusion.** Same as RM-0 under the joint law. Uniformity over all X-measurable partitions is stronger than needed. No rate and no delivery.

**(c) Routes.**
- Easy: add (N-χ) and sample (N-D)(i) as alternatives to (D-R) by conditioning on X; leak rate o(√(log n/n)); localisation rate o((log n/n)^{1/4}).
- Medium: η-budget leak; PTV(a_n) with vanishing θ_min.
- Hard: removing localisation; non-Gaussian rows.
