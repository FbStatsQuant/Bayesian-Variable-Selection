**Lemma X1.1** (X1_representation.md:22-35)
- ‖e‖ is independent of e₁, and e₁ is uniform on the sphere of V (dimension n−1), so ‖e‖²/σ² ~ χ²_{n−1}.
- Given e, f is still standard Gaussian in V. Hence η = fᵀe₁ ~ N(0,1) and f_⊥ (standard Gaussian in V ∩ e₁^⊥, dimension n−2) are conditionally independent, and φ² ~ χ²_{n−2}.
- The conditional law of (η, φ) is free of (e₁, ‖e‖), so ‖e‖, η, φ are mutually independent.
- Given (e₁, e₂, ‖e‖, η, φ), which are functions of (e, f), the u_j remain iid standard Gaussian in V. In the orthonormal split span(e₁) ⊕ span(e₂) ⊕ V₃, the components ξ_j, a_j, u_j^⊥ are independent, with ξ_j, a_j ~ N(0,1) and u_j^⊥ standard Gaussian in V₃ (dimension n−3 = N).
- The conditional law of (ξ, a, W) is therefore N(0,1)^{⊗2p} ⊗ W_p(N, I) whatever the conditioning values. This gives both the independence of (ξ, a, W) from (‖e‖, η, φ) and the mutual independence of ξ, a, W.
- The quantifier order is right: the frame is random, but the conditional law does not depend on it.

**Lemma X1.3 and drift identities** (X1_representation.md:62-70)
- Sherman–Morrison: (NI + ℓ²φ²11ᵀ)⁻¹ = N⁻¹(I − c/(N+kc)·11ᵀ) with c = ℓ²φ², and c/(N+kc) = 1/(κ+k) = γ_k.
- N(A⁰)⁻¹ṽ1 has entries ξ_j + ℓη − γ_k(s_T + kℓη). Since 1 − kγ_k = κγ_k and κℓη = Nη/(ℓφ²) = ϱ, this equals ξ_j − γ_k(s_T − ϱ) = ξ_j − x_T.
- (A⁰⁻¹)_jj = N⁻¹(1−γ_k), hence √N√(1−γ_k)ω_j = ξ_j − x_T.
- Drift: (k+κ)x_T − ξ_j = (k−1+κ)x_{T∖j} gives x_{T∖j} = x_T − (ξ_j − x_T)/(k−1+κ). The addition form x_{T∪l} = x_T + (ξ_l − x_T)/(k+1+κ) follows the same way.
- κ and ϱ are functions of (η, φ) only, so they are T-independent and independent of ξ by X1.1.
