## W3-POST:Lemma-1a′ (design-free size tail)

Statement at `lemmas/L1a_size_tail.md:9-19`, proof at `:21-42`. I take the lemma as correct. The counterexample and the proof modifications below are my own sketches; I have not checked them numerically.

### (a) Hypotheses

**Exact representation X_Tθ = μ (`L1a_size_tail.md:10-11`) is stronger than the proof needs.**
- The only step that uses it is the "changed step" at `:27-31`, where ϑ = θ is plugged into the variational form of Q^c_T.
- Any ϑ can be plugged in. With r := μ − X_Tϑ this gives Q^c_T ≤ ‖ε_c‖² + 2ε_cᵀUᵀr + ‖r‖² + ϑᵀ(G_T+I)ϑ/n.
- The cross term is N(0, 4σ*²‖Uᵀr‖²), so it is O_P(1) when ‖r‖ = O(1); add its absolute value to Λ′_n at `:35-37`.
- The weaker sufficient condition is therefore a bounded noise-free ridge bias of the reference: B_T(μ) := min_ϑ {‖μ − X_Tϑ‖² + ϑᵀ(G_T+I)ϑ/n} ≤ ρ̄.
- This covers approximate sparsity (as in Song–Liang's approximate-sparsity setting and Narisetty–He's Condition 4.3, both as quoted in `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:41,74-75`). It also covers large coefficients in directions where μ has negligible energy.
- The ticket already names this route ("bounded penalised approximation", `L1a_size_tail.md:55`; `REPORT.md:185`) but does not carry it out.

**Coefficient bound ‖θ‖² ≤ nM′: some control of this type is necessary; the specific form is not.**
The ticket records this as open (`L1a_size_tail.md:50-55`; `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:16`). I believe the following design settles it at leading order.
- Take p = k + 2 with k = ⌈C log n⌉. Let x₃,…,x_{k+2} be orthogonal to each other and to x₁, u := Σ_{j≥3} x_j, and x₂ ∝ x₁ − ηu with η = n⁻².
- Set μ = (b/√k)u, so ‖μ‖² = b²(n−1) and (E) holds.
- T = {1,2} represents μ exactly, but only with ‖θ‖² ≈ 2b²/(η²k) ≫ n.
- T′ = {3,…,k+2} represents μ with ‖θ′‖² = b².
- Every model with at most k/2 columns of T′ has ridge bias at least about b²n/2. The x₁−x₂ direction has Gram eigenvalue ≈ η²kn/2, so the ridge prior shrinks it to nothing.
- Such a model's marginal likelihood is e^{−cn} relative to T′. Prior and Occam factors are only e^{O(log² n)}.
- Choosing C so that k/2 exceeds the cutoff m^T_n(½ log n) at t = 2 (which is O(log n)) gives π(|S| > m^T_n) → 1.
- So the conclusion with t = |T| fails when only ‖θ‖² ≤ nM′ is dropped. The numerical example L1a′(e) survived only because no larger model fitted better there.
- The stated form (‖μ‖² + ‖θ‖²)/n is still loose. It matches B_T(μ) up to a constant only on eigen-directions with λ_i ≳ 1/n; B_T(μ) is the exact quantity.

**(E) ‖μ‖² ≤ nM.**
- It is used for the scale event 𝓑_n and the constants b±, κ, c_IG (`:23`; `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:15`).
- I cannot tell from the material how c♯ depends on M, so I cannot judge whether slowly growing M_n is admissible.

**(G) Gaussian noise.**
- The numerator identity (`lem:num`, `:22`) needs the true density.
- Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart (2015) also assume Gaussian errors for their dimension result. I see no cheap relaxation.

**No use of p ≤ n^A, fixed s₀ or any eigenvalue floor.** I find nothing to remove there.

### (b) Conclusion

**Tail in L (`:16`) is weaker than the proof ingredients give.**
- The term C′_Λ/log(√L/c♯) comes from Markov's inequality applied to Λ′_n (`:41`).
- Λ′_n is a centred χ²_{n−1} deviation over 2√n plus bounded terms (`:35-36`). Laurent–Massart gives Λ′_n ≤ C + √x + O(x/√n) with probability at least 1 − 2e^{−x}.
- Taking x = log L and applying Markov to Θ^{(1)} at level L/(c♯e^{Λ′}) gives P(Θ > L) ≤ P(𝓑_nᶜ) + C_ε L^{−(1−ε)} for log L ≲ √n.
- This is a polynomial tail in place of the logarithmic one. For example, P(π(|S| > m♯_n) > n^{−1/4}) drops from O(1/log n) to n^{−1/4+o(1)}.
- It also yields an in-expectation bound E π(|S| > m^T_n(ω)) ≲ e^{−(1−ε)ω} + P(𝓑_nᶜ). The current log tail gives only O(1/ω).
- That is the mode of Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart's dimension theorem (in expectation, uniform). It would feed `REPORT.md:186` and the bound P(HPM ≠ S₀) ≤ 2Eδ_n at `STATEMENT.md:82`.
- The term (8+24M/σ*²)/(n−1) looks like a Chebyshev bound. If 𝓑_n is the s_y scale event, as `ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:15` suggests, Gaussian concentration should make it e^{−cn}. I cannot confirm this without [P1].

**Normalisation (n+1)^{t/2} and the single reference model.**
- `:36` discards the actual D_T in favour of (n+1)^t. Keeping D_T^{1/2} helps when T is rank-deficient or near-collinear (`REPORT.md:185` notes this).
- `:40` uses Z ≥ ϖ(T)m(T) for one T. Summing over a family 𝒯 of admissible references replaces w(t)(n+1)^{−t/2} by Σ_{T∈𝒯} w(|T|)D_T^{−1/2}. All share the same E′, so Λ′ is common.
- In the Proposition-D design (p duplicates of one truth) this removes the t·log₂ p part of the cutoff entirely.

**Cutoff order.**
- Excess O(log n) over t is necessary uniformly over designs for an O(n^{−1/2}) tail (Proposition D, `:63-65`).
- It is a feature of the coded prior, whose size-class masses decay like 2^{−k}. Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart get an excess of order s₀ only under priors with p^{−A₄} decay per added variable. As I recall, BB(1,p) does not satisfy that condition.
- The constant gap, [(A+½)t+1]/log 2 against ½/log 2 (`:66`), is genuine.
- The n^{(t+1)/2} factor comes from the first-moment numerator (E[m(S)/f₀] = 1). That step ignores the fact that competing models pay their own σ²-integration factor n^{−1/2} and an Occam factor D_S^{−1/2} ≤ n^{−1/2} on typical data.
- Recovering those factors needs a truncated-moment argument uniform over all S with no design condition. That is new work.

### (c) Cheapest route to a stronger version

Three changes inside the existing proof, each under a page:
1. Replace "X_Tθ = μ, ‖θ‖² ≤ nM′" by B_T(μ) ≤ ρ̄ (edit `:27-37`; one extra Gaussian cross term).
2. Replace Markov on Λ′_n by χ² concentration (edit `:41`). This gives the polynomial L-tail and an expectation corollary.
3. Keep D_T and allow a reference family (edit `:36`, `:40`).

Risk is low for all three. The counterexample above can be recorded as showing that ridge-bias control of the reference cannot be dropped. Closing the cutoff constant is harder and is a separate task.
