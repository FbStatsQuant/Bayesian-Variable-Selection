## Claim W2-UNI:F-UNI (Proposition F-UNI, `lemmas/L5_delivery.md:15-21`; premises `:8-13`)

F-UNI is a reduction: its proof (`L5_delivery.md:23-34`) is two deterministic inclusions plus one Markov step. The statement compresses these into hypotheses and a conclusion that are respectively stronger and weaker than what the proof delivers.

### (a) Hypotheses

**(P) E[1 − π(S₀)] → 0 (`L5_delivery.md:19`) is stronger than needed.**
- It is used only at `L5_delivery.md:32-33`: "if π(S₀ | y) > ½ its score strictly exceeds every competitor", followed by Markov to get the 2E[1 − π(S₀)] term.
- The proof therefore needs only P(π(S₀ | y) ≤ ½) → 0. More precisely it needs mode consistency: P(S₀ is the strict score maximiser over Ω) → 1.
- Since 1 − π ∈ [0, 1], (P) is equivalent to π(S₀) → 1 in probability, which is strictly stronger than mode consistency. A posterior with π(S₀) → 0.6, or one where S₀ is the mode while supersets keep non-vanishing total mass, satisfies the weaker condition but not (P).
- The weaker condition is also necessary given the mechanism: Ŝ⁺ is the best scored model, so if S₀ is beaten by a scored competitor then Ŝ⁺ ≠ S₀.
- Practical gain inside this ticket is modest, because UNI-P already gives π(S₀) → 1 under LSF. The gain is for classes where LSF fails or only pairwise Bayes-factor control is available. It would also let HPM-type results be plugged in directly without full posterior concentration. For comparison, Johnson–Rossell and Narisetty–He prove the stronger π(S₀) → 1.

**(S) full-lattice NA(𝓛_n) ∩ TL(𝓛_n) with deterministic Q_n (`L5_delivery.md:17-18`) is stronger than needed.**
- Step 3 uses (S) only to get P(𝒦_nᶜ) → 0, where 𝒦_n is "LS0 scores S₀" (`L5_delivery.md:28-31`).
- Determinism of Q_n is never used in this proof. C-GROW L1.2 is a pathwise statement "for every centre C ∈ 𝓛" on NA ∩ TL. Determinism is needed upstream for the union bounds, not here.
- The whole lattice [S₀, Q_n] is not needed. It suffices that the emitted start mode S̃₀ ⊇ S₀ and that NA/TL hold on [S₀, S̃₀] (a random Q = S̃₀, automatically in Ω). Weaker still, it suffices that the realised greedy path strictly improves to S₀ within the cap.
- This matters, and the ticket shows it. For exact CS above exponent 1/6, full-lattice NA fails (SHARP) while FAC-B still delivers by a path argument (`REPORT.md:165`, `L4_k1.md:74-75`, `L6_frontier.md:32-37`).
- As stated, F-UNI therefore cannot serve as the delivery reduction for the path tickets in the absorption table (`L6_frontier.md:39-45`). Each of them will need its own delivery proposition unless (S) is replaced by a path form.
- Some path condition is necessary for 𝒦_n: if no strictly improving move exists at some point of the path, LS0 stops and does not score S₀.
- "Every maximiser" in Sand is forced only because the emitted centre is "some maximiser" (e4/N3 OPEN, `L5_delivery.md:39-40`). I cannot judge from the material whether a tie-break rule would allow a weaker Sand.

**(V) under (R), λ_min(Σ_n) > 0 (`L5_delivery.md:16`) appears redundant.**
- Step 1 uses only Var = Σ_jj = 1 > 0 so that no column is dropped, and says explicitly "no full-column-rank condition is needed (rank-deficient subsets are allowed)" (`L5_delivery.md:23-26`).
- Unit diagonal is part of setting (R), so (V) is vacuous there. This is cosmetic, since (S) and (P) would fail for unidentifiable designs anyway.
- I could not see FACA L7 Step 1 itself, so I cannot rule out a hidden use there.
- Gaussianity of rows and noise is likewise unused in this proof. Step 1 needs only almost surely nonconstant columns, and Steps 2–3 are law-free.
- F-UNI could therefore be stated for any joint law of (X, Y). That would be directly reusable for the "non-Gaussian rows" open item (`REPORT.md:182`).

**Cap premise a_n = ℓ_n + |S̃₀| (`L5_delivery.md:9`).**
- By C-GROW L1.2(ii), S₀ is scored whenever the cap is at least |C ∖ S₀| − 1, so the proof needs only a_n ≥ |S̃₀ ∖ S₀| − 1.
- The |S̃₀| term is necessary in order: paths are one deletion per move, and order-p deletion paths occur (`REPORT.md:4-5`). The ℓ_n + s₀ + 1 slack is not needed, which is harmless.

**Other premises.**
- "s₀ ≤ K eventually" (`L5_delivery.md:33-34`) is already implied for every n by 𝓛_n ⊆ Ω.
- The implementation premises (EX-A, IDEAL-W, MACH, allocation inequalities, `L5_delivery.md:8-13`) cannot be assessed for necessity from the material.

### (b) Conclusion

**Only a qualitative limit is stated, though the proof gives an explicit inequality.**
- The statement gives P(Ŝ⁺ = S₀) → 1 (`L5_delivery.md:20`).
- The proof gives, for every n: P(Ŝ⁺ ≠ S₀) ≤ 2E[1 − π(S₀)] + P((Sand ∩ NA ∩ TL)ᶜ) (`L5_delivery.md:33`, with P(no output) = 0).
- With the weakened hypotheses this becomes P(Ŝ⁺ ≠ S₀) ≤ P(S₀ not the strict mode over Ω) + P(𝒦_nᶜ).
- Stating the inequality gives rates wherever the inputs have them. Examples are the 5/log n event of U-LOC (`L3_ULOC.md:121-122`) and the n^{−1/4+o(1)} sandwich bounds (`L2_k0.md:122-123`).
- It also gives uniformity over any design or parameter class on which the right side is uniformly small. That uniformity is what FACA L5.0's every-sequence argument re-derives by hand (`L4_k1.md:36-38`, `:62-64`).

**Conclusions the proof yields but the statement omits.**
- On output ∩ 𝒦_n ∩ {π(S₀) > ½}, the reported model equals the exact posterior mode over Ω, so search error vanishes.
- On Sand ∩ NA ∩ TL, LS0's endpoint is S₀, reached after exactly |S̃₀ ∖ S₀| deletions without `local_limit` (C-GROW L1.2(i)–(ii)). This appears in UNI-1's statement (`STATEMENT.md:123-126`) but not in F-UNI.

**Mode of convergence.**
- Joint-law convergence in probability cannot be improved to a quenched statement with any gain; for bounded indicators the two are equivalent.
- Almost-sure convergence is out of reach with 1/log n input rates.
- The exact claim P(no output) = 0 is sharp.

**Scope relative to the literature.**
- The conclusion concerns only the reported model found by deterministic LS0. Nothing is said about the particle approximation itself, such as total-variation closeness of the sampler output or inclusion probabilities.
- Yang–Wainwright–Jordan-type mixing results do address the sampler's law. That would be a genuinely different and hard result, outside this reduction.

### (c) Cheapest route to a stronger version

Restate F-UNI in three layers. All are already contained in `L5_delivery.md:23-34`; no new mathematics is required.

1. **Deterministic core, any law with almost surely nonconstant columns.** P(no output) = 0, and {output} ∩ 𝒦_n ∩ {S₀ strict score maximiser over Ω} ⊆ {Ŝ⁺ = S₀}.
2. **Inequality.** P(Ŝ⁺ ≠ S₀) ≤ P(𝒦_nᶜ) + P(S₀ not the strict maximiser), stated for each n.
3. **Sufficient conditions as corollaries.**
   - (S) ⇒ P(𝒦_nᶜ) ≤ P((Sand ∩ NA ∩ TL)ᶜ), with Q allowed to be random, e.g. Q = S̃₀.
   - (P) ⇒ P(S₀ not the strict maximiser) ≤ 2E[1 − π(S₀)].
   - Drop λ_min(Σ_n) > 0 from (V)(R), after confirming FACA L7 Step 1 has no hidden use.

Difficulty is low: a re-statement plus one check of FACA L7 Step 1 and of the definition of Ŝ⁺ under ties. The payoff is that the path tickets (FAC-B/S5/X/PK) and any future non-Gaussian or mode-consistency result plug into one delivery proposition instead of each re-proving delivery.
