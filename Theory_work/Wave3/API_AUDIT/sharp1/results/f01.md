## W1-N1:Thm-N1 — Theorem N1 (clause M1(b)), `Theory_work/Wave1/W1-N1/STATEMENT.md:98-104`

**Summary.** The core conclusion is an exact identity and cannot be improved as such. The statement is nevertheless narrower than what its own three lemmas (B, P, F) deliver, in four places that cost a few lines each. These are gains in generality, not in rate.

### (a) Hypotheses

**Necessary (no weakening I can justify):**

- **Evidence weight Ẑ_r inside the expectation.** The unweighted average is biased by up to 0.16 on the ticket's own cases (`lemmas/R_remarks.md:12-13`).
- **q_φ-invariance of the round kernel at every grid point, and the final reweighting to φ = 1.** Invariance is used in the induction step (`lemmas/L2_post_terminal.md:39-40`) and in the post-terminal step (`:44-45`). The negative controls NC1 and NC2 break the identity when either is removed (`lemmas/R_remarks.md:18-19`).
- **Grid, N_e, L and h frozen before the final stream.** NC3 shows a post-hoc h fails (`lemmas/R_remarks.md:20-22`). More generally, adaptive-tempering SMC evidence estimators are consistent but not unbiased in general (Beskos–Jasra–Kantas–Thiery 2016, as I recall it).
- **Potential run, START and EX-A.** The identity is linear in Z̄₀ and is an exact equality, so each is needed for exactness (`STATEMENT.md:75, 86-88`; `lemmas/R_remarks.md:23-24`).
- **Data premises.** The regression structure at `STATEMENT.md:20-25` (n ≥ 3, cap K, g = n, BB(1,p)) defines the method. The proof uses only Ω finite, m > 0, ϖ > 0, which the ticket already says (`lemmas/N1_theorem.md:29`).

**Stronger than the proof needs:**

1. **Deterministic uniform bound sup_{S,c}|h| ≤ B_h (`STATEMENT.md:62-64`).** It is used only for integrability in Lemma F (`lemmas/L3_freezing.md:34-36, 41-42`).
   - Ω is finite, so h(·;𝓒) = Σ_{S∈Ω} h(S;𝓒)·1{·=S} with σ(𝓒)-measurable coefficients.
   - N1 for the |Ω| deterministic indicators, plus "taking out what is known", gives N1 for every such h.
   - The condition E[1_{E₀} max_S |h(S;𝓒)|] < ∞ suffices for the ordinary conditional expectation.
   - With no integrability at all, the identity still holds for the conditional expectation under the regular conditional law, because Ẑ_r ≤ Z₀e^{max d} is a data constant (`L3_freezing.md:35`).
   - "Jointly measurable" reduces to "c ↦ h(S;c) measurable for each S".
   - I see no counterexample that makes the uniform bound necessary.

2. **SI: stream 9 + r independent of streams 0–9 (`STATEMENT.md:80-82`).**
   - The proof uses only U_r ⊥ σ(𝓒) (`L3_freezing.md:10` "hence of 𝓒", and `:37`).
   - So either weaken the premise to U_r ⊥ 𝓒, or keep SI and strengthen the conclusion as in (b)(ii) below.

3. **KERNEL: "uniform state-independent order" (`STATEMENT.md:76-78`), reversibility, and the iid/product parts of (R1)–(R3).**
   - Lemma P needs only that each elementary kernel leaves q_φ invariant and that the order is drawn independently of the entering state. Any mixture of products is then invariant (`L2_post_terminal.md:19-20`).
   - Uniformity of the Fisher–Yates order (N9 row 5, used at `lemmas/L1_bridge.md:96-99`) is therefore not needed for N1.
   - N1 is a first-moment identity, so only per-particle marginal laws enter: E[q̂₀ f] = q₀ f (`L2_post_terminal.md:31-32`), marginal ancestor probabilities (`:21`), and the per-particle round law (`:22`).
   - The product-law argument (`L1_bridge.md:101-107`) and the conditional independence of ancestors (`:93-94`) are surplus for this theorem.
   - Benefit: N1 inherits less from the "PROVED (candidate)" N9, and holds for any unbiased resampling scheme.

4. **R′ = 4k (`STATEMENT.md:30`; `L1_bridge.md:24`).** Only the event {r ≤ R′} with R′ σ(𝓒)-measurable is used. This is cosmetic.

### (b) Conclusion

- **Rate and regime.** It is an equality, for each q, for every admissible (X, y), every N_e ≥ 1, L ≥ 0 and every frozen grid (`STATEMENT.md:104`). There is no rate, p-versus-n range, beta-min or sparsity condition to sharpen.
- **Literature.** Results of the Johnson–Rossell, Narisetty–He, Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart and Yang–Wainwright–Jordan type are not comparable here. They would enter only through δ₁ in Corollary E or through variance clauses outside this claim. The right benchmark is the unbiasedness of unnormalised Feynman–Kac particle measures (Del Moral 2004; Del Moral–Doucet–Jasra 2006; the property used by Andrieu–Doucet–Holenstein 2010). N1 matches it, plus the routine post-terminal invariant-kernel extension.

Three extensions already follow from the ticket's lemmas:

- **(i) Which populations.** Lemma P is proved for every s ≥ 0 and every t ≤ T (`L2_post_terminal.md:26-27, 42-45`). N1 states only s = L + q, q = 1..4 (`STATEMENT.md:98, 101`). The same proof gives:
  - the identity for the post-stage-T population and each of the L burn-in rounds;
  - any σ(𝓒)-measurable weighting over the L + 5 post-terminal populations;
  - the tempered identities E[Ẑ_{r,t} q̂_t(f) | 𝓒] = Z_t q_{φ_t}(f).
  
  Burn-in is therefore not needed for unbiasedness. Whether including those rounds helps the variance is not something I can judge from the material.

- **(ii) Conditioning σ-field.** Under SI as written, the same freezing step with V = (streams 0–9) in place of 𝓒 gives
  E[1_{E₀} Ẑ_r H_r(h(·;V)) | V] = 1_{E₀} Z π(h(·;V)).
  This is strictly stronger by the tower property. It also admits h measurable in streams 0–9, which the 𝓒-conditional form must exclude (`STATEMENT.md:64-65`).

- **(iii) Cross-fitted h.** If the final streams are mutually independent (IND-T, not discharged here), conditioning on V and on the other islands' streams U_{−r} gives
  E[W_r H_r(h) | V, U_{−r}] = π(h) for h = h(·; V, U_{−r}).
  This yields exact orthogonality of the island errors. It also makes admissible a leave-one-island-out version of the "frequency of the returned HPM" summary, which is currently excluded (`STATEMENT.md:120-121`; `R_remarks.md:20-22`).

**Not counted against this claim.** A second-moment bound, such as E[W_r² | 𝓒] or the conditional variance of W_r H_r(h), is what would make N1 quantitatively useful. Results of that kind exist for general SMC under mixing assumptions (Cérou–Del Moral–Guyader 2011; Beskos–Crisan–Jasra 2014). For this tempered variable-selection kernel it would need new work. I am unsure whether Yang–Wainwright–Jordan mixing transfers to the coded kernels and tempered targets. This belongs to clauses (f), (g) and (i).
