## W2-FAC-B:Thm-FAC-CS

**Overall.** The theorem is tight on the LS0 cap and on the swap claim, but several hypotheses are stronger than the proof uses. The cheapest gain is replacing the fixed coefficient vector by an n-dependent one at the √(log n/n) level. The design class and p > n/2 are the hard frontier. I traced the lemma steps for these points but did not re-derive constants.

### (a) Hypotheses

**1. Fixed ϑ (STATEMENT.md:76-78) is stronger than needed.** ϑ enters the proof in only three places, and each needs only a √(log n/n) scale:
- **Start mode, positive pairs** (L2_start.md:54-58). The step needs (n−1)c_FS to dominate 2σ√(n−1)G_n and (n/2)c₂ > 3 log n. Both hold if the smallest nonzero |c(U,l)| is at least C√(log n/n) for a large C. Zero pairs (L2_start.md:60-65) do not involve ϑ.
- **Truth moves** (L4_path.md:49-54). The step needs λ_θ|ϑ|_min ≫ H̄ + L_n and ¼τ_θ² > log(2p) + ½log(n+1) + 1. Both hold if |ϑ|_min ≥ Cσ√(log n/((1−ρ)n)).
- **Posterior input** (L5_assembly.md:25). T03♯ is invoked only through BM-∞, i.e. θ*²_min·n/log n → ∞, together with ‖μ‖² ≤ Mn.

All other uses are bounds that hold for bounded ϑ_n (L1_cs_reduction.md:100-102, L3_event.md:23, L4_path.md:168). So the theorem should hold for ϑ = ϑ_n bounded with |ϑ_n|_min·√(n/log n) → ∞ and the nonzero |c(U,l)| also ≫ √(log n/n). This rests on T03♯'s hypotheses being exactly those listed at L5_assembly.md:25.

This matters because √(log p/n) is the standard beta-min level in Narisetty–He, Yang–Wainwright–Jordan and Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart. It is also the information-theoretic order for exact support recovery (Wainwright 2009). Fixed ϑ leaves the whole range between √(log n/n) and order 1 uncovered.

**2. (FS) (STATEMENT.md:79-84) is not necessary for (C3)/(C4).** It is used only in the start-mode lemma (L2_start.md:33-35, 72-74); the path and assembly lemmas do not use it (L4_path.md:10, L2_start.md:93-94). Three levels:
- **Free weakening.** The start-mode lemma only needs c(U,l) ≠ 0 for pairs realisable in the actual partition (U ⊆ C_b∖{l}), not for all V ⊆ S₀ and u ≤ 15. With truths in distinct blocks this collapses to V ∈ {S₀, S₀∖{l}}. The statement concedes "sufficient, not necessary" (STATEMENT.md:84) but does not state the weaker form.
- **Modest further weakening.** If ϑ(V) = 0 for some V ⊊ S₀, only nulls in truth-containing blocks are affected, at most 15·s₀ of them. The path lemma already tolerates a bounded deterministic set of outside nulls with |ξ| ≤ L_n (the role of N₀; L3_event.md:22, L4_path.md:134, 148). Enlarging N₀ to this set looks feasible but needs a reworked start-mode lemma.
- **Necessary for (C1) as stated.** ϑ(S₀) ≠ 0 is needed whenever a truth-free block exists: otherwise c(∅,l) = ρϑ(S₀) = 0 for its nulls and the start mode is not S*. The R09 control (REPORT.md:125-127) suggests (C3)/(C4) still survive there.

**3. ρ fixed (STATEMENT.md:73).** Every constant (n₀, C_ρ, c_D, c_FS) is continuous in ρ on compact subsets of (0,1). ρ = ρ_n ∈ [ρ_lo, ρ_hi] with a uniform (FS) margin is therefore free.
- **ρ_n → 1** is a real limit for the stated log-n margin when s₀ = 1: c_D = ½ − 1/(s₀+1+(1−ρ)/ρ) → 0 (L4_path.md:182-183).
- **ρ_n → 0** is plausible down to ρ_n ≫ √(log n/n), which the full start needs (c(∅,l) = ρϑ(S₀)). It is not automatic.

**4. p ≤ ⌊n/2⌋ (STATEMENT.md:75).** This is the method's size limit K, not a weakness of the proof. It is used at three points:
- the feasibility step |S| < p in L2_start.md:53-54;
- the residual degrees of freedom N ≥ n/2 − 1 in L3_event.md:43-44;
- the bound M_T ≤ 1 + o(1) in L4_path.md:46-47, 149.

Exact-Gram compound symmetry already forces p ≤ n−1 (STATEMENT.md:74), so the only missing window is n/2 < p ≤ n−1. Necessity there is unknown. One finite-n data set breaks the cap (REPORT.md:103-109); no asymptotic counterexample exists.

**5. Exact-Gram compound symmetry is the dominant restriction.** It is one fixed correlation structure, whereas the cited literature works under restricted-eigenvalue or compatibility conditions on general designs. The proof needs it for the exact one-dimensional reduction (L1_cs_reduction.md:84-85). The ticket's own numerics show the same mechanism on S5 and factor designs (REPORT.md:110-124). Nothing indicates the conclusion fails off this class.

**6. s₀ fixed (STATEMENT.md:47-48, 76).** The literature allows growing sparsity (Yang–Wainwright–Jordan roughly s₀ ≲ n/log p; Castillo et al. likewise). Here s₀ enters through:
- a union bound over s₀ + 16 normals (L3_event.md:42);
- the terms s₀L_n in the centre (L4_path.md:115-117, 164);
- the size of the (FS) list.

c_D improves as s₀ grows. Slow growth (s₀ = o(√log n) as written, more with |s_{S₀}| ≲ √s₀·L_n) looks reachable inside the path lemma. I cannot tell whether T03♯ permits growing s₀.

**7. Gaussian noise (STATEMENT.md:46).** It is used essentially, in three ways:
- independence of g and r (L1_cs_reduction.md:22-24);
- sign symmetry for the Rademacher comparison (L3_event.md:51-53);
- Gaussian extreme-value spacings (L3_event.md:55-65).

A sub-Gaussian version would need a different end-phase argument.

**8. LS0 cap.** This is necessary and tight in both directions on the LS0 route: a cap ≥ m₀−1 suffices and the legacy cap fails when m₀ ≥ ℓ_n+2 (STATEMENT.md:121-123, L5_assembly.md:38-40).

### (b) Conclusion

- **(C1), (C2)(i), (iii), (iv)** are exact characterisations (unique maximiser, exact move, exact move count). Nothing to improve.
- **(C2)(ii), swaps.** Claiming only "below the chosen deletion" is sharp: the stronger log-n margin was refuted by an O_P(1) counterexample (REPORT.md:15-17).
- **(C2)(ii), addition constant.** c = min{0.29, c_D/2} (STATEMENT.md:114, L4_path.md:17) is loose by about a factor 2.
  - Achievable: the end-phase bounds give −c_D·log n + o(log n) at |I| = 1 and better elsewhere (L4_path.md:191-195). ε_b can be shrunk in the bulk.
  - Not beatable: for p ≍ n the configuration T = S₀ ∪ {one extreme null}, adding the opposite extreme null, attains about −(½ − a/(s₀+1+κ_∞))·log n with a = log p/log n.
  - So any c < c_D holds and c_D cannot be exceeded at a = 1. This is an immaterial constant.
- **Mode and rate.** P(Ŝ⁺ = S₀) → 1 is the right mode. The implicit rate is only about 1/log n, because the slack is L_n = log log n (L2_start.md:43-49; L3_event.md:46, 50, 53).
  - For (C1) the margins are order n against log n, and ¼log n for zero pairs. Thresholds of order √(c log n) would give failure probability O(n^{−c′}) at no cost.
  - For the path, a polynomial rate is plausible since all margins are ≍ log n. It needs the bulk/end split (m₁ = ⌈K₁L_n⌉) and the m₁δ_e bookkeeping redone (L4_path.md:162-164). That is not modest.
  - Yang–Wainwright–Jordan and Narisetty–He give polynomially small failure probabilities in their settings.
- **Scaling range.** log p/log n up to 1 is the full range available to the method on this design class, far beyond the companion ticket's 1/6. The literature's p ≫ n regime is excluded by the exact Gram, not by the argument.

### (c) Cheapest routes

1. **Easy: n-dependent coefficients.** Restate (θ)/(FS) with ϑ_n bounded, |ϑ_n|_min·√(n/log n) → ∞, and min nonzero |c(U,l)|·√(n/log n) → ∞. Change three lines:
   - L2_start.md:54-58 (c_FS becomes c_FS,n);
   - L4_path.md:50-52 (replace the constant c_θ by ¼τ_θ² ≥ 1.6 log n);
   - L5_assembly.md:25 (already BM-∞).
2. **Easy:** restrict (FS) to pairs realisable in the partition; allow ρ_n in a compact subset of (0,1); state c as any constant below c_D; give an O(n^{−c′}) rate for (C1).
3. **Moderate:** enlarge N₀ to cover ϑ(V) = 0 for V ⊊ S₀; allow slowly growing s₀, subject to T03♯.
4. **Hard:** block or multi-class exchangeable designs via a two-level gap invariant (REPORT.md:131-133); iid rows; n/2 < p ≤ n−1 with a swap-phase bound; non-Gaussian noise; polynomial rate for the path.
