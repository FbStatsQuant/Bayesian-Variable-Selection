## W3-POST:Thm-1 — Theorem 1 (STATEMENT.md:61-100; premises :16-39, :63-76)

The theorem is taken as correct. The heuristic calculations below are mine and not proved; I did not see [T03R], [P1] or [P3], so the proof-step attributions rely on ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:14-25.

### (a) Hypotheses

- **(F_w), STATEMENT.md:71-73 — stronger than needed.**
  - It enters only through the determinant bound and the exact χ² law for supersets in the middle range j₀ < |J| ≤ m♯_n (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:19).
  - By interlacing it forbids any exact dependence among O(log n) columns. That excludes duplicated columns, which are common for discrete predictors when p ≫ n.
  - E2 violates it with no visible effect.
  - Narisetty–He's Condition 4.5 uses the minimum nonzero eigenvalue, so it allows exact singularity.
  - The weaker determinant-ratio condition DG(c_D) (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:112-114) should suffice: a rank-deficient superset loses the Occam factor only on its redundant columns but still pays about 1/(2p) prior per column, so it is dominated by a full-rank sub-superset.

- **(F_λ), :68-70 — stronger than needed off the masking direction.**
  - Through interlacing it floors every set of size ≤ j₀, null-only sets included. E3 shows null duplicates are harmless.
  - What the underfit step needs is residual signal: ‖(I−P_S)μ‖² ≳ σ*² log n for underfits with |S∖S₀| ≤ j₀. This is Narisetty–He's Condition 4.4, and VIS at ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:114-115.
  - Some anchored identifiability is necessary. In E4 with γ = 1 the residual signal n·θ²(1−corr²) stays O(1) and consistency fails.
  - Consequently (F_λ) and (BM) are needed only through the product θ²_min·n·φ². The fixed φ₀ (n₀ depends on it, ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:18) rules out φ_n → 0 with a compensating signal, which Song–Liang's vanishing floor permits.

- **(BM), :74-75 — order necessary for this target, constant far from sharp.**
  - The g = n Occam factor forces nθ² ≳ log n even at fixed p (BIC-type). At p = n^A this matches the necessary √(log p/n) scale for support recovery (Wainwright 2009).
  - c₀ = max{4C_*, 32[s₀(A+1)+2]} is at least 128 at s₀ = A = 1. The deletion obstruction is 2a+2 (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:20).
  - With C_* ≍ A·j₀ (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:108-109), c₀ grows like A²s₀. In units of log p the threshold therefore grows with A and s₀. Yang–Wainwright–Jordan's constant does not depend on sparsity.

- **p ≤ n^A and s₀ fixed, :63 — technique restrictions.**
  - Narisetty–He allow log p = o(n).
  - Yang–Wainwright–Jordan allow s* ≲ n/log p.
  - Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart allow growing s₀ and general p.
  - No lower bound in the ticket shows either restriction is needed for the coded target.
  - s₀ = 0 is also excluded, although Lemma 1a′ covers t = 0. I cannot tell whether the chain needs s₀ ≥ 1 beyond [T03R] E2(ii).

- **(E) with fixed M, :67 — probably mildly improvable.**
  - Yang–Wainwright–Jordan's Assumption A reads, at g = n, ‖Xβ*‖² ≤ nσ² log p (ASSUMPTION_MAP.md:91).
  - That suggests M_n ≍ log p is the natural ceiling for g-type shrinkage. I am unsure whether the scale-event constants in [P1] survive it.

- **Gaussian noise.** All the comparators assume it too. The numerator identity uses the true density, so I see no cheap relaxation.

### (b) Conclusion

- **Rate, item 1 (:81).**
  - Proposition LB (:101-104) pins the rate to within √log n. Faster rates such as Yang–Wainwright–Jordan's O(1/p) belong to a different prior, so n^{−1/2} is a true barrier here.
  - The remaining factor looks like √(log p), not √(log n). The one-variable superset mass is about (s₀+1)/(2p)·n^{−1/2}·Σ_{j∉S₀} e^{χ²_j/2}. The variable e^{χ²₁/2} has tail about 1/(x√(π log x)), so by the weak law for truncated means the average over p near-independent nulls is 2√(log p/π)(1+o_P(1)).
  - This gives δ_n ≈ (s₀+1)√(log p/(πn)) on orthogonal-type designs with p → ∞.
  - So I expect:
    - at p = n^a with a > 0, the stated √(log n/n) is attained and the conjecture at STATEMENT.md:104 is true;
    - at bounded or sub-polynomial p, which regime R1 allows, the truth is O_P(√((1+log p)/n)), meeting LB exactly for bounded p.
  - E0's flat δ√n ≈ 2.1–2.6 over n = 100…6400 is consistent with the second point.

- **PIPs and thresholding, items 2–3 (:82-85).**
  - Bounding every PIP error by δ_n is lossy for growing p. The sum over nulls is of order √(log p/n), but the maximum is not.
  - max_{j∉S₀} PIP_j ≈ max_j π(S₀∪{j}) ≈ (s₀+1)e^{max_j χ²_j/2}/(2p√n) = O_P((n log p)^{−1/2}).
  - The misses 1−PIP_j for j ∈ S₀ are underfit mass, which should be polynomially smaller when c is large.
  - The condition r_n = o(min(q_n, 1−q_n)) could then become asymmetric, roughly q_n√n → ∞ on the lower side.

- **Mode of convergence.**
  - Only O_P is claimed, with "no expectation rate" (:85) and P(HPM ≠ S₀) → 0 without rate (:82).
  - δ_n/r_n has an intrinsic tail of order 1/L from e^{χ²/2}, so exponential-tail statements at rate r_n are impossible.
  - An expectation rate should hold. My heuristic gives Eδ_n ≍ √(log(np)/n) even at fixed p. That would yield P(HPM ≠ S₀) = O(n^{−q/2}), and the true order is plausibly (n log n)^{−1/2}.

- **Uniformity, item 6 (:95-100).** A statement valid along every deterministic array in the class {(E), (F_λ), (F_w), BM(c)} is equivalent to uniformity over that class. Stating Theorem 1 as a supremum over the class is a free strengthening and makes item 6 immediate.

### (c) Cheapest routes

1. **Uniform restatement.** Zero work.
2. **Rate √((1+log p)/n).** Replace the union levels τ_j = C_A·j·log n by C·j·(log p + ω) with ω fixed and large. The truncated mean of e^{χ²₁/2} is then √(log p + ω), and the |J| ≥ 2 terms stay geometrically smaller. This looks modest, provided the underfit and size-tail terms are already O_P(n^{−1/2}), which holds for large c and ω = ½ log n.
3. **Matching lower bound.** On an orthogonal design with p → ∞, p ≤ n−1, apply the truncated weak law to LB's one-variable terms. This closes the √log question (REPORT.md:192) with modest work.
4. **Expectation rate and HPM error rate.** Replace the Markov step on Λ in Lemma 1a (the 1/log L tail, STATEMENT.md:47) by χ² concentration and enlarge ω. The ticket rates this low risk (REPORT.md:186).
5. **DG in place of (F_w).** Low risk per REPORT.md:181-182.
