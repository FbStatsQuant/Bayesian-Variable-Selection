## C-MIS:Thm-MIS(i) — Theorem MIS (i), fixed candidate design

**(a) Hypotheses**

1. **"σ² fixed" and "τ² fixed" are not needed; this is the main finding.**
   - Where imposed: `STATEMENT.md:49` and `STATEMENT.md:55`. The only place they are used is `L1_reduction.md:47-50`, to meet T03♯'s "σ*² fixed".
   - Why removable: the shipped posterior is exactly invariant under y ↦ c·y, c > 0. The method standardises y (`STATEMENT.md:40`). Equivalently, the IG rate is b = b₀‖y_c‖²/N (`STATEMENT.md:146`, `L4_theoremF.md:32-36`), so in the score ratio (D_{S₀}/D_S)^{1/2}(1 + (t_S − t_{S₀})/(2b + Q_S))^ν (`L4_theoremF.md:86`, `L6_null.md:16`) the terms t, Q and b all scale by c².
   - Weaker condition that suffices: any sequence σ²_{𝒞,n} = σ_n² + τ_n² > 0, with ‖μ^𝒞‖² ≤ M·n·σ²_{𝒞,n} and beta-min stated relative to σ²_{𝒞,n} (it already is). Apply T03♯ to (X_𝒞, y/σ_{𝒞,n}), a G-model with variance 1 and truth θ^𝒞/σ_{𝒞,n}.
   - If τ_n² is merely bounded and σ² is fixed, the hypotheses as written already suffice, with M replaced by M/σ².
   - Consequences elsewhere in the ticket:
     - The caveat "GR additionally requires τ_n² ≡ τ²" (`STATEMENT.md:83-85`, `L2_gaussian.md:19-20`) disappears.
     - The fixed-σ_𝒞² assumption in GD(b) (`L2_gaussian.md:74-75`) disappears. The R02 "counterexample" there is a beta-min failure, not a failure of the transfer.
     - The "plausible but not claimed" remark at `L3_hpm.md:71-73` is settled for the posterior part.
   - Caveat: I infer the invariance from the score formulas quoted in the ticket, not from the T03♯ source.

2. **GR asks for more than Lemma R uses.**
   - The proof (`L1_reduction.md:33-36`, with `L1_reduction.md:20-22`) needs only that Uᵀ(ε + ξ) ~ N_N(0, σ_𝒞²I).
   - So the following can go: independence of ξ and ε; individual Gaussianity of each; a deterministic intercept a. Any random common shift, or an equicorrelated component ρ11ᵀ, is annihilated by C.
   - Independence of the uniforms (`STATEMENT.md:56`) plays no role for the exact posterior.
   - What cannot go is the scalar covariance of the contrasts with a deterministic γ. Example E2 (`L5_frontier.md:80-88`) shows the target changes otherwise.

3. **Gaussianity of ε′.** It is needed by the present proof, which uses exact χ² laws. I do not believe it is needed for the conclusion, since a sub-Gaussian version of T03♯ would transfer identically. Song–Liang's Theorem 4, as described at `STATEMENT.md:163-166`, covers some non-Gaussian omitted parts at fixed size. Removing it is hard, because T03♯ would have to be redone.

4. **s₀^𝒞 fixed and ≥ 1; p_𝒞 ≤ n^A** (`STATEMENT.md:61`). Both are inherited from T03♯.
   - They are weaker than the well-specified literature. Castillo–Schmidt-Hieber–van der Vaart, Narisetty–He and Yang–Wainwright–Jordan allow growing s₀ and, I believe, log p = o(n) up to sparsity factors, for their own priors.
   - Fixed s₀^𝒞 costs more here than in the well-specified case, because projection densifies the support (one-factor example, `L2_gaussian.md:112-115`).
   - s₀^𝒞 = 0 is covered only for bounded p_𝒞 (Lemma Z).
   - Improving any of this is hard, as it means extending T03♯.

5. **Beta-min.**
   - The order σ_𝒞² log n/n is necessary for this prior. With g = n, a single-variable Bayes factor is about n^{−1/2}e^{z²/2}, so detection needs z² ≳ log n. For p = n^a it also matches the known information-theoretic log p/n order for exact support recovery.
   - The constant c₀ is admitted to be a proof artefact (`L4_theoremF.md:135`). Tightening it is hard.

**(b) Conclusion**

- **Rate for p_𝒞 = n^a, a > 0.** √(log n/n) looks unimprovable for the shipped prior. My own heuristic (not a cited theorem): one-variable supersets carry mass about ((s+1)/2)·n^{−1/2}·p⁻¹Σ_j e^{χ²_j/2}, and for near-orthogonal nulls the average is of order √(log p) in probability. The polynomial rates in Yang–Wainwright–Jordan (1 − π ≲ p^{−c}, as I recall) come from a much stronger dimension penalty and g, so they reflect a different prior, not a better proof.
- **Rate for bounded or sub-polynomial p_𝒞.** Here the log factor is loose. The right rate is n^{−1/2}, exactly as Lemma Z obtains (`L6_null.md:35`). So Lemma GD(a)'s "with a rate" (`L2_gaussian.md:62-63`) is off by √(log n). The natural statement is O_P(√((1 ∨ log p_𝒞)/n)).
- **Mode of convergence.** "No expectation rate is claimed" (`STATEMENT.md:67`). Since 1 − π ≤ 1, a truncated-moment calculation gives E min(1, a·e^{χ²/2}) ≍ a√(log(1/a)). So E[1 − π] = O(r_n) looks reachable from (C5) (`L4_theoremF.md:91-92`) plus P(𝒢ᶜ) ≤ 5/n. That would give rates for P(S† ≠ S₀^𝒞), P(MPM ≠ S₀^𝒞) and Corollary H. I cannot confirm that T03♯'s Step 0 delivers it in expectation.

**(c) Cheapest route**

- Items (a)1 and (a)2: add a three-line Lemma R′ (scale invariance, contrast-only law) in `L1_reduction.md` §L1.1 and restate GR. This is trivial.
- n^{−1/2} for bounded p_𝒞: copy Lemma Z's uniform bound ‖(P_S − P_{S₀})e‖² ≤ ‖P_𝒞e‖² for supersets and reuse (C6)–(C10) for underfits. This is easy.
- log p_𝒞 refinement in general, and the expectation rate: both need T03♯'s (C5) and Step 0 reopened. Moderate.

## C-MIS:Thm-MIS(ii) — Theorem MIS (ii)/(ii′), random design, envelope form

**(a) Hypotheses**

1. **The same variance and GR relaxations apply, and go further here.**
   - f_n^L(x) is a fixed-design probability (`L1_reduction.md:108-111`). By scale invariance the conditional variance may be any measurable σ_𝒞²(x, n).
   - So ε′ need not be independent of X_𝒞 (`STATEMENT.md:58`, `L1_reduction.md:92-94`). It need only be homoscedastic within a design, with envelopes ‖μ^𝒞(x)‖² ≤ M·n·σ_𝒞²(x) and beta-min relative to σ_𝒞²(x).
   - This makes MIS(iii) valid for n-dependent Σ without the τ_n² ≡ τ² restriction.

2. **"P(𝒜_n) → 1 with fixed constants" is stronger than the proof needs.**
   - The bound P(Y_n > L·r_n) ≤ sup_{𝒜_n} f_n^L + P(X ∉ 𝒜_n) (`L1_reduction.md:115`) works with sets 𝒜_n^η and constants depending on η, where liminf P(𝒜_n^η) ≥ 1 − η. Then let η ↓ 0.
   - So constants (M, φ₀, g₀) that are merely tight suffice.
   - The deterministic envelope M_n is not a real restriction: it is equivalent to the beta-min ratio tending to ∞ in probability.

3. **s₀^𝒞(x) = s exactly** (`STATEMENT.md:75`). This can be 1 ≤ s₀^𝒞(x) ≤ s̄: split 𝒜_n into finitely many s-slices and apply Lemma DR to each. There is minor bookkeeping for slices that are empty at some n.

4. **Conditioning.** It could be on any σ-field containing σ(X_𝒞) and independent of the contrast noise. Lemma DR is abstract (`L1_reduction.md:78-86`). This would cover E2-type random-slope cases with a random target.

5. The fixed-s, p ≤ n^A and Gaussianity limitations are inherited as in (i). Improving them is hard.

**(b) Conclusion**

- The proof already delivers more than is stated. It shows lim_L limsup_n sup_{x∈𝒜_n} P(1 − π > L·r_n | X = x) = 0 (`L1_reduction.md:114`), a statement uniform over designs and hence over any parameter class sharing the envelope. That is closer to the uniform-in-class statements of Castillo et al. than the joint-law O_P at `STATEMENT.md:77`.
- The rate remarks from (i) carry over: sharp for p_𝒞 = n^a, loose by √(log n) for bounded p_𝒞 (GD(a)).
- No rate for E[1 − π] or P(S† ≠ S₀^𝒞) is possible without a rate on P(𝒜_nᶜ). Lemma GD(b) does give O(log n/n²) + 1/n (`L2_gaussian.md:105`), so with an expectation rate from (i) a rate would follow there.

**(c) Cheapest route**

- State the uniform-over-𝒜_n conclusion: zero new work.
- Insert the scale-invariance lemma and replace "τ² fixed" by a design-measurable τ²(x): trivial.
- Add the η-family version of the envelope hypothesis: two lines in §L1.4.
- Bounded-s̄ slicing: easy.
- Everything else (growing s, sub-Gaussian noise, log p = o(n)) is hard and belongs to T03♯.
