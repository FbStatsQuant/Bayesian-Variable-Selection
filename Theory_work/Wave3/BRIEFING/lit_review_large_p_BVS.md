# Literature review: how large a p has Bayesian variable selection (BVS) reached?

Compiled 2026-11-13 by a research sub-agent (effort high). Web search and fetch only. Every number below was read in the source text unless marked UNVERIFIED.

## 5-line answer

1. **Largest p for genuine joint BVS, in the model-space / PIP sense, run in the open literature: p = 7.5×10⁵** (n = 2,267). Jankowiak, *Bayesian Variable Selection in a Million Dimensions* (AISTATS 2023, "millipede", Subset wTGS). It took about 35 min for 50k MCMC samples on one Tesla T4 GPU. The design is real maize SNPs (p = 98,385) with N(0,1) noise covariates appended to reach 7.5×10⁵ (Sec. 7.1, Fig. 2). Jankowiak says the method scales "to P∼10⁶", but I found no run at P ≥ 10⁶ and no run on real correlated covariates at that size.
2. **p > 1M with a "decent" time has been done only with spike-and-slab-type mixture models fitted jointly on all SNPs, and not with model-space samplers.** Two cases: (i) GMRM, a BayesR-type MCMC mixture (Orliac et al., PNAS 2022): p = 2,174,071 SNPs, n ≈ 400,000, about 30 h on 24 CPUs. A later paper re-measured the same job at 82 h on 60 threads. (ii) gVAMP (Depope et al., Cell Genomics 2026), an approximate message passing (AMP) algorithm, not MCMC, with spike-and-slab prior and PIPs: p = 2.17M in 16 h on 32 threads, p = 8,430,446 "in hours", and p = 16,854,878 WGS variants (time UNVERIFIED). These are the only p > 10⁶ joint fits I found. Neither uses a model-space sampler with convergence diagnostics on PIPs. GMRM is exact-ish MCMC with a 4-class mixture; gVAMP is approximate (state evolution).
3. **Split-and-merge (Song & Liang 2015, JRSS-B) reached p = 500,000 (n = 100) but is category (b)**, not joint BVS on all p. The cost was about 16 h serial per dataset, or about 0.5 h on 100 cores. The same paper reports a full-data SSVS-horseshoe baseline: "up to one day to run only 20+ iterations". The classic MCMC/SSS/SMC literature (Hans–Dobra–West, Schäfer–Chopin, Zanella–Roberts, Griffin et al., Liang et al., MAdaSub, ARNI, S3) stops at p ≈ 10⁴–10⁵ (largest: p = 98,385 in S3 and p = 79,748 in Griffin et al.).
4. **GWAS summary-statistic or blockwise software (SBayesR, SBayesRC, LDpred2, PRS-CS, FINEMAP, DAP-G, SuSiE) routinely handles 1–7M SNPs, but all are category (b)**: summary statistics plus an LD reference, LD blocks or windows, or fine-mapping regions of ≤ 10⁴ SNPs. Runtimes are hours (SBayesRC 7M SNPs: 8.5 h on 4 cores; LDpred2 1.1M SNPs: 12–36 h on 30 cores).
5. **Bottom line for the project:** "BVS at p > 10⁶ in hours" exists only (a) as an approximate spike-and-slab fit (gVAMP), (b) as a BayesR-type mixture Gibbs sampler on a cluster (GMRM, about a day), or (c) as blockwise summary-statistic methods. A model-space MCMC or SMC with PIPs at p ≥ 10⁶ on correlated real designs, in hours, is not in the literature I could find. The gap is a joint-p ≥ 10⁶ model-space sampler with a convergence claim.

Search was web-based and not exhaustive. Absence of a result means "not found", not "does not exist".

## Notes and caveats

1. **p > 10⁶ and "decent time": what is actually established.**
   - *Joint, approximate, and fast:* gVAMP (row 3). Joint spike-and-slab on 8.43M SNPs, with PIPs available. The paper says "in hours". The one hard number I found is 16 h on 32 threads at p = 2.17M, and this is not MCMC. Whether its PIPs are calibrated is argued only in simulations (Note S1 of that paper; not read here).
   - *Joint MCMC, exact-ish, about a day:* GMRM (row 2). 30 h on 24 CPUs per the original paper, 82 h on 60 threads per the gVAMP paper. At 8.4M markers the gVAMP paper calls GMRM "computationally infeasible" (several days already at 2.17M). So GMRM-type MCMC is feasible at 2M, not at 8M.
   - *GPU model-space sampler:* millipede (row 1) tops out at 7.5×10⁵ with independent noise covariates appended. It does not reach 10⁶.
2. **The Song–Liang number.** p = 500,000 at n = 100 is real and verified, but the algorithm is split-and-merge. Each subset has only 500 predictors, and the second stage runs on the pooled survivors. It is not joint BVS on 500,000 predictors. The design is mostly independent columns (2,000 correlated at ρ = 0.25). The time is 16 h serial or about 0.5 h on 100 cores. I read this in Liang's lecture slides (Purdue STAT 598, part I4) and through a fetch of the OUP article page (Sec. 4.3), not in a PDF of the article itself.
3. **The classical MCMC / SSS / SMC literature stays at p ≤ 10⁵.** Largest verified cases: S3 p = 98,385 (per-iteration cost only), Griffin et al. p = 79,748 (2.5 h), ARNI p = 50,000 (simulated), Chung 2026 p = 10⁵ (60–75 min, one core), Zanella–Roberts p = 10,172 (115 s). Convergence to the posterior at p ≥ 10⁵ is demonstrated in none of these papers except through finite-run comparisons.
4. **Summary-statistic methods are not comparable.** They use p × p (sparse or blocked) LD matrices and avoid n. Their p of 1–7M should not be quoted as "BVS at p = 7M" in the sense of individual-level model-space sampling.
5. **Individual-level windowed MCMC.** A 2022 fine-mapping paper (Eur. J. Hum. Genet., "Fine mapping and accurate prediction of complex traits using Bayesian Variable Selection models applied to biobank-size data", about 5.5M SNPs, ~300k samples) was seen only in a search snippet. The snippet says the spike-and-slab regressions were run on overlapping 7,000-SNP segments. It is category (b). I did not read the paper (DOI not retrieved; UNVERIFIED).
6. **Not found.** I found no paper reporting a model-space MCMC, SSS or SMC sampler with PIPs at p ≥ 10⁶ on real correlated individual-level data, and no GPU BVS above 7.5×10⁵. I did not search every Bayesian GWAS tool (for example BayesR/BayesRR variants, GCTB individual-level, BGLR, ESS++, Skinny Gibbs, EMVS and BVSNLP scale claims, piMASS, BIMBAM). EMVS (Ročková–George) and Skinny Gibbs were not checked at all. Treat the list as a strong sample, not a census.
7. **Hardware comparisons.** GPU (T4), 24–64 CPUs and 128 cores are not equivalent. The "hours" in rows 2 and 3 are on multi-core nodes with ≥ 200 GB RAM.
8. **Quote provenance.** Rows 1, 3, 5–9, 12, 21–25 quotes are from article text or PDFs I retrieved (PDFs converted with pdftotext). Row 2 and row 4 quotes come via the fetch tool's page extraction (PMC, OUP) and slides; I did not open the PDFs. The fetch tool summarises, so quote wording is close to but not guaranteed to be character-exact for rows 2, 4, 20–26.
