1. **MINOR — `STATEMENT.md:43,59-60`; `lemmas/L1_bridge.md:41`. Evidence-update citations conflate computing the increment with adding it.** In the production `cpp/v3_engine.h`, line 320 computes `inc`; line 323 performs `logZ += inc`. The product formula is correct, but the statement cites 300 and 320 as if they identify the complete evidence update. L1 supplies the correct 323 citation later in the same paragraph. **Fix:** consistently cite `v3_engine.h:300,318-323` for the evidence product and distinguish increment computation from accumulation.

2. **MINOR — `lemmas/L2_post_terminal.md:42`. Temperature and stage subscripts collide.** The file uses stage-indexed `q_t` in its recursion, but then writes `q_T = q₁ = π`. With stage subscripts, `q₁` is generally the first intermediate target, not π when T > 1. The intended equality is to the temperature-one target. **Fix:** write `q_T = q_{φ_T} = q_{φ=1} = π`, or use different symbols for stage and temperature indexing.

3. **MAJOR — `numerics/n1_exact.py:161-165`; `numerics/RESULTS.md:28-39`; `lemmas/L2_post_terminal.md:56`. Four purported maximum row-sum checks are averages.** For |Ω| > 130, line 162 computes `abs(((1/M)1ᵀ R).sum() - 1)`, the error in the average row sum. It does not compute `max_x |sum_y R[x,y]-1|`; row errors can cancel. This affects d0, d1, d4 and d8, and their archived `round_checks.*.row_sum_err` fields. I independently applied the transposed-kernel DP to the all-ones vector, which computes R1 without constructing R. For d8 the maximum was approximately 1.33e-15, whereas the report gives 2.22e-16. The corrected value remains comfortably below tolerance, but the stated statistic and the blanket 5.6e-16 bound are unsupported as written. **Fix:** compute R1 by the transposed DP, or construct R, and regenerate the JSON, table and downstream bound.

4. **MAJOR — `numerics/cpp/n1_harness.cpp:11-12,143-159,173-181,214-220,244-246`; `lemmas/L1_bridge.md:119-121`. The extraction is only partly black-box.** Swap and independence acceptances are probed through production steps, but Gibbs probabilities are taken directly from the internal RB accumulator. Two endpoint calls at u = 0 and u just below 1 do not verify the transition threshold at `oj`: a wrong comparison threshold between those extremes could pass this extraction. The exported start law is calculated by `script_pi0` from internal cumulative-table widths, without calling `draw_pi0` in that export loop. Independence calls provide partial cross-checking of those scripts, but self-proposals and zero-width proposals are skipped, and `ScriptVS::all_used()` is never checked. Thus this is a hybrid internal-table/production-step calculation, not the advertised black-box extraction of every kernel and start law. **Fix:** bisect Gibbs outcome transitions too; execute and validate each positive-probability start script directly, verify its returned model and consumed script, and distinguish internal-table calculations from black-box checks in the report.

5. **MINOR — `numerics/cpp/n1_harness.cpp:128-139`. Threshold accuracy has no stated bound, and the returned threshold is not always exact.** The loop stops after at most 200 iterations, so a sufficiently small positive acceptance probability need not be bracketed by adjacent doubles. Returning 1 when the largest double below 1 accepts also rounds a possible threshold in that final interval to 1. The comment that the smallest rejected u “is alpha” requires qualifications about representability and convergence. This does not threaten the reported 1e-10 tolerance, but “exact” needs an explicit bisection/rounding qualification. **Fix:** return or track the final bracket and propagate its width, or continue until adjacent representable values and document endpoint rounding.

6. **MINOR — `numerics/cpp/n1_harness.cpp:48,86`. The “two blocks rho” design is not generated as described.** In kind 4, second-block coordinates use different `z[(j+1)%p]` values as their common component. They therefore do not share a block factor; the wraparound also creates a connection to a first-block noise coordinate. d3 and d8 are valid correlated designs, but they are not the stated two equicorrelated blocks. **Fix:** use one separately drawn common Gaussian for each block, or describe the actual dependence pattern and avoid claiming the intended block-design coverage.

7. **MAJOR — `numerics/cpp/n1_harness.cpp:248-265`; `numerics/RESULTS.md:10,50-129,176`; `lemmas/L3_freezing.md:47-48`. “Production grid” overstates calibration fidelity.** The harness disables the ledger and constructs V from the exact top-20p models over all Ω. This does not harm N1, which allows any frozen grid. **Fix:** call these “pilot-derived grids built with production build_grid on an enumerated V”, or run and archive the actual calibration path and its frozen record.

8. **MAJOR — `numerics/n1_mc.py:60-64`; `numerics/RESULTS.md:181-182,219-220`; `lemmas/R_remarks.md:20-22`. NC3 does not directly test the N1 centering.** It measures E[W(H(hω)−π(hω))], using the evidence-weighted target E[Wπ(hω)]. Marginalizing a proposed extension of N1 to a random post-run h instead gives the discrepancy E[WH(hω)]−E[π(hω)]. The two differ by E[(W−1)π(hω)], which need not vanish after selection. Consequently the reported NC3 differences and z-scores cannot simply be called biases of the N1 identity. The control is a meaningful test of a different selected-function weighted-centering identity. As a direct check, I recomputed the N1-centered discrepancy from the archived raw scratch data: d2, N=2 gives about 0.045914 (z≈96.2), so a correctly centered demonstration is available. **Fix:** report `W*Hm - pi[mode]` with its paired SE for an unconditional necessary consequence of N1; to demonstrate the conditional failure, use a specified calibration and show that this mean differs from zero. Alternatively retain the current statistic but label its distinct target explicitly.

11. **MINOR — `numerics/RESULTS.md:221-222`; `numerics/make_results.py:110-111`. The π₀ discrepancy is attributed to an RNG-grid effect that this calculation does not include.** `script_pi0` multiplies differences of stored cumulative doubles. Also, saying it “does not affect the residuals” is stronger than the evidence: the exported law is explicitly used in the residual calculation, and its relative error can be inconsequential because the affected masses are tiny. **Fix:** separate cumulative-table rounding from discrete-RNG effects; say the observed discrepancies coexist with the reported small absolute residuals, and quantify grid-point masses separately if claiming to test them.
